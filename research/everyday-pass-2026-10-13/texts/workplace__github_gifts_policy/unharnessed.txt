## Examples of acceptable and unacceptable gifts

So what does "of value" mean? Keep in mind that no gifts are okay for government officials (all right, maybe stickers). You should always ask them what their policies allow and don't allow. But if you're not sure, please run it past Legal to make sure. For example, plenty of U.S. congresspeople won't let you take them out for dinner and pick up the bill.

As for other customers or potential customers, some things might seem like small potatoes but be worth a lot to someone else. For example, getting your mate's daughter a gig as an intern might not seem like a big deal to you, but it could be worth heaps to them. But if she's not qualified and you pull strings to make it happen, then it's not okay. On top of that, if the idea is to improperly influence someone, anything at all, however small, might be considered "of value."

🙅 _Here are some other things that aren't okay:_

* First class airfare to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️+🍾
* Winery tours 🍷
* Recommending a mate's kid to a posh private school for admission 🎓
* Tickets to Disneyland 👸, the World Cup 🥅, the Olympics 🏅
* A soccer ball ⚽️ signed by a famous player
* A donation to a charity

🙆 _Here's some stuff that actually is okay for customers or potential customers:_

* Swag
* Economy class airfare to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️
* Cheap-seats tickets to a sporting event ⚾️
* A reasonably priced dinner in town 🍽
* A small box of chocolates as an end of year thank you 🍫

All of the above goes for _taking_ bribes too. So if a vendor or potential vendor sends you something "of value," don't accept it—or at least run it past Legal before you do.

## Process

If you're planning to give or accept an informal gift (one that isn't one of our promotional items like t-shirts and sticker swag), please make sure you follow the process below.

Keep in mind that what counts as an acceptable amount can vary. For example, spending $50 on dinner might be seen as a lot of money in some countries but not so much in others. So, if in doubt, ask!

1. Generally speaking, for any expenses over $150, you'll need approval (every time) from Legal in writing. And for any gifts you receive over $150, you'll need to report it (every time) to Legal in writing. (Legal is only weighing in on the bribery question. You might still need to check with Finance for other reasons.)

1. When you put in your expense report for reimbursement, please:
     * Note the recipient and the purpose of the gift in the notes; and
     * Include a copy of the email approval

## Consequences

What happens if you don't follow the policy and GitHub ends up shouting a potential customer and his family first class flights to Japan to go to the Tokyo Olympics? Or if you drop off a suitcase full of Euros for a government official in a pre-arranged train station locker?

FINES and JAIL. And not necessarily just for GitHub, either. You might end up doing a few years in the slammer yourself, and it won't matter whether the customer ever put in an order with GitHub after their trip to Japan, or whether the government official even picked up the suitcase of cash. Basically, it doesn't matter whether you actually _made_ a bribe. Yoda notwithstanding, it's the trying that counts.
