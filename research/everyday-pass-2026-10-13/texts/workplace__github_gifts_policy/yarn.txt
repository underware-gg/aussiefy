## Examples of acceptable and unacceptable gifts

So what does “of value” mean? Keep in mind that no gifts are acceptable for government officials (ok, maybe stickers). You should always ask them what their policies allow and rule out. But if you're not sure, please run it past Legal to make sure. For example, many U.S. congresspeople won't let you shout them dinner.

As for other customers or prospective customers, some things might seem like small potatoes to you but be worth a lot to someone else. For example, getting your mate’s daughter a job as an intern might not seem like a big deal to you, but it might be worth heaps to them. But if she isn't qualified and you pull strings to make it happen, then it isn't ok. On top of that, if the idea is to improperly influence someone, anything at all, however small, might be considered "of value."

🙅 _Here are some other things that aren't ok:_

* First class airfares to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️+🍾
* Winery tours 🍷
* Recommending a mate’s kid to a posh private school for admission 🎓
* Tickets to Disneyland 👸, the World Cup 🥅, the Olympics 🏅
* A soccer ball ⚽️ signed by a famous player
* A donation to a charity

🙆 _Here is some stuff that is actually ok for customers or prospective customers:_

* Swag
* Economy class airfares to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️
* General admission tickets to a sporting event ⚾️
* A reasonably priced dinner in town 🍽
* A small box of chocolates as an end-of-year thank you 🍫

All of the above applies to _taking_ bribes too. So if a supplier or prospective supplier sends you something “of value,” don’t accept it—or at least run it past Legal before you do.

## Process

If you're planning to give or accept an informal pressie (one that isn't one of our promotional items like t-shirts and sticker swag), please make sure you follow the process below.

Keep in mind that what counts as an acceptable amount can vary. For example, spending $50 on dinner might be seen as a lot of money in some countries but not so much in others. So, if you're not sure, ask!

1. Generally speaking, for any expenses over $150, you'll need approval (each time) from Legal in writing. And for any pressies you receive worth over $150, you'll need to report them (each time) to Legal in writing. (Legal is only weighing in on the bribery question. You might still need to check with Finance for other reasons.)

1. When you put in your expense claim for reimbursement, please:
     * List the recipient and the purpose of the gift in the notes; and
     * Include a copy of the email approval

## Consequences

What happens if you don’t follow the policy and GitHub ends up paying for a prospective customer and his family to fly first class to Japan and go to the Tokyo Olympics? Or if you drop off a suitcase of Euros for a government official in a pre-arranged train station locker?

FINES and JAIL. And not just for GitHub, either. You might cop a few years in jail yourself, and it won’t matter whether the customer ever put in an order with GitHub after their trip to Japan, or whether the government official even picked up the suitcase of cash. Basically, it doesn’t matter whether you actually _made_ a bribe. Yoda notwithstanding, it’s the trying that counts.
