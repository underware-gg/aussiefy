Is your bathroom cabinet full of out-of-date drugs or medications you don't use any more? The best way to get rid of most types of expired, unwanted or unused medicines is through a drug take-back program.

To dispose of your prescription or over-the-counter medicines, you can:

Drop them off at a drug take-back location.

Post them using a prepaid drug mail-back envelope.

If you can't use those options, follow these guidelines:

If your medicine is on the U.S. Food and Drug Administration's flush list, flush these potentially dangerous medicines down the toilet.

If your medicine isn't on the flush list, follow the instructions below to bin it at home.

Drug Take-Back Options

The U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration sponsors National Prescription Drug Take Back Day in communities right across the country. Plenty of communities also run their own drug take-back events. Check with your local police to find a location near you, or with the DEA to find a DEA-authorised collector in your community.

Some chemists have on-site medicine drop-off boxes or kiosks, mail-back programs and other ways to help you dispose of medicines safely.

Use Prepaid Drug Mail-Back Envelopes

A prepaid drug mail-back envelope is a handy and secure disposal option. These envelopes are sold in all sorts of places, including retail chemists and online. Some chemists hand them out for free.

To use prepaid drug mail-back envelopes:

Fill the envelope with your unused or expired medicines and seal it.

Post the envelope using the U.S. Postal Service.

How to Dispose of Medicines at Home

When a take-back option isn't easily available, there are two ways to dispose of medicines at home, depending on the drug.

Flushing medicines: Because some medicines could be especially harmful to others, they come with specific directions to flush them straight down the sink or dunny once they're no longer needed and there's no take-back option handy.

How will you know? Check the label or the patient information leaflet that comes with your medicine. Or have a squiz at the list of medicines recommended for disposal by flushing when a take-back option isn't easy to get to. Remember, don't flush your medicine unless it's on the flush list.

Putting medicines in the household bin: If a take-back option isn't available, almost all medicines, except those on the FDA flush list, can go in your household bin. These include prescription and OTC drugs in pills, liquids, drops, patches and creams.

Follow these steps:

Take the drugs out of their original containers and mix them with something nobody would want, such as used coffee grounds, dirt or kitty litter. This makes the medicine less appealing to kids and pets, and unrecognisable to anyone who might go through the bin on purpose looking for drugs.

Put the mixture in something you can close (a resealable zip-lock bag, empty tin or other container) to stop the drug leaking or spilling out.

Chuck the container in the bin at home.

Scratch out all your personal details on the empty medicine packaging to protect your identity and privacy. Throw the packaging out.
If you've got a question about your medicine, ask your health professional or chemist.

Disposing Fentanyl Patches

The fentanyl patch is one example of a product that contains a powerful opioid medicine that can be dangerous to anyone it's not prescribed for. This sticky patch delivers a strong painkiller through the skin.

Even after a patch has been used, heaps of the medicine is left in it. That's why the drug comes with instructions to flush used or leftover patches.
