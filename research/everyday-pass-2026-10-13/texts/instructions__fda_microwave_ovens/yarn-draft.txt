The microwaves are made inside the oven by an electron tube called a magnetron. They bounce around the metal inside of the oven till they're soaked up by the food. Microwaves get the water molecules in your tucker jigglin' about, and that makes the heat that cooks it. That's why food with heaps of water in it, like fresh veggies, cooks quicker than other stuff. The microwave energy turns into heat as the food soaks it up, and it doesn't make your food "radioactive" or "contaminated".

Even though the heat's made right there in the food, microwave ovens don't cook food from the "inside out". When you cook something thick, the outside layers get heated and cooked mostly by the microwaves, while the inside cooks mainly from the heat movin' in from those hot outside layers.

Cookin' in the microwave can use less energy than cookin' the usual way, because food cooks faster and the energy only heats the food, not the whole oven. Microwavin' doesn't knock the goodness out of food any more than normal cookin' does. In fact, food done in a microwave can hang on to more of its vitamins and minerals, because the microwave cooks quicker and without addin' water.

Glass, paper, ceramic or plastic containers get used in the microwave because the microwaves go straight through them. The microwaves can't heat those containers themselves, but they can still get hot from the hot food cookin' inside. Some plastic containers shouldn't go in the microwave at all, because the heat of the food inside can melt them. Generally you shouldn't put metal pans or alfoil in a microwave either, because the microwaves bounce off them, so the food cooks unevenly and you could stuff up the oven. The instructions that come with each microwave tell you what sort of containers to use. They also tell you how to test a container to see whether it's right to go in the microwave or not.

Not Gettin' Hurt by Super-Heated Water in the Microwave

The FDA's had reports in the past of bad skin burns and scalds on people's hands and faces from hot water bustin' out of a cup after it was overheated in a microwave. Super-heated water (water heated past its boilin' point) doesn't look like it's boilin', and it happens when you heat plain water on its own in a clean cup. If it's gone super-heated, the slightest bump or movement, like pickin' up the cup or tippin' in a spoonful of instant coffee, can set off a flamin' violent eruption, with the boilin' water explodin' out of the cup. Puttin' something like instant coffee or sugar in before you heat it cuts the risk right down.

Stick closely to the precautions and recommendations in your microwave's instruction manual, especially on heatin' times. When you're workin' out the best time settings to get your water to the temperature you want, make sure you don't go over the recommended heatin' times.
