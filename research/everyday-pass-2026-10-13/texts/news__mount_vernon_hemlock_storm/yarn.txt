Friday's big winter storm across the north-east of the United States has claimed a 227-year-old tree on Mount Vernon, the Virginia estate of the first US President George Washington. As of yesterday, the people running the Mount Vernon estate said the Canadian hemlock had been gathered up to be kept and used for special projects.

The hemlock, planted in 1791, was knocked over on Friday by gusts reportedly up to 70 mph (about 110 km/h).

Mount Vernon was Washington's own estate, where he worked through most of his two terms as president between 1789–1797, because the first White House wasn't finished yet. According to Mount Vernon officials, the hemlock is the best documented tree on the estate. It was sent by New York Governor George Clinton as a gift, delivered in a whisky barrel. The New York governor is no relation to the 42nd President Bill Clinton, but George Clinton did go on to become Vice President of the United States under the Thomas Jefferson and James Madison administrations between 1805–1812.

Other historic US trees to come down lately include the seventh President Andrew Jackson's southern magnolia at the White House, and the Pioneer Cabin giant sequoia in California's Calaveras Big Trees State Park, known for the car-sized tunnel cut through its trunk.
