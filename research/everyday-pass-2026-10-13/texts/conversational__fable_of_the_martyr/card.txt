unharnessed: A clean, faithful level-4 retelling with the satire intact; a British "chuffed" and a stagey "sheila" are the only blemishes.
  minor: 'So everyone was pretty chuffed' (for 'There was General Satisfaction'): "chuffed" is British; an Australian would say "rapt" or "stoked".
  minor: "If the sheila reckons he's all right" (for "If the Woman thinks he's All Right"): "sheila" is dated and reads as tourist colour in the moral, where plain "woman" or "missus" would sit better.

yarn: A natural, well-paced level-4 retelling that keeps every beat and the deadpan satire; only a small added tag and a stock "fair dinkum".
  minor: 'Talk about never satisfied.' (added): Added comment explains the irony and tilts it into a jab at the widow; the original lets the deadpan line and the moral do the work.
  minor: 'a fair dinkum good riddance of bad rubbish' (for 'it was certainly a Good Riddance of Bad Rubbish'): "fair dinkum" as an intensifier here is a stock bolt-on; an Australian would say "a bloody good riddance" or just "good riddance".
  minor: 'A good many punters reckoned' (for 'A good many People said'): "punters" means gamblers or customers; for townsfolk it strains a little.

engine: Largely the original with a few awkward swaps and filler openers; far too light for level 4 and clunky where it intervenes.
  major: "what Parliament House wuz thinkin' of" (for "what Congress wuz thinkin' of"): Localises Congress while the rest stays American (Uncle Sam, Alaskan Boundary); wrong referent.
  major: 'Mind you, Consequently, she was down in the dumps' (for 'Consequently, she was very Miserable'): Opener bolted onto a connective; broken grammar that spoils the punchline.
  minor: 'Mind you, he was willing to divide the Labor, however;' (for 'He was willing to divide the Labor, however;'): "Mind you" and "however" double up.
  minor: 'it was defo a Good Riddance of Bad Rubbish' (for 'it was certainly a Good Riddance of Bad Rubbish'): "defo" is British slang, not Australian.
  major: 'Once in a Country Town there was a Man with a Weak Back.' (for 'Once in a Country Town there was a Man with a Weak Back.'): Most of the text is left untranslated, far under a level-4 brief.
