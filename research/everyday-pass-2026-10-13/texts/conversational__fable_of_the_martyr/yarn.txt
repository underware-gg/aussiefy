Once, in a little country town, there was this bloke with a crook back.

Now, he could heave a grindstone up into a farm wagon if anyone wanted to bet him the cigars, but every time he picked up an axe, something grabbed him right in the spine and he had to go inside and have a lie-down. So his missus took in boarders and did all the cooking herself.

Fair's fair, he was happy enough to split the work, so he did the shopping. Only, once he'd bought the tucker, he'd plonk himself on a shoe-box with the basket between his knees and carry on about how he couldn't for the life of him see what Congress was thinkin' of.

He had his theories about the Alaskan boundary, and he was dead against any Anglo-American alliance, cos Uncle Sam could look after himself at any bend in the road, when you get right down to it, and the American people were better than every other nationality goin' in every way, shape, manner and form, as far as that goes. Then his missus would have to send word for him to get a wriggle on with the groceries so she could get the tea on.

Just about the whole town felt for her, stuck with a great useless lump of a husband who wasn't worth a cracker. Folks had her down as a proper martyr.

Well, one day the husband copped sunstroke, too much of a bludger to shift into the shade, and the next day he carked it without breakin' a sweat. The widow gave him the best send-off of the year, then scraped together every last bit of dosh she could find and sank it into a marble column with "At Rest" on it.

A good many punters reckoned she was better off without the lazy bugger, and it was a fair dinkum good riddance of bad rubbish.

They hoped that if she ever got hitched again she'd pick a bloke who wasn't scared of hard yakka and had the nous to pound sand down a rat-hole.

So every man and his dog was pretty rapt when she married Mr Gladden, who owned the general store. He built her a brand-spankin'-new house, got a girl in to help, and had the washing sent out. She could wander into the store and grab whatever took her fancy, and every evening he took her for a spin in his new runabout.

So naturally she was down in the dumps, pinin' for the treasure she'd lost. Talk about never satisfied.

MORAL: _If the woman reckons he's all right, you keep on your own side of the fence._
