Petrol (the Yanks call it "gas") is sold by the gallon, and the servos are mostly self-service (ya pump yer own gas), except in New Jersey, where self-service is flamin' illegal, and in Oregon, where servos in the cities are made by law to have at least 50% full-service pumps. Mind you, the American gallon's smaller than the imperial gallon: it's 3.785 litres.

Thanks to all the "drive offs" (people fillin' up and doin' a runner) in the crime waves of the 1990s and 2000s, near enough every American servo now makes ya pay before ya pump (it used to be the other way round). These days ya have to preauthorise a credit or debit card at the pump. Or else ya have to guess how much gas ya need, hand over that much of the folding stuff to whoever's on the till, and tell 'em which pump yer on (an American might say, "I need 50 bucks on pump number 1"). The pump cuts out by itself when the gas ya've put in hits the amount of cash ya handed over, or if ya guessed too high ya can go back in and ask for yer leftover dosh back.

Now, the U.S. octane scale isn't the same as the one they use in Europe: a regular gallon of U.S. petrol is rated 87 octane, which is about 92 in Europe. In most states the servos give ya three levels of octane to pick from: 87 (regular), 89 (midgrade or plus) and 91 (premium). Unless yer hirin' some flash luxury car, yer car'll prob'ly only need 87 (regular). If yer drivin' round the Rocky Mountains, ya might come across servos sellin' gas with a lower octane rating, usually 85. That's fine for 87-octane cars up high, but if yer headin' down to lower country, make bloody sure ya fill up with 87.

Diesel's not as common, but it's still used heaps and ya can get it at most servos, especially the ones that look after the truckies. Untaxed "offroad diesel", sold out in the bush for farm use, is dyed red (and sometimes called "red diesel"), and ya shouldn't put it in a car, cos there's whoppin' great fines if ya get sprung.

In the United States, the old colour code at the servo is that petrol comes out of nozzles with a black handle and diesel comes out of nozzles with a green handle. That's the opposite of the colour code in the United Kingdom. Don't get 'em mixed up, whatever ya do.
