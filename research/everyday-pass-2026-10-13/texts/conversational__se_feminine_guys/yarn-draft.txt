Okay, so. What's a feminine version of 'guys'?

I use the word 'guys' all the time for a group of blokes, casual-like. It's casual but it's not rude, off-puttin', condescendin' or patronisin' (though I wouldn't use it on a mob of blokes at a board meetin' (hypothetically, of course), unless I knew 'em). So here's the ones I wouldn't have a bar of as replacements:

- buddies: too old-fashioned

- dudes: too informal

- boys: too patronising

- men, gentlemen: too formal, or false respect

Then I twigged I'd also been usin' it for any group of people at all, males and females, even just females. It worked for me and I never gave the gender side of it a second thought, that women mightn't be too keen on it. No bugger ever whinged, not to me face or behind me back (rule of life: sometimes people don't whinge about things they bloody well should).

But it dawned on me (maybe from readin' somethin' that got the penny droppin') that the people I was talkin' to mightn't all be keen on it.

So what's a female-gendered or non-gendered version of 'guys', ay?

Here's some I've had a go at that don't sit right with me (though they might be fair enough answers):

- dolls: too old-fashioned

- babes: too informal

- ladies: too formal, or, I've heard from women, too creepy

- women: too factual

- girls: too patronising (as much as 'boys')

- everyone, you all: too bland

- 'y'all': perfectly non-sexist but too regional (might work, but not everywhere)

Hopefully I haven't knocked off every option -- anyone got any ideas?

"Guys" can be used in English as gender-neutral, for a group that's a mix of both.

You'll even hear women callin' other women "guys".

The closest thing in the language with a feminine lean'd be "gals". "Guys and gals" is a fair bit more casual take on "ladies and gentlemen". (Note it's the other way round.)

---

Edit: Like @kitukwfyer pointed out in the question comments, "gals" can come across as a put-down when it's used on its own, so watch yerself. It's not likely to be a problem in the pairin' above. Used carefully it's prob'ly safe, but on its own it could be heard as takin' the piss.

Sayin' this is unlikely to get anyone's back up:

> "The gals talked in the kitchen while the guys went out to see Fred's brand-spankin'-new ute."

But wanderin' up to a couple of women on a street corner and sayin' this might get you a funny look:

> "Excuse me gals, where's the supermarket?"

Sayin' this to a couple of girls might make 'em wonder what you're up to:

> "Hey gals, come with me."

But if you're explainin' the rules of some game and say this, it's likely to be fine:

> "Alright, guys on this side of the room, gals on the other."
