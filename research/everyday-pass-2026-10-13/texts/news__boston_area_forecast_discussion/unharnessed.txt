KEY MESSAGE 2...Might cop a stray shower Wednesday, but drier all round. Back to dry for Thursday, with temps on the cool side, about right for this time of year.

A trailing cold front slides across the region Wednesday, with high pressure at the surface building in from the west through the day. Model soundings show more mixing in the boundary layer, which backs up drier conditions and also westerly winds gusting 20-30 mph. That westerly, downslope flow, plus 925mb temperatures of +17- 18C, will give us a warm autumn day with highs in the 70s in plenty of spots. There's a hint of a stray shower or two as a weak wave wanders through, but it won't cover much ground and it'll be a light shower/sprinkle at most, because the lower levels are drier than a dead dingo's donger.

As the high pressure settles in further into Thursday, the airmass dries out even more. Thursday will most likely be dry, though cooler than Wednesday. A cooler airmass will have rolled in too, with 925mb temperatures about 10C cooler than Wednesday. That'll give us seasonably cool temps, mostly in the lower 60s.

KEY MESSAGE 3...Low pressure system expected to bring rain Friday into early Saturday. Gusty nor'westers and colder than normal.

Both the deterministic and ensemble guidance show a stronger/deeper northern stream shortwave trough shoving across the Great Lakes and the northeast Friday into Saturday. This system's got synoptic support behind it, with an upper level jet parking itself near southern New England, giving large scale ascent and also dragging a plume of wetter-than-normal air north into the region. That sets things up for widespread rain. Even with all that moisture about, instability looks pretty limited, so the chances of heavy rain doing much damage are slim. Ensemble guidance has totals generally from 0.25 to 0.60", with the ECMWF and Canadian leaning wetter than the GFS solutions. In those particular models, totals closer to an inch are on the cards. Those bigger numbers aren't out of the question if we can tap into better forcing with the warm front. The finer details, like exactly when the rain turns up and clears out, are less certain, as the guidance still doesn't agree. Rain most likely turns up some time Friday morning and keeps going overnight. Most guidance has this system moving along pretty smartly, so it won't be hanging about the whole weekend. Most of the rain likely clears out early Saturday morning. On top of that, the system's first southerly LLJ nudges in Friday, and if it mixes down enough, it'll bring breezy to gusty southerlies (gusts 20-30 mph).

A strong cold front barrels across the region by early Saturday morning, putting the northeast smack in a classic cold air advection pattern, with gusty NW winds, cooler temps and the odd shower. A NW 40-50 kt LLJ sets up across the region Saturday. The tighter gradient, and the CAA helping steepen lapse rates, will back up deeper mixing and so gusty NW winds. How much mixing we get and how strong the LLJ is are still a bit up in the air this far out, but the ensembles are showing a signal for gusts of 35-45 mph, maybe stronger over the coastal waters. 925mb temperatures range between - 2C to +2C Saturday, likely giving us the coldest temps of the season so far. Highs will probably struggle to get out of the 40s in the interior, with low 50s in the valleys and coastal plain. RH fields show some leftover moisture in the cyclonic flow pattern. That might be enough to back up a few isolated showers. Some of the higher spots across the Berkshires might only just scrape the 40 degree mark on Saturday. It's a bit early to be saying the "S word" just yet (snow). But...if the timing lines up, a morning flurry or shower can't be ruled out up in that higher country out west. Expect a bloody cold night Saturday night, with good odds of the interior dropping to at least freezing. The higher spots might drop into the upper 20s.
