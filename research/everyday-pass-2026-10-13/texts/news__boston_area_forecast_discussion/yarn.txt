KEY MESSAGE 2...The odd shower possible Wednesday, but drier overall. Dry weather's back for Thursday, with temperatures on the cool side for this time of year.

A trailing cold front pushes across the region Wednesday, with high pressure at the surface building in from the west through the day. Model soundings show more mixing in the boundary layer, which backs drier conditions and also westerly winds gusting 20-30 mph. Westerly, downslope flow together with 925mb temperatures of +17- 18C will make for a warm autumn day, with highs in the 70s in a lot of spots. There are signs of a stray shower or two as a weak wave moves through, but they won't cover much ground, and at most it'll be a light shower or a sprinkle, since the lower levels are drier.

As the high settles in further into Thursday, the airmass dries out even more. Thursday will likely be dry, though cooler than Wednesday. A cooler airmass will have moved in too, with 925mb temperatures around 10C cooler than Wednesday. That means temperatures about right for the time of year, mostly in the lower 60s.

KEY MESSAGE 3...Low pressure system expected to bring rain Friday into early Saturday. Gusty northwest winds and below normal temperatures.

Deterministic and ensemble guidance show a stronger/deeper northern stream shortwave trough pushing across the Great Lakes and the northeast Friday into Saturday. There's synoptic support for this system, with an upper level jet sitting near southern New England, giving large scale ascent while also drawing a plume of above normal moisture north into the region. That sets the stage for widespread rain. Despite heaps of moisture, instability looks limited, so the chance of heavy rain causing real trouble is low. Ensemble guidance puts amounts mostly from 0.25 to 0.60", with the ECMWF and Canadian leaning wetter than the GFS solutions. In those particular models, amounts closer to an inch are possible. Those higher amounts aren't out of the question if we can tap into better forcing with the warm front. Finer details, like exactly when the rain arrives and clears out, are less certain, as the guidance still differs. Rain is more likely to roll in around Friday morning and keep going overnight. Most guidance shows this system moving through fairly quickly, so it won't hang about the whole weekend. Most of the rain will likely clear out early Saturday morning. On top of that, the initial southerly LLJ with this system nudges in Friday, and if it mixes down enough, it'll bring a breezy to gusty southerly (gusts 20-30 mph).

A strong cold front pushes across the region by early Saturday morning, putting the northeast in a classic cold air advection pattern, with gusty NW winds, cooler temperatures and isolated showers. A NW 40-50 kt LLJ sets up across the region Saturday. The tighter gradient, and CAA helping steepen lapse rates, will support deeper mixing and so gusty NW winds. The degree of mixing and the strength of the LLJ are still less certain this far out, but the ensembles show a signal for gusts of 35-45 mph, possibly higher over the coastal waters. 925mb temperatures range between - 2C to +2C Saturday, likely giving the coolest temperatures of the season so far. Highs will likely battle to get out of the 40s across the interior, with low 50s in the valleys and coastal plain. RH fields show leftover moisture in the cyclonic flow pattern. That may be enough for a few isolated showers. Some of the higher spots across the Berkshires may only hit the 40 degree mark on Saturday. It's a bit early to be saying the "S word" just yet (snow). But...if the timing lines up, a morning flurry or shower can't be ruled out on that higher ground out west. Expect a cold night Saturday night, with a good chance the interior drops to at least freezing. Higher spots may drop into the upper 20s.
