# FOLD Token

This FAQ explains what FOLD does in the Interfold network. It's for token holders and for anyone
keen to run a ciphernode. The questions fall into three groups: the role of FOLD,
transferability, and ciphernode operation. For supply and allocation figures, have a look at
[FOLD Tokenomics](/tokenomics).

## The role of FOLD

### What role does FOLD play in the Interfold network?

The Interfold is a distributed network for confidential coordination. Applications ask for Encrypted
Execution Environments (E3s). Ciphernodes coordinate and secure each computation.

FOLD underpins the economic side of how the network runs.

### What is the utility of the FOLD token?

FOLD has three main jobs, and all three hang together: ciphernode bonding, protocol
incentives, and governance.

**Ciphernode bonding.** Ciphernode operators bond FOLD to become eligible for network duties. Bonded
FOLD stays in the `BondingRegistry` contract. When the bond owner (the wallet that funds the bond)
unbonds it, the FOLD sits in an exit queue before the bond owner can claim it. Bonded FOLD may be
subject to protocol-defined withdrawal periods. It may also cop penalties for provable
misbehaviour or for not carrying out assigned responsibilities. These mechanisms line up operational
responsibility with economic accountability.

**Protocol incentives.** Ciphernode operators get a cut of the fees from completed E3s. On top of
that, they may get FOLD incentives for successfully finishing protocol-defined duties as
members of an E3 committee.

**Governance.** FOLD is expected to back governance participation through the Interfold DAO.
Governance may cover decisions about network parameters, protocol upgrades, and treasury
administration. Governance processes may include secret-ballot mechanisms powered by the Interfold
network. For the current voting rules, see [Governance](/governance).

## Transferability

### When did FOLD become generally transferable?

General FOLD transferability could be switched on at or after Aug 19 at **14:00 UTC (10 AM ET)**.

At or after Unix timestamp **`1787148000`**, anyone could call the `tge()` function on the FOLD
contract to switch on general transferability. Once that transaction went through, FOLD became generally
transferable.

That transaction ran on Aug 19, 2026 at 14:00:23 UTC. `tgeTimestamp()` on the FOLD contract returns
`1787148023`, so FOLD is generally transferable now.

General FOLD transferability and Network Alpha are separate milestones in the wider Interfold
launch sequence. For more info, see [FOLD Transferability](/faq/transferability).

## Ciphernode operation

### How can I use FOLD to operate a ciphernode?

Running a ciphernode is designed to be permissionless. The setup comes in four parts:

1. Provision cloud-based or personal hardware.
2. Run the ciphernode software.
3. Bond FOLD to the ciphernode.
4. Complete on-chain registration.

Once the ciphernode is registered, it can take part in E3 committees when the protocol rules
pick it. To be in the running for committee selection, a registered ciphernode also needs a minimum ticket
balance. See [Tickets and sortition](/operate/tickets-and-sortition).

For current setup requirements, bonding instructions, and operator documentation, see
[Ciphernode Operators](/operate) and [Registration and bonding](/operate/registration).

### Can I delegate my FOLD to a ciphernode operator?

No. The Interfold's bonding model is operator-only, and that's on purpose. Every FOLD bonded to a
ciphernode is capital on the line for the operator who runs it.

Delegated capital would let operators run part-owned ciphernodes. That would water down
accountability and weaken the network's economic security guarantees.

In the `BondingRegistry` contract, each ciphernode has one bond owner, and only the bond owner can
bond FOLD to it. The operator sets the bond owner. The bond owner can be the operator address or a
separate wallet, for example a cold wallet or a Safe.
