Should you let a littlie win?

We've started playing a few simple games with my 2-year-old son, including a simple matching game that's right for his age.

He loves playing, but seems (to me, anyway) more keen on the playing than the winning. I reckon that's perfect.

Thing is, I noticed last night that he always wins.

Turns out my missus has literally been stacking the deck in his favour. When the game gets near the end, she starts having a squiz at the tiles, checking whether the next one dealt (she's the dealer) would be the winning card. If it'd end the game with either her or me winning, she pops it aside and picks the next one. In at least one game she apparently went and put all the tiles that matched her card right at the bottom so they wouldn't get drawn.

When I asked her about it, she said she reckoned he should win, and that winning would be more fun for him.

Leaving aside what it teaches him about cheating (let's say it was sneaky enough, and my son was distracted enough, that he had no idea the deck was being rigged), is there any problem with making sure he wins every time?

My worry, which I told the missus, is that I want him to enjoy playing games for the sake of playing, not for the sake of winning. I don't want him expecting to win every time and then getting upset when he doesn't. Is that a fair worry at his age, or is he just too young to care about the competitive side of games, and letting him win is nothing more than a harmless way to get him enjoying playing at this age?

If it's not a problem at this age, when does it become one?

What makes a kids' game a kids' game is that luck generally counts for heaps more than strategy. In other words, it doesn't take much for a kid to win fair and square.

I just had the exact opposite experience to yours with my five-year-old son. He got a new game for Chrissie where you flip over two cards to find a match. I made up my mind not to let him win, and he didn't for the first 10 goes or so. When he finally did win, he was over the moon, cos he knew I hadn't just handed it to him. Now he wins every third game or so, and he's learning to win and lose like a good sport.

When he's old enough for games that need more strategy, I still won't let him win, but I'll give myself a handicap to even things up. For example, when he learns chess I'll start out playing without a queen, but still do my best to win. That way winning is a real achievement for either of us, and watching my handicap shrink over time will be a real long-term achievement for him.

There's also the fact that the game has to be fun for the grown-up too, or they won't stay interested enough to keep playing. When the deck's stacked against you, it stops being fun. For example, when I play games with them, my stepdad often throws away his own position to help my mum win. I dunno if she doesn't notice or just doesn't care, but it fair takes the fun out of it for me.

I just read an interesting article on this. It basically says that trying to keep your kids happy all the time often turns them into unhappy adults, cos they can't cope with disappointment. It's a long article, but well worth a read.
