SHERIFF: Sweet bugger-all here but kitchen stuff.

(_The_ COUNTY ATTORNEY, _after another squiz round the kitchen, opens the door of a cupboard. He gets up on a chair and looks on a shelf. Pulls his hand away, sticky_.)

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Here's a beaut mess.

(_The women draw nearer_.)

MRS PETERS: (_to the other woman_) Oh, her fruit; it did freeze, (_to the_ LAWYER) She was worried about that when it turned so cold. She said the fire'd go out and her jars would crack.

SHERIFF: Well, wouldn't it! Locked up for murder and fretting over her preserves.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: I'd say before we're done she might have something a fair bit heavier than preserves to worry about.

HALE: Well, women are used to worrying over trifles.

(_The two women move a little closer together_.)

COUNTY ATTORNEY: (_with all the charm of a young pollie_) And yet, for all their worries, where would we be without the ladies? (_the women don't thaw. He goes to the sink, takes a dipperful of water from the bucket and, pouring it into a basin, washes his mitts. Starts to wipe them on the roller towel, turns it for a cleaner bit_) Grotty towels! (_kicks his foot against the pans under the sink_) Not much chop as a housekeeper, would you say, ladies?

MRS HALE: (_stiffly_) There's heaps of work to be done on a farm.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Fair enough. And yet (_with a little bow to her_) I know there are a few Dickson county farmhouses that don't have roller towels like these. (_He gives it a pull to show its length again_.)

MRS HALE: Those towels get filthy awful quick. Men's mitts aren't always as clean as they could be.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Ah, sticking up for your own, I see. But you and Mrs Wright were neighbours. I s'pose you were mates, too.

MRS HALE: (_shaking her head_) I've not seen much of her these last few years. I've not been in this house--it's more than a year.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: And why was that? You didn't like her?

MRS HALE: I liked her well enough. Farmers' wives have their hands full, Mr Henderson. And then--

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Yes--?

MRS HALE: (_looking about_) It never seemed a very cheery sort of place.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: No--it's not cheery. I wouldn't say she had much of a knack for homemaking.

MRS HALE: Well, I dunno that Wright had, either.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: You mean they didn't get on too well?

MRS HALE: Yeah, but nah, I don't mean anything. But I don't reckon a place'd be any cheerier for John Wright being in it.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: I'd like to have a yarn about that a bit later. I want to get the lay of the land upstairs now. (_He goes to the left, where three steps lead to a stair door_.)

SHERIFF: I s'pose anything Mrs Peters does'll be right. She was to take in some clothes for her, you know, and a few little things. We left in such a flamin' hurry yesterday.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Righto, but I'd like to see what you take, Mrs Peters, and keep an eye out for anything that might be some use to us.

MRS PETERS: Yeah, Mr Henderson.

(_The women listen to the men's steps on the stairs, then look about the kitchen_.)

MRS HALE: I'd hate to have blokes coming into my kitchen, stickybeaking around and picking holes in everything.

(_She straightens the pans under the sink that the_ LAWYER _had shoved out of place_.)

MRS PETERS: Of course it's no more than their duty.

MRS HALE: Duty's all well and good, but I reckon that deputy sheriff who came out to make the fire might've got a bit of this on. (_gives the roller towel a pull_) Wish I'd thought of that sooner. Seems a bit mean to have a go at her for not having things spick and span when she had to clear out in such a hurry.
