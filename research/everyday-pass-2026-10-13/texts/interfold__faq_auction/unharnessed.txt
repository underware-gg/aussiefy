the auction or who've got to claim FOLD after it. The questions are split into four lots: the auction
itself, participation, claiming and transferability, and safety.

## About the auction

### What is FOLD Auction 2?

FOLD Auction 2 was the second public FOLD auction. It ran on the Uniswap Continuous Clearing Auction
mechanism.

It came after the first FOLD Auction, which was held July 8–10, 2026.

Auction 2 was kept to **2% of total FOLD supply** on purpose. It was held round the time FOLD
switched over to general transferability. The point of it was to spread FOLD around more widely and
help get deeper, longer-term on-chain liquidity off the ground.

### Why was there a second FOLD Auction?

Auction 2 was a limited distribution step before general FOLD transferability kicked in on Aug 19.

It was kept small on purpose, at **2% of total FOLD supply**. It started with a floor that matched
the clearing price of the first FOLD Auction.

The auction gave a transparent, public way to hand out more FOLD before it became generally
transferable. It also backed long-term on-chain liquidity.

**50% of proceeds were locked in for long-term on-chain liquidity support.**

Auction 2 wrapped up not long before general FOLD transferability was switched on.

### What happened in the first FOLD Auction?

The first FOLD Auction ran from **July 8–10, 2026**. It used the Uniswap Continuous Clearing
Auction mechanism.

The first auction's done and dusted. Its clearing price was **0.02154816 USDC / FOLD**, which is
also the floor for FOLD Auction 2.

People who took part in the first auction can still get to claiming and post-auction info through
the official [Interfold auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction).

### What is a Uniswap Continuous Clearing Auction?

A Continuous Clearing Auction (CCA) is a way of working out the price when tokens get handed out.
It's built on Uniswap infrastructure.

- The auction runs over a number of separate periods.
- Participants put their bids in through the official Uniswap CCA interface.
- Winning bids in a period all clear at the same clearing price.
- The minimum price for each period after that is whichever's higher: the original floor price or
  the clearing price of the period before.
- The minimum price can go up in later periods. It doesn't drop below the relevant earlier clearing
  price or the original floor.
- Any tokens not handed out earlier are still up for grabs in the final period.

The CCA hands out tokens bit by bit and uses the one price for everyone within each period. It's
set up that way to cut down gas wars, stop sniping, and let clearing prices settle through people
taking part in the auction.

[Read more about Uniswap CCAs](https://cca.uniswap.org).

### Did the auction determine the price of FOLD after transferability?

Nope. The auction worked out allocations and a clearing price within the auction itself. It didn't
set the market price of FOLD when general transferability kicked off.

No one can tell you what the market price will do down the track.

## Participation

<Callout type='warning'>
  Auction 2 closed on Aug 19, 2026 at 13:06 UTC. The steps in this section show how the auction
  worked. You can't put in a new bid.
</Callout>

### How did participation work?

1. Participants headed to the official [FOLD Auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction).
2. They followed the verified link to the Uniswap CCA interface.
3. They got through the required verification process via the official flow.
4. They put their bids in during the auction window.
5. Once the auction closed, they could claim any FOLD they'd been allocated and any unspent USDC
   through the verified auction interface.

Participants needed USDC on Ethereum Mainnet. They had to read over the auction terms and
eligibility requirements before they had a go.

### Who could participate?

Anyone eligible could take part. That came down to jurisdiction, identity verification, AML and
sanctions screening, and other restrictions that applied.

The official verification process decided who was eligible. The auction terms set the rules on
eligibility.

Getting through verification didn't guarantee you were eligible or that you'd get an allocation.

### Was KYC required?

Yep. Participants had to get through identity verification via the official verification process
before they took part.

The verification process worked out eligibility and did the relevant AML and sanctions screening.

Taking part was subject to the auction terms and any restrictions that applied.

## Claiming and transferability

### When can participants claim FOLD?

Claiming was set to open straight after the auction closed.

Participants claim their allocated FOLD and any unspent USDC through the verified auction interface.

Claiming and general FOLD transferability are two separate things.

### When did general FOLD transferability begin?

FOLD Auction 2 closed Aug 19 at **13:06 UTC (9:06 AM ET)**.

General FOLD transferability could be switched on not long after, at or after **14:00 UTC (10 AM
ET)**.

At or after Unix timestamp **`1787148000`**, anyone could call the `tge()` function on the FOLD
contract to switch on general transferability. The TGE transaction went through on Aug 19, 2026 at
14:00:23 UTC. `tgeTimestamp()` returns `1787148023`. FOLD is now generally transferable.

FOLD Auction 2, claiming, general FOLD transferability, and Network Alpha are separate milestones
in the wider Interfold launch sequence. For the details, have a look at
[FOLD Transferability](/faq/transferability).

## Safety

### How do I know I’m using the official auction?

Only use auction, claiming, verification and support links put out through official Interfold
channels.

The official Interfold channels are:

- [Interfold auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction)
- [Interfold website](https://www.theinterfold.com/)
- [Interfold X account](https://x.com/theinterfold)
- [Interfold documentation](https://docs.theinterfold.com/)

Don't trust:

- Auction, claiming or support links that land in your DMs out of the blue.
- Token or contract addresses copied from dodgy, unofficial sources.
- Accounts pretending to be someone they're not.
- Private wallet-connection links.
- Anyone asking for your seed phrase, private key or wallet backup.
- Anyone asking you to pay for support.

<Callout type='warning'>
  Interfold team members and moderators will never, ever ask for your seed phrase, private key,
  wallet backup, or access to your wallet.
</Callout>
