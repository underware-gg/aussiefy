# The FOLD Auction

This FAQ covers FOLD Auction 2, the second public FOLD auction. It's for anyone who had a go in the
auction or who has to claim FOLD after it. The questions come in four lots: the auction itself,
participation, claiming and transferability, and safety.

## About the auction

### What is FOLD Auction 2?

FOLD Auction 2 was the second public FOLD auction. It ran on the Uniswap Continuous Clearing Auction
mechanism.

It came after the first FOLD Auction, which ran July 8–10, 2026.

Auction 2 was kept to **2% of total FOLD supply** on purpose. It was held around the switch to
general FOLD transferability. The idea was to spread FOLD round more widely and help get deeper,
longer-term on-chain liquidity up and running.

### Why was there a second FOLD Auction?

Auction 2 was a limited distribution step before general FOLD transferability on Aug 19.

It was kept small on purpose, at **2% of total FOLD supply**. It started with a floor that matched the
clearing price of the first FOLD Auction.

The auction gave an open, public way to hand out more FOLD before it became generally transferable,
with everything above board. It also backed long-term on-chain liquidity.

**50% of proceeds were committed to long-term on-chain liquidity support.**

Auction 2 closed not long before general FOLD transferability was switched on.

### What happened in the first FOLD Auction?

The first FOLD Auction ran from **July 8–10, 2026**. It used the Uniswap Continuous Clearing
Auction mechanism.

The first auction's done and dusted. Its clearing price was **0.02154816 USDC / FOLD**, which is also the
floor for FOLD Auction 2.

Participants from the first auction can still get to claiming and post-auction information
through the official [Interfold auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction).

### What is a Uniswap Continuous Clearing Auction?

A Continuous Clearing Auction (CCA) is a way of working out the price when tokens are handed out. It's
built on Uniswap infrastructure.

- The auction runs over a number of separate periods.
- Participants put in bids through the official Uniswap CCA interface.
- Successful bids within a period all clear at the same clearing price.
- The minimum price for each period after that is whichever's higher: the original floor price or the
  clearing price of the period before.
- The minimum price can go up in later periods. It won't drop below the relevant earlier clearing
  price or the original floor.
- Any tokens not handed out earlier are still up for grabs in the final period.

The CCA hands out tokens bit by bit and charges the one price within each period. It's set up this way
to cut down on gas competition, take the sting out of sniping, and let clearing prices settle through
the bidding itself.

[Read more about Uniswap CCAs](https://cca.uniswap.org).

### Did the auction determine the price of FOLD after transferability?

No. The auction worked out allocations and a clearing price within the auction itself. It didn't
set the market price of FOLD when general transferability kicked off.

There's no telling what future market prices will be.

## Participation

<Callout type='warning'>
  Auction 2 closed on Aug 19, 2026 at 13:06 UTC. The steps in this section show how the auction
  worked. You can't put in a new bid.
</Callout>

### How did participation work?

1. Participants went to the official [FOLD Auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction).
2. They followed the verified link through to the Uniswap CCA interface.
3. They got through the required verification process via the official flow.
4. They put in bids while the auction was open.
5. Once the auction closed, they could claim any FOLD allocated to them and any unspent USDC through the
   verified auction interface.

Participants needed USDC on Ethereum Mainnet. They had to go over the auction terms and eligibility
requirements before they had a go.

### Who could participate?

Participation was open to eligible participants. It depended on jurisdiction, identity
verification, AML and sanctions screening, and any other restrictions that applied.

The official verification process decided eligibility. The auction terms governed eligibility.

Getting through verification didn't guarantee eligibility or an allocation.

### Was KYC required?

Yes. Participants had to get through identity verification via the official verification process
before they took part.

The verification process decided eligibility and did whatever AML and sanctions screening applied.

Participation was subject to the auction terms and any restrictions that applied.

## Claiming and transferability

### When can participants claim FOLD?

Claiming was set to open straight after the auction closed.

Participants claim their allocated FOLD and any unspent USDC through the verified auction interface.

Claiming and general FOLD transferability are two separate things.

### When did general FOLD transferability begin?

FOLD Auction 2 closed Aug 19 at **13:06 UTC (9:06 AM ET)**.

General FOLD transferability could be switched on not long after, at or after **14:00 UTC (10 AM
ET)**.

At or after Unix timestamp **`1787148000`**, anyone could call the `tge()` function on the FOLD
contract to switch on general transferability. The TGE transaction went through on Aug 19, 2026 at
14:00:23 UTC. `tgeTimestamp()` returns `1787148023`. FOLD is now generally transferable.

FOLD Auction 2, claiming, general FOLD transferability, and Network Alpha are separate milestones
in the bigger Interfold launch sequence. For the details, see
[FOLD Transferability](/faq/transferability).

## Safety

### How do I know I’m using the official auction?

Only use auction, claiming, verification and support links put out through official Interfold
channels.

The official Interfold channels are:

- [Interfold auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction)
- [Interfold website](https://www.theinterfold.com/)
- [Interfold X account](https://x.com/theinterfold)
- [Interfold documentation](https://docs.theinterfold.com/)

Don't trust:

- Auction, claiming or support links sent in DMs out of the blue.
- Token or contract addresses copied from unofficial sources.
- Accounts pretending to be someone they're not.
- Private wallet-connection links.
- Anyone asking for your seed phrase, private key or wallet backup.
- Anyone asking you to pay for support.

<Callout type='warning'>
  Interfold team members and moderators will never ask for your seed phrase, private key, wallet
  backup, or access to your wallet.
</Callout>
