DETROIT, Dec. 13 (AP).—Henry Ford's first big crack at his already announced push to teach the American public the old-fashioned square dances will come off on Tuesday night at one of the biggest dance halls in Detroit. For the occasion Mr. Ford is advertising for couples to dance the square dance and make up the core of the do.

Asked tonight whether he reckoned the old-fashioned steps would really make a comeback, Mr. Ford exclaimed:

"Come back? They're here! We simply are falling back in line. What we are trying to do in getting out a book of dances is to standardise them. The first edition is being revised in the direction of simplicity."

Of the twenty-one quadrilles, Mr. Ford explained, four will be picked out to make them easier to teach and help them catch on.

"Mellie" Dunham, the champion fiddler of Maine, who's been Mr. Ford's guest for a week, playing for an old-fashioned dance party and chipping in several old-time tunes for the Ford "Americana" of such pieces now being got ready for publication, was meant to turn out for the Tuesday night dance. But plans changed, and "Grandpa Mellie," with his missus, is on his way tonight to New York, where he may answer the call of the footlights and take up a vaudeville contract.

"Mellie" Dunham will get into New York this morning. It was reported last night that he'll be under the management of Tex Rickard, who's said to be planning a barn dance in his honour at the brand-spankin'-new Madison Square Garden. It was added that putting on the dance was hoped to further Henry Ford's aim of making square dances popular again.
