Ingredients

2 sticks (½ lb / 225 g / 1 cup) butter, softened

¾ cup (180 ml) white sugar

¾ cup (180 ml) packed brown sugar

¼ teaspoon vanilla extract

2 standard (100 g) eggs

2 ¼ cups (600 ml / 290 g) plain flour

1 teaspoon bicarb soda

1 teaspoon salt

1 teaspoon dry ground lemon or orange peel (optional)

2 cups (480 ml / 12 oz) semi-sweet (dark) choc bits / chips

5 oz (150 g) chopped nuts, such as groundnuts (optional)

Method

Preheat the oven to 375°F (210°C), or 350°F (195°C) if you want chewy bikkies.

Cream the butter, white sugar and brown sugar in a big mixing bowl.

Add the eggs one at a time, beating till they're properly mixed in.

Add the vanilla extract and beat it in.

In a small bowl, sift together the dry ingredients (not the choc bits or nuts). Add them to the wet mix in two lots and fold till combined. If you beat it for too long, you can get some gluten forming, which will change the texture of the bikkies.

Stir in the choc bits (chips), and the nuts if you're using them.

Drop the dough by heaped tablespoons onto ungreased baking trays. There should be enough fat in the dough to stop it sticking, but baking paper or non-stick trays can help if you find it does stick.

Bake in the preheated oven for 10–12 minutes or till golden brown (cooking times can vary). Let them sit for about 1 minute, then take them off the hot tray to cool on a wire rack.

Notes, tips and variations

Ingredients

Use cake flour instead of plain, swap the white sugar for brown sugar, and use butter instead of marg. Cake flour and brown sugar let you use butter without your bikkies turning into puddles (which is the only reason to use marg in the first place).

Use some vegetable shortening in place of the butter/marg to cut down the "spread" (the biscuits will be thicker).

Add some vegetable oil (no more than 1/5 of the total fat) instead of the butter/marg to increase the spread, so you get thinner bikkies.

Chocolate with a cocoa content over 65% will taste quite a bit different from the "traditional" recipe. Generally, if you find the chocolate too bitter or not much chop to eat on its own, you mightn't like it in the bikkies either (no matter how good it is).

A few shakes of ground cinnamon with the dry ingredients gives them more flavour too.

If you want to use regular flour as well as butter, the trick is to bump the flour up to 3+ cups, which should give you a fairly stiff dough that won't spread out heaps. This trick even works with wholemeal flour.

You can change the mix of white to brown sugar to suit your taste. More brown sugar gives chewier biscuits. More white sugar gives crisper ones.

More vanilla extract (between ½ and ¾ of a teaspoon, rather than ¼) gives the bikkies a stronger flavour.

If you can't get hold of vanilla extract, use two teaspoons of vanilla sugar in place of the same amount of white sugar.

Add a small amount of rolled oats to give the bikkies more flavour and a better feel in the mouth.

Method

Some people also reckon you should chill the dough before baking, though you usually don't need to. Chilling the dough can help keep the spread down, so you get thicker bikkies.

Use an ungreased, non-stick baking tray instead of a greased one to help rein in the spread, for thicker biscuits. The other way round, grease your tray if you like thinner (crispier) bikkies.

The quality of the flavouring ingredients makes a big difference to how they turn out. Top-quality butter, choccy and vanilla will make a better bikkie.

Chopped block chocolate instead of ready-made chips gives a more rustic texture -- just make sure the pieces are small enough to eat comfortably.

Don't stack hot bikkies, or they'll bend out of shape and won't cool as quick. Use a cooling rack instead for the best results.

For a stronger nut flavour, toast the nuts. Spread them out evenly on a baking tray and put them in the 350°F oven for ten minutes. Take them out and let them cool before you add them to the dough.

If you like a soft and squishy middle in your bikkies, take them out one minute sooner for a nice soft middle.
