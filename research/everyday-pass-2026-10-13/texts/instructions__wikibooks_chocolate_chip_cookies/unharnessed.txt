Ingredients

2 sticks (½ lb / 225 g / 1 cup) butter, softened

¾ cup (180 ml) white sugar

¾ cup (180 ml) packed brown sugar

¼ teaspoon vanilla extract

2 standard (100 g) eggs

2 ¼ cups (600 ml / 290 g) plain flour

1 teaspoon bicarb soda

1 teaspoon salt

1 teaspoon dried ground lemon or orange peel (optional)

2 cups (480 ml / 12 oz) dark choc bits / chips

5 oz (150 g) chopped nuts, like peanuts (optional)

Procedure

Get the oven heating to 375°F (210°C), or 350°F (195°C) if you want chewy bikkies.

Cream the butter, white sugar and brown sugar in a big mixing bowl.

Add the eggs one at a time, beating till they're properly mixed in.

Chuck in the vanilla and beat it through.

In a small bowl, sift together the dry ingredients (not the choc or the nuts). Add to the wet mix in two lots and fold till it's all combined. If you beat this too long, you might get some gluten forming, which'll change the texture of your bikkies.

Stir in the choc bits (chips), and the nuts if you're using them.

Drop the dough by heaped tablespoons onto ungreased baking trays. There should be enough fat in the dough to stop it sticking, but baking paper or non-stick trays might help if you find that's not the case.

Bake in the hot oven for 10–12 minutes or till golden brown (cooking times can vary). Let them sit for about 1 minute, then take them off the hot tray to cool on a wire rack.

Notes, tips, and variations

Ingredients

Use cake flour instead of plain, swap the white sugar for brown sugar and use butter instead of marg. Using cake flour and brown sugar means you can use butter without worrying about your bikkies turning into puddles (which is the only reason to use marg in the first place).

Use some vegetable shortening instead of butter/marg to cut down the "spread" (bikkies will come out thicker).

Add some vegetable oil (no more than 1/5 of the total fat) instead of butter/marg to bump up the spread, so you get thinner bikkies.

Using chocolate with more than 65% cocoa will give you a pretty different taste from the "traditional" recipe. Generally, if you reckon the chocolate's too bitter or not much chop to eat on its own, you probably won't like it in the bikkies either (no matter how good it is).

A few shakes of ground cinnamon in with the dry ingredients gives them more flavour too.

If you want to use regular flour as well as butter, the trick is to bump the flour up to 3+ cups, which should give you a fairly stiff dough that won't spread out much. This trick even works with wholemeal flour.

You can tweak how much white sugar to brown sugar to suit your taste. More brown sugar makes chewier bikkies. More white sugar makes crispier bikkies.

Putting in more vanilla (between ½ and ¾ of a teaspoon, rather than ¼) gives the bikkie a stronger flavour.

If you can't easily get vanilla extract, swap two teaspoons of the white sugar for vanilla sugar.

Add a little bit of rolled oats to give your bikkies more flavour and a better feel in the mouth.

Method

Some people reckon you should chill the dough before baking, though you usually don't need to. Chilling the dough can help cut down the spread, so you get thicker bikkies.

Use an ungreased, non-stick baking tray instead of a greased one to help cut the spread, for thicker bikkies. On the flip side, grease your tray if you like them thinner (crispier).

The quality of your flavouring ingredients makes a big difference to how they turn out. Top-shelf butter, chocolate and vanilla will make a cracker of a bikkie.

Using chopped-up block chocolate instead of ready-made chips gives a more rustic texture -- just make sure the bits are small enough to eat comfortably.

Don't stack hot bikkies or they'll bend out of shape and won't cool as quick. Use a cooling rack instead for the best results.

For a stronger nutty flavour, toast the nuts. Spread them out evenly on a baking tray and pop them in the 350°F oven for ten minutes. Take them out and let them cool before adding them to the dough.

If you like a soft, squishy middle in your bikkie, pull them out one minute earlier for a nice soft middle.
