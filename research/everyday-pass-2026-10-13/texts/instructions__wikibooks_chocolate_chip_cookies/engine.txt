Ingredients

2 sticks (½ lb / 225 g / 1 cup) butter, softened

¾ cup (180 ml) granulated white sugar

¾ cup (180 ml) packed brown sugar

¼ teaspoon vanilla extract

2 standard (100 g) eggs

2 ¼ cups (600 ml / 290 g) plain flour

1 teaspoon bicarb soda

1 teaspoon salt

1 teaspoon dry ground lemon or orange peel (optional)

2 cups (480 ml / 12 oz) semi-sweet choc bits / chips

5 oz (150 g) chopped nuts, such as groundnuts (optional)

Procedure

Preheat oven to 375°F (210°C), or 350°F (195°C) if you want chewy biscuits.

Cream butter, granulated sugar, and brown sugar in a large mixer bowl.

Add eggs one at a time, beating until fully incorporated.

Add vanilla extract and beat in.

In a small bowl, sift together the dry ingredients (except choccy and nuts). Add to the wet mixture in two parts and fold until combined. If you beat this too long, you may get some gluten formation which will change the biscuit texture.

Stir in chocolate morsels (chips), and nuts if using.

Drop dough by heaped tablespoons onto ungreased baking trays. There should be enough fat in the bikkie dough to prevent sticking, but using baking paper or non-stick pans may help if you find this not to be the case.

Bake in the preheated oven for 10–12 minutes or until golden brown (cooking times may vary). Let stand for about 1 minute and remove from hot baking tray to cool on a wire rack.

Notes, tips, and variations

Ingredients

Use cake flour instead of all-purpose, replace granulated sugar with brown sugar and use butter instead of marg. Using cake flour and brown sugar allows you to use butter without fear of your bikkies becoming puddles (which is the only reason to use marg in the first place).

Use some vegetable shortening in place of butter/marg to reduce "spread" (biscuits will be thicker).

Add some vegetable oil (no more than 1/5 of total fat) in place of butter/margarine to increase spread, resulting in thinner bikkies.

Using chocolate with a cocoa content greater than 65% will result in a significantly different taste than the "traditional" recipe. In general, if you find the raw chocolate to be too bitter or not enjoyable to eat, you may not like them in the bikkies (no matter the quality).

Adding a few shakes of ground cinnamon with the dry ingredients also gives more flavour.

If you want to use regular flour as well as butter, the trick is to increase the amount of flour to 3+ cups, which should result in a fairly stiff dough that will not spread out heaps. This trick even works with wholemeal flour.

The proportion of granulated to brown sugar may be adjusted to suit taste. More brown sugar will result in chewier bickies. More white sugar will result in crisper biscuits.

Adding a greater quantity of vanilla extract (between ½ and ¾ of a teaspoon, rather than ¼) gives the bickie a more pronounced flavour.

If vanilla extract is not readily available, substitute two teaspoons of vanilla sugar for granulated sugar.

Add a small amount of oatmeal to give biscuits more flavour and mouth-feel.

Method

Some punters also recommend chilling dough before baking, though this is usually not necessary. Chilling dough may help to reduce spread, resulting in thicker bikkies.

Use an ungreased, non-stick baking tray instead of a greased sheet to help reduce spread, for thicker biscuits. Conversely, grease your sheet if you like thinner (crispier) bikkies.

The quality of the flavouring ingredients makes a great difference in the outcome. Using top-quality butter, choccy, and vanilla will produce a superior biscuit.

Using chopped bar choccy instead of pre-formed chips will result in a more rustic texture -- just make sure the pieces are small enough to eat comfortably.

Do not stack hot bickies otherwise they will bend out of shape and not cool as quickly. Use a cooling rack instead for the best results.

For a stronger nut flavour, toast the nuts. Spread them evenly on a baking pan and set them in the 350°F oven for ten minutes. Take them out and let them cool before adding them to the dough.

If you like a soft and squishy middle in your bickie, take them out one minute sooner for a nice soft middle.
