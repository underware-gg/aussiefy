What's gotta be done before ya start the Car?

Before ya give the car a burl, fill the radiator (take the cap off the top) with clean fresh water. If ya can't lay yer mitts on perfectly clean water, it's a good idea to strain it through muslin or some other cloth like it, so no muck gets in 'n blocks up the little tubes in the radiator. The system holds about three gallons. Don't ever run the car under its own power till the water circulatin' system's been filled, and that's important. Keep pourin' the water in till yer sure both the radiator and the cylinder water jackets are full. When the whole water system's properly full, the water'll run out the overflow pipe onto the ground. For the first few days ya drive a brand-spankin'-new car, it's a beaut idea to check the radiator often and make sure it's kept properly topped up. Top the water up as often as ya find ya need to. Soft rain water, if ya can get it clean, beats hard water hands down, cos hard water can have alkalies 'n other salts in it that drop sediment 'n clog up the radiator. (See chapter on Cooling System.)

What about Petrol?

The ten-gallon petrol tank wants fillin' (nearly full), and don't ever let it get low. When yer fillin' the tank, make bloody sure there's no naked flames within several feet, cos the vapour's flamin' volatile 'n travels fast. Always be careful strikin' matches anywhere near where petrol's been spilt, cos the air for several feet around is full of that highly explosive vapour. Don't let the little vent hole in the petrol tank cap get bunged up, cos that'd stop the petrol flowin' proper to the carburettor. Ya can drain the petrol tank by openin' the pet cock in the sediment bulb at the bottom.

How about the Oilin' System?

Soon as ya get the car, see that a good lot of medium light, top-shelf petrol engine oil gets poured into the crank case through the breather pipe at the front of the engine (there's a metal cap over it). Down under the car in the flywheel casing (the reservoir that holds this oil) you'll find two pet cocks. Pour the oil in slow till it runs out the upper cock. Leave the cock open till it stops runnin', then shut it. Once the engine's properly limbered up, ya get the best results keepin' the oil at a level halfway between the two cocks, but don't ever, come hell or high water, let it get below the lower cock. Every other part of the car's properly oiled when it leaves the factory. Still, it's worth checkin' that all the grease cups are filled and the parts that need oil are gettin' it. (See Cut No. 18, also chapter on Lubrication.)

How do ya use the Spark and Throttle Levers?

Under the steerin' wheel there's two little levers. The right-hand (throttle) lever controls how much mixture (petrol 'n air) goes into the engine. With the engine goin', the further down toward the driver ya move this lever (that's called "openin' the throttle"), the faster the engine runs 'n the more grunt ya get. The left-hand lever controls the spark, which sets off the gas in the engine's cylinders. Movin' this lever "advances the spark," and ya want to bring it down notch by notch till the motor seems to hit its top speed. If ya push the lever past that point, you'll notice a dull knock in the engine. (See chapter on Ignition.)

Where do these levers go when ya start the Engine?

The spark lever usually goes in about the third or fourth notch of the quadrant (the notched half-circle the levers work on). The throttle usually wants openin' about five or six notches. A bit of practice'll soon teach ya where to put these levers to start her proper. Watch ya don't advance the spark lever too far, cos the engine can "back kick."
