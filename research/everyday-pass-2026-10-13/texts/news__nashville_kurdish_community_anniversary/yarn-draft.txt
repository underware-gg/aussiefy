The Metropolitan Council of Nashville and Davidson County, in the south-eastern US state of Tennessee, has officially marked 50 years since the first group of Kurdish families arrived in the US, honouring what they've given the city culturally, socially and economically.

According to an official proclamation obtained by VOA, the first group of Kurdish families landed in the United States on 1 May 1976. Many later settled in Nashville, Tennessee, while others set up communities in cities and states right across the country.

The proclamation singles out the Kurdish community's efforts to hang on to its language, culture and traditions over the past five decades, while playing a big part in Nashville's growth and development.

According to the document, Nashville is now home to more than 20,000 Kurds, making it one of the biggest Kurdish communities in the United States. The city has become an important hub for Kurdish cultural and community life.

The proclamation also acknowledges the long-standing ties between the United States and Kurdistan, pointing to Kurdish Peshmerga forces, US troops and their allies working together in the fight against terrorism.

Metropolitan Nashville councillor Mike Cortese, who represents District 4, led the official recognition of the anniversary along with other councillors.

The proclamation was signed on 6 October 2026.
