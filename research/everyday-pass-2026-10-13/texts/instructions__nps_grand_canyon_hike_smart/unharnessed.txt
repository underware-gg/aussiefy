Bushwalking in the Grand Canyon throws up some real challenges, what with the heat, the altitude and how hard the terrain is on your body. To make sure your walk's a good one and a safe one, you've gotta stay on top of your water and your tucker.

Stay Hydrated and Energized

While you're walking, especially uphill and out in the full sun, you've got to keep your fluids up. In the Grand Canyon's hot, dry climate your sweat dries off quick smart, so you mightn't twig how much fluid you're losing. Keep an eye out for salt stains or rings on your clothes, a common sign you're getting dehydrated.

To stay properly hydrated, keep sipping water the whole way, even if you're not thirsty. It's just as important to top up the electrolytes you've lost by eating salty snacks, which help your body hang on to fluid and keep everything in balance. Wondering what the inner canyon rangers chuck in their day packs? Some of their favourite salty snacks are pretzels, Pringles, chips and cheese crackers: tasty tucker that's ready for the track!

Fuel Your Body for the Journey
Walking in the Grand Canyon can take it out of you, so make sure you eat enough to keep the tank full. Having regular snacks, with a mix of carbs, protein and electrolytes, will keep your energy up. Drink and eat even if you're not feeling thirsty or hungry, because your body won't always let you know when it's time to refuel. Getting the balance right between water and food is the key to not running out of puff and keeping your body working at its best.

If you stay ahead of the game with water, food and rest, you'll have a much better, more rewarding time out on the tracks.

Even if you're eating and drinking right, you still need to stay out of the direct sun in the hottest part of the day. It's 15F to 20F (9C-11C) degrees hotter in the sun than the posted shade temperatures. And keep in mind, the further down into the canyon you go, the hotter it gets!

Plan your day so you're not walking between 10am and 4pm. Have a spell somewhere near shade and water to dodge the worst of the heat. Get going before sunrise and finish up late arvo. Seasoned desert walkers know the timing of their walk is the biggest thing in staying out of strife. Most of the people who need emergency medical help in the canyon for heat illness are walking between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m.

KEEP YOURSELF SOAKING WET TO STAY COOL.

One of the best ways to keep your body temperature in check while you're walking in the Grand Canyon is to stay sopping wet. The fierce heat, especially in the inner canyon, can send your core temperature up in a hurry, so you've gotta grab every chance to cool down. Whenever you're near water, whether it's a creek or a water station, take the time to soak your clothes and yourself.

Wear clothes that hold water well (cotton's great in the desert!), and if you can, wear a hat or bandanna you can wet down often. It's a simple but bloody effective way to stay cool and keep your energy up all day. When you're walking in the canyon, where the temperature can go through the roof, take every chance to get wet, because it'll make your walk a whole lot more comfortable. By the end of the day you'll feel the difference, with your body staying cooler and fresher.

Remember, staying soaking wet isn't just about feeling better. It's a critical tool to help keep your energy up and stop heat exhaustion, so don't skip this simple but vital way to cool down!
