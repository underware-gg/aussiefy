Bushwalking in the Grand Canyon throws up its own challenges, thanks to the mix of air temperature, altitude and how hard the terrain is on your body. To keep your walk enjoyable and safe, you need to get your water and your tucker right.

Stay Hydrated and Energised

As you walk—especially uphill and in direct sun—it's essential to stay hydrated. In the Grand Canyon's hot, dry climate, sweat dries off quick smart, so you mightn't realise how much fluid you're losing. Keep an eye out for salt stains or rings on your clothes, a common sign of dehydration.

To stay properly hydrated, sip water regularly the whole way, even if you don't feel thirsty. It's just as important to top up the electrolytes you've lost by eating salty snacks, which help your body hang on to fluids and keep its balance. Wondering what the inner canyon rangers chuck in their day packs? Some of their favourite salty snacks are pretzels, Pringles, chips and cheese crackers—tasty, track-ready fuel for the trip.

Fuel Your Body for the Journey
Walking in the Grand Canyon can be hard yakka, so make sure you eat enough to fuel your body. Regular snacks with a mix of carbs, protein and electrolytes will help keep your energy up. Drink and eat even if you're not feeling thirsty or hungry—your body won't always tell you when it's time to refuel. Getting the balance of water and food right is the key to avoiding fatigue and keeping your body working at its best.

If you stay on top of your water, food and rest, you'll help make your time on the tracks more enjoyable and more rewarding.

Even if you're eating and drinking right, you still need to avoid walking in direct sun during the hottest part of the day. Sun temperatures are 15F to 20F (9C-11C) degrees hotter than the posted shade temperatures. And keep in mind, the further into the canyon you go, the hotter it gets.

Plan your day so you're not walking between the hours of 10am and 4pm. Take a break near shade and water to avoid the worst heat of the day. Make an early start before sunup and finish late in the arvo. Experienced desert bushwalkers know that the timing of their walk is the most important factor in avoiding hazards. Most of the people who need emergency medical help in the canyon because of heat illness are walking between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m.

KEEP YOURSELF SOAKING WET TO STAY COOL.

One of the best ways to keep your body temperature in check while bushwalking in the Grand Canyon is to stay soaking wet. The intense heat, especially in the inner canyon, can push your core body temperature up fast, so it's essential to take every chance you get to cool down. Whenever you're near water—whether it's a creek or a water station—take the time to soak your clothes and yourself.

Wear clothes that hold water well (cotton's a ripper in the desert), and if you can, wear a hat or bandanna you can soak often. It's a simple but effective way to stay cool and full of beans all day. When you're walking in the canyon, where temperatures can soar, take every chance to get wet, as it'll make your walk far more comfortable. By the end of the day you'll feel the difference, as your body stays cooler and fresher.

Remember, staying soaking wet isn't just about feeling better—it's a critical tool to keep you going and avoid heat exhaustion, so don't overlook this simple but vital way of cooling down.
