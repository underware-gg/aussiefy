## Travel comp time, for everyone

You can ask for travel comp time (time in lieu for travel) when you've been approved to travel for work and that travel happens outside your normal working hours. Travel comp time is for “temporary duty,” also known as “TDY” travel, _not_ local travel or getting to and from work. TDY travel runs longer than 12 hours and needs an authorisation approved beforehand in Concur.

There are no limits on earning travel comp time based on your salary level or the type of work schedule you're on.

Check OPM’s [Travel Comp Time Fact Sheet](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/pay-administration/fact-sheets/compensatory-time-off-for-travel/) for more detail.

### Understanding travel comp time

Travelling during your usual work day counts as normal hours, and that includes travelling from home to the airport.

Generally, travel comp time works as a “gap filler” when you're travelling outside your normal work hours on a normal work day, but not officially “performing work.”

For more detail, current GSA staff can check the [Compensation for Government Travel slidedeck](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RFSq_4KdMza_pkcHGp9Hy2RNR2ryLs0b/view?usp=sharing) to work out how your hours break down. If you've got questions, have a chat to your Timekeeper or an HR Specialist at your agency.

### Calculating travel comp time

When you're working out travel comp time hours, travel ends when you check in at the hotel or get home. Travel comp time hours are based on elapsed time. Use the time zone you left from to work out elapsed time.

To report your travel time accurately, we suggest keeping a log of:

- When you left home and got to the airport
- When your flights actually took off and landed
- When you left the destination airport and got to your hotel

### Using travel comp time

Travel comp time expires one year (26 pay periods) after you earn it. Once you hit the expiry date, you lose the leave. If you leave GSA, any travel comp time you haven't used is forfeited.
