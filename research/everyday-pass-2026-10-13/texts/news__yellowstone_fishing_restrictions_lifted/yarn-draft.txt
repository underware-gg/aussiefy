MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – From Friday 4 September, the last of the fishing restrictions on the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon (downstream of Norris Campground) rivers and all the creeks that feed them will be lifted, now the weather and the water have cooled off. Anglers'll be able to fish these rivers, along with most of the park's other rivers, creeks and lakes, every day from sunup to sundown, as set out in the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet.

The rivers were first shut to fishing altogether on 15 July, then wound back to partial restrictions on 28 August as conditions picked up. The restrictions and closures had to go in during a stretch of low flows and water temperatures over 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius), which can be lethal to fish.

Park staff will keep an eye on water temperatures. Temporary restrictions or closures could go back in, depending on how conditions look.

Update: Yellowstone lifts full closures for Firehole, Madison and Gibbon rivers

Partial restrictions stay in place from 2pm to sunup the next day

MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – From Friday 28 August, anglers can fish the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon (downstream of Norris Campground) rivers and all the creeks that feed them from sunup till 2pm each day. Cooler weather and water, plus rain in the current forecast, mean the full closures can come off.

The full closures went in on 15 July because low flows and water temperatures over 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius) can be lethal to fish. The water's been cooling off overnight, so fishing's back on for part of the day. Anglers can fish most of the park's other rivers, creeks and lakes every day from sunup to sundown, as set out in the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet.

Park staff will keep an eye on water temperatures. Temporary restrictions could be lifted, or more closures put in, depending on how conditions look.

Anglers: please fish in the coolest part of the day and land your fish quick smart. Don't play a hooked trout till it's done in. Handle fish gently and keep them in the water as much as you can, and let them get their wind back before you let them go. Doing your bit will look after the park's fisheries, and it might save them having to ban fishing at all hours on some rivers and creeks if things get worse.
