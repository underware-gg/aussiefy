Where do I book a place to stay in Yellowstone?
Yellowstone National Park Lodges runs the nine lodges in the park. They're all open from late spring right through autumn, but only two stay open over winter: Old Faithful Snow Lodge and Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel. Get your booking in early!

Do I need a booking to get into the park?
You don't need a vehicle booking to get into the park – just a park entrance pass. Save yourself some time at the entrance stations by buying your pass online before you lob in. Keep in mind that summer is flat chat, so expect crowds at the popular spots and queues at the entrance gates, through the roadworks, and wherever folks have pulled over to gawk at the wildlife. Please be patient and enjoy your visit.

Where can I see animals?

How did Yellowstone get its name?
Yellowstone National Park is named after the Yellowstone River, the main river running through the park. According to French-Canadian trappers in the 1800s, they asked the Minnetaree tribe, who live in what's now eastern Montana, what the river was called. They answered “Mi tse a-da-zi,” which literally means “Rock Yellow River.” The trappers put this into French as “Roche Jaune” or “Pierre Jaune.” In 1797, explorer-geographer David Thompson used the English version—“Yellow Stone.” Lewis and Clark called the Yellowstone River by both the French and English names. Over time the name settled down as “Yellowstone.”

Is Yellowstone the largest national park?
Nah. More than half of Alaska’s national park units are bigger, including Wrangell–St. Elias National Park and Preserve, which is the biggest unit (13 million acres) in the National Park System. Until 1994, Yellowstone (at 2.2 million acres) was the biggest national park in the lower 48, the contiguous United States. That year Death Valley National Monument was expanded and became a national park—it's got more than 3 million acres.

Is Yellowstone the most visited national park?
Yellowstone is in the top five national parks for number of recreational visitors. Great Smoky Mountains National Park often gets the most. Head to https://irma.nps.gov/Stats/ for up-to-date figures on all the national parks.

What's the difference between a national park and a national forest?
National parks are run by the Department of the Interior and national forests by the Department of Agriculture. The National Park Service's job is to keep resources unimpaired, while the Forest Service's job is to manage resources wisely for heaps of sustainable uses. Six national forests surround Yellowstone National Park.

How many rangers work in Yellowstone?
Around 750 people work for the National Park Service in the peak summer season. Around 392 of them are permanent, year-round staff. Park rangers work in education, resource management, law enforcement, emergency medical services, and backcountry operations. Other staff specialise in research, maintenance, management, admin, track maintenance, fire management, and fee collection.

How do you become a park ranger?
Plenty of National Park Service staff kick off their careers as volunteers or seasonal workers. Jobs are hotly contested, and hiring is done through the Office of Personnel Management. Find out more about working with us.

Can we swim in rivers and lakes?
Swimming isn't recommended because most lakes and creeks are dangerously cold – cold enough to knock the wind right out of you.

What is the highest peak in the park?
Eagle Peak, in the south-east of Yellowstone, is the highest at 11,358 feet.

Why is Yellowstone called a biosphere reserve and a world heritage site?
The United Nations named Yellowstone National Park a biosphere reserve and a world heritage site in recognition of how much its natural and cultural resources matter to the whole world.

What's the Continental Divide?
Think of the Continental Divide as the top of the ridge of the continent. In theory, when rain or snow falls on the west side of the Divide, it ends up in the Pacific Ocean. When it falls on the east side of the Divide, it ends up in the Atlantic Ocean. In Yellowstone (same as everywhere else), this ridgeline isn't straight. You cross the Continental Divide three times between the South Entrance and the Old Faithful area. Craig Pass is the highest crossing, at 8,262 feet.

How did Mt. Washburn form?
At 10,219 feet, this peak can be seen from plenty of spots in the park. It's what's left of an extinct volcano from the Absaroka Volcanics of about 50 million years ago. The volcano was literally cut in half by an eruption 631,000 years ago. Only the northern part of the original volcano can still be seen.

Does Yellowstone include a federally designated wilderness?
Nah. Most of the park was recommended for this designation in 1972, but Congress hasn't got round to acting on the recommendation.
