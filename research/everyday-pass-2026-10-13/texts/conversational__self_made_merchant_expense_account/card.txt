yarn: A natural, well-judged level-3 Aussie retelling that keeps every point and the father's wry voice; minor currency mix.
  minor: 'with eight quid every Saturday night' (for 'with eight dollars every Saturday night'): Changes the currency of a Chicago letter; then leaves 'cents' beside 'quid'.
  minor: "But a quid's worth a hundred and six cents" (for 'But a dollar is worth one hundred and six cents'): Quid with cents is a mixed unit.
  minor: "cos he won't wear it once you've left uni" (for "because he won't stand it after you leave college"): 'Cos' repeated four times becomes a tic.

unharnessed: A natural, faithful level-3 retelling that keeps the dollars and the father's voice; one garbled simile.
  minor: "feeding a mongrel steer that won't fatten up its own weight in corn" (for "feeding his weight in corn to a scalawag steer that won't fat up"): Word order garbles the image; it should be feeding the steer its own weight in corn.
  minor: "once you're out of college" (for 'after you leave college'): Keeps the American 'college' where uni would be the Aussie choice; defensible in context.

engine: Largely the original with mechanical word swaps; several wrong-sense substitutions, a broken phrase and mixed currency, and well under the level asked.
  major: 'very, dead careful' (for 'very, very careful'): Broken construction; 'dead' cannot replace the second 'very' here.
  major: 'Bloody hell, I simply mention these things because I am scared stiff' (for 'I simply mention these things because I am afraid'): Bolted-on oath, and 'scared stiff' takes 'afraid' in the wrong sense (polite regret, not fear).
  major: 'sixty cents on any quid that comes to him. But a quid is worth one hundred and six cents to a beaut business man, and he never spends the dollar' (for 'sixty cents on any dollar that comes to him. But a dollar is worth one hundred and six cents to a good business man, and he never spends the dollar'): Currency swap leaves quids, cents and dollars mixed in one argument.
  minor: 'It is all with the bloke.' (for 'It is all with the man.'): Mechanical 'man' to 'bloke' swap reads oddly in an aphorism.
  minor: 'the first travelling bloke' (for 'the first traveling man'): 'Traveling man' is a commercial traveller; 'bloke' loses the sense.
  minor: 'a bloody good deal like feeding his weight in corn' (for 'a good deal like feeding his weight in corn'): Oath inserted where it attaches to the wrong phrase.
