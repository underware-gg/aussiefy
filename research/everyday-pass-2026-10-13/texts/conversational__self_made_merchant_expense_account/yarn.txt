CHICAGO, May 4, 189-

_Dear Pierrepont:_ The cashier's just handed me your expense account for the month, and it fair makes a bloke go round-shouldered just looking it over. When I told you I wanted you to get a liberal education, I didn't mean I wanted to buy Cambridge. Course, the bills won't break me, but they'll break you unless you're dead careful.

I've noticed for the last two years your accounts have been getting heavier every month, but I haven't seen a skerrick of you taking honours to justify the extra running costs; and that's bloody bad business, a fair bit like feeding a no-hoper steer his own weight in corn when he won't fatten up.

I haven't said anything about it before, cos I trusted a fair bit to your native nous to stop you making a mug of yourself the way some of these young blokes who've never had to work for it do. But just cos I've sat tight, don't go getting it into your noggin that the old man's rolling in it and can wear it, cos he won't wear it once you've left uni. The sooner you cut your spending to fit what you'll be earning, the easier the two of them will find it to live together.

The only sure way a bloke can get rich quick is to have it handed to him or to inherit it. You're not getting rich that way, not till you've shown you can hold down a pretty important job with the firm; and, of course, there's just one place a bloke can start from for that job at Graham & Co. Makes no difference whether he's the old man's son or the cellar boss's: that place is the bottom. And the bottom in the office end of this business is a seat at the mailing-desk, with eight quid every Saturday night.

I can't hand you any ready-made success. It'd do you no good, and it'd do the house harm. There's heaps of room at the top here, but there's no lift in the building. Starting out, as you are, with a beaut education, you ought to climb quicker than a fella without one; but there'll come a time, when you start at the factory, when you can't lick stamps as fast as the other young fellas at the desk. And the bloke who hasn't licked stamps isn't fit to write letters. That's the time, naturally, when knowing whether the pie comes before the ice-cream, and how to drive a motor car, won't be a lot of use to you.

I only mention all this cos I'm afraid your ideas about the terms you're coming into the house on have got a bit swelled up back East. I can give you a start, but after that you'll have to blast your own way to the front. It's all up to the bloke. Give some fellas a talent wrapped in a napkin to start in business with, and they'd swap the talent for a dud gold brick and lose the napkin too; and there's others you could start off with nothing but the napkin, who'd set up in the drapery game in a small way with it, and then talk the other bloke's talent into it.

I'm proud enough to reckon you've got the right stuff in you, but I want to see some of it come out. You'll never make a decent merchant of yourself by turning round the order the Lord laid down for us, learning the spending end of business before the earning end. Pay day's always a month off for a big spender, and he can never get more than sixty cents out of any quid that comes his way. But a quid's worth a hundred and six cents to a good businessman, and he never spends the quid. It's the bloke who keeps his savings up and his expenses down who buys a share in the concern. That's where you'll find yourself weak if your expense accounts aren't telling porkies; and they don't generally tell porkies that particular way, though Baron Munchausen was the first travelling salesman, and my commercial travellers' bills still show his influence.
