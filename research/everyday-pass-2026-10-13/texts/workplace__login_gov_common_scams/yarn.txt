Scammers are always cookin' up brand-spankin'-new ways to con punters into handin' over their personal information or makin' dodgy payments. Keepin' up with their tricks is your best defence. Below are some of the most common ones, and how to look after yourself.

## Fake emails and texts

Scammers send emails or text messages that look official, often with government logos and the government's way of talkin'. This is called phishing. Phishing attacks might:

- Hurry you up to click on a link to "verify your account"
- Claim there's an urgent problem with your benefits or tax return
- Ask for personal information like Social Security numbers or bank details

### How to protect yourself

- Don't click on links or download attachments from senders you don't know.
- Check the message is fair dinkum by gettin' in touch with the agency directly, usin' its official contact details.
- Report shonky messages to the agency the scammer's pretendin' to be

## Impersonation phone calls

Shonks might ring you up pretendin' to be from the government. They'll often:
- Threaten you with arrest, losin' your licence, or losin' your benefits
- Demand you pay up on the spot with gift cards, cryptocurrency or wire transfers

### How to protect yourself

- Government agencies will never ask you to pay over the phone.
- Hang up and ring the official number listed on the Login.gov or agency website.

## Fake websites and online portals

Some scams use dodgy websites built to look like official government pages. These sites might:

- Ask you to log in with your personal details
- Charge fees for services that are free on the official sites

### How to protect yourself

- Always check the website URL ends in .gov.
- Don't use search engines to find sign-in links—type the official URL in yourself and double-check the spellin'.

## Social media scams

Scammers set up fake social media profiles makin' out they're government officials. They might:
- Offer grants or financial help if you pay fees upfront
- Ask for personal or bankin' details in direct messages

### How to protect yourself

- Remember that government agencies don't send messages about benefits out of the blue.
- Report suss profiles to the social media platform they're on.
