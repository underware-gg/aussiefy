Southern Cornbread is a solid, no-sugar kind of cornbread that gets served all over the Southeastern United States. It's easy as to make and usually turns up as the bread with lunch or tea (dinner). It's not unusual for some people to throw together a quick, simple feed now and then—even brekkie—by crumbling cornbread into a glass of buttermilk and getting stuck into it.

Ingredients

2 cups yellow or white self-raising corn meal

1 large egg

1¼–1½ cups buttermilk or full-cream milk

¼ cup olive oil or vegetable oil

Equipment

Well-seasoned 8-inch or 10-inch cast-iron frying pan (or else: an ovenproof frying pan or an 8x8-inch baking tin)

A few measuring cups

Medium or large mixing bowl

Method

Preheat the oven to 425°F. If you're using a cast-iron frying pan, whack it in the oven to heat up. Don't preheat any other kind of frying pan or baking tin.

If you're using a frying pan that isn't cast iron, or a baking tin, give it a coat of non-stick spray.

Beat the egg in the bowl. Stir in the buttermilk and oil.

Stir in the corn meal and mix until it's just moistened. The batter'll be a bit lumpy. Go easy and don't overmix it.

Pour the batter into the pan and pop it in the oven.

Bake until the crust is a light golden brown and a skewer poked into the centre comes out clean. For an 8-inch frying pan that can be 25–30 minutes; for a 10-inch frying pan or an 8x8-inch baking tin about 20–25 minutes.

Let it cool for 5 minutes in the pan before you serve it up.

Notes, tips and variations

Any leftover cornbread can be wrapped in a damp tea towel (to keep it moist) and kept in the fridge for several days. It can go in the freezer in freezer bags for several months, but it'll usually come out more crumbly if you store it that way.
