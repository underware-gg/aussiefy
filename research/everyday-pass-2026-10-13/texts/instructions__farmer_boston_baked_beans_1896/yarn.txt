Snags

Cut apart a string of snags. Stab each snag several times with a carvin' fork. Put 'em in the frying-pan, cover with boilin' water, and cook fifteen minutes; drain, put 'em back in the pan, and fry till they're nice and brown. Serve with fried apples. Snags are often grilled same as bacon, with apples baked in a pan under 'em.

Boston Baked Beans

Pick over one quart pea beans, cover with cold water, and let 'em soak overnight. In the mornin', drain, cover with fresh water, heat slowly (keep the water under the boil), and cook till the skins'll burst. Best way to tell is to take a few beans on the tip of a spoon and blow on 'em: if they're cooked enough, the skins'll burst. Beans you've tested like that have to be chucked out, naturally. Drain the beans, and chuck the bean-water outside, not down the sink. Scald the rind of three-quarters pound fat salt pork, scrape it, cut off a one-quarter inch slice and put it in the bottom of the bean-pot. Cut through the rind of the rest of the pork every one-half inch, makin' the cuts one inch deep. Put the beans in the pot and bury the pork in 'em, leavin' the rind showin'. Mix one tablespoon salt, one tablespoon molasses, and three tablespoons sugar; add one cup boilin' water, and pour it over the beans; then add enough more boilin' water to cover the beans. Put the lid on the bean-pot, whack it in the oven, and bake slowly six or eight hours, takin' the lid off for the last hour of cookin' so the rind goes brown and crisp. Add water as needed. Heaps of people swear blind that if you add one-half tablespoon mustard in with the seasonin's, the beans go down a fair bit easier on the gut. If you'd rather pork with a bit of lean through it, use less salt.

The bonza name Boston Baked Beans have earned has been put down to the earthen bean-pot, small at the top and bulgin' at the sides, that they're meant to be cooked in. Mind you, beans every bit as beaut have often been scoffed where a five-pound lard bucket got pressed into service cos the bean-pot was cactus.

Yellow-eyed beans are bloody good baked, too.
