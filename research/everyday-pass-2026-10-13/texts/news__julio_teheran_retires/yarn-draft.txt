Julio Teherán, a two-time Major League Baseball All-Star, called time on his baseball career on Monday, at the age of 35. The decision came two days after he was pulled from a scheduled start for Team Colombia at the 2026 World Baseball Classic (WBC) with a shoulder injury.

Teherán had helped Team Colombia to a 3-1 win over Team Panama at the WBC before announcing his retirement.

Teherán had a thirteen-year MLB career, mostly with the Atlanta Braves, though he also played for the Los Angeles Angels, Detroit Tigers, Milwaukee Brewers and New York Mets. He made his MLB debut with the Braves in 2011 at the age of 20 and spent his first nine seasons with the club, picking up All-Star selections in 2014 and 2016.

Over his MLB career, Teherán put together an 81-82 record, with a 3.85 ERA, 1.223 WHIP and 1,260 strikeouts in 1,470.2 innings. He had spent the 2022 and 2025 seasons playing in the Mexican Baseball League.
