There's nowhere safe outside when there's thunderstorms about. If you can hear thunder, you're likely close enough for the storm to have a crack at you. Just remember, "When Thunder Roars, Go Indoors!" Way too many people muck about for far too long before getting somewhere safe when a storm's rolling in. Trouble is, that dragging of the feet is behind a lot of the lightning deaths and injuries in the U.S.

The best way to look after yourself is to stay out of harm's way altogether. You just don't wanna get caught outside in a storm, simple as that. Have a lightning safety plan, and call off or put off whatever you're doing early if storms are on the cards. Keep an eye on the weather and get yourself somewhere safe before it turns nasty. Proper solid buildings and hard-topped cars are safe bets. Rain shelters, little sheds and open vehicles are bloody useless.

Once you're inside, don't touch anything plugged into a power point, the plumbing, or phones with cords. Mobiles and cordless phones are fine. And keep clear of outside doors and windows, and don't go lying on the garage floor.

Minimizing the Risk

To keep your own risk of getting struck as low as you can, when you're heading outside, plan ahead so you can get somewhere safe quick smart if a storm turns up. If the sky's looking angry or you hear thunder, get inside somewhere safe straight away. Once you're in, stay away from corded phones, electrical gear, plumbing, and windows and doors. And finally, wait 30 minutes after the last lightning or thunder before you go back out. If everyone stuck to those simple rules, the number of people killed and hurt by lightning in this country could drop a hell of a lot.

Lightning Victims

If someone gets struck by lightning, they might need medical help straight away. People who've been struck don't carry an electrical charge, so they're safe to touch. Call 911 and keep an eye on them. Start CPR or use an Automated External Defibrillator if you need to.
