There's no safe place outside when thunderstorms are about. If you can hear thunder, you're likely within striking distance of the storm. Just remember, “When Thunder Roars, Go Indoors!” Too many people wait far too bloody long to get to a safe place when a storm's rolling in. Trouble is, that dawdling is behind a lot of the lightning deaths and injuries in the U.S.

The best way to look after yourself is to stay out of lightning's way altogether. You just don't want to get caught outside in a storm. Have a lightning safety plan, and pull the pin on things early, or put them off, if storms are on the cards. Keep an eye on the weather and get to a safe place before it turns nasty. Proper solid buildings and hard-top cars are safe bets. Rain shelters, little sheds and open vehicles are not safe.

Once you're inside, don't touch anything plugged into a power point, the plumbing, or corded phones. Mobiles and cordless phones are fine. Also, keep away from outside doors and windows, and don't lie on the garage floor.

Minimising the Risk

To keep your own risk of being struck by lightning down, plan ahead whenever you're heading outside, so you can get to a safe place quick smart if a thunderstorm threatens. If the sky's looking nasty or you hear thunder, get inside a safe place straight away. Once you're in, keep clear of corded phones, electrical gear, plumbing, and windows and doors. Finally, wait 30 minutes after the last lightning or thunder before heading back outside. If every bloke and sheila stuck to those simple rules, the number of lightning casualties in this country could be cut right down.

Lightning Victims

If someone is struck by lightning, they may need medical help straight away. Lightning victims don't carry an electrical charge and are safe to touch. Call 911 and keep an eye on the victim. Start CPR or use an Automated External Defibrillator if needed.
