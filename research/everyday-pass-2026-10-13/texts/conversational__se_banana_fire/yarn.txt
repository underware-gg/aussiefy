Why did me banana catch fire in the microwave?

Crikey, I know that sounds like a silly question, but fair dinkum! The missus froze a heap of bananas in the freezer with the peel still on. You can't peel the buggers frozen. I chucked one in the microwave for one minute at 50% power. After 30 seconds the thin end of the peel (where a bunch join up to each other) was on fire, with a proper flame you could see (about the size of a lighter). What on earth happened?

* * *

It's down to the high amount of potassium in the banana. Microwaves react with metals, bouncing off them and causing arcing. You can even put on a bonza little light show by bunging a raw peeled banana in the microwave. No worries, it won't explode, but it'll make a dog's breakfast of the inside, and it's harmless too.

This can happen with some frozen veggies as well, depending on the soil they grew in. Like the linked article says, high concentrations of other metals such as magnesium, iron and zinc can be the mineral to blame.

A quick bit of sparking is harmless and won't hurt your microwave, but the USDA still recommends turning your microwave off if you see sparks. If it does start a fire, unless it's a very small fire, don't open the microwave door. The safest thing is to turn the microwave off and let the fire burn itself out once it's used up all the oxygen. Opening the door could set off a dangerous backdraft fireball.
