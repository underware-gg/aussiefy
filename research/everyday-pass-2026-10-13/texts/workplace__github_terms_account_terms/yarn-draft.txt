## B. Account Terms

**Short version:** _Personal Accounts and Organizations have different admin controls; a human has to create your Account; you have to be 13 or over; you have to give us a valid email address; and you can't have more than one free Account. You alone are responsible for your Account and anything that happens while you're signed in to or using your Account. It's up to you to keep your Account secure._

### 1. Account Controls

* Users. Subject to these Terms, you keep final admin control over your Personal Account and the Content in it.

* Organizations. The "owner" of an Organization that was created under these Terms has final admin control over that Organization and the Content in it. Within the Service, an owner can manage User access to the Organization’s data and projects. An Organization can have more than one owner, but at least one Personal Account has to be named as an owner of an Organization. If you're the owner of an Organization under these Terms, we hold you responsible for anything done on or through that Organization.

### 2. Required Information

You have to give us a valid email address to finish signing up. Any other information we ask for, like your real name, is optional, unless you're accepting these terms on behalf of a legal entity (in which case we need more information about the legal entity) or you go for a [paid Account](#l-payment), in which case we'll need more information for billing.

### 3. Account Requirements

We've got a few simple rules for Accounts on GitHub's Service.
* You have to be a human to create an Account. Accounts registered by "bots" or other automated methods aren't allowed. We do allow machine accounts:
* A machine account is an Account set up by an individual human who accepts the Terms on behalf of the Account, gives a valid email address, and is responsible for what it does. A machine account is used only for running automated tasks. More than one user can direct what a machine account does, but at the end of the day the owner of the Account is responsible for what the machine does. You can keep no more than one free machine account on top of your free Personal Account.
* One person or legal entity can keep no more than one free Account (if you choose to control a machine account as well, no worries, but it can only be used for running a machine).
* You have to be 13 or older. We're stoked to see clever young coders getting excited about learning to program, but we have to comply with United States law. GitHub doesn't target our Service at children under 13, and we don't allow any Users under 13 on our Service. If we find out about any User under the age of 13, we will [terminate that User’s Account immediately](#m-cancellation-and-termination). If you live in a country outside the United States, your country’s minimum age may be older; if so, it's up to you to comply with your country’s laws.
* Your login can only be used by one person — i.e., a single login can't be shared by more than one person. A paid Organization can only give access to as many Personal Accounts as your subscription allows.
* You can't use GitHub in breach of the export control or sanctions laws of the United States or any other applicable jurisdiction. You can't use GitHub if you are, or are working on behalf of, a [Specially Designated National (SDN)](https://www.treasury.gov/resource-center/sanctions/SDN-List/Pages/default.aspx) or a person subject to similar blocking or denied party prohibitions administered by a U.S. government agency. GitHub may let people in certain sanctioned countries or territories access certain GitHub services under U.S. government authorisations. For more information, please see our [Export Controls policy](/site-policy/other-site-policies/github-and-trade-controls).
