Picnic and barbie season brings heaps of chances for a good time outdoors with family and mates. But these warm-weather get-togethers also give foodborne bacteria plenty of chances to thrive. As tucker heats up in summer temperatures, bacteria multiply like rabbits.

To keep yourself, your family and your mates safe from food poisoning in the warmer months, handling your tucker safely when you're eating outdoors is critical. Read on for simple food safety tips for getting your tucker to the picnic spot, and prepping and serving it safely once you've rocked up.

Pack and Transport Food Safely

Keep your tucker safe: from the fridge or freezer — all the way to the picnic table.

Keep cold tucker cold. Put cold tucker in an esky with ice or frozen ice bricks. Cold tucker should be kept at 40 °F or below to stop bacteria growing. Meat, poultry and seafood can be packed while still frozen so they stay colder for longer.

Sort out what goes in the esky. Think about packing the drinks in one esky and the tucker that goes off in another. That way, as everyone opens and reopens the drinks esky to grab another drink, the perishable tucker won't cop the warm air outside.

Keep eskies shut: Once you're there, open the esky as few times as you can. This helps keep what's inside colder for longer.

Don't cross-contaminate. Make sure raw meat, poultry and seafood are wrapped up tight. This stops their juices getting into prepped or cooked tucker, or tucker that'll be eaten raw, like fruit and veg.

Clean your produce. Rinse fresh fruit and veg under running tap water before packing them in the esky — including the ones with skins and rinds you don't eat. Rub anything firm-skinned under running tap water, or scrub them with a clean veggie brush while rinsing under running tap water. Dry it all off with a clean tea towel or paper towel. Packaged produce labelled "ready-to-eat," "washed," or "triple washed" don't need washing.

Quick Tips for Setting Up at the Picnic Spot

Food safety starts with washing your hands properly — outdoors too. Before you start laying out your picnic spread, check your mitts and any surfaces are clean.

Washing Hands Outdoors: If you can't get to running water, use a water jug, some soap and paper towels. Or think about using moist disposable wipes to clean your hands.

Utensils and Serving Dishes: Take care to keep all utensils and platters clean when you're prepping tucker.

Follow Safe Barbie Tips

A barbie and a picnic often go hand in hand. And just like cooking indoors, there are important rules you should follow to make sure your barbecued tucker gets to the table safely.

Marinate safely. Marinate tucker in the fridge — never on the kitchen bench or outdoors. And if you're planning to use some of the marinade as a sauce on the cooked tucker, set some aside separately before you add the raw meat, poultry or seafood. Don't reuse marinade.

Cook it straight away after "partial cooking." Partly cooking tucker before it goes on the barbie is only safe when it can go straight onto the hot barbie, say at a place with a barbie on the patio or deck.

Cook tucker right through. When it's time to cook, have your food thermometer handy. Always use it to be sure your tucker is cooked right through. (See Safe Food Temperature Chart)

Keep "ready" tucker hot. Barbecued tucker can be kept hot until it's served by shifting it to the side of the grill rack, just away from the coals. This keeps it hot but stops it overcooking.

Don't reuse platters or utensils. Using the same platter or utensils that held raw meat, poultry or seafood lets bacteria from the raw tucker's juices spread to the cooked tucker. Instead, have a clean platter and utensils ready next to the barbie to serve your tucker.
