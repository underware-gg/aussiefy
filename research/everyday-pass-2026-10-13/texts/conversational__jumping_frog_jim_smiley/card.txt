yarn: A faithful, lively Aussie retelling that keeps Twain's dialect and rhythm; slightly under the brief in weight and vulgarity, with a couple of stock touches.
  minor: 'how long it was on the frog and toad' (for 'how long he was on the road'): Rhyming slang reads as a stock Aussie-ism dropped in; plain 'on the road' or 'on the track' would sound more natural.
  minor: "Wheezin' like a busted bellows, and still she'd get up." (added): Added tag; 'get up' (win) is apt, but the line repeats the finish just described and slightly blunts the paragraph's ending.
  minor: "strewth, if there was two birds sittin' on a fence" (for 'why, if there was two birds setting on a fence'): 'Strewth' as a drop-in for 'why' is a little stagey.

unharnessed: A fluent, natural, properly salty Aussie yarn that keeps every point; loses some of Twain's dialect texture and leans on 'bloody'.
  minor: 'he was lucky as all get-out' (for 'uncommon lucky'): 'As all get-out' is American, not Australian.
  minor: "every bloody time she'd pull up at the stand" (for 'always fetch up at the stand'): 'Bloody' is leaned on through the text (four or more times).
  minor: 'thank the Lord for his infinite mercy' (for "thank the Lord for his inf'nite mercy"): Normalising the dialect spellings loses some of the original's character.

engine: Mostly the original with Aussie tokens sprinkled in; several wrong-sense swaps, a broken word, and bolted-on interjections and swearing that change the narrator.
  critical: "the bloody big flume stoushn't finished" (for "the big flume warn't finished"): 'stoushn't' is not a word; 'warn't' was mangled into stoush (a fight), breaking the sentence.
  major: "and if he couldn't he'd change sides, ya flamin' mongrel" (for "and if he couldn't he'd change sides"): Abuses the listener (a reverend) for no reason; changes the narrator's manner.
  major: 'and a dead-set legend, mate' (for 'and a good man'): 'Legend' is wrong sense for a pious, good parson and shifts the register to modern pub praise.
  major: "for a fuckin' good while" (for 'for a good while'): Swearing about the parson's dying wife jars with the solemn moment the joke depends on.
  major: 'before he reckoned' (for 'before he thought'): 'Reckon' means to judge or suppose, not to pause and think; the sense is wrong.
  major: "she get rapt'n desperate like" (for 'she get excited and desperate like'): 'Rapt' means delighted, not agitated.
  minor: "Stone the flamin' crows." (added): Stock exclamation bolted onto the end, rendering nothing.
  minor: "or somethin' of that kind, cobber" (for 'or something of that kind'): Dated 'cobber' tag stuck on as decoration.
