## Preserved broader ranges and contextual choices

- A scorcher/stinker is normally a hot day. The seven-day heatwave probes retain “seven days”, and broader hot-event metaphors remain possible; the ledger does not impose a strict one-day rule from a learner definition. An ABC weather report uses a weekend-scorcher URL for a weekend heatwave, offering a useful broader-use lead rather than a compulsory synonym rule.
- No-way, get-stuffed and get-fucked can perform refusal, contempt or dismissal without a literal departure. Meanings and speech acts are distinguished from the choice of rude register. The same caution applies to person-swear compounds: good-bastard and good-cunt can praise; modifiers, intonation and relationships govern friendliness and offence. No national harmlessness guarantee is inferred.
- Mad-cunt has positive community reports alongside the scholarly eccentric-person reading; neither is forced to exclude the other. Bludgeoning ordinary praise into a universal eccentricity requirement would be an unsupported narrowing.
- Quantified person-swear phrases (every/no/some bastard, bugger or cunt) are recorded as productive compositions from attested person nouns. Component evidence is not presented as fresh independent attestation of every complete string. Culprit, contempt and affection are context-dependent additions, not compulsory facts about every person referred to.
- Dry-as-a-nun’s-nasty is directly glossed as general dryness by the independent source, **not explicitly thirst there**. A separately edited dry adjective means needing a drink; the thirsty-person reading composes those meanings. Physical dryness remains valid. Related nun’s-cunt evidence is not silently substituted for exact nasty wording.
- Fuck-all can mean very little as well as nothing. The near-zero probe does not establish a precision bug merely because one learner entry defines only nothing. Pig’s-back likewise covers fortunate/established circumstances beyond finance. Yakka retains ordinary work, and blue retains verbal arguments without compulsory physical violence.
- Properly’s informal really/thoroughly use is explicit in Collins’ English-German material. Its gate remains intact; the diagnostic properly-cold output required unlock. Correctness/manners-only definitions in other references do not disprove an intensifying sense.
- Regional or shared-English sources do not prove non-Australian usage. Scots box-and-dice history, New Zealand easy-as commentary, UK silence/departure idioms and international swear-person uses are labelled by source and selected meaning, without inventing Australian-exclusive origins or frequency claims.

## Source and verification limits

Sources include edited dictionaries and bilingual references; ANDC/Oxford historical commentary; primary Australian editorial and reported speech; corpus-based linguistic research; and explicitly labelled community or independent glossaries. Generic source-catalogue access says a public page **or indexed passage** was read; detailed locators identify narrower scopes. A copied dictionary or original publisher/mirror is one source family, not multiple independent witnesses. Original creative works referenced inside dictionaries were not independently retrieved unless explicitly stated. Notes are original paraphrases, not copied dictionary prose.

The 18 probe cases use `Translator(lookup_embedder=None)`, level 5, vulgarity 3, Deadset embellishment, no embedder and explicit unlock, with all registers; the two bare-tipsy seeds were also verified with those same settings. These are diagnostic observations, not default-policy recommendations. Failed selection in the tested seeds is reported honestly; it is not a proof of universal rejection. Degree/gloss observations do not settle the parent’s pending product choice about preserving severity versus configurable exaggeration/understatement.
