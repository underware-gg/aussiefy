### bag

### blanky

- [Institute of Australian Culture; republished Reggie the Wroughter poem](https://www.australianculture.org/honest-tom-reggie-the-wroughter-1915/), Introduction28; poem35/66; issue118; editor gloss137. 1915 Perth racing poem uses blanky in adjective/intensifier positions; modern editor identifies euphemistic swearing. Limits: Different historical work from film review. Modern explanatory glosses share IAC editorial provenance and are not independent dictionary testimony. No present-day commonness certification.

### hump-the-swag

### a-shonk

Current target: a shonk; A dishonest operator or con man.

- [ABC7.30 / Alex Mann](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2015-12-08/shonk-forensic-expert-could-trigger-legal-crisis/7012110), Headline123/137; introduction144–149. Headline uses shonk about a discredited forensic expert. Limits: Headline usage alone does not establish dishonest/con-man meaning versus incompetence. Keep partial; do not infer fraud from allegations or certify factual guilt.
- [Liudmyla Naumenko, Liliia Bilas and Vira Ponomarova / Acta Linguistica Lithuanica](https://journals.lki.lt/actalinguisticalithuanica/article/download/1024/1399/), The Lexical-Semantic Group Aussie in Contemporary Australian Slang, PDF p12 lines367–369; source identification575–577. Published lexical-semantic analysis includes shonk/shonky in a dishonest-person group and attributes definition to Hunter's True Blue Guide to Australian Slang2004p96. Limits: Actual paper inspected; cited original guide was not inspected. This is one source chain, not an independent original usage witness plus separate dictionary check. Historical slur sense does not disprove this contemporary sense.

### sundowner

Current target: sundowner; A tramp or vagrant; especially a traveller arriving at sundown seeking food and lodging.

- [State Library of NSW; Frank W.Bungardy manuscript transcript](https://acms.sl.nsw.gov.au/_transcript/2010/D04276/a2922.html), Manuscript page71, web lines117–118. Narrator says a limeburner took him for a sundowner or tramp and gave him tea. Limits: Strong historical Australian meaning evidence. Does not itself establish habitual dusk arrival or deliberate work avoidance.
- [Collins English Dictionary / HarperCollins](https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/sundowner), British-English sense1, lines218–226; American dictionary branch254–256. Publisher defines the historic Australia/NZ tramp sense with sundown arrival for food/lodging. Limits: Other senses include drink and nautical officer. British-English section heading is not origin evidence. Source marks this tramp sense obsolete; preserve dialect and register rather than discard the sense.
