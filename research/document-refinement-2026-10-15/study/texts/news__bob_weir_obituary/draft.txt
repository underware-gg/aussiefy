On Saturday, the family of American muso and songwriter Bob Weir announced he'd died with loved ones around him. The former guitarist and singer with rock band Grateful Dead was 78. His family shared the news on Instagram, saying he'd recently fought a cancer diagnosis but died from "underlying lung issues". They didn't say what date Weir died.

Weir spent decades playing rhythm guitar and singing with American psychedelic rock band the Grateful Dead. He wrote some of their songs too, including the hit "Sugar Magnolia", a regular at Grateful Dead concerts. He also had a long solo career, putting together a range of groups with fellow Grateful Dead alumni, and was still playing live until months before his death.

Born Robert Hall Parber on October 16, 1947, in San Francisco, California, Weir grew up in the San Francisco Bay Area. He spent some of his school years in Colorado, where he met future Grateful Dead member John Perry Barlow. Weir took up guitar at 13 and was drawn to the local bluegrass scene. That's where he first came across Jerry Garcia playing banjo. On December 31, 1965, Garcia and Weir agreed to start their own group, Mother McCree's Uptown Jug Champions, which grew into the Grateful Dead.

Weir played rhythm guitar and sang for the group for decades. From the 1970s, he also tried his hand at a solo career and a range of other groups outside the Grateful Dead: Kingfish, Bobby and the Midnites, RatDog, Furthur, The Other Ones, The Dead, Wolf Bros, and Dead & Company. Every group included fellow Grateful Dead collaborators and tried out styles from country rock to jam band to jazz-influenced roots rock, with blues and country at their heart. He mostly sang harmonies and backing vocals, but took the lead on several tracks. Among them was Grateful Dead anthem "Truckin'", which the Library of Congress recognised as a national treasure in 1997.

Away from playing music, Weir also set up Tamalpais Research Institute, a platform for performing virtually. He was active in politics, backing causes including environmentalism, poverty relief and matters to do with the arts. He kept a hand in the Bay Area music scene, playing live with local musicians and co-owning Sweetwater Music Hall in Mill Valley, California.
