Scammers keep coming up with brand-spankin'-new ways to trick people into handing over personal information or making fraudulent payments. Knowing what they’re up to is your best defence. Here are some of the most common tricks and how to protect yourself.

## Fake emails and texts

Scammers send emails or texts that look official, often with government logos and wording. That’s phishing. A phishing attack may:

- Push you to click a link to “verify your account”
- Claim there’s an urgent problem with your benefits or tax return
- Ask for personal information such as Social Security numbers or bank details

### How to protect yourself

- Don’t click links or download attachments from senders you don’t know.
- Check the message by getting in touch with the agency directly, using its official contact information.
- Report fraudulent messages to the agency the scammer is pretending to represent

## Impersonation phone calls

Fraudsters may ring you up pretending to be government representatives. They often:
- Threaten arrest, licence suspension, or loss of benefits
- Demand you pay straightaway via gift cards, cryptocurrency, or wire transfers

### How to protect yourself

- Government agencies will never ask for payment over the phone.
- Hang up and ring the official number listed on the Login.gov or agency website.

## Fake websites and online portals

Some scams use dodgy websites made to look like official government pages. These sites may:

- Ask you to log in with personal details
- Charge you for services that are free on official sites

### How to protect yourself

- Always check that the website URL ends in .gov.
- Steer clear of search engines for sign in links—type the official URL directly and double-check the spelling.

## Social media scams

Scammers set up fake social media profiles, claiming they’re government officials. They may:
- Offer grants or financial assistance if you pay fees upfront
- Ask for personal or banking details in direct messages

### How to protect yourself

- Remember, government agencies don’t send unsolicited messages about benefits.
- Report suspicious social media profiles to the social media platform.
