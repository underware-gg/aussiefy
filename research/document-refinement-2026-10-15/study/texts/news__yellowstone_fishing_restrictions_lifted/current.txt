MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – From Friday, Sept. 4, the remaining fishing restrictions on the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon (downstream of Norris Campground) rivers and all their tributaries will be lifted, with the weather and water cooling down. Anglers will be able to fish these rivers, along with most other park rivers, creeks and lakes, every day from sunrise to sunset, as set out in the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet.

These rivers were fully closed to fishing on July 15. As conditions improved, that was eased to partial restrictions on Aug. 28. The restrictions and closures were needed cos water flows were low and temperatures above 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius) can kill fish.

Staff will keep checking water temperatures. Temporary restrictions or closures may be brought in, depending on how conditions shape up when assessed.

Update: Yellowstone lifts full closures for Firehole, Madison and Gibbon rivers

Partial restrictions stay in place from 2 p.m. to sunrise the next day

MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – From Friday, Aug. 28, anglers can fish the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon (downstream of Norris Campground) rivers and all their tributaries from sunrise until 2 p.m. each day. Cooler weather and water, along with rain in the current forecast, mean the full closures can be lifted.

Full closures began on July 15 cos low water flows and water temperatures above 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius) can kill fish. The water's been cooling at night, allowing fishing for part of the day. On most other park rivers, creeks and lakes, anglers can fish every day from sunrise to sunset, as set out in the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet.

Staff will keep checking water temperatures. Depending on how conditions shape up when assessed, temporary restrictions may be lifted or more closures put in place.

Anglers: Please fish in the coolest parts of the day and land fish quickly. Don't wear hooked trout out by playing them to exhaustion. Handle fish gently, keeping them in the water as much as you can, and let them recover before release. Doing your bit will protect the park’s fisheries. It may also avoid the need to ban fishing at all times of day on some rivers and creeks if conditions get worse.
