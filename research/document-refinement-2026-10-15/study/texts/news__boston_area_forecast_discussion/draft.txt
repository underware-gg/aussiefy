KEY MESSAGE 2...The odd shower possible Wednesday, but drier overall. Dry weather returns Thursday, with temperatures cool for the season.

A trailing cold front moves across the region Wednesday, while surface high pressure builds in from the west through the day. Model soundings show more mixing in the boundary layer, bringing drier conditions and westerly winds gusting 20-30 mph. Westerly, downslope flow, along with 925mb temperatures of +17- 18C, will make for a warm autumn day, with highs in the 70s in many areas. A weak wave moving through could bring a stray shower or two, but they’ll be few and far between. With the lower levels drier, a light shower/sprinkle is about the most to expect.

As high pressure settles in further into Thursday, the airmass dries out even more. Thursday looks likely to stay dry, though cooler than Wednesday. A cooler airmass will have moved in too, with 925mb temperatures around 10C cooler than Wednesday. That puts temperatures mainly in the lower 60s, cool for the season.

KEY MESSAGE 3...Low pressure system expected to bring rain Friday into early Saturday. Gusty northwest winds and temperatures below normal.

Deterministic and ensemble guidance show a stronger/deeper northern stream shortwave trough pushing across the Great Lakes and the northeast Friday into Saturday. The system has synoptic support: an upper level jet near southern New England produces large scale ascent, while also advecting a plume of above normal moisture northward into the region. That sets things up for widespread rain. There’s plenty of moisture, but instability looks limited, keeping the potential for heavy rain impacts down. Ensemble guidance generally puts amounts from 0.25 to 0.60", with the ECMWF and Canadian leaning wetter than the GFS solutions. Those particular models allow for amounts closer to an inch. That much rain isn’t out of the question if we can tap into better forcing with the warm front. The finer details, including exactly when the rain arrives and clears out, are still up in the air, with guidance differing. Rain is more likely to arrive Friday morning and carry on overnight. Most guidance shows a fairly progressive system, so it won’t hang around the whole weekend. The bulk of the rain is likely to clear out early Saturday morning. The system’s initial southerly LLJ also nudges in Friday. If mixing is sufficient, that’ll bring breezy to gusty southerly winds (gusts 20-30 mph).

A strong cold front crosses the region by early Saturday morning, putting the northeast in a classic cold air advection pattern: gusty NW winds, cooler temperatures, and isolated showers. A NW 40-50 kt LLJ sets up across the region Saturday. The tighter gradient and CAA, helping steepen lapse rates, will support deeper mixing and gusty NW winds. Just how much mixing there’ll be, and how strong the LLJ will be, remain less certain this far out. Still, ensembles show a signal for possible gusts of 35-45 mph, potentially higher across the coastal waters. Saturday’s 925mb temperatures range between - 2C to +2C, likely making it the coolest day of the season so far. Highs will likely struggle to get out of the 40s across the interior, with low 50s in the valleys and coastal plain. RH fields show moisture left over within the cyclonic flow pattern, which may be enough for the odd isolated shower. Some higher points across the Berkshires may only reach the 40 degree mark on Saturday. It’s a bit too early to say the "S word" just yet (snow). But if the timing lines up, a morning flurry or shower can’t be ruled out in those higher terrain spots out west. Expect a cold Saturday night, with high probabilities that the interior will at least drop to freezing. Higher elevation locations may drop into the upper 20s.
