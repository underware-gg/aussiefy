DETROIT, Dec. 13 (AP).—Henry Ford's first big go at his already announced campaign to teach the American public old-fashioned square dances is on Tuesday night, in one of Detroit's biggest dance halls. Mr. Ford is advertising for couples to dance the square dance and get the party's core group together.

Asked tonight whether he really reckoned the old-fashioned steps would come back, Mr. Ford exclaimed:

"Come back? They're here! We simply are falling back in line. What we are trying to do in getting out a book of dances is to standardize them. The first edition is being revised in the direction of simplicity."

Mr. Ford explained that four of the twenty-one quadrilles will be picked to make them easier to teach and help them catch on.

"Mellie" Dunham, Maine's champion fiddler, has been Mr. Ford's guest for a week. He's played for an old-fashioned dance party and chipped in several old-time tunes for the Ford "Americana" collection of those pieces, now being readied for publication. He was meant to be at the Tuesday night do. But the plans changed, and "Grandpa Mellie," along with the missus, is on his way to New York tonight. He may take up the call of the footlights there and sign a vaudeville contract.

"Mellie" Dunham will lob in New York this morning. Last night's reports said he'll be managed by Tex Rickard, who's said to be planning a barn dance in his honour at the brand-spankin'-new Madison Square Garden. The reports added that the hope in putting on the dance was to help Henry Ford make square dances popular again.
