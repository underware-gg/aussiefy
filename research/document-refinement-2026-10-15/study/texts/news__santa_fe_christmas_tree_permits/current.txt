Chrissy tree permits go on sale from November 13, 2025, online through Recreation.gov and in person at all Santa Fe National Forest District Offices and the Supervisor’s Office. Details of designated cutting areas, dates, and the types of trees you may cut are available here.

Buy your Christmas Tree permit:

Online through Recreation.gov: search for Santa Fe National Forest Christmas Tree Permits. Make sure you carefully read the overview and need-to-know information before buying a permit. You’ll also need to set up a Recreation.gov account or log in to finish the transaction.

In person at the Supervisor’s Office in Santa Fe, N.M., or the Jemez, Cuba, Coyote, Española, and Pecos/Las Vegas District Offices. Contact us for current hours.

At the Walatowa Visitor Center, permits will be sold in person only on Saturdays, from 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., from November 22 through December 20, 2024.

District offices may have pre-cut Christmas Trees for sale. Availability depends on weather conditions and supply, with priority going to people who can’t cut their own tree.

The SFNF’s Christmas tree permits cost $10 and are non-refundable. Buying online through recreation.gov adds a $2.50 service fee. One tag covers a tree up to 5 inches in diameter and 10 feet in height. A tree taller than 10 feet and/or wider than 5 inches needs an extra tag. A 15-foot tree, for example, needs two tags.

Fourth graders with an Every Kid Outdoors pass are eligible for a free Christmas tree permit. To apply, enter the pass or voucher number when buying a permit (note: a $2.50 service fee will be applied).

Cutting a Christmas tree helps forest health too. The permit system helps thin out densely populated stands of small-diameter trees. Local forest health experts identify areas that benefit from thinning trees that tend to be just the right size for Christmas trees. Removing these trees in designated areas helps other trees grow larger and can open up areas that provide forage for wildlife.
