The Metropolitan Council of Nashville and Davidson County in Tennessee, the southeastern U.S. state, has officially recognised the 50th anniversary of the first group of Kurdish families arriving in the U.S. It honours what they've put into the city's cultural, social and economic development.

An official proclamation obtained by VOA says the first group of Kurdish families lobbed in the United States on May 1, 1976. Many went on to settle in Nashville, Tennessee. Others set up communities in cities and states right across the country.

The proclamation singles out the Kurdish community's work over the past five decades to keep its language, culture and traditions alive, alongside its important part in Nashville's growth and development.

The document says more than 20,000 Kurds now call Nashville home, making it one of the largest Kurdish communities in the United States. The city's become an important centre of Kurdish cultural and community life.

The proclamation also recognises the longstanding relationship between the United States and Kurdistan. It points to Kurdish Peshmerga forces, U.S. troops and their allies working together in the fight against terrorism.

Metropolitan Nashville Council member Mike Cortese, who represents District 4, led the official recognition of the anniversary, working with other councillors.

The proclamation was signed on October 6, 2026.
