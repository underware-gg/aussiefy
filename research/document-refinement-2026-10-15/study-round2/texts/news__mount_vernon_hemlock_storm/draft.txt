Friday's big winter storm across the northeastern United States brought down a 227-year-old tree at Mount Vernon in Virginia, the estate of the first US President George Washington. Yesterday, the officials looking after Mount Vernon said the Canadian hemlock had been collected to preserve it and use in special projects.

The hemlock was planted in 1791. Friday's gusts, reportedly reaching 70 mph (about 110 kph), knocked it over.

Mount Vernon was Washington's own estate. He carried out most of his two presidential terms there between 1789–1797, cos the first White House wasn't finished yet. Mount Vernon officials say the hemlock is the estate's best documented tree: New York Governor George Clinton sent it as a gift, delivered in a whisky barrel. That governor wasn't related to the 42nd President Bill Clinton. George Clinton later served as the United States's Vice President under the Thomas Jefferson and James Madison administrations between 1805–1812.

Other historic US trees to come down recently include the seventh President Andrew Jackson's southern magnolia at the White House, and the Pioneer Cabin giant sequoia in California's Calaveras Big Trees State Park, known for the car-sized tunnel cut into its trunk.
