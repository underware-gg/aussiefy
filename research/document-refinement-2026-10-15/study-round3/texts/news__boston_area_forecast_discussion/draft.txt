KEY MESSAGE 2...The odd shower possible Wednesday, but drier on the whole. Dry again Thursday, with the sort of cool temperatures you’d expect this time of year.

A trailing cold front makes its way across the region Wednesday, with surface high pressure building in from the west as the day goes on. Model soundings show more mixing in the boundary layer, making things drier and bringing westerly winds with gusts 20-30 mph. Westerly, downslope flow, together with 925mb temperatures of +17- 18C, will make for a warm autumn day: highs in the 70s across plenty of areas. There are signs of a stray shower or two as a weak wave comes through, but they’ll be pretty few and far between. With the lower levels drier, there won’t be much in them—at most a light shower/sprinkle.

As high pressure settles in further into Thursday, the airmass dries out even more. Thursday’s likely to stay dry, though it’ll be cooler than Wednesday. A cooler airmass will have moved in as well, with 925mb temperatures around 10C cooler than Wednesday. That’ll put temperatures mainly in the lower 60s, the usual sort of cool for this time of year.

KEY MESSAGE 3...Low pressure system expected to bring rain Friday into early Saturday. Gusty northwest winds and temperatures below normal.

Deterministic and ensemble guidance show a stronger/deeper northern stream shortwave trough pushing across the Great Lakes and the northeast Friday into Saturday. There’s synoptic support behind this system: an upper level jet sets up near southern New England, producing large scale ascent while also advecting a plume of above normal moisture northward into the region. That sets things up for rain across the region. There’s a fair bit of moisture about, but instability looks limited, so the potential for heavy rain impacts is kept down. Ensemble guidance generally puts amounts from 0.25 to 0.60", with the ECMWF and Canadian leaning wetter than the GFS solutions. In those particular models, amounts closer to an inch are on the cards. We can’t rule those higher amounts out if we can tap into better forcing with the warm front. The finer details—just when the rain turns up and clears off—are still a bit up in the air, as guidance doesn’t yet agree. It’s more likely to turn up Friday morning and keep going overnight. Most guidance shows a fairly progressive system, so it won’t hang about all weekend. The bulk of the rain’s likely to clear off early Saturday morning. The initial southerly LLJ with this system nudges in Friday too. If there’s enough mixing, it’ll bring breezy to gusty southerly winds (gusts 20-30 mph).

A strong cold front pushes across the region by early Saturday morning, putting the northeast in a classic cold air advection pattern: gusty NW winds, cooler temperatures, and isolated showers. A NW 40-50 kt LLJ sets up across the region Saturday. The tighter gradient and CAA, helping steepen lapse rates, will support deeper mixing and gusty NW winds. Just how much mixing we’ll get, and the strength of the LLJ, are still hard to pin down this far out. But ensembles show a signal for possible gusts of 35-45 mph, potentially higher across the coastal waters. Saturday’s 925mb temperatures range between - 2C to +2C, likely bringing the coolest temperatures of the season so far. Highs will likely have a job getting out of the 40s across the interior, with low 50s in the valleys and coastal plain. RH fields show moisture still hanging about within the cyclonic flow pattern. That may be enough for the odd isolated shower. Some higher spots across the Berkshires may only make it to the 40 degree mark on Saturday. It’s a bit too early to say the "S word" just yet (snow). But if the timing lines up, we can’t rule out a morning flurry or shower in those higher terrain spots out west. Saturday night looks a cold one, with high probabilities that the interior will at least drop to freezing. Higher elevation locations may drop into the upper 20s.
