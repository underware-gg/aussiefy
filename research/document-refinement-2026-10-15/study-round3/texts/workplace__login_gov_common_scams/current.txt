Scammers are forever dreamin' up brand-spankin'-new tricks to con people into handin' over personal information or coughin' up fraudulent payments. Knowin' what they’re up to is your best defence. Here are some of the tricks that crop up most, and how to keep yourself safe.

## Fake emails and texts

Scammers send emails or texts dressed up to look official, often usin' government logos and wording. That’s called phishing. A phishing attack may:

- Push you to click a link to “verify your account”
- Say there’s an urgent problem with your benefits or tax return
- Ask for personal information such as Social Security numbers or bank details

### How to protect yourself

- Don’t go clickin' links or downloadin' attachments from senders you don’t know.
- Suss out whether the message is legit by gettin' in touch with the agency directly, usin' its official contact information.
- Report scam messages to the agency the scammer is tryin' to pass themselves off as

## Impersonation phone calls

Fraudsters may give you a ring, pretendin' they’re government representatives. They often:
- Threaten you with arrest, licence suspension, or loss of benefits
- Demand that you cough up straightaway via gift cards, cryptocurrency, or wire transfers

### How to protect yourself

- Government agencies will never ask you to pay over the phone.
- Hang up and ring the official number on the Login.gov or agency website.

## Fake websites and online portals

Some scams use shonky websites made to look like official government pages. These sites may:

- Ask you to log in with personal details
- Make you pay fees for services you can get free on official sites

### How to protect yourself

- Always check that the website URL ends in .gov.
- Give search engines a miss for sign in links—type the official URL straight in and double-check the spelling.

## Social media scams

Scammers set up fake social media profiles and pass themselves off as government officials. They may:
- Offer grants or financial assistance if you pay fees upfront
- Ask for personal or banking details in direct messages

### How to protect yourself

- Government agencies don’t send messages out of the blue about benefits.
- Report suss social media profiles to the social media platform.
