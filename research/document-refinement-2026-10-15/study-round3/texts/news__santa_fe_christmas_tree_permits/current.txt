You can get a Chrissy tree permit from November 13, 2025, online through Recreation.gov or in person at all Santa Fe National Forest District Offices and the Supervisor’s Office. Have a look here for the designated cutting areas, dates, and types of trees you’re allowed to cut.

Get your Christmas Tree permit:

Online through Recreation.gov: search for Santa Fe National Forest Christmas Tree Permits. Give the overview and need-to-know information a careful read before you buy. You’ll also need to set up a Recreation.gov account or log in to finish the purchase.

In person at the Supervisor’s Office in Santa Fe, N.M., or the Jemez, Cuba, Coyote, Española, and Pecos/Las Vegas District Offices. Get in touch with us for current hours.

At the Walatowa Visitor Center, you can buy permits in person only on Saturdays, from 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., from November 22 through December 20, 2024.

District offices may have pre-cut Christmas Trees for sale, depending on the weather and supply. People who can’t cut their own tree get first priority.

The SFNF’s Christmas tree permits are $10, with no refunds. Buying through recreation.gov adds a $2.50 service fee. One tag covers a tree up to 5 inches in diameter and 10 feet in height. If your tree is taller than 10 feet and/or wider than 5 inches, you’ll need another tag. A 15-foot tree, for instance, needs two tags.

Fourth graders with an Every Kid Outdoors pass are eligible for a free Christmas tree permit. They can apply by entering their pass or voucher number when buying a permit (note: a $2.50 service fee will be applied).

Cutting a Christmas tree does the forest some good too. The permit system helps thin out densely populated stands of small-diameter trees. Local forest health experts pick out areas that benefit from thinning trees that tend to be just the right size for Christmas trees. Taking those trees out of designated areas helps other trees grow bigger and can open up areas that provide forage for wildlife.
