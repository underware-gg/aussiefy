## B. Account Terms

**The guts of it:** _Personal Accounts and Organizations don't have the same administrative controls. A human must create your Account, you must be 13 or over, and you must give us a valid email address. You may not keep more than one free Account. You alone are responsible for your Account and anything that happens while you're signed in to it or using it. Keeping your Account secure is your responsibility._

### 1. Account Controls

* Users. Subject to these Terms, ultimate administrative control of your Personal Account and the Content in it stays with you.

* Organizations. The "owner" of an Organization created under these Terms has ultimate administrative control of that Organization and its Content. Within the Service, an owner can look after User access to the Organization’s data and projects. An Organization can have more than one owner, but at least one Personal Account must be designated as an owner of an Organization. If you're the owner of an Organization under these Terms, we consider you responsible for whatever's done on or through that Organization.

### 2. Required Information

To finish signing up, you must give us a valid email address. Any other details we ask for, such as your real name, are up to you, with two exceptions. If you're accepting these terms on behalf of a legal entity, we need extra information about that entity. If you go for a [paid Account](#l-payment), we'll need extra information for billing.

### 3. Account Requirements

We've got a few straightforward rules for Accounts on GitHub's Service.
* You must be a human to create an Account. Accounts registered by "bots" or other automated methods aren't allowed. Machine accounts are allowed:
* A machine account is an Account set up by an individual human who accepts the Terms on behalf of the Account, gives us a valid email address and is responsible for its actions. It's used just for automated tasks. More than one user may tell a machine account what to do, but at the end of the day, the Account owner is responsible for the machine's actions. You may keep no more than one free machine account on top of your free Personal Account.
* One person or legal entity may keep no more than one free Account. If you want to control a machine account as well, that's fine, but it's only for running a machine.
* You must be 13 or older. We're rapt to see brilliant young coders getting keen on learning to program, but we must stick to United States law. GitHub doesn't aim our Service at kids under 13, and we don't allow any Users under 13 on our Service. If we find out about a User under 13, we will [terminate that User’s Account immediately](#m-cancellation-and-termination). If you're a resident of a country outside the United States, your country’s minimum age might be higher; if it is, you're responsible for complying with your country’s laws.
* Your login is for one person only: you can't pass a single login round for several people to share. A paid Organization may only give access to as many Personal Accounts as your subscription allows.
* You may not use GitHub in breach of export control or sanctions laws of the United States or any other applicable jurisdiction. You may not use GitHub if you are, or are working on behalf of, a [Specially Designated National (SDN)](https://www.treasury.gov/resource-center/sanctions/SDN-List/Pages/default.aspx) or a person subject to similar blocking or denied party prohibitions administered by a U.S. government agency. GitHub may let people in certain sanctioned countries or territories access certain GitHub services under U.S. government authorisations. For more information, have a squiz at our [Export Controls policy](/site-policy/other-site-policies/github-and-trade-controls).
