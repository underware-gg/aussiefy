MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – From Friday, Sept. 4, the last fishing restrictions on the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon (downstream of Norris Campground) rivers and all their tributaries will come off, thanks to the weather and water cooling down. Anglers will be able to wet a line in these rivers, as well as most other park rivers, creeks and lakes, every day from sunrise to sunset, as set out in the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet.

These rivers were put off limits to fishing altogether on July 15. When conditions picked up, that was eased back to partial restrictions on Aug. 28. The restrictions and closures were needed cos water flows were low and water temperatures above 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius) can kill fish.

Staff will keep tabs on the water temperatures. Depending on what they find when they assess conditions, they may bring in temporary restrictions or closures.

Update: Yellowstone lifts full closures for Firehole, Madison and Gibbon rivers

Partial restrictions still apply from 2 p.m. to sunrise the next day

MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – From Friday, Aug. 28, anglers can wet a line in the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon (downstream of Norris Campground) rivers and all their tributaries from sunrise until 2 p.m. each day. The weather and water are cooling down, and there's rain in the current forecast, so the full closures can come off.

The rivers were closed altogether on July 15 cos low water flows and water temperatures above 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius) can kill fish. The water's cooled at night, making fishing possible for part of the day. On most other park rivers, creeks and lakes, anglers can fish every day from sunrise to sunset, as set out in the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet.

Staff will keep tabs on the water temperatures. Depending on what the condition assessments show, temporary restrictions may come off, or more closures may go on.

Anglers: Please pick the coolest times of day to fish, and land fish quickly. Don't play hooked trout till they're knackered. Go easy when handling fish, keeping them in the water as much as you can, and let them recover before release. Doing your bit will protect the park’s fisheries. It may also head off the need to ban fishing at all times of day on some rivers and creeks if conditions take a turn for the worse.
