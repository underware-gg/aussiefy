## Travel comp time, for everyone

You can ask for travel comp time when you’re approved to travel for work and the trip falls outside regular working hours. It’s for “temporary duty,” also known as “TDY” travel, _not_ local travel or commuting. TDY travel runs for more than 12 hours and needs a pre-approved authorisation in Concur.

You can earn travel comp time whatever your salary level or schedule type.

For more detail, have a squiz at OPM’s [Travel Comp Time Fact Sheet](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/pay-administration/fact-sheets/compensatory-time-off-for-travel/).

### Understanding travel comp time

Travel during your usual work day counts as regular hours, including getting to the airport from home.

Generally, travel comp time is a “gap filler” when you’re travelling outside your regular work hours on a regular work day, but aren’t officially “performing work.”

If you’re a current GSA employee, you can look through the [Compensation for Government Travel slidedeck](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RFSq_4KdMza_pkcHGp9Hy2RNR2ryLs0b/view?usp=sharing) for further details to work out how your hours break down. If you’ve got questions, please get in touch with your Timekeeper or an HR Specialist at your agency.

### Calculating travel comp time

To work out travel comp time hours, travel ends when you check in at the hotel or get home. The hours are based on elapsed time. Use the time zone you departed from to calculate that elapsed time.

To get your reported travel time right, we recommend keeping a log of:

- When you left home and got to the airport
- When your flights actually departed and arrived
- When you left the destination airport and got to your hotel

### Using travel comp time

Travel comp time expires one year (26 pay periods) after you earn it. Once that date comes round, you forfeit the leave. If you leave GSA, you forfeit any unused travel comp time.
