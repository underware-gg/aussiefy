A 227-year-old tree at Mount Vernon in Virginia, the estate of the first US President George Washington, copped it in Friday's big winter storm across the northeastern United States. Yesterday, the officials looking after Mount Vernon said the Canadian hemlock had been gathered up for preservation and special projects.

Planted in 1791, the hemlock was bowled over on Friday by gusts reportedly up to 70 mph (about 110 kph).

Mount Vernon was Washington's own place. He carried out most of his two presidential terms there between 1789–1797, cos the first White House still wasn't finished. Mount Vernon officials say there's a better record of this hemlock than any other tree on the estate: New York Governor George Clinton sent it as a present, delivered in a whisky barrel. That governor was no relation to the 42nd President Bill Clinton. George Clinton went on to be the United States's Vice President under the Thomas Jefferson and James Madison administrations between 1805–1812.

Other historic US trees to come down lately include the seventh President Andrew Jackson's southern magnolia at the White House, and the Pioneer Cabin giant sequoia in California's Calaveras Big Trees State Park, the one known for the car-sized tunnel cut through its trunk.
