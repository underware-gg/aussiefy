DETROIT, Dec. 13 (AP).—Henry Ford's first big crack at his already announced campaign to teach the American public old-fashioned square dances comes on Tuesday night, at one of Detroit's biggest dance halls. Mr. Ford's advertising for couples to give the square dance a go and make up the core of the party.

Asked tonight whether he really reckoned the old-fashioned steps would get another guernsey, Mr. Ford exclaimed:

"Come back? They're here! We simply are falling back in line. What we are trying to do in getting out a book of dances is to standardize them. The first edition is being revised in the direction of simplicity."

Four of the twenty-one quadrilles will be picked, Mr. Ford explained, to make teaching them less of a rigmarole and help them catch on.

"Mellie" Dunham, Maine's champion fiddler, has been Mr. Ford's guest for a week, playing at an old-fashioned dance do and chipping in several old-time tunes for the Ford "Americana" collection of such pieces, now being readied for publication. He was meant to put in an appearance at the Tuesday night bash. But the plans changed, and "Grandpa Mellie," with the missus, is off to New York tonight. He may answer the call of the footlights there and take up a vaudeville contract.

"Mellie" Dunham will lob in New York this morning. The word last night was that he'll be managed by Tex Rickard, who's said to be lining up a barn dance in his honour at the brand-spankin'-new Madison Square Garden. The reports added that the hope in putting on the dance was to give Henry Ford's push to make square dances popular again a leg-up.
