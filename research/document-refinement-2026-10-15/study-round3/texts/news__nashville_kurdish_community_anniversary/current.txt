The Metropolitan Council of Nashville and Davidson County in the southeastern U.S. state of Tennessee has officially recognised the 50th anniversary of the first group of Kurdish families reaching the U.S. It's giving them their due for what they've put into the city's cultural, social and economic development.

The first mob of Kurdish families lobbed in the United States on May 1, 1976, according to an official proclamation obtained by VOA. Many went on to put down roots in Nashville, Tennessee; others got communities up and running in cities and states right across the country.

The proclamation singles out the Kurdish community's work over the past five decades to keep its language, culture and traditions alive, while playing a big part in Nashville's growth and development.

More than 20,000 Kurds call Nashville home these days, the document says. That makes it one of the largest Kurdish communities in the United States. The city's become a key spot for Kurdish cultural and community life.

The proclamation also recognises the longstanding ties between the United States and Kurdistan, pointing to Kurdish Peshmerga forces, U.S. troops and their allies pulling together in the fight against terrorism.

Metropolitan Nashville Council member Mike Cortese, who represents District 4, took the lead on the official recognition of the anniversary, with other councillors lending a hand.

The proclamation was signed on October 6, 2026.
