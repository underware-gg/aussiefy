# The FOLD Auction

This FAQ gives you the good oil on FOLD Auction 2, the second public FOLD auction. It’s for people
who took part or need to claim FOLD afterwards. The questions break down into four groups: the
auction itself, participation, claiming and transferability, and safety.

## About the auction

### What is FOLD Auction 2?

FOLD Auction 2 was the second public FOLD auction. It ran on the Uniswap Continuous Clearing Auction
mechanism.

It came after the initial FOLD Auction, held July 8–10, 2026.

Auction 2 was kept to **2% of total FOLD supply** on purpose. It took place around the move to
general FOLD transferability. The aim was to spread distribution further and help get deeper,
longer-term on-chain liquidity off the ground.

### Why was there a second FOLD Auction?

Auction 2 was a limited distribution step ahead of general FOLD transferability on Aug 19.

It was small by design: **2% of total FOLD supply**. The floor it started with was the same as the
clearing price from the first FOLD Auction.

The auction gave a transparent, public way to distribute more FOLD before general transferability
kicked in. It also backed long-term on-chain liquidity.

**50% of proceeds were committed to long-term on-chain liquidity support.**

Auction 2 wrapped up shortly before general FOLD transferability was switched on.

### What happened in the first FOLD Auction?

The initial FOLD Auction ran from **July 8–10, 2026**, using the Uniswap Continuous Clearing
Auction mechanism.

The first auction’s done. It cleared at **0.02154816 USDC / FOLD**, and that’s also the floor for
FOLD Auction 2.

If you took part in the first auction, you can still get the details on claiming and what happens
after the auction through the official [Interfold auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction).

### What is a Uniswap Continuous Clearing Auction?

A Continuous Clearing Auction (CCA) is a price-discovery mechanism for distributing tokens. It’s
built on Uniswap infrastructure.

- The auction runs over a number of discrete periods.
- Participants put their bids in through the official Uniswap CCA interface.
- Every successful bid in a given period clears at the same clearing price.
- For each period after that, the minimum price is whichever is higher: the original floor price or
  the previous period’s clearing price.
- The minimum price may go up in later periods. It doesn’t drop below the applicable prior clearing
  price or the original floor.
- Any tokens left undistributed earlier are still available in the final period.

The CCA distributes tokens as it goes, with a uniform price within each period. The idea is to cut
gas competition, reduce sniping, and let clearing prices develop as people take part in the
auction.

[Read more about Uniswap CCAs](https://cca.uniswap.org).

### Did the auction determine the price of FOLD after transferability?

Nah. The auction settled allocations and a clearing price inside the auction itself. It didn’t set
FOLD’s market price when general transferability kicked in.

There’s no telling what future market prices will be.

## Participation

<Callout type='warning'>
  Auction 2 closed on Aug 19, 2026 at 13:06 UTC. These steps run through how it worked.
  You can’t put in a new bid.
</Callout>

### How did participation work?

1. Participants headed to the official [FOLD Auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction).
2. They followed the verified link to the Uniswap CCA interface.
3. They went through the required verification process, using the official flow.
4. They put their bids in during the auction window.
5. Once the auction had wrapped up, they could claim any allocated FOLD and unspent USDC through
   the verified auction interface.

Participants needed USDC on Ethereum Mainnet. They had to go over the auction terms and eligibility
requirements before taking part.

### Who could participate?

Eligible participants could take part, subject to jurisdiction, identity verification, AML and
sanctions screening, and other applicable restrictions.

The official verification process determined who was eligible. The auction terms governed eligibility.

Getting through verification wasn’t a guarantee of eligibility or an allocation.

### Was KYC required?

Yep. Participants had to get their identity verification done through the official verification
process before taking part.

The verification process determined eligibility and carried out the applicable AML and sanctions screening.

Participation was subject to the auction terms and applicable restrictions.

## Claiming and transferability

### When can participants claim FOLD?

Claiming was due to open as soon as the auction closed.

To claim allocated FOLD and any unspent USDC, participants go through the verified auction interface.

Claiming is one action; general FOLD transferability is another.

### When did general FOLD transferability begin?

FOLD Auction 2 closed Aug 19 at **13:06 UTC (9:06 AM ET)**.

General FOLD transferability could be switched on shortly afterwards, at or after **14:00 UTC (10 AM
ET)**.

At or after Unix timestamp **`1787148000`**, anyone could call the `tge()` function on the FOLD
contract to switch general transferability on. The TGE transaction went through on Aug 19, 2026 at
14:00:23 UTC. `tgeTimestamp()` returns `1787148023`. FOLD is now generally transferable.

FOLD Auction 2, claiming, general FOLD transferability, and Network Alpha are separate milestones
in the broader Interfold launch sequence. For the details, have a squiz at
[FOLD Transferability](/faq/transferability).

## Safety

### How do I know I’m using the official auction?

Stick to auction, claiming, verification, and support links published through official Interfold
channels.

These are the official Interfold channels:

- [Interfold auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction)
- [Interfold website](https://www.theinterfold.com/)
- [Interfold X account](https://x.com/theinterfold)
- [Interfold documentation](https://docs.theinterfold.com/)

Don’t trust:

- Auction, claiming, or support links that turn up in unsolicited DMs.
- Token or contract addresses copied from unofficial sources.
- Accounts passing themselves off as someone else.
- Private wallet-connection links.
- Requests for your seed phrase, private key, or wallet backup.
- Requests to cough up money for support.

<Callout type='warning'>
  Interfold team members and moderators will never ask you to hand over your seed phrase, private
  key, wallet backup, or access to your wallet.
</Callout>
