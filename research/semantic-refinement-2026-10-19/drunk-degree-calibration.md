# DRUNK semantic-degree calibration — 19 October 2026

Read-only research. No repository, runtime, status/gate, style intensity or register edits. All 18 current DRUNK entry IDs and all 19 stored forms (including bare `full`) are accounted below. This calibrates the alcohol sense; cross-associated FULL/TIRED/HIGH senses need their own evidence.

## Policy recommendation

Use three reviewed semantic bands: **mild**, **unmarked intoxication**, and **strong intoxication**. Keep **unknown** distinct from unmarked. Unmarked means the reviewed lexical description asserts intoxication without an independently established narrowing degree; it does not mean a measured middle BAC. Very/extremely are one strong band here, not scientifically distinct thresholds. Existing 1–5 `Entry.intensity` values are legacy soft intensity scores, with mixed historical strength/style usage; they are not automatically semantic certificates. Likewise, profanity, colourful imagery, age/register, level 5 and narrator-opinion permission do not certify an alcohol degree.

Preserve source degree in both directions. Apply the rule to the *realised proposition*, including qualifiers that survive substitution: retaining `very` before a compatible unmarked term can preserve a source degree; deleting a mild/strong degree embedded in tipsy/legless cannot. Do not classify every word in a dictionary's synonym list as degree-equivalent. Unknown/provisional degree remains available under existing rules when the source states no degree, and for writer discovery. Against an explicit mild/strong source, require known compatible realised degree; do not silently certify compatibility by inheriting a historical numeric value. A lower-degree candidate is not an acceptable automatic euphemism for a stronger explicitly stated source, and a stronger candidate must not replace a mild one. Intentional understatement remains a writer suggestion.

The bands are conventional lexical calibration, not claims about impairment, physiological risk, ability to consent, legal thresholds or an individual's condition. A broad drunk gloss does not prove a word can never occur in an extreme context. An explicit very-drunk edited gloss establishes a conventional strong reading even if another dictionary abbreviates it to drunk.

## Focused behavioural expectations for parent implementation

- Tipsy/tiddly or explicitly slight intoxication must not become legless, shit-faced, off-face or sozzled, even with narrator-opinion permission.
- Legless/smashed or explicit very/extreme intoxication must not become a-bit-full, nor bare unqualified pissed if that would discard embedded source degree.
- In an actually retained/composed `very pissed`, the surface modifier can preserve strong degree; compare realised output, not only a bare target entry value.
- An unmarked drunk input can use unmarked pissed/sloshed/shickered when existing dial/register checks permit; no assumption that all vivid DRUNK-family entries are semantically interchangeable.
- Unknown-degree vocabulary retains existing availability when input has no stated degree and remains discoverable; lack of an explicit-degree compatibility certificate is not a fabricated/non-Australian gate or a deletion.
- Test same candidate across ordinary and opinion-enabled modes so opinion allowance cannot bypass explicit severity preservation.

No new universal scalar rule is inferred for unrelated concepts. This is a bounded reviewed calibration of one genuinely graded concept and its current forms, suitable for a generic concept-policy/knownness mechanism.
