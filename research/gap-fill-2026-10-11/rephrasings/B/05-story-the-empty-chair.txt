The Empty Chair

Every night for forty years, Arthur and his missus had their tea at the little table by the kitchen window. Now there was only the one bowl on the table, and her chair just sat there empty.

For weeks after the funeral, Arthur was doin' it real tough. Wouldn't answer the phone. He was buggered all the time, even after a good long kip. His son'd come round Saturdays and find him out in the garden with a face as long as a wet week, watchin' the birds on the feeder without really seein' 'em.

One arvo a little nipper from next door knocked on the door. She was about eight, and she had a little scrap of a kitten in both hands.

"Mum reckons we can't keep him," she said. "D'ya want him?"

Arthur looked at the kitten for a good long while. Then, nice and slow, he held out his hands.

That night he put a second bowl down on the floor, next to the empty chair. The little fella drank its milk and conked out on the cushion where his missus used to sit. Arthur sat down beside it and had a bit of a howl, first time since the funeral. Next mornin', he felt a bit lighter.
