Eighty-seven years back, our forefathers set up a brand new country on this continent, dreamt up in freedom, and built on the idea that all men are created equal.

Now we're in the thick of a bloody great civil war, our own mob goin' at each other, findin' out whether that country, or any country dreamt up like that and built on the same idea, can go the distance. We've rocked up on a big battlefield from that war. We've come to set aside a patch of that paddock as a last restin' place for the blokes who gave their lives so that country could keep goin'. It's only fair and right that we do this.

But, lookin' at the bigger picture, we can't set aside — we can't bless — we can't make holy — this bit of dirt. The brave blokes, livin' and dead, who slugged it out here, have already made it holy, way beyond anythin' our piddlin' efforts could add or take away. The world won't take much notice, or remember for long, what we say here, but it'll never forget what they did here. It's on us still kickin', instead, to throw ourselves into the unfinished job that the blokes who fought here have got so far down the track, and done it proud. It's on us instead to knuckle down to the big job still in front of us — that from these honoured dead we take on even more of the devotion to what they were fightin' for, the cause they gave every last bit of themselves for — that we here swear, dead set, that these blokes won't have died for nothin' — that this country, under God, gets a fresh start at bein' free — and that government of the people, by the people, for the people, won't be wiped off the face of the earth.
