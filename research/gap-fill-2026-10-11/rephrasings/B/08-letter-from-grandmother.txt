Dear Emily,

Ta for your lovely letter and the snaps from the weddin'. You looked a real stunner, and the flowers in your hair were pretty as a picture. Your grandad reckons your new hubby is a good sort, which gave me a laugh, 'cause he never notices that kind of thing.

You asked about the old days, when I was just a kid. Well, we were poor as church mice. After me dad lost his job, our family didn't have a brass razoo for nearly two years. We lived in two rooms over a shop, and there were five of us crammed into the one bedroom. Plenty of families in our street were doin' it just as tough. Me mum sold her mum's ring to buy us shoes for school.

But people'd give ya their last bob back then. The baker left a loaf on our step every Friday, and he never said a dicky bird about it. I've never forgotten that. It's why I always chip in what I can, even now.

The garden's goin' great guns this year. The lemon tree's loaded and the roses are out. Pop round when you're back from your trip, and bring that hubby of yours. I'll knock up a cake.

All my love,
Nan
