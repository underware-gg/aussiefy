The New Neighbours

When the new mob moved into the place across the road, the whole street was stickybeakin'. They rocked up late on a Thursdy night with a van, a trailer and a big bastard of a dog.

Inside a week, everyone had made their minds up. Mrs Patel at number twelve reckoned they were a bit up themselves, 'cause the dad hadn't waved back at her. Mr Doyle reckoned they were a rowdy lot, 'cause the mutt barked at the postie. Old Mr Fenwick, who in his day could never keep his trap shut about anyone, said they looked dodgy as and prob'ly owed money.

Then, one stinkin' hot Satdy, a fire took off in the dry grass behind Mrs Patel's fence. The new dad spotted the smoke first. He legged it across the road with a hose, jumped the fence and had it out before the firies showed up. He came back absolutely drenched and black with ash.

Next mornin' Mrs Patel knocked on their door with a cake. She stayed an hour, havin' a good old yak with the mum on the front step. By the end of the month, no-one on the street remembered they'd ever seemed a bit funny at all.
