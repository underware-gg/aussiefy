Eighty-seven years back, our forefathers set up a new nation on this continent, dreamt up in freedom and built on the idea of a fair go: that all men are created equal.

Now we're in the thick of a big civil war, finding out whether that nation, or any nation set up like that and built on the same idea, can go the distance. We've come together on a big battlefield of that war. We've come to set aside a patch of that field as a last resting place for the blokes who gave their lives here so that nation could live. It's only right and proper that we do this.

But when you think about it, we can't really dedicate, we can't bless, we can't make holy, this ground. The brave blokes, living and dead, who battled it out here have already done that, way beyond our poor power to add anything or take anything away. The world won't take much notice of, or remember for long, what we say here, but it'll never forget what they did here. It's really up to us who are still standing to throw ourselves into the unfinished job that those who fought here have pushed so far, and done so proud. It's really on us to get stuck into the big job still in front of us: that from these honoured dead we take even more devotion to the cause they gave everything for; that we here make a fair dinkum promise that these blokes won't have died for nothing; that this nation, under God, gets a fresh start at freedom; and that government of the people, by the people, for the people won't be wiped off the face of the earth.
