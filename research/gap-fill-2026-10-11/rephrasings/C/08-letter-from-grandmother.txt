Dear Emily,

Thanks ever so much for your lovely letter and the photos from the wedding. You looked a picture, and the flowers in your hair were just gorgeous. Your grandfather reckons your new hubby's a bit of a spunk, which gave me a good laugh, because he never notices anything like that.

You asked about the old days, when I was a little tacker. Well, we were dirt poor. After my father lost his job, we didn't have a brass razoo for nearly two years. We lived in two rooms above a shop, and there were five of us in the one bedroom. Plenty of families in our street were doing it just as tough. My mother sold her mother's ring to buy us shoes for school.

But people were big-hearted. The baker left a loaf on our step every Friday, and he never once said a word about it. I've never forgotten that. It's why I always give what I can, even now.

The garden's going great guns this year. The lemon tree's loaded, and the roses are out. Come round when you're back from your trip, and bring that hubby of yours. I'll bake a cake.

All my love,
Nan
