The New Neighbours

When the new mob moved into the place across the road, the whole street was having a stickybeak. They rocked up late on a Thursday night with a van, a trailer and a bloody big dog.

Within a week, everyone had made up their minds. Mrs Patel at number twelve reckoned they were up themselves, because the father hadn't waved back at her. Mr Doyle reckoned they were a rowdy lot, because the dog yapped at the postie. Old Mr Fenwick, who'd been a shocking gossip in his day, said they looked a bit dodgy and probably owed money.

Then, one stinking hot Saturday, a fire took off in the dry grass behind Mrs Patel's fence. The new dad spotted the smoke first. He bolted across the road with a hose, hopped the fence and put it out before the fireys got there. He came back absolutely saturated and covered in ash.

The next morning Mrs Patel knocked on their door with a cake. She stayed an hour, having a good old chinwag with the mum on the front step. By the end of the month, nobody on the street remembered they'd ever seemed a bit funny at all.
