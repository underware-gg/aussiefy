The Empty Chair

Every night for forty years, Arthur and his missus had their tea at the little table by the kitchen window. Now there was only the one bowl on the table, and her chair sat empty.

For weeks after the funeral, Arthur was doing it tough. He didn't pick up the phone. He felt buggered all the time, even after a good long sleep. His son came round on Saturdays and found him flat as a tack, sitting out in the backyard, watching the birds at the feeder without really seeing them.

One arvo a kid from next door knocked on the door. She was about eight, and she had a little scrap of a kitten cupped in both hands.

"Mum says we can't keep him," she said. "D'you want him?"

Arthur looked at the kitten for a good long while. Then, ever so slowly, he held out his hands.

That night he put a second bowl on the floor, by the empty chair. The kitten lapped up its milk and dropped off on the cushion where his missus used to sit. Arthur sat down beside it and had a good cry for the first time since the funeral. In the morning, he felt a bit lighter.
