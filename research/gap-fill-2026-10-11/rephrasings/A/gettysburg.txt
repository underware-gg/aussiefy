Eighty-seven years back, our forefathers set up a new nation on this continent, dreamt up in the name of freedom, and built on the idea that everyone's created equal.

Now we're caught up in a massive civil war, a real test of whether that nation, or any nation started up like that and built on those ideas, can hang on for the long haul. We've met on a big battlefield of that war. We've come to set aside a patch of that field as a final resting place for the blokes who gave their lives here so that nation could live. It's only fitting and right that we do this.

But, when you get down to it, we can't dedicate — we can't make holy — we can't bless — this ground. The brave blokes, living and dead, who battled it out here have already made it sacred, way beyond anything the likes of us could add or take away. The world won't take much notice, or remember for long, what we say here, but it'll never forget what they did here. It's really up to us, the living, to get stuck into the unfinished work that the ones who fought here have pushed along so well so far. It's up to us instead to knuckle down to the big job still in front of us — that from these honoured dead we take on even more devotion to the cause they gave every last bit of themselves for — that we here make up our minds, fair dinkum, that these dead won't have died for nothing — that this nation, under God, gets a fresh start at freedom — and that government of the people, by the people, for the people, won't vanish off the face of the earth.
