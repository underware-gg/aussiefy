The Empty Chair

Every night for forty years, Arthur and his wife had their tea at the little table by the kitchen window. Now there was only one bowl on the table, and her chair sat empty.

For weeks after the funeral, Arthur was doing it tough. He didn't answer the phone. He was stuffed all the time, even after a long night's sleep. His son came round on Saturdays and found him flat as a tack, sitting out the back, watching the birds at the feeder without really seeing them.

One arvo a little girl from next door knocked on the door. She'd have been about eight, and she had a tiny kitten cupped in both hands.

"Mum says we can't keep him," she said. "D'you want him?"

Arthur looked at the kitten for a long while. Then he slowly held out his hands.

That night he put a second bowl on the floor, next to the empty chair. The kitten lapped up its milk and nodded off on the cushion where his wife used to sit. Arthur sat with it and had a howl for the first time since the funeral. In the morning, he felt a bit lighter.
