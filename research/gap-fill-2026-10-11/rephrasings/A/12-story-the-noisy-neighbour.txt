The New Neighbours

When the new family moved into the house across the road, the whole street was curious. They rolled in late on a Thursday night with a van, a trailer and a flamin' big dog.

Within a week, everyone had made up their minds. Mrs Patel at number twelve reckoned they were a bit up themselves, because the father hadn't waved back at her. Mr Doyle reckoned they were a rowdy lot, because the dog barked at the postie. Old Mr Fenwick, who'd once been the worst stickybeak in the street, said they looked a bit suss and probably owed money.

Then, one stinking hot Saturday, a fire started in the dry grass behind Mrs Patel's fence. The new dad spotted the smoke first. He bolted across the road with a hose, hopped the fence and had it out before the fireys turned up. He came back saturated and covered in ash.

The next morning Mrs Patel knocked on their door with a cake. She stayed for an hour, having a good old chinwag with the mum on the front step. By the end of the month, nobody in the street could remember they'd ever seemed a bit funny at all.
