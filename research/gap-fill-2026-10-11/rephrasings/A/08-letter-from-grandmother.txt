Dear Emily,

Thank you for your lovely letter and the photos from the wedding. You looked a million dollars, and the flowers in your hair were so pretty. Your grandfather reckons your new hubby is a real good sort, which made me laugh, because he never notices anything like that.

You asked about the old days, when I was a littlie. Well, we were very poor. After my father lost his job, we didn't have a brass razoo for nearly two years. We lived in two rooms above a shop, and there were five of us in the one bedroom. Plenty of families in our street were doing it just as tough. My mother sold her mother's ring to buy us shoes for school.

But people would give you the shirt off their back. The baker left a loaf on our step every Friday, and he never once said a word about it. I've never forgotten that. It's why I always give what I can, even now.

The garden's going well this year. The lemon tree is loaded, and the roses are out. Come and visit when you're back from your trip, and bring that husband of yours. I'll bake a cake.

With all my love,
Nan
