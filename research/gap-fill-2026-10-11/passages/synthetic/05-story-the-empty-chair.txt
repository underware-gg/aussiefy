The Empty Chair

Every evening for forty years, Arthur and his wife had eaten dinner at the small table by the kitchen window. Now there was only one bowl on the table, and her chair stood empty.

For weeks after the funeral, Arthur was despondent. He did not answer the phone. He felt weary all the time, even after a long night's sleep. His son came round on Saturdays and found him dejected, sitting in the garden, watching the birds at the feeder without really seeing them.

One afternoon a girl from next door knocked on the door. She was about eight, and she held a tiny kitten in both hands.

"Mum says we can't keep him," she said. "Would you like him?"

Arthur looked at the kitten for a long time. Then he slowly held out his hands.

That night he put a second bowl on the floor, by the empty chair. The kitten drank its milk and fell asleep on the cushion where his wife used to sit. Arthur sat beside it and cried for the first time since the funeral. In the morning, he felt a little lighter.
