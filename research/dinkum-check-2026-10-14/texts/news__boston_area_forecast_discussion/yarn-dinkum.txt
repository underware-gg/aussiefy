KEY MESSAGE 2...Maybe the odd shower Wednesday, but drier all up. Dry again Thursday, and about as cool as you'd expect this time of year.

A trailing cold front slides across the region Wednesday, with surface high pressure building in from the west through the day. Model soundings show more mixing in the boundary layer, which backs up the drier conditions and also brings westerly winds gusting 20-30 mph. That westerly, downslope flow, on top of 925mb temperatures of +17- 18C, should give us a warm autumn day, with highs in the 70s (low to mid 20s C) in a fair few spots. There's a hint of a stray shower or two as a weak wave wanders through, but it won't cover much ground, and at most you'll cop a light shower/sprinkle, cos the lower levels are that bit drier.

As the high settles in further into Thursday, the airmass dries out even more. Thursday's likely to be dry, though cooler than Wednesday. A cooler airmass will have rolled in by then too, with 925mb temperatures around 10C cooler than Wednesday. That works out to about the usual cool for the season, mostly in the lower 60s (round 16C).

KEY MESSAGE 3...Low pressure system tipped to bring rain Friday into early Saturday. Gusty nor'westers and colder than normal.

Deterministic and ensemble guidance both show a stronger/deeper northern stream shortwave trough pushing across the Great Lakes and the northeast Friday into Saturday. This system's got synoptic support behind it, with an upper level jet parking itself near southern New England, giving large scale ascent and also advecting a plume of above normal moisture north into the region. That sets things up for a widespread rain. Mind you, for all that moisture, instability looks limited, so the chance of heavy rain doing any real damage is pretty slim. Ensemble guidance has amounts mostly from 0.25 to 0.60", with the ECMWF and Canadian leaning wetter than the GFS solutions. Going by those particular models, closer to an inch is on the cards. Those bigger totals aren't out of the question either, if we can tap into better forcing with the warm front. The finer details, like exactly when the rain rocks up and when it clears off, are less certain, cos the guidance still doesn't quite agree. The rain's more likely to show up sometime Friday morning and keep going overnight. Most guidance has this system being fairly progressive, meaning it won`t hang about the whole weekend. The bulk of the rain should clear out early Saturday morning. On top of that, the initial southerly LLJ with this system nudges in Friday, and if it mixes down enough, it'll bring breezy to gusty southerlies (gusts 20-30 mph).

A strong cold front barrels across the region by early Saturday morning, putting the northeast in a classic cold air advection pattern: gusty NW winds, cooler temperatures and the odd isolated shower. A NW 40-50 kt LLJ sets up across the region Saturday. The tighter gradient and CAA helping steepen lapse rates will mean deeper mixing, and so gusty NW winds. How much mixing there'll be, and how strong the LLJ gets, is still a bit up in the air this far out, but the ensembles are flagging gusts of 35-45 mph, maybe more across the coastal waters. 925mb temperatures range between - 2C to +2C Saturday, which'll likely make it the coldest day of the season so far. Highs will likely struggle to get out of the 40s (single figures C) across the interior, with low 50s in the valleys and coastal plain. RH fields show some leftover moisture in the cyclonic flow pattern, and that might be enough for a few isolated showers. Some of the higher spots across the Berkshires might only just scrape the 40 degree mark (about 4C) on Saturday. It`s a bit early to be saying the "S word" just yet (snow). But...if the timing lines up, a morning flurry or shower can`t be ruled out up in that higher country out west. Saturday night'll be brass monkeys, with a good chance the interior drops to freezing at least. Up on the higher ground it may get down into the upper 20s (round -2C).
