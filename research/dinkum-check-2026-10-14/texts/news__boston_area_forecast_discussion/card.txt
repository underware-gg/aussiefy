yarn-dinkum: The most natural and accurate retelling: varied, genuinely Australian forecaster talk, and the only one to get seasonably cool right.
  minor: 'Maybe the odd shower Wednesday, but drier all up.' (for 'Isolated shower possible Wednesday, but drier overall.'): "All up" means in total (all up it cost $50); for "overall" in a weather sense it strains a little.
  minor: 'so the chance of heavy rain doing any real damage is pretty slim' (for 'thus minimizing the potential for heavy rain impacts'): Narrows "impacts" to damage and makes the call a little more definite than the original.
  minor: 'Mind you, for all that moisture, instability looks limited' (for 'Despite plentiful moisture, instability appears to be limited'): "Mind you" is fine here, but a touch British-leaning.

dinkum-3: A lively, mostly natural level-4 retelling with useful conversions, let down by turning seasonably cool into below normal.
  major: "Then it's dry again for Thursday, and a bit cool for this time of year." (for 'Dry conditions return for Thursday with seasonably cool temperatures.'): "Seasonably cool" means normal for the season; "a bit cool for this time of year" says below normal, changing the forecast (repeated as "a bit on the cool side for this time of year").
  minor: 'That sets things up for rain right across the board.' (for 'This will set the stage for a widespread rain.'): "Across the board" means in all categories, not over the whole area; strains for widespread.
  minor: 'which backs drier weather and westerly winds' (for 'supporting drier conditions and also westerly winds'): "Backs" for physically supporting conditions is stiff and unidiomatic.
  minor: "There's heaps of moisture, mind, but instability looks limited" (for 'Despite plentiful moisture, instability appears to be limited'): The "mind" tag leans British; a weaker choice.

dinkum-4: Solid, natural level-4 retelling with good conversions, but it mistranslates seasonably cool and repeats a couple of tics.
  major: "Dry weather's back for Thursday, and it'll be cool for this time of year." (for 'Dry conditions return for Thursday with seasonably cool temperatures.'): "Seasonably cool" means typical for the season; this says cooler than usual, changing the forecast (also "on the cool side for the season").
  minor: 'Expect a brass monkey night Saturday night' (for 'Expect a cold night Saturday night'): Repeats "night"; clunky join.
  minor: 'which backs up drier conditions' (for 'supporting drier conditions'): "Backs up" for a physical driver is a strained sense, repeated later as "will back up deeper mixing".
