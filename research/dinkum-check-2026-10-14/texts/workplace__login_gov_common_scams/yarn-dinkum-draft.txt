Scammers are forever cookin' up brand-spankin'-new ways to con people into handin' over their personal info or makin' dodgy payments. Keepin' yer wits about ya on their tricks is yer best defence. Down below are some of the most common swifties they pull, and how to look after yerself.

## Fake emails and texts

Scammers send emails or texts that look all official, often usin' government logos and the lingo to match. This is called phishing. Phishing attacks might:

- Hassle ya to click on a link to “verify your account”
- Reckon there's some urgent drama with yer benefits or tax return
- Ask for personal info like Social Security numbers or bank details

### How to protect yourself

- Don't click on links or download attachments from senders ya don't know from a bar of soap.
- Make sure the message is fair dinkum by gettin' onto the agency directly, usin' its official contact details.
- Dob in dodgy messages to the agency the scammer's tryin' to pass themselves off as

## Impersonation phone calls

Shonks might give ya a ring makin' out they're government representatives. They'll often:
- Threaten ya with arrest, a licence suspension, or losin' yer benefits
- Want the dosh right this second, by gift cards, cryptocurrency, or wire transfers

### How to protect yourself

- Government agencies will never ask ya for payment over the phone.
- Hang up and ring the official number listed on the Login.gov or agency website.

## Fake websites and online portals

Some scams use slanter websites knocked together to look like official government pages. These sites might:

- Get ya to log in with yer personal details
- Sting ya fees for services that are free on the official sites

### How to protect yourself

- Always check the website URL ends in .gov.
- Don't go usin' search engines for sign in links—type the official URL in yerself and double-check the spellin'.

## Social media scams

Scammers set up fake social media profiles makin' out they're government officials. They might:
- Offer grants or a bit of financial help, so long as ya cough up fees upfront
- Ask for yer personal or bankin' details through direct messages

### How to protect yourself

- Bear in mind government agencies don't send ya messages out of the blue about benefits.
- Dob in suss profiles on social media to the social media platform.
