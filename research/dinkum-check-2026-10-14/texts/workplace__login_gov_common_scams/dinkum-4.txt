Scammers are forever cookin' up brand-spankin'-new ways to con people into handin' over their personal details or makin' dodgy payments. Keepin' up with their tricks is yer best defence. Down below are some of the most common cons goin' round, and how to look after yerself.

## Fake emails and texts

Scammers send emails or texts that look official, often with government logos and the government lingo. This is called phishing. Phishing attacks might:

- Hurry ya up to click a link to “verify your account”
- Make out there's an urgent problem with yer benefits or tax return
- Ask for personal details like Social Security numbers or bank details

### How to protect yourself

- Don't click on links or download attachments from senders ya don't know.
- Check the message is fair dinkum by gettin' onto the agency directly, usin' their official contact details.
- Report dodgy messages to the agency the scammer's makin' out to be

## Impersonation phone calls

Fraudsters might ring up pretendin' to be from the government. They'll often:
- Threaten ya with arrest, losin' yer licence, or losin' yer benefits
- Demand ya pay up on the spot with gift cards, crypto, or wire transfers

### How to protect yourself

- Government agencies will never ask ya to pay over the phone.
- Hang up and ring the official number listed on the Login.gov or agency website.

## Fake websites and online portals

Some scams use dodgy websites made up to look like official government pages. These sites might:

- Ask ya to log in with yer personal details
- Sting ya fees for services that are free on the official sites

### How to protect yourself

- Always check the website URL ends in .gov.
- Don't use search engines to find sign-in links—type the official URL in yerself and double-check the spellin'.

## Social media scams

Scammers set up fake social media profiles makin' out they're government officials. They might:
- Offer grants or money to help ya out, if ya pay fees up front
- Ask for yer personal or bankin' details through direct messages

### How to protect yourself

- Keep in mind government agencies don't send messages out of the blue about benefits.
- Report suss profiles on social media to the social media platform.
