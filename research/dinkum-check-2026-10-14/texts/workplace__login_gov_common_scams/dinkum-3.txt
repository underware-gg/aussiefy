Scammers never stop cookin' up brand-spankin'-new ways to con ya into handin' over yer personal info or makin' a dodgy payment. Knowin' their tricks is the best defence ya've got. So here's a rundown of the most common rorts goin' round, and how to look after yerself.

## Fake emails and texts

Scammers send emails or texts that look like the real deal, often with government logos and the official lingo slapped on. This is called phishing. A phishing attack might:

- Hurry ya along to click a link and “verify your account”
- Make out there's some urgent problem with yer benefits or yer tax return
- Ask for personal info, like Social Security numbers or bank details

### How to protect yourself

- Don't click links or download attachments from anyone ya don't know.
- Check whether the message is fair dinkum by gettin' onto the agency yerself, usin' its official contact details.
- Report dodgy messages to whatever agency the scammer's pretendin' to be

## Impersonation phone calls

Fraudsters might give ya a ring makin' out they're from the government. Nine times outta ten they'll:
- Threaten ya with arrest, a licence suspension, or losin' yer benefits
- Want payin' on the spot, by gift cards, cryptocurrency, or wire transfers

### How to protect yourself

- Government agencies will never ask ya to pay over the phone.
- Hang up, then ring the official number listed on the Login.gov or agency website.

## Fake websites and online portals

Some scams run dodgy websites made up to look just like official government pages. These sites might:

- Get ya to log in with yer personal details
- Charge ya fees for services that are free on the official sites

### How to protect yourself

- Always check the website URL ends in .gov.
- Don't go lookin' up sign in links on a search engine—type the official URL in yerself and double-check the spellin'.

## Social media scams

Scammers set up fake social media profiles makin' out they're government officials. They might:
- Offer grants or a bit of financial help, as long as ya pay some fees up front
- Ask for yer personal or bankin' details in a direct message

### How to protect yourself

- Bear in mind government agencies don't send messages about benefits out of the blue.
- Report suss profiles on social media to the platform they're on.
