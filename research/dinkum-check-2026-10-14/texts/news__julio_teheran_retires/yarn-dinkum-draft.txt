Julio Teherán, a two-time Major League Baseball All-Star, has called it a day on baseball, announcing his retirement on Monday at the age of 35. The call came two days after he was pulled from a scheduled start for Team Colombia in the 2026 World Baseball Classic (WBC) with a crook shoulder.

Before he hung up the glove, Teherán had helped Team Colombia knock off Team Panama 3-1 in the WBC.

Teherán had a thirteen-year MLB career, mostly with the Atlanta Braves, though he also had stints with the Los Angeles Angels, Detroit Tigers, Milwaukee Brewers, and New York Mets. He made his MLB debut with the Braves in 2011 as a 20-year-old and stuck with them for his first nine seasons, picking up All-Star selections in 2014 and 2016.

All up, Teherán finished his MLB career with an 81-82 record, a 3.85 ERA, a 1.223 WHIP, and 1,260 strikeouts in 1,470.2 innings. He'd also spent the 2022 and 2025 seasons playing in the Mexican Baseball League.
