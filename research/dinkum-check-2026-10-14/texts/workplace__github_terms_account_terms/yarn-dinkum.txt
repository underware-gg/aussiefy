## B. Account Terms

**Short version:** _Personal Accounts and Organizations get different admin controls; a human must create your Account; you must be 13 or over; you must provide a valid email address; and you may not have more than one free Account. You alone are responsible for your Account and anything that happens while you're signed in to or using it. Keeping your Account secure is on you, too._

### 1. Account Controls

* Users. Subject to these Terms, you keep the final say on admin control over your Personal Account and the Content in it.

* Organizations. The "owner" of an Organization that was created under these Terms has the final say on admin control over that Organization and the Content in it. Inside the Service, an owner can sort out which Users get access to the Organization’s data and projects. An Organization can have more than one owner, but there must be at least one Personal Account designated as an owner of an Organization. If you're the owner of an Organization under these Terms, we reckon you're responsible for whatever's done on or through that Organization.

### 2. Required Information

You must provide a valid email address to finish signing up. Anything else we ask for, like your real name, is optional, unless you're accepting these terms on behalf of a legal entity (in which case we need more info about the legal entity) or you go for a [paid Account](#l-payment), in which case we'll need a bit more info for billing.

### 3. Account Requirements

We've only got a few simple rules for Accounts on GitHub's Service.
* You must be a human to create an Account. Accounts registered by "bots" or other automated methods aren't allowed. Machine accounts are fine, though:
* A machine account is an Account set up by an individual human who accepts the Terms on behalf of the Account, provides a valid email address, and is responsible for what it does. A machine account is only ever used for running automated tasks. Multiple users can direct what a machine account does, but at the end of the day the owner of the Account is responsible for the machine's actions. You may maintain no more than one free machine account on top of your free Personal Account.
* One person or legal entity may maintain no more than one free Account (if you want to run a machine account as well, that's no worries, but it can only be used for running a machine).
* You must be age 13 or older. We're stoked to see bright young coders get excited about learning to program, but we have to comply with United States law. GitHub doesn't aim our Service at kids under 13, and we don't permit any Users under 13 on our Service. If we find out about any User under the age of 13, we'll [terminate that User’s Account immediately](#m-cancellation-and-termination). If you live in a country outside the United States, your country’s minimum age may be older; if so, it's up to you to comply with your country’s laws.
* Your login may only be used by one person — i.e., a single login may not be shared by multiple people. A paid Organization may only provide access to as many Personal Accounts as your subscription allows.
* You may not use GitHub in violation of export control or sanctions laws of the United States or any other applicable jurisdiction. You may not use GitHub if you are or are working on behalf of a [Specially Designated National (SDN)](https://www.treasury.gov/resource-center/sanctions/SDN-List/Pages/default.aspx) or a person subject to similar blocking or denied party prohibitions administered by a U.S. government agency. GitHub may let people in certain sanctioned countries or territories access certain GitHub services under U.S. government authorisations. For more info, have a squiz at our [Export Controls policy](/site-policy/other-site-policies/github-and-trade-controls).
