DETROIT, Dec. 13 (AP).—Henry Ford's already let on he's got a campaign goin' to teach the American public the old-fashioned square dances, and his first big crack at it'll be Tuesday night at one of the biggest dance halls in Detroit. For the do, Mr. Ford's puttin' ads out for couples to come 'n dance the square dance and make up the core of the party.

Asked tonight whether he really reckoned the old-fashioned steps'd come back, Mr. Ford fair shot back:

"Come back? They're here! We simply are falling back in line. What we are trying to do in getting out a book of dances is to standardise them. The first edition is being revised in the direction of simplicity."

Of the twenty-one quadrilles, Mr. Ford explained, they'll pick four, so they're easier to teach and more punters'll get keen on 'em.

"Mellie" Dunham, the champion fiddler of Maine, who's been stayin' as Mr. Ford's guest for a week, playin' for an old-fashioned dance party and chippin' in a fair few old-time tunes for the Ford "Americana" of them pieces that's bein' got ready to print, was meant to rock up at the Tuesday night do. But the plans got changed, and "Grandpa Mellie," with the missus, is on his way tonight to New York, where he might answer the call of the footlights and sign up for a vaudeville contract.

"Mellie" Dunham'll lob in New York this mornin'. Word was last night he'll be managed by Tex Rickard, who's s'posed to be plannin' a barn dance in his honour at the brand-spankin'-new Madison Square Garden. They reckon puttin' on this dance should help Henry Ford along with gettin' square dances goin' again.
