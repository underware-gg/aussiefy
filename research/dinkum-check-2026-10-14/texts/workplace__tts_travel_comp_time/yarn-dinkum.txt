## Travel comp time, for everyone

You can ask for travel comp time when you've been approved to travel for work and that travel falls outside your regular working hours. Travel comp time is for “temporary duty,” also known as “TDY” travel, _not_ local trips or your commute. TDY travel runs more than 12 hours and needs a pre-approved authorisation in Concur.

It doesn't matter what salary level or schedule type you're on: there are no limits on earning travel comp time either way.

Have a squiz at OPM’s [Travel Comp Time Fact Sheet](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/pay-administration/fact-sheets/compensatory-time-off-for-travel/) if you're after more detail.

### Understanding travel comp time

Travelling during your usual work day counts as regular hours, and that includes getting from home out to the airport.

Generally, travel comp time is a “gap filler” for when you're travelling outside your regular work hours on a regular work day, but not officially “performing work.”

For the finer points, current GSA employees can check the [Compensation for Government Travel slidedeck](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RFSq_4KdMza_pkcHGp9Hy2RNR2ryLs0b/view?usp=sharing) to work out how your hours break down. Got questions? Please get in touch with your Timekeeper or an HR Specialist at your agency.

### Calculating travel comp time

When you're working out travel comp time hours, travel ends once you check in at the hotel or get home. The hours go on elapsed time, and you use the time zone you left from to work that out.

To get your travel time right, we reckon it's worth keeping a log of:

- When you left home and when you got to the airport
- When your flights actually departed and got in
- When you left the destination airport and when you got to your hotel

### Using travel comp time

Travel comp time runs out one year (26 pay periods) after you earn it. Once you hit the expiry date, you lose the leave. And if you leave GSA, any travel comp time you haven't used is gone too: it's forfeited.
