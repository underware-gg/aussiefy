## Examples of acceptable and unacceptable gifts

So what does “of value” actually mean? First up, keep in mind that no gifts are acceptable for government officials (ok, maybe stickers). Always ask them what their policies allow and what they rule out. And if you're not sure, please run it by Legal to make sure. For example, plenty of U.S. congresspeople won't let you take them out for dinner and pick up the bill.

As for other customers or prospective customers, some things might look like small potatoes to you but mean a lot to someone else. Say you get your mate’s daughter a gig as an intern: it mightn't seem like a big deal to you, but it could be worth heaps to them. And if she's not qualified and you pull strings to make it happen, then it isn't ok. On top of that, if the idea is to improperly influence someone, anything at all, however small, might be counted as "of value."

🙅 _Here are some other things that aren't ok:_

* First class airfare to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️+🍾
* Winery tours 🍷
* Putting in a good word to get a friend’s kid into a prestigious private school 🎓
* Tickets to Disneyland 👸, the World Cup 🥅, the Olympics 🏅
* A futbol ⚽️ signed by a famous player
* A donation to a charity

🙆 _Here is some stuff that is actually ok for customers or prospective customers:_

* Swag
* Economy class airfare to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️
* Cheap seats in the stands at a sporting event ⚾️
* A reasonably priced dinner in town 🍽
* A small box of chocolates as an end of year thank you 🍫

All of the above goes for _taking_ bribes too. So if a supplier or prospective supplier sends you something “of value,” don’t accept it—or at the very least run it by Legal before you do.

## Process

If you're planning to give or accept an informal gift (one that isn't our promotional gear like t-shirts and sticker swag), please make sure you follow the process below.

Keep in mind that what counts as an acceptable amount can vary. For example, $50 on dinner might be a fair bit of money in some countries but not so much in others. So, if you're not sure, ask!

1. Generally speaking, for any expenses over $150, you'll need approval (each time) from Legal in writing. And for any gifts you get that are over $150, you'll need to report them (each time) to Legal in writing. (Legal is only having a say on the bribery question. You might still need to check with Finance for other reasons.)

1. When you put in your expense report to get reimbursed, please:
     * Note down who got the gift and what it was for in the notes; and
     * Include a copy of the email approval

## Consequences

So what happens if you don’t follow the policy and GitHub ends up shouting a prospective customer and his family a first class trip to Japan to go to the Tokyo Olympics? Or if you drop off a suitcase of Euros for a government official in a pre-arranged train station locker?

FINES and JAIL. And not just for GitHub, either. You might cop a few years in jail yourself, and it won’t matter whether the customer ever put an order in with GitHub after their trip to Japan, or whether the government official even picked up the suitcase of cash. Basically, it doesn’t matter if you actually _made_ a bribe. Yoda notwithstanding, it’s the trying that counts.
