Friday's big winter storm across the northeastern United States took out a 227-year-old tree on Mount Vernon, the Virginia estate of the first US President George Washington. As of yesterday, the mob that runs the Mount Vernon estate said the Canadian hemlock had been gathered up to be preserved and used for special projects.

The hemlock, planted back in 1791, came down on Friday in gusts reportedly up to 70 mph (about 110 kph).

Mount Vernon was Washington's own place, and he ran most of his two terms as president from there between 1789–1797, cos the first White House wasn't finished yet. According to the Mount Vernon people, the hemlock is the best documented tree on the whole estate: New York Governor George Clinton sent it as a present, and it turned up in a whisky barrel. The New York governor's no relation to the 42nd President Bill Clinton, but George Clinton did go on to be the United States's Vice President under the Thomas Jefferson and James Madison administrations between 1805–1812.

It's not the only bit of US history to go over lately, either. The seventh President Andrew Jackson's southern magnolia at the White House has come down too, and so has the Pioneer Cabin giant sequoia in California's Calaveras Big Trees State Park, the one famous for the car-sized tunnel cut through its trunk.
