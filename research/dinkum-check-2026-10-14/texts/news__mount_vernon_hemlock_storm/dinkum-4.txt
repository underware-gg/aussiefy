Friday's big winter storm across the northeastern United States has knocked over a 227-year-old tree at Mount Vernon, the Virginia estate of the first US President George Washington. As of yesterday, the people running the Mount Vernon estate reckoned the Canadian hemlock had been gathered up to be preserved and put towards special projects.

The hemlock went in the ground in 1791 and came down in Friday's gusts, which were reportedly up to 70 mph (about 110 kph).

Mount Vernon was Washington's own place, and he ran most of his two terms as president from there between 1789–1797, cos the first White House wasn't finished yet. Mount Vernon officials say the hemlock is the best documented tree on the estate. It was sent as a pressie by New York Governor George Clinton, and it rocked up in a whisky barrel. The New York governor's no relation to the 42nd President Bill Clinton, but George Clinton did go on to be the United States's Vice President under Thomas Jefferson and James Madison between 1805–1812.

It's not the only bit of US history to come down lately, either. The seventh President Andrew Jackson's southern magnolia at the White House has gone, and so has the Pioneer Cabin giant sequoia in California's Calaveras Big Trees State Park, famous for the car-sized tunnel cut through its trunk.
