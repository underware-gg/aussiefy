On Saturday, the family of American muso and songwriter Bob Weir let everyone know he'd died with his loved ones around him. The old Grateful Dead guitarist and singer was 78. His family broke the news on Instagram, saying he'd lately been battling cancer but died of "underlying lung issues". They didn't say what day he died.

Weir played rhythm guitar and sang with American psychedelic rock band the Grateful Dead for yonks, and wrote a fair few of their songs too, including the hit "Sugar Magnolia", a fixture at every Grateful Dead concert. He had a long solo run as well, putting together all sorts of bands with his old Grateful Dead mates, and he was still playing live gigs till just months before he died.

Weir was born Robert Hall Parber on 16 October 1947, in San Francisco, California, and grew up round the San Francisco Bay Area, with a stint at school in Colorado, where he met future Grateful Dead member John Perry Barlow. He picked up the guitar at 13 and found his way into the local bluegrass scene, which is where he first came across Jerry Garcia playing the banjo. On 31 December 1965, Garcia and Weir decided to start their own outfit, Mother McCree's Uptown Jug Champions, and that band grew into the Grateful Dead.

Weir played rhythm guitar and sang for the group for decades. From the 1970s he also had a crack at a solo career and a whole swag of other bands outside the Grateful Dead, including Kingfish, Bobby and the Midnites, RatDog, Furthur, The Other Ones, The Dead, Wolf Bros, and Dead & Company. Every one of them had fellow Grateful Dead collaborators in it, and they ranged over everything from country rock to jam band to jazz-tinged roots rock built on blues and country. Mostly he sang harmonies and backing vocals, but he took the lead on a few tracks, including the Grateful Dead anthem "Truckin'", a song the Library of Congress named a national treasure in 1997.

Away from the stage, Weir also set up the Tamalpais Research Institute, an online platform for live shows, and he was into politics, getting behind a few causes, including the environment, helping people doing it tough, and the arts. He'd stayed a regular on the Bay Area music scene, playing live with local musos and co-owning the venue Sweetwater Music Hall in Mill Valley, California.
