Chrissy tree permits go on sale from November 13, 2025, online through Recreation.gov and in person at all Santa Fe National Forest District Offices and the Supervisor’s Office. You'll find the details on the designated cutting areas, the dates, and which types of trees you're allowed to cut here.

Getting your Chrissy tree permit:

Online through Recreation.gov: just search for Santa Fe National Forest Christmas Tree Permits. Have a good read of the overview and the need-to-know info before you buy the permit, cos it matters. You'll also need to set up or log in to a Recreation.gov account to get the purchase through.

In person at the Supervisor’s Office in Santa Fe, N.M., or the Jemez, Cuba, Coyote, Española, and Pecos/Las Vegas District Offices. Give us a ring for current hours.

At the Walatowa Visitor Center, permits will only be sold in person on Saturdays, from 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., from November 22 through December 20, 2024.

District offices may also have pre-cut Chrissy trees for sale. That depends on the weather and the supply, and people who can't cut their own tree get first dibs.

The SFNF’s Chrissy tree permits are $10, and there are no refunds. Permits bought online through recreation.gov carry an extra $2.50 service fee. One tag covers a tree up to 5 inches (about 13 cm) across and 10 feet (about 3 m) tall. A tree taller than 10 feet and/or wider than 5 inches needs another tag. So a 15-foot tree, for example, needs two tags.

Fourth graders with an Every Kid Outdoors pass can get a Chrissy tree permit for free: just pop in the pass or voucher number when you buy the permit (mind you, the $2.50 service fee still applies).

Cutting a Chrissy tree does the bush a good turn, too. The permit system helps thin out thick stands of skinny little trees. Local forest health experts pick the areas that do better for a thinning, and the trees there tend to be just the right size for a Chrissy tree. Clearing them out of the designated areas gives the other trees room to grow bigger and can open up patches where wildlife can find a feed.
