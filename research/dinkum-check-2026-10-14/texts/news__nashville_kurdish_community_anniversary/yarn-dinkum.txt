The Metropolitan Council of Nashville and Davidson County, down in the southeastern U.S. state of Tennessee, has officially marked the 50th anniversary of the first lot of Kurdish families turnin' up in the U.S., givin' 'em a big wrap for everythin' they've put into the city's culture, community and economy.

Goin' by an official proclamation VOA got its mitts on, the first mob of Kurdish families lobbed in the United States on May 1, 1976. Plenty of 'em ended up settlin' in Nashville, Tennessee, while others set up their own communities in cities and states right across the country.

The proclamation gives a shout-out to how hard the Kurdish community's worked to hang onto its language, culture 'n traditions over the past five decades, while pullin' its weight in Nashville's growth and development.

Accordin' to the document, Nashville's now home to more than 20,000 Kurds, which makes it one of the biggest Kurdish communities in the United States. The city's turned into a proper hub for Kurdish culture and community life.

The proclamation also gives a nod to the long-runnin' ties between the United States and Kurdistan, and to Kurdish Peshmerga forces, U.S. troops and their allies workin' together in the fight against terrorism.

Metropolitan Nashville councillor Mike Cortese, who looks after District 4, got the official recognition of the anniversary up, workin' alongside other councillors.

The proclamation was signed on October 6, 2026.
