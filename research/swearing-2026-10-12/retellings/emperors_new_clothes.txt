Years and bloody years ago there was this emperor, right, and the bloke was so fuckin' obsessed with new clobber he blew every last cent on it. All he gave a shit about was lookin' flash. Didn't give a rat's arse about his soldiers, the theatre bored him shitless, and the only bloody thing he cared about was cruisin' round showin' off a new suit. Had a different coat for every hour of the day. You know how you'd say of a king, "He's in a meeting"? Well, with this clown you'd say, "The emperor's in the fuckin' wardrobe."

The big city where he lived was always pumpin'. Every day there's heaps of blow-ins turnin' up from all over the joint. Then one day two shonky bastards rock up, reckon they're weavers, and they're carryin' on that they can make the best bloody cloth you ever laid eyes on. Colours and patterns, they reckon, not just bloody gorgeous, but the gear made out of it's got this magic trick where it's invisible to any bloke who's useless at his job or thick as two short planks.

"Fuck me, that'd be some cloth," thinks the emperor. "If I had a suit outta that stuff I'd suss out which bastards in me empire are useless at their jobs, and I'd be able to pick the smart ones from the dickheads. I gotta have this stuff made up for me, pronto." So he flicks the two con men a big wad of cash up front so they'll get cracking straight away. They set up two looms and make out they're flat out like a lizard drinkin', but they're doin' fuck all on the looms. They ask for the best silk and the flashest gold cloth, pocket the whole bloody lot, and keep fartarsin' about at the empty looms till all hours.

"I'd bloody love to know how they're goin' with that cloth," thinks the emperor. But he's gettin' a bit toey when he remembers that any bloke who's no good at his job can't see it. Personally, he reckons he's got sweet fuck all to worry about, but he figures he'd better send some other poor bugger first to see how it's all goin'. Every bastard in town knew about this magic bloody cloth, and they were all hangin' out to see how useless or thick their neighbours were.

"I'll send me honest old minister down to the weavers," thinks the emperor. "He'll know best what the stuff looks like, 'cause he's a smart bugger and no cunt knows that job better than him."

So the good old minister wanders into the room where the two shonks are sittin' at their empty looms. "Holy shit," he thinks, eyes poppin' outta his head, "I can't see a bloody thing," but he keeps his trap shut. The two con men call him over and ask him isn't the pattern a ripper and aren't the colours gorgeous, pointin' at the empty looms. The poor old bugger tries his bloody hardest but he can't see a thing, 'cause there's fuck all there to see. "Oh, shit," he thinks, "am I that thick? Never would've picked it, and no bastard can ever find out! Am I bloody useless at me job? No, no, no fuckin' way am I sayin' I couldn't see the cloth."

"Well, cat got your tongue, mate?" says one of the shonks, makin' out he's flat out weavin'.

"Oh, it's bloody lovely, absolutely fuckin' gorgeous," says the old minister, peerin' through his glasses. "What a ripper of a pattern, what bloody brilliant colours! I'll tell the emperor I reckon it's the duck's nuts."

"Good to hear, mate," say the two weavers, and they run him through the colours and explain the weird bloody pattern. The old minister listens real careful so he can tell the emperor everything they said, and that's exactly what he does.

Then the two shonks put their hands out for more cash, more silk and more gold cloth, which they reckon they need for the weavin'. They pocket the bloody lot, not one thread gets anywhere near the loom, and they keep on fartarsin' about at the empty looms same as before.

Not long after, the emperor sends another honest bloke from the court down to the weavers to see how they're goin' and if the cloth's nearly done. Same as the old minister, he looks and looks but he can't see a bloody thing, 'cause there's fuck all there to see.

"Isn't it a cracker of a bit of cloth?" ask the two con men, showin' him and explainin' this magnificent bloody pattern that doesn't even exist.

"I'm not thick," says the bloke to himself. "So it must be me cushy job I'm not fit for. Bloody weird, but no bastard can ever find out," and he bangs on about how good the cloth is, which he can't even see, and carries on about the gorgeous colours and the fine pattern. "It's bloody excellent," he tells the emperor.

Every bastard in town's yakkin' about this precious bloody cloth. In the end the emperor wants to have a squiz himself while it's still on the loom. So with a mob of his courtiers, includin' the two who'd already been, he heads down to the two cunning bastards, who are now goin' hell for leather, only without usin' a single bloody thread.

"Isn't it magnificent?" say the two old statesmen who'd been there before. "Your Majesty's gotta check out them colours and that pattern." And they point at the empty looms, 'cause they reckon all the others can see the cloth.

"What the fuck?" thinks the emperor. "I can't see a bloody thing. This is fuckin' terrible! Am I thick? Am I not fit to be emperor? That'd be the most shithouse thing that could ever happen to me."

"Yeah, nah, really," he says, turnin' to the weavers, "your cloth gets the royal tick of approval." And he's noddin' away happy as Larry, starin' at the empty loom, 'cause he's buggered if he's gonna admit he can't see a thing. All his hangers-on with him look and look, and even though they can't see any more than the others, they all go, same as the emperor, "It's bloody beautiful." And they all reckon he should wear the flash new gear in a big parade that's comin' up soon. "Magnificent, bloody beautiful, excellent," you'd hear 'em sayin'. Everyone seemed stoked, and the emperor made the two shonks "Imperial Court Weavers," no less.

The whole night before the parade, the two shonks made out they were workin', and burnt through more than sixteen bloody candles, so everyone could see they were flat out finishin' the emperor's new suit. They made out they were takin' the cloth off the loom, waved big scissors round in thin air, sewed with needles with no fuckin' thread in 'em, and finally said, "The emperor's new suit's ready, mate."

So the emperor and all his barons come into the hall, and the two shonks hold their arms up like they're holdin' somethin' and go, "Here's the strides!" "Here's the coat!" and "Here's the cloak!" and on and on. "They're all as light as a bloody cobweb, and you'll feel like you've got fuck all on, but that's the beauty of 'em, eh."

"Too right!" say all the courtiers, but they can't see a bloody thing, 'cause there's fuck all there to see.

"Would Your Majesty kindly get his gear off," say the shonks, "so we can help Your Majesty into the new suit in front of the big mirror?"

So the emperor strips off, and the shonks make out they're puttin' the new suit on him, piece by piece, and the emperor's checkin' himself out in the mirror from every angle.

"Don't they look bloody good! Don't they fit like a glove!" they all go. "What a ripper pattern! What bloody beautiful colours! That's one fuckin' magnificent suit!"

The master of ceremonies sings out that the blokes carryin' the canopy in the parade are ready to go.

"I'm ready," says the emperor. "Doesn't me suit fit like a bloody dream?" And he turns round to the mirror one more time so people'll think he's admirin' his gear.

The chamberlains, who were supposed to carry the train, reach down to the ground like they're liftin' up a train, and make out they're holdin' somethin'. No bloody way were they lettin' anyone know they couldn't see a thing.

So the emperor marches off in the parade under this beautiful bloody canopy, and everyone in the street and hangin' out the windows is goin', "Strewth, the emperor's new suit's bloody unreal! Look at the length of that train! Fits him like a fuckin' glove!" No bastard wanted to let on he couldn't see anything, 'cause then he'd be useless at his job or thick as shit. Never was an emperor's gear so bloody admired.

"But he's got fuck all on!" says this little kid in the end. "Jesus Christ, listen to the little bugger, out of the mouths of babes," says the kid's old man, and the whisper went round from one bloke to the next what the kid had said. "He's got fuck all on!" the whole bloody crowd yells in the end. That rattled the emperor somethin' shocking, 'cause he reckoned they were bloody right, but he thinks to himself, "Fuck it, I've gotta see this through to the end." And the chamberlains strutted along all the more up themselves, like they were carryin' a train that didn't even bloody exist.
