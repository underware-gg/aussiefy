MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – From Friday, Sept. 4, the last of the fishing restrictions on the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon (downstream of Norris Campground) rivers and all the creeks that feed 'em are comin' off, now the weather and the water have cooled down. Anglers'll be able to wet a line on these rivers, as well as most of the park's other rivers, creeks and lakes, every day from sunrise to sunset, as set out in the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet.

The rivers were shut to fishin' altogether from July 15, then that got wound back to part-day restrictions on Aug. 28 once things picked up. The restrictions and closures had to happen while the rivers were runnin' low and the water was hotter than 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius), which can kill fish.

Park staff'll keep an eye on the water temperatures. Temporary restrictions or closures could be brought back in, dependin' on how conditions look.

Update: Yellowstone lifts full closures for Firehole, Madison and Gibbon rivers

Part-day restrictions still on from 2 p.m. till sunrise the next day

MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – From Friday, Aug. 28, anglers can fish the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon (downstream of Norris Campground) rivers and all the creeks that feed 'em from sunrise until 2 p.m. every day. Coolin' weather and water temperatures, plus rain in the current forecast, mean the full closures can come off.

The full closures went in on July 15 cos low flows and water hotter than 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius) can kill fish. The water's been coolin' off overnight, so there's fishin' for part of the day. Anglers can fish most of the park's other rivers, creeks and lakes every day from sunrise to sunset, as set out in the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet.

Park staff'll keep an eye on the water temperatures. Temporary restrictions could be lifted, or more closures put in, dependin' on how conditions look.

Anglers: Please fish in the coolest part of the day and land fish quick smart. Don't play hooked trout till they're knackered. Handle fish gently and keep 'em in the water as much as ya can, and let 'em get their breath back before ya let 'em go. Pitchin' in like this'll look after the park's fisheries and might save the need to ban fishin' all day on some rivers and creeks if conditions get worse.
