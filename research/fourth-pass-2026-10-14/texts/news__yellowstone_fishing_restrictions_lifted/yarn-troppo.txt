MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – Good news for the fishin' crowd, and it's a ripper. From Friday, Sept. 4, the last of the fishing restrictions on the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon (downstream of Norris Campground) rivers, and every creek that runs into 'em, are gettin' the flick, now the weather's turned and the water's finally cooled off. Anglers can wet a line on these rivers again, plus most of the park's other rivers, creeks and lakes, every day from sunrise to sunset, as per the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet. Dust off the rods.

It's been a long, hot slog. The rivers were shut to fishin' full stop from July 15, then that got eased back to part-day restrictions on Aug. 28 once things started lookin' up. The rangers didn't do it for fun, mind: the rivers were runnin' low and the water was gettin' hotter than 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius), and that can kill fish.

Park staff'll keep a close eye on the water temperatures, though, so don't get too comfy. Temporary restrictions or closures could come back in, dependin' on how conditions shape up.

Update: Yellowstone lifts full closures for Firehole, Madison and Gibbon rivers

Part-day restrictions still on from 2 p.m. till sunrise the next day

MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – Here's one for ya. From Friday, Aug. 28, anglers can get back on the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon (downstream of Norris Campground) rivers, and every creek that runs into 'em, from sunrise until 2 p.m. each day. The weather and the water are coolin' down, and there's rain in the forecast, so the full closures can come off.

The full closures went on back on July 15, cos low flows and water hotter than 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius) can kill fish, simple as that. The water's been coolin' off overnight, though, so there's a window for fishin' for part of the day. And on most of the park's other rivers, creeks and lakes, anglers can fish every day from sunrise to sunset, as per the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet.

Park staff'll keep watchin' the water temperatures like hawks. Temporary restrictions might get lifted, or more closures might go in, dependin' on how conditions look.

And a word to the anglers from the park, so listen up: please fish in the coolest part of the day and get yer fish in quick smart. Don't play a hooked trout till the poor bugger's knackered. Handle fish gently, keep 'em in the water as much as ya can, and give 'em time to come good before ya let 'em go. Do the right thing and you'll be lookin' after the park's fisheries, and ya might just save 'em havin' to ban fishin' all day on some rivers and creeks if things get worse.
