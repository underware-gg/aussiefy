No word of a lie, there's nowhere safe outside when there's thunderstorms about. Nowhere. If ya can hear thunder, you're prob'ly close enough for that storm to reach out and get ya. So just remember, “When Thunder Roars, Go Indoors!” Too many people leave it far too bloody late to get somewhere safe when a storm's comin' in. And sadly, that dawdlin' is behind heaps of the lightnin' deaths and injuries in the U.S.

Best way to keep yerself safe from lightnin'? Don't give it a chance at ya. Ya just don't wanna be caught out in the open in a storm, full stop. Have a lightnin' safety plan, and if storms are forecast, call off or put off whatever you've got on, and do it early. Keep an eye on the sky and get somewhere safe before it turns nasty. A proper solid buildin' or a car with a hard roof, they're yer safe bets. Rain shelters, little sheds and open-top vehicles? Not safe. Don't kid yerself.

Once you're inside, hands off anythin' plugged into a power point, the plumbin', and phones with a cord. Yer mobile and the cordless are fine. And stay away from the outside doors and windows, and don't go lyin' on the garage floor.

Cuttin' the Risk

To cut yer own risk of gettin' struck, when you're headin' out, plan ahead so ya can get somewhere safe quick smart if a storm blows up. If the sky's lookin' ugly or ya hear thunder, get inside somewhere safe, straight away, no muckin' about. Once you're in, keep off phones with a cord, electrical gear, the plumbin', and the windows and doors. And last of all, wait 30 minutes after the last lightnin' or thunder before ya head back out. Thirty minutes, the full half hour. If every bloke and sheila stuck to those simple rules, the number of people killed or hurt by lightnin' in this country could come right down.

So remember, it's what you do when a storm's around that decides yer own risk of gettin' struck by lightnin'. When Thunder Roars, Go Indoors!

If Someone Gets Struck

If someone's been struck by lightnin', they might need medical help straight away. Folks who've been struck don't carry an electrical charge, so they're safe to touch. Don't hang back. Call 911 and keep a close eye on 'em. Start CPR or use an Automated External Defibrillator if needed.
