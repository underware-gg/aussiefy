Bushwalking in the Grand Canyon is a tough one, what with the air temperature, the elevation and how hard the terrain is on your body, all at once. If you want a hike that's safe and a good time, it pays to keep on top of your water and your tucker.

Stay Watered and Fuelled Up

While you're walking, especially uphill and in full sun, don't let yourself dry out. The Grand Canyon's hot and dry, so your sweat dries off quick smart and you mightn't realise how much fluid you're losing. Keep an eye out for salt stains or rings on your clothes, a common sign you're getting dehydrated.

To stay watered, sip water regularly the whole way, even when you don't feel thirsty. It's just as important to top up the electrolytes you've lost by eating salty snacks, which help your body hang on to fluids and keep things in balance. Wondering what the inner canyon rangers chuck in their day packs? Some of their favourite salty snacks are pretzels, Pringles, chips and cheesy bikkies: tasty tucker that's ready for the track!

Fuel Up for the Walk
Hiking in the Grand Canyon takes it out of you, so make sure you eat enough to keep yourself going. Regular snacks with a mix of carbs, protein and electrolytes will keep you powering along. Drink and eat even if you're not thirsty or peckish, cos your body won't always tell you when it's time to refuel. Getting the balance right between water and tucker is how you avoid getting knackered and have your body running at its best.

If you stay on top of your water, your food and your rest, you'll have a better time of it and get more out of the tracks.

Even if you're eating and drinking right, you still need to stay out of the direct sun in the hottest part of the day. Sun temperatures are 15F to 20F (9C-11C) degrees hotter than the posted shade temperatures. And keep in mind, the further down into the canyon you go, the hotter it gets!

Plan your day so you're not walking between 10am and 4pm. Take a break near shade and water to dodge the worst of the heat. Get going before dawn and knock off late in the arvo. Experienced desert bushwalkers know that when you walk is the biggest thing in steering clear of trouble. Most of the people who need emergency medical help in the canyon for heat illness are walking between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m.

KEEP YOURSELF DRIPPING WET TO STAY COOL.

One of the best ways to hold your body temperature in check while you're walking in the Grand Canyon is to stay soaking wet. The fierce heat, especially in the inner canyon, can push your core body temperature up fast, so grab every chance to cool down. Whenever you're near water, whether it's a creek or a water station, take the time to soak your clothes and yourself.

Wear clothes that hold water well (cotton is ripper in the desert!), and if you can, wear a hat or bandana you can soak often. It's a simple trick, but it works, and it keeps you cool and full of beans all day. When you're walking in the canyon, where the temperatures can go through the roof, take every chance to get wet, cos it'll make your hike a whole lot more comfortable. By day's end you'll feel the difference, with your body cooler and fresher.

Remember, staying soaking wet isn't just about feeling better. It's a crucial way to hang on to your energy and avoid heat exhaustion, so don't skip this simple but vital way of cooling down!
