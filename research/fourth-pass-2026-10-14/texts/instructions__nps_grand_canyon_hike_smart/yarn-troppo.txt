Now listen, cos this one matters. Bushwalking in the Grand Canyon throws a fair bit at ya all at once: the air temperature, the elevation, and terrain that'll give your legs a proper workout. If you want to come back up having had a good time and in one piece, you've gotta stay on top of your water and your tucker.

Keep the Tank Full

Once you're walking, especially uphill and out in the full sun, keeping your fluids up is non-negotiable. The Grand Canyon's hot and dry as a dead dingo's donger, so your sweat's gone off your skin before you even notice it, and you mightn't realise how much fluid you're losing. Keep an eye out for salt stains or white rings on your clothes. That's a common sign you're getting dehydrated, and it's your body waving a little flag at ya.

So sip water regularly the whole way along, even when you don't feel thirsty. And just as important, top up the electrolytes you've sweated out by eating salty snacks, which help your body hang on to its fluids and keep everything in balance. Ever wondered what the inner canyon rangers chuck in their day packs? Some of their favourite salty snacks are pretzels, Pringles, chips and cheesy bikkies. Tasty, and ready for the track: proper fuel for the trip!

Feed the Engine
Hiking in the Grand Canyon takes it out of ya, so make sure you're eating enough to keep the engine running. Regular snacks with a mix of carbs, protein and electrolytes will keep your energy levels up. Drink and eat even if you're not thirsty or peckish, because your body won't always put its hand up and tell ya it's time to refuel. Get the balance between your water and your tucker right and you'll dodge being knackered and keep your body firing on all cylinders.

Stay on top of the water, the food and the rest, mate, and you'll have a better time of it out on the tracks, and get a whole lot more out of them.

Now here's the catch. Even if you're eating and drinking like a champ, you still need to keep out of the direct sun in the hottest part of the day. Sun temperatures are 15F to 20F (9C-11C) degrees hotter than the posted shade temperatures. And keep this in your noggin: the further down into the canyon you go, the hotter it gets!

So plan your day so you're not walking between 10am and 4pm, full stop. Take a break near shade and water and sit out the worst heat of the day. Get yourself a predawn start and a late arvo finish instead. Experienced desert bushwalkers know that when you walk is the single biggest thing in staying out of strife. Most of the people who need emergency medical help in the canyon for heat illness are walking between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. Don't be one of 'em.

KEEP YOURSELF SOAKED TO STAY COOL.

One of the best ways to keep your body temperature in check while you're walking in the Grand Canyon is to stay soaking wet. The heat down there, especially in the inner canyon, is fierce, and it can push your core body temperature up in a hurry, so grab every chance you get to cool down. Whenever you're near water, whether it's a creek or a water station, stop and soak your clothes and yourself.

Wear clothes that hold the water (cotton is ripper in the desert, believe it or not!), and if you can, wear a hat or bandana you can dunk often. Easy as, and it works a treat for staying cool and full of beans all day. Out in the canyon, where the temperatures can go through the roof, take every chance to get wet, because it'll make your hike a heap more comfortable. By day's end you'll feel the difference, with your body cooler and fresher for it.

And remember, staying soaking wet isn't just about feeling better. It's a crucial bit of kit for hanging on to your energy and avoiding heat exhaustion, so don't go skipping this simple but vital way of cooling down!
