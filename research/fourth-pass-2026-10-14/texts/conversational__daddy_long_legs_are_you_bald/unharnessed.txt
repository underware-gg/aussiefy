December 19th.

_Dear Daddy-Long-Legs_,

You never answered my question, and it was a really important one.

ARE YOU BALD?

I've got it all worked out exactly what you look like--and pretty well, too--right up until I get to the top of your head, and then I'm _properly_ stuck. I can't make up my mind whether you've got white hair or black hair or a sort of sprinkly grey sort of hair, or maybe none at all.

Here's your portrait:

But the trouble is, should I add some hair?

Would you like to know what colour your eyes are? They're grey, and your eyebrows stick out like a verandah roof (beetling, they call it in novels), and your mouth is a straight line that tends to turn down at the corners. Oh, you see, I know! You're a crabby old thing with a temper on you.

(Chapel bell.)

9.45 P. M.

I've got a new rule that can't be broken: never, ever study at night, no matter how many written reviews are coming up in the morning. Instead I read just plain books--I have to, you know, because there are eighteen blank years behind me. You wouldn't believe it, Daddy, what a great hole of ignorance my mind is; I'm only just working out how deep it goes myself. The things most girls with a proper assortment of family and a home and friends and a library pick up without trying, I've never even heard of. For example:

I never read "Mother Goose" or "David Copperfield" or "Ivanhoe" or "Cinderella" or "Blue Beard" or "Robinson Crusoe" or "Jane Eyre" or "Alice in Wonderland" or a single word of Rudyard Kipling. I didn't know Henry the Eighth was married more than once, or that Shelley was a poet. I didn't know people used to be monkeys and that the Garden of Eden was a beautiful myth. I didn't know R.L.S. stood for Robert Louis Stevenson or that George Eliot was a lady. I'd never seen a picture of the "Mona Lisa" and (it's true, but you won't believe it) I'd never heard of Sherlock Holmes.

Now I know all of those things and plenty of others besides, but you can see how much catching up I've got to do. And oh, it's fun! I look forward all day to the evening, and then I put an "engaged" sign on the door and hop into my nice red dressing-gown and furry slippers and pile all the cushions behind me on the couch and light the brass student lamp at my elbow, and read and read and read. One book isn't enough. I've got four on the go at once. Right now they're Tennyson's poems and "Vanity Fair" and Kipling's "Plain Tales" and--don't laugh--"Little Women." Turns out I'm the only girl in college who wasn't brought up on "Little Women." I haven't told anybody, mind (that _would_ mark me as a bit odd). I just quietly went and bought it with $1.12 of last month's allowance, and next time somebody mentions pickled limes, I'll know what she's on about!

(Ten o'clock bell. This is a very interrupted letter.)

Saturday.

_Sir_,

I have the honour to report fresh explorations in the field of geometry. On Friday last we gave up our former work on parallelopipeds and moved on to truncated prisms. We're finding the road rough going and very uphill.

Sunday.

The Christmas holidays start next week and the trunks are up. The corridors are so chock-a-block you can hardly get through, and everybody's so bubbling over with excitement that the study's getting left by the wayside. I'm going to have a lovely time over the holidays; there's another Freshman from Texas staying back too, and we're planning to go on long walks and--if there's any ice--learn to skate. Then there's still the whole library to get through--and three empty weeks to do it in!

Hooroo, Daddy, I hope you're feeling as happy as I am.

Yours ever,

JUDY.

P. S. Don't forget to answer my question. If you can't be bothered writing, have your secretary send a telegram. He can just say:

Mr. Smith is quite bald, or Mr. Smith is not bald, or Mr. Smith has white hair.

And you can take the twenty-five cents out of my allowance.

Bye till January--and a merry Christmas!
