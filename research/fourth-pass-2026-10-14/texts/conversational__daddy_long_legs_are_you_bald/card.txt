yarn-dinkum: Light, natural and faithful; exactly the weight of a level-2 brief.
  minor: 'my beaut red bath robe' (for 'my nice red bath robe'): 'Beaut' as attributive for a bathrobe is a touch forced, and it is repeated later in 'a beaut time'.

dinkum-3: Natural, varied and on-brief, with a few small flattenings.
  minor: 'The stuff most girls with a proper family and a home and mates and a library just soak up' (for 'most girls with a properly assorted family and a home and friends and a library know by absorption'): Loses the comic 'properly assorted' that marks the orphan's view.
  minor: "everyone's so excited that nobody's doing any study" (for 'everybody is so bubbling over with excitement that studying is getting left out'): Overstates and flattens the original.
  minor: "I've got you all worked out--down to a tee--right up to the top of your head" (for 'I have it planned exactly what you look like--very satisfactorily--until I reach the top of your head'): Drops 'very satisfactorily'.

unharnessed: Natural and faithful, slightly light on Aussie, with a couple of clumsy joins.
  minor: 'a sort of sprinkly grey sort of hair' (for 'sort of sprinkly gray hair'): Repeated 'sort of' clunks.
  minor: "I've got it all worked out exactly what you look like" (for 'I have it planned exactly what you look like'): Awkward join of two constructions.

yarn-troppo: Lively and full of meaning, but overdone for a level-2 brief, with stock phrases bolted on.
  major: 'Fair dinkum.' (added): Stock Aussie tag bolted onto the end of a paragraph; nothing in the original calls for it.
  minor: 'the whole lot of them empty as a pub at sunrise' (for 'because there are eighteen blank years behind me'): Forced simile, and a pub image is out of character for a college girl at level 2.
  minor: '(more than once! the old goat)' (for 'I didn’t know that Henry the Eighth was married more than once'): Added aside; colour, but heavier than the brief.
  minor: 'a sort of sprinkly grey, salt-and-pepper job' (for 'sort of sprinkly gray hair'): Stacked synonyms clog the line.
  minor: 'read and read and read till the cows come home' (for 'read and read and read'): Stock idiom bolted on to a repetition that already did the work.

engine: Faithful because barely touched; a few swaps, one of them ungrammatical.
  major: 'I’m going to have a beautiful time in holiday' (for 'I’m going to have a beautiful time in vacation'): Mechanical swap leaves broken grammar; an Australian says 'over the holidays'.
  minor: 'Good-by till January--and a merry Chrissie!' (for 'Good-by till January--and a merry Christmas!'): Archaic US 'Good-by' left beside a slang 'Chrissie'; the mix jars.
  major: 'You never answered my question and it was very important.' (for 'You never answered my question and it was very important.'): The text is largely left untranslated, well under the level-2 brief.
