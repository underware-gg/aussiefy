Southern Cornbread is a hearty, unsweetened kind of cornbread, served all over the Southeastern United States. It's dead easy to make and usually goes on the table as the bread with lunch or tea (supper). It's not unusual for some folks to have a quick, simple feed every now and then, even brekkie, by crumbling cornbread into a glass of buttermilk and tucking in.

Ingredients

2 cups yellow or white self-raising cornmeal

1 large egg

1¼–1½ cups buttermilk or full-cream milk

¼ cup olive oil or vegetable oil

Gear

Well-seasoned 8-inch (20 cm) or 10-inch (25 cm) cast-iron frying pan (or else: an ovenproof frying pan or an 8x8-inch (20x20 cm) baking tin)

Assorted measuring cups

Medium or large mixing bowl

Method

Preheat the oven to 425°F (220°C). If you're using a cast-iron frying pan, whack it in the oven to heat up. Don't preheat any other kind of frying pan or baking tin.

If you're using a frying pan that isn't cast iron, or a baking tin, give it a coat of non-stick spray.

Beat the egg in the bowl. Stir in the buttermilk and oil.

Stir in the cornmeal and mix until it's just wet through. The batter will be a bit lumpy. Go easy and don't overmix it.

Pour the batter into the pan and pop it in the oven.

Bake until the crust is a light golden brown and a toothpick stuck into the centre comes out clean. For an 8-inch frying pan that can be 25–30 minutes; for a 10-inch frying pan or an 8x8-inch baking tin, about 20–25 minutes.

Let it cool for 5 minutes in the pan before you serve it up.

Notes, tips and variations

Any leftover cornbread can be wrapped in a damp tea towel (to keep it moist) and kept in the fridge for several days. You can freeze it in freezer bags for several months, but it'll usually go more crumbly if you store it that way.
