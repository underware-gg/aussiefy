Righto, Southern Cornbread. This is a hearty one, and unsweetened, mind, none of your cakey stuff. It's served all over the Southeastern United States, and it's dead easy to make. Over there it usually turns up as the bread with lunch or tea (supper, they call it). And here's the bit that gets ya: it's not unusual for some folks, every now and then, to make a quick, simple feed of it, even brekkie, by crumbling the cornbread into a glass of buttermilk and getting stuck into it. Fair dinkum.

Ingredients

2 cups yellow or white self-raising cornmeal

1 large egg

1¼–1½ cups buttermilk or full-cream milk

¼ cup olive oil or vegetable oil

Gear

Well-seasoned 8-inch (20 cm) or 10-inch (25 cm) cast-iron frying pan (or, if you haven't got one: an ovenproof frying pan or an 8x8-inch (20x20 cm) baking tin)

Assorted measuring cups

Medium or large mixing bowl

Method

Crank the oven up to 425°F (220°C). If you're using a cast-iron frying pan, whack it in the oven to heat up with it. Don't preheat any other kind of frying pan or baking tin, just the cast iron.

If you're using a frying pan that isn't cast iron, or a baking tin, give it a good coat of non-stick spray.

Beat the egg in the bowl. Stir in the buttermilk and the oil.

Stir in the cornmeal and mix till it's just wet through, no more. The batter's meant to be a bit lumpy, so don't stress. Go easy on it and don't overmix.

Pour the batter into the pan and pop it in the oven.

Bake till the crust's a light golden brown and a toothpick stuck into the centre comes out clean. In an 8-inch frying pan that can take 25–30 minutes; in a 10-inch frying pan or an 8x8-inch baking tin, about 20–25 minutes.

Then leave it to cool for 5 minutes in the pan before you dish it up. Hard, I know.

Notes, tips and variations

Got leftovers? Any leftover cornbread can be wrapped in a damp tea towel (keeps it moist) and kept in the fridge for several days. You can freeze it in freezer bags for several months too, but fair warning, it'll usually go more crumbly if you store it that way.
