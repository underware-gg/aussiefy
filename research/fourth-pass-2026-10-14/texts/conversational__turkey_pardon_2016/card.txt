yarn-troppo: The fullest and most natural yarn, puns intact, with a few small liberties.
  minor: "went down swingin'" (for 'who met their fate with courage and sacrifice'): Implies the turkeys fought back, a small invented detail.
  minor: "More kids are finishin' high school than ever before" (for 'Our high school graduation rate is at an all-time high'): A rate becomes a count, a slight change of claim.
  minor: "keep havin' a go at discrimination" (for 'continually fight discrimination'): "have a go at" usually means to criticise or pick on; strains for "fight".
  minor: "millions of little buggers among 'em" (for 'including millions of children'): A little jarring in the health-insurance boast.

dinkum-3: Faithful, natural and smooth, with tone and puns intact; slightly restrained for level 5.
  minor: 'the crook and the oldies' (for 'the sick and the elderly'): "oldies" is a shade flippant in the passage on caring for the vulnerable.
  minor: "There's heaps more we've got in common" (for 'We have a lot more in common'): Third "heaps"; noticeable repetition.

yarn-dinkum: A faithful, natural retelling with the puns intact; a little light for level 5 and with a couple of slanted word choices.
  minor: "buildin' a mixed-up, diverse mob of a society" (for 'building a diverse society'): "mixed-up" suggests confusion, a slant the original's praise of diversity does not have.
  minor: "millions of little buggers among 'em" (for 'including millions of children'): Affectionate but jars in the health-insurance boast; "kids" would carry it without the swear.
  minor: "Thanksgivin's a chance" (for 'Thanksgiving is a chance'): Dropped g on every -ing, even proper nouns, reads as a written accent rather than speech.

unharnessed: Accurate and natural, with tone intact, but noticeably lighter than the level-5 brief.
  minor: 'after a long arse of a campaign season' (for 'after a long campaign season'): Awkward construction; "long-arse" as a modifier exists but "a long arse of a" does not run naturally.
  minor: 'I reckon that to really live up to those ideals' (for 'I believe that in order to truly live up to those ideals'): Second "I reckon" in a row; the repetition is noticeable.
  minor: "Our high school graduation rate's at an all-time high." (for 'Our high school graduation rate is at an all-time high.'): Representative of the stretches left close to the original, light for a level-5 brief.

engine: Mostly the original with interjections and swears pasted on; breaks the key puns and swears through the solemn passages.
  critical: "who weren't lucky as fuck" (for "who weren't so lucky"): Intensifier turns "not so lucky" (killed) into a vulgar non-sequitur that reads as the opposite.
  critical: "proved that they weren't chook" (for "proved that they weren't chicken"): "Chicken" means coward; "chook" is just a hen, so the pun is lost.
  major: 'Yep, We Cran' (for 'Yes, We Cran'): Breaks the pun on the "Yes We Can" slogan, the punchline of the speech.
  major: 'Oh, shit, and there are many families of servicemembers who had empty chairs at the table' (for 'And there are many families of servicemembers who had empty chairs at the table'): Swear at the most solemn line about absent troops.
  major: "every bastard who's tryin' to get a second chance" (for "everybody who's trying to get a second chance"): Insulting word for the vulnerable in a passage about compassion.
  major: 'no matter what some drongo reckons' (added): Invented jibe at an unnamed opponent; not in the original.
  major: 'Jeez Louise, Malia and Sasha' (for 'Malia and Sasha'): Stock interjection bolted on; representative of "Crikey,", "Struth,", "Bugger me," openers throughout.
  minor: 'Now, come one.' (for 'Now, come one.'): Typo carried over unchanged.
