Ingredients

2 sticks (½ lb / 225 g / 1 cup) butter, softened

¾ cup (180 ml) white sugar (granulated)

¾ cup (180 ml) brown sugar, packed down firm

¼ teaspoon vanilla extract

2 standard (100 g) eggs

2 ¼ cups (600 ml / 290 g) plain flour

1 teaspoon bicarb soda

1 teaspoon salt

1 teaspoon dried ground lemon or orange peel (optional)

2 cups (480 ml / 12 oz) semi-sweet choc bits / chips

5 oz (150 g) chopped nuts, groundnuts or whatever (optional)

Procedure

First up, get the oven going at 375°F (210°C), or 350°F (195°C) if you're after chewy bickies.

Cream the butter, the white sugar and the brown sugar together in a big mixing bowl.

Crack the eggs in one at a time, and beat each one till it's disappeared right into the mix.

Tip in the vanilla extract and beat that in too.

In a small bowl, sift all the dry stuff together (leave the choccy and the nuts out of it for now). Add that to the wet mix in two goes and fold it through till it's all come together. Don't go mad here: beat it too long and the gluten starts getting going, and that changes the texture of your biscuit.

Now stir in the choc bits (chips), and the nuts if you're using 'em.

Drop the dough in heaped tablespoons onto ungreased baking trays. There should be enough fat in the dough that nothing sticks, but if yours does, a bit of baking paper or a non-stick tray will sort you out.

Bake in the hot oven for 10–12 minutes or till they're golden brown (every oven's different, so the time can vary). Let 'em sit for about 1 minute, then get them off that hot tray and onto a wire rack to cool.

Notes, tips and variations

Ingredients

Go cake flour instead of plain, swap the white sugar for brown, and use butter instead of marg. With cake flour and brown sugar you can use butter and not have to worry about your bickies turning into puddles (and puddles are the only reason anyone uses marg in the first place).

Use some vegetable shortening in place of the butter/marg to keep the "spread" down (you'll get thicker biscuits).

Swap some vegetable oil (no more than 1/5 of the total fat) in for the butter/marg if you want more spread and thinner bickies.

Chocolate with more than 65% cocoa gives you a fair bit different taste from the "traditional" recipe. Rule of thumb: if you have a nibble of the chocolate on its own and it's too bitter, or it's just not much chop, you mightn't like it in the biscuits either, no matter how flash it is.

A few shakes of ground cinnamon in with the dry stuff gives it more flavour as well.

If you want to use ordinary flour and butter, the trick is to bump the flour up to 3+ cups. That should give you a fairly stiff dough that won't spread out heaps. Works with wholemeal flour too, believe it or not.

You can muck about with how much white sugar you use against brown, to suit your taste. More brown sugar, chewier bickies. More white sugar, crisper ones.

A bit more vanilla extract (between ½ and ¾ of a teaspoon, instead of ¼) gives the biscuit a stronger flavour.

Can't get hold of vanilla extract? Swap two teaspoons of vanilla sugar in for white sugar instead.

Chuck in a little oatmeal to give the bickies more flavour and a bit more to chew on.

Method

Some people reckon you should chill the dough before baking, though you usually don't need to bother. Chilling it can help keep the spread down, so you end up with thicker biscuits.

Use an ungreased, non-stick baking tray instead of a greased one and they'll spread less, for thicker bickies. Going the other way, grease your tray if you like 'em thinner (crispier).

The better your flavouring ingredients, the better they turn out, and it makes a big difference. Top-quality butter, choccy and vanilla make a top-shelf biscuit.

Chopped-up block chocolate instead of ready-made chips gives you a more rustic texture -- just make sure the bits are small enough to eat without a struggle.

Don't stack the hot bickies, or they'll bend out of shape and take longer to cool. Use a cooling rack instead for the best results.

For a stronger nutty flavour, toast the nuts. Spread them out evenly on a baking tray and bung them in the 350°F oven for ten minutes. Take them out and let them cool down before they go in the dough.

And if you like a soft, squishy middle in your bickie, pull them out one minute earlier and you'll have a beaut soft centre.
