Ingredients

2 sticks (½ lb / 225 g / 1 cup) butter, softened

¾ cup (180 ml) white sugar (granulated)

¾ cup (180 ml) brown sugar, packed down

¼ teaspoon vanilla extract

2 standard (100 g) eggs

2 ¼ cups (600 ml / 290 g) plain flour

1 teaspoon bicarb soda

1 teaspoon salt

1 teaspoon dried ground lemon or orange peel (optional)

2 cups (480 ml / 12 oz) semi-sweet choc bits / chips

5 oz (150 g) chopped nuts, like peanuts (groundnuts) (optional)

Procedure

Preheat the oven to 375°F (210°C), or 350°F (195°C) if you're after chewy bickies.

Cream the butter, white sugar and brown sugar in a big mixing bowl.

Add the eggs one at a time, beating till each one's mixed right through.

Add the vanilla extract and beat it in.

In a small bowl, sift the dry ingredients together (not the choccy or the nuts). Add them to the wet mix in two lots and fold till it's all combined. Beat it too long and you can get some gluten forming, which changes the texture of the biscuit.

Stir in the choc bits (chips), and the nuts if you're using them.

Drop the dough in heaped tablespoons onto ungreased baking trays. There should be enough fat in the dough to stop it sticking, but if it does stick, baking paper or non-stick trays can help.

Bake in the preheated oven for 10–12 minutes or till golden brown (cooking times can vary). Leave them sit for about 1 minute, then lift them off the hot tray onto a wire rack to cool.

Notes, tips and variations

Ingredients

Use cake flour instead of plain, swap the white sugar for brown sugar, and use butter instead of marg. Cake flour and brown sugar let you use butter without your bickies turning into puddles (which is the only reason to use marg in the first place).

Use some vegetable shortening in place of the butter/marg to cut down the "spread" (the biscuits come out thicker).

Add some vegetable oil (no more than 1/5 of the total fat) in place of the butter/marg to get more spread and thinner bickies.

Chocolate with a cocoa content over 65% gives a fair bit different taste from the "traditional" recipe. As a rule, if you find the chocolate too bitter or not much chop to eat on its own, you mightn't like it in the biscuits either (however good it is).

A few shakes of ground cinnamon in with the dry ingredients gives more flavour too.

If you want to use ordinary flour and butter, the trick is to bump the flour up to 3+ cups, which should give you a fairly stiff dough that won't spread out heaps. The trick even works with wholemeal flour.

You can change the mix of white to brown sugar to suit your taste. More brown sugar makes chewier bickies. More white sugar makes crisper ones.

A bit more vanilla extract (between ½ and ¾ of a teaspoon, instead of ¼) gives the biscuit a stronger flavour.

If you can't get hold of vanilla extract, swap two teaspoons of vanilla sugar in for white sugar.

Add a little oatmeal to give the bickies more flavour and a better feel in the mouth.

Method

Some people reckon you should chill the dough before baking, though you usually don't need to. Chilling the dough can help keep the spread down, so you get thicker biscuits.

Use an ungreased, non-stick baking tray instead of a greased one so they spread less, for thicker bickies. The other way round, grease your tray if you like thinner (crispier) ones.

How good your flavouring ingredients are makes a big difference to how they turn out. Top-quality butter, choccy and vanilla make a better biscuit.

Chopped-up block chocolate instead of ready-made chips gives a more rustic texture -- just make sure the bits are small enough to eat easily.

Don't stack hot bickies, or they'll bend out of shape and won't cool as quick. Use a cooling rack instead for the best results.

For a stronger nutty flavour, toast the nuts. Spread them out evenly on a baking tray and pop them in the 350°F oven for ten minutes. Take them out and let them cool before you add them to the dough.

If you like a soft, squishy middle in your bickie, take them out one minute earlier for a beaut soft centre.
