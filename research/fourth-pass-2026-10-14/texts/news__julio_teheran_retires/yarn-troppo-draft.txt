Well, that's the last we'll see of this fella on the mound. Julio Teherán, a two-time Major League Baseball All-Star, has hung up the glove for good, announcing his retirement on Monday at the age of 35. And the timing's no fluke: it came just two days after he was scratched from a scheduled start for Team Colombia at the 2026 World Baseball Classic (WBC) with a crook shoulder. When the arm that's been earning you a living starts saying no, I reckon you listen to it.

Mind you, he didn't go out empty-handed. Before he called it, Teherán had helped Team Colombia knock off Team Panama 3-1 at the WBC. Not a bad note to bow out on.

The bloke had a thirteen-year MLB career, and most of it was spent with the Atlanta Braves, though he also did time with the Los Angeles Angels, Detroit Tigers, Milwaukee Brewers and New York Mets. Got around a bit, he did. He made his MLB debut with the Braves in 2011 as a 20-year-old, barely old enough to shout a round, and stuck with them for his first nine seasons, picking up All-Star selections in 2014 and 2016.

Now, for the stats tragics: over his MLB career Teherán put together an 81-82 record, with a 3.85 ERA, a 1.223 WHIP and 1,260 strikeouts in 1,470.2 innings. Near enough to even on the ledger, but that's a fair few innings to have in the legs. He'd also spent the 2022 and 2025 seasons playing in the Mexican Baseball League.
