Picture this: picnic and barbie season, and heaps of chances to get outdoors and have a good time with the family and your mates. Trouble is, all that warm weather is a field day for foodborne bacteria as well. Once your tucker starts warming up in the summer heat, those little buggers breed like rabbits.

So if you want to keep yourself, your family and your mates clear of foodborne illness over the warm months, handling your food safely when you're eating outdoors is dead important. Here's the drum: some simple food safety guidelines for lugging your tucker out to the picnic spot, and for preparing and serving it safely once you've rocked up.

Pack and Cart Your Tucker Safely

Keep your tucker safe the whole way: from the fridge/freezer — all the way to the picnic table.

Keep cold tucker cold. Chuck the cold stuff in an esky with ice or frozen ice bricks. Cold tucker wants to be kept at 40 °F (4 °C) or below, so the bacteria can't get a start. Meat, poultry and seafood can go in still frozen, and that way they stay colder for longer.

Get the eskies sorted. Have a think about packing the drinks in one esky and the perishables in another. That way, when every man and his dog is in and out of the drinks esky for a top-up, the perishables aren't copping a blast of warm air every time the lid comes up.

Keep the lids shut: Once you've set up, open the esky as few times as you can manage. The less it's opened, the longer everything in there stays cold.

Don't cross-contaminate. Make sure the raw meat, poultry and seafood stay wrapped up good and tight. That stops their juices dribbling onto anything that's prepared/cooked, or onto anything that'll be eaten raw, like your fruit and veg.

Wash your fruit and veg. Give fresh fruit and veg a rinse under running tap water before it goes in the esky — even the ones with skins and rinds nobody eats. The firm-skinned ones get a rub under running tap water, or a scrub with a clean vegie brush while you rinse 'em under running tap water. Dry the lot off with a clean cloth tea towel or paper towel. Anything packaged that says “ready-to-eat,” “washed,” or “triple washed” on the label doesn't need washing, so don't bother.

Quick Tips for Setting Up at the Picnic Spot

Food safety starts with washing your hands properly — out in the open too. Before you start laying out the big spread, make sure your hands and the surfaces are clean.

Washing Hands Outdoors: No running water handy? Use a water jug, some soap and paper towels. Or think about bringing moist disposable wipes to clean your hands with.

Utensils and Serving Dishes: Make sure every utensil and platter stays clean while you're getting the food ready.

Follow Safe Barbie Tips

Picnics and a cook-up on the barbie go together like a pie and sauce. And same as cooking inside, there are some important guidelines you need to stick to so your barbecued tucker makes it to the table safely.

Marinate safely. Do your marinating in the fridge — never on the kitchen bench and never outdoors. And if you're planning to use some of the marinade as a sauce on the cooked food, put a bit aside separately before the raw meat, poultry or seafood goes in. Don't reuse marinade. Once the raw stuff's been in it, that's it.

Cook it straight after “partial cooking.” Giving food a head start before it goes on the barbie is only safe when the partly cooked food can go straight onto a hot barbie, say at home with the barbie right there on the patio or deck.

Cook food right through. When it's time to cook, have your food thermometer handy. Always use it to make sure your food's cooked right through. Don't go by looks. (See Safe Food Temperature Chart)

Keep “ready” tucker hot. Once it's done, the tucker can be kept hot till it's served by shoving it over to the side of the grill rack, just away from the coals. It stays hot there but it won't overcook.

Don't reuse platters or utensils. Put cooked food back on the same platter, or use the same utensils, that held the raw meat, poultry or seafood, and bacteria from the raw juices go straight onto your cooked food. Have a clean platter and utensils ready next to the barbie to serve up instead.
