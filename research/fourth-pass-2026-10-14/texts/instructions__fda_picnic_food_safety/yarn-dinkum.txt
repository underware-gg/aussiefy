Picnic and barbie season gives you heaps of chances for a bit of fun outdoors with the family and your mates. But all that warm weather also gives foodborne bacteria a field day. As tucker warms up in the summer heat, bacteria breed like rabbits.

To keep yourself, your family and your mates safe from foodborne illness in the warm months, handling food safely when you're eating outdoors is dead important. Read on for some simple food safety guidelines for getting your tucker to the picnic spot, and for preparing and serving it safely once you've rocked up.

Pack and Cart Your Tucker Safely

Keep your tucker safe: from the fridge/freezer — all the way to the picnic table.

Keep cold tucker cold. Put cold tucker in an esky with ice or frozen ice bricks. Cold tucker should be kept at 40 °F (4 °C) or below so bacteria can't grow. Meat, poultry and seafood can go in while they're still frozen, so they stay colder for longer.

Sort out what goes in which esky. Think about packing the drinks in one esky and the perishables in another. That way, when everyone's opening and reopening the drinks esky for another one, the perishables won't cop the warm air outside.

Keep the eskies shut: Once you've set up, open the esky as few times as you can. That keeps everything in it cold for longer.

Don't cross-contaminate. Make sure raw meat, poultry and seafood stay wrapped up tight. That stops their juices getting on prepared/cooked food, or on food that'll be eaten raw, like fruit and veg.

Give your fruit and veg a wash. Rinse fresh fruit and veg under running tap water before you pack it in the esky — including the ones with skins and rinds you don't eat. Rub the firm-skinned ones under running tap water, or give them a scrub with a clean vegie brush while you rinse them under running tap water. Dry them off with a clean cloth tea towel or paper towel. Packaged fruit and veg labelled “ready-to-eat,” “washed,” or “triple washed” doesn't need washing.

Quick Tips for Setting Up at the Picnic Spot

Food safety starts with washing your hands properly — outdoors as well. Before you start laying out your picnic spread, make sure hands and surfaces are clean.

Washing Hands Outdoors: If you can't get to running water, use a water jug, some soap and paper towels. Or think about using moist disposable wipes to clean your hands.

Utensils and Serving Dishes: Make sure all the utensils and platters stay clean while you're preparing the food.

Follow Safe Barbie Tips

Cooking on the barbie and picnics go hand in hand. And just like cooking inside, there are important guidelines you need to follow so your barbecued tucker gets to the table safely.

Marinate safely. Marinate food in the fridge — never on the kitchen bench or outdoors. And if you're planning to use some of the marinade as a sauce on the cooked food, put a bit aside separately before the raw meat, poultry or seafood goes in. Don't reuse marinade.

Cook it straight after “partial cooking.” Partly cooking food before it goes on the barbie is only safe when the partly cooked food can go straight onto the hot barbie, say at home with a barbie out on the patio or deck.

Cook food right through. When it's time to cook, have your food thermometer handy. Always use it to make sure your food's cooked right through. (See Safe Food Temperature Chart)

Keep “ready” tucker hot. Cooked tucker can be kept hot till it's served by shifting it to the side of the grill rack, just away from the coals. That keeps it hot but stops it overcooking.

Don't reuse platters or utensils. Using the same platter or utensils that held raw meat, poultry or seafood lets bacteria from the raw food's juices get onto the cooked food. Have a clean platter and utensils ready next to the barbie to serve your tucker instead.
