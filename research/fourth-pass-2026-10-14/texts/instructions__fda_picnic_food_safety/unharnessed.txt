Picnic and barbie season means heaps of chances for a good time outdoors with the family and your mates. But all that warm-weather carry-on also gives the nasty bugs in your tucker a real chance to go off. As food heats up in the summer temps, bacteria breed like bloody rabbits.

To keep yourself, your family and your mates from copping a dose of food poisoning in the warmer months, handling your food properly when you're eating outdoors is dead important. Read on for some simple food safety tips on lugging your tucker to the picnic spot, and getting it ready and dishing it up safely once you've rocked up.

Pack and Transport Food Safely

Keep your food safe: from the fridge/freezer — all the way to the picnic table.

Keep cold food cold. Stick cold food in an esky with ice or frozen gel packs. Cold food should be kept at 40 °F or below to stop bacteria growing. Meat, chook and seafood can go in still frozen so they stay colder for longer.

Sort out what goes in the eskies. Think about packing the drinks in one esky and the perishables in another. That way, when everyone's opening and shutting the drinks esky to grab another one, the perishables aren't copping the warm outside air.

Keep the eskies shut: Once you're at the picnic spot, open the esky as little as you can. That keeps everything in it colder for longer.

Don't cross-contaminate. Make sure raw meat, chook and seafood are wrapped up good and tight. That stops their juices getting onto food that's already made or cooked, or food that'll be eaten raw, like fruit and veg.

Clean your fruit and veg. Give fresh fruit and veg a rinse under the running tap before they go in the esky — even the ones with skins and rinds you don't eat. Rub firm-skinned fruit and veg under the running tap, or give 'em a scrub with a clean veggie brush while you rinse. Dry them off with a clean tea towel or paper towel. Packaged fruit and veg labelled "ready-to-eat," "washed," or "triple washed" don't need washing.

Quick Tips for Picnic Site Preparation

Food safety starts with washing your hands properly — out in the bush too. Before you start laying out your picnic spread, make sure your hands and surfaces are clean.

Outdoor Hand Cleaning: If there's no running water, use a water jug, a bit of soap and some paper towels. Or think about bringing moist disposable wipes to clean your hands.

Utensils and Serving Dishes: Make sure all your utensils and platters stay clean while you're getting the food ready.

Follow Safe Grilling Tips

The barbie and a picnic usually go hand in hand. And just like cooking inside, there's some important rules to stick to so your barbecued food gets to the table safe.

Marinate safely. Marinate food in the fridge — never on the kitchen bench or outside. And if you're planning to use some of the marinade as a sauce on the cooked food, set a bit aside separately before the raw meat, chook or seafood goes in. Don't reuse marinade.

Cook it straight after "partial cooking." Partly cooking food before it goes on the barbie is only safe if the partly cooked food can go straight onto the hot grill, like at home with a barbie out on the patio or deck.

Cook food right through. When it's time to cook, have your food thermometer handy. Always use it to make sure your food's cooked right through. (See Safe Food Temperature Chart)

Keep "ready" food hot. Barbecued food can be kept hot till it's served by shifting it to the side of the grill, just away from the coals. That keeps it hot but stops it getting cooked to buggery.

Don't reuse platters or utensils. Using the same platter or tongs that had raw meat, chook or seafood on them lets the bacteria from the raw juices spread to the cooked food. Instead, have a clean platter and utensils ready by the barbie to serve up your food.
