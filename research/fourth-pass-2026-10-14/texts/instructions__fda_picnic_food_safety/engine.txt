Picnic and barbie season offers heaps of opportunities for outdoor fun with family and mates. But these warm weather events also present opportunities for foodborne bacteria to thrive. As tucker heats up in summer temperatures, bacteria multiply rapidly.

To protect yourself, your family, and mates from foodborne illness during warm-weather months, safe food handling when eating outdoors is critical. Read on for simple food safety guidelines for transporting your tucker to the picnic site, and preparing and serving it safely once you’ve fronted up.

Pack and Transport Food Safely

Keep your tucker safe: from the fridge/freezer — all the way to the picnic table.

Keep cold tucker cold. Place cold tucker in an esky with ice or frozen ice bricks. Cold tucker should be stored at 40 °F or below to prevent bacterial growth. Meat, poultry, and seafood may be packed while still frozen so that they stay colder longer.

Organize esky contents. Consider packing beverages in one cooler and perishable tucker in another. That way, as picnickers open and reopen the beverage esky to replenish their drinks, the perishable tucker won’t be exposed to warm outdoor air temperatures.

Keep coolers closed: Once at the picnic site, limit the number of times the esky is opened as much as you can. This helps to keep the contents cold longer.

Don’t cross-contaminate. Be sure to keep raw meat, poultry, and seafood securely wrapped. This keeps their juices from contaminating prepared/cooked tucker or foods that will be eaten raw, such as fruit and veg.

Clean your produce. Rinse fresh fruits and vegetables under running tap water before packing them in the esky — including those with skins and rinds that aren't eaten. Rub firm-skinned fruits and vegetables under running tap water or scrub with a clean vegetable brush while rinsing with running tap water. Dry fruits and vegetables with a clean cloth towel or paper towel. Packaged fruits and vegetables that are labelled “ready-to-eat,” “washed,” or “triple washed” need not be washed.

Quick Tips for Picnic Site Preparation

Food safety begins with proper hand cleaning — including in outdoor settings. Before you begin setting out your picnic feast, make sure hands and surfaces are clean.

Outdoor Hand Cleaning: If you don’t have access to running water, use a water jug, some soap, and paper towels. Or, consider using moist disposable towelettes for cleaning your hands.

Utensils and Serving Dishes: Take care to keep all utensils and platters clean when preparing tucker.

Follow Safe Grilling Tips

Grilling and picnicking often go hand-in-hand. And just as with cooking indoors, there are important guidelines that should be followed to ensure that your grilled tucker reaches the table safely.

Marinate safely. Marinate tucker in the fridge — never on the kitchen bench or outdoors. In addition, if you plan to use some of the marinade as a sauce on the cooked tucker, reserve a portion separately before adding the raw meat, poultry, or seafood. Don’t reuse marinade.

Cook immediately after “partial cooking.” Partial cooking before grilling is only safe when the partially cooked tucker can go on the hot grill immediately, for example at a home with a grill on the patio or deck.

Cook tucker thoroughly. When it’s time to cook the tucker, have your food thermometer ready. Always use it to be sure your tucker is cooked thoroughly. (See Safe Food Temperature Chart)

Keep “ready” tucker hot. Grilled tucker can be kept hot until served by moving it to the side of the grill rack, just away from the coals. This keeps it hot but prevents overcooking.

Don’t reuse platters or utensils. Using the same platter or utensils that previously held raw meat, poultry, or seafood allows bacteria from the raw food’s juices to spread to the cooked tucker. Instead, have a clean platter and utensils ready at grill-side to serve your tucker.
