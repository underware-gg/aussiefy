## dinkum
- Accepted on the first submit; nothing declined. Kept "dead careful", "a mug", "blokes", "uni", "a bloke", "eight quid", "young fellas".
- "quid" for "dollars" is borderline here. It fits the wage ("eight quid every Saturday night", quid as loose for a dollar), but the same text's "sixty cents on any dollar ... a dollar is worth one hundred and six cents" needs "dollar" for the cents to add up, and the engine draft turned two of those into "quid" ("sixty cents on any quid", "a quid is worth one hundred and six cents"). The planned list only names the plural "dollars", so the draft went past its own plan. I kept "dollar" in the cents lines.
- Engine draft problems outside the planned list: "Bloody hell, I simply mention..." (a swear tacked onto a sentence's edge that the father never says); "a bloody good deal like" ("a good deal like" means "much like", not "good"); "the old bloke" for "the old man" (here the old man *is* the Aussie for one's father, so the swap loses the sense).
