CHICAGO, May 4, 189-

_Dear Pierrepont:_ The cashier's just plonked your expense account for the month on my desk, son, and I tell ya, one look at it fair bends a bloke's shoulders over. When I said I wanted you to get a liberal education, I didn't mean I wanted to buy the whole of Cambridge. Now, the bills won't break me, but they'll break you, my lad, unless you're dead careful. Dead, dead careful.

I've been watching your accounts get fatter every month for the last two years, and I haven't seen a single sign of you taking honours to justify the extra running costs. That's bad business. It's a good deal like feeding a mongrel steer his own weight in corn when he won't put on an ounce, no matter what you shovel into him.

I've kept my trap shut about this till now, cos I was leaning a fair bit on your native nous to stop you making a mug of yourself, the way some of these young blokes do when they've never had to work for a quid. But just cos I've sat tight, don't go getting it into your noggin that the old man's rollin' in it and can wear it. He won't wear it, not once you're out of uni. The sooner you cut your spending down to fit what you'll actually be earning, the easier those two'll find it to live under the one roof.

There's only one sure way a bloke gets rich quick, and that's to have it handed to him or to inherit it. You won't be getting rich that way, not till you've proved you can hold down a pretty important job with the firm, anyway. And of course there's only one spot a bloke can start from for that job at Graham & Co. Doesn't matter a skerrick whether he's the old man's son or the cellar boss's: that spot is the bottom. And the bottom in the office end of this business is a seat at the mailing-desk, with eight quid every Saturday night. That's it. That's the lot.

I can't hand you any ready-made success on a plate. It'd do you no good, and it'd do the house harm. There's plenty of room at the top here, mind, but there's no lift in the building. You'll have to take the stairs like everyone else. Starting out with a good education, like you are, you ought to climb quicker than the fella who hasn't got one. But there'll come a day, when you start at the factory, when you can't lick stamps as fast as the other young fellas at the desk. And yet the bloke who's never licked stamps isn't fit to write letters. Naturally, that's when knowing whether the pie comes before the ice-cream, and how to drive a motor car, won't be worth a cracker to you.

I'm only bringing all this up cos I'm afraid your ideas about the footing you're coming into the house on have got a bit puffed up over there in the East. I can give you a start, but after that you'll have to blast your own way to the front, dynamite and all. It's all down to the man. Give some blokes a talent wrapped in a napkin to start out in business with, and they'd swap the talent for a gold brick and lose the napkin into the bargain. Then there are others you could start off with nothing but the napkin, and they'd set up with it in the drapery business in a small way, and before you know it they'd have coaxed the other fella's talent in there with it.

I've got pride enough to reckon you've got the right sort of stuff in you, son, but I want to see some of it come out. You'll never make a decent merchant of yourself by turning round the order the Lord laid down for us all: learning the spending end of business before the earning end. For the spendthrift, pay day's always a month off, and he never gets more than sixty cents out of any dollar that lands in his hand. But to a good businessman a dollar's worth one hundred and six cents, and he never spends the dollar at all. It's the man who keeps his savings up and his expenses down who ends up buying a share in the concern. And that's exactly where you'll find yourself weak, if your expense accounts aren't telling porkies. They don't generally tell porkies that particular way, mind you, though Baron Munchausen was the first travelling salesman, and my drummers' bills still show his influence. Every one of them reads like a fairy story.
