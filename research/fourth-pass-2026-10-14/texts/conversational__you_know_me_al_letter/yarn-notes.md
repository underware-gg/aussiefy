## dinkum

- Accepted on the first submit. Declined "sweet bugger-all" (for "won't have nothing on you"): right sense,
  but the plan also puts "bugger all" two sentences earlier for "there won't be nothing to it", so taking both
  repeats the swear. The plan should pick one of the two for a text this short.
- Lint suggestions "nobody" -> "no bugger"/"no bastard" (twice) would have pushed the swearing up in a letter
  that already had "bugger all"; not taken. "a look" -> "a squiz" was a good catch.
- "the old man" (the club boss) planned as "the old bloke" loses the "boss" sense a little; acceptable.
- Kept Jack's misspellings ("dum", "appresiated", "serious", "misjuged", "excepted", "could of"), which are
  the voice and the joke; lint and submit didn't trip on them, which is right.

## troppo

- Accepted on the first submit; same "sweet bugger-all" decline, same reason.
- OPENINGS ("Here's one for ya.", "You'll like this one.") don't suit a letter, whose greeting has to stay
  in place; the troppo guidance says "a letter stays a letter", so I ignored them. The brief shouldn't offer
  openings for the personal genre's letters.
- Troppo here meant more of Jack's own colour inside the letter ("stood there like a stunned mullet",
  "They're only bricks.", "Wasn't my fault, that one.", "Same old Jack."), not a pub frame.
- Lookups came back empty for "strike me pink" and "running the ruler over" (both genuine Australian);
  "on song" was there. Lint "heavy lifting" -> "hard yakka" was a good catch.
