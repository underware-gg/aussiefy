Petrol ("gas", as the Yanks call it) is sold by the gallon, at servos that are mostly self-serve (you pump your own), except in New Jersey, where self-serve is against the law, and Oregon, where servos in the cities have to have at least 50% full-service pumps by law. The American gallon's smaller than the imperial gallon and works out to 3.785 litres.

Thanks to all the bloody "drive offs" during the crime waves of the 1990s and 2000s, nearly every American servo now makes you pay before you pump (it used to be the other way round). These days you've gotta preauthorise a credit or debit card at the pump. Otherwise you have to guess how much fuel you need, hand over that much cash to whoever's on the counter, and tell 'em which pump you're on (a Yank might say, "I need 50 bucks on pump number 1"). The pump cuts out by itself once the cost of the fuel hits the amount of cash you've handed over, or if you guessed too high, you can go back in and ask for your unused cash back.

The U.S. octane scale's different from the one they use in Europe; a regular gallon of U.S. petrol has an octane rating of 87, which is about 92 in Europe. In most states, servos give you three octane levels to pick from: 87 (regular), 89 (midgrade or plus), and 91 (premium). Unless you've hired some flash luxury car, your vehicle will most likely only need 87 (regular). If you're driving round the Rocky Mountains, you might come across servos selling fuel with lower octane ratings, most often 85. That's fine for 87-octane cars up at high altitude, but if you're heading down lower, make bloody sure you fill up with 87.

Diesel's not as common, but it's still used heaps and you'll find it at most servos, especially the ones that look after truckies. Untaxed "offroad diesel", sold out in the bush for farm use, is dyed red (and sometimes called "red diesel"), and you shouldn't put it in your car, 'cause you'll cop a massive fine if you're caught.

In the United States, the old colour code at servos is that petrol comes out of nozzles with a black handle, while diesel comes out of nozzles with a green handle. That's the opposite of the colour code they use in the United Kingdom. Don't get 'em mixed up, or you'll be well and truly fucked.
