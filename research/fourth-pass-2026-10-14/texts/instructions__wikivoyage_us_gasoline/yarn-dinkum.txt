Petrol (what the Yanks call "gas") is sold by the gallon, at servos that are mostly self-service (ya gotta pump yer own), except for the ones in New Jersey, where self-service is against the law, and in Oregon, where servos in the cities are required by law to have at least 50% full-service pumps. Mind you, the American gallon's smaller than the imperial gallon and works out at 3.785 litres.

Now, cos of all the "drive offs" durin' the crime waves of the 1990s and 2000s, nearly every American servo makes drivers pay before they can pump (it used to be the other way round). These days ya have to preauthorise a credit or debit card at the pump. Otherwise, ya have to guess how much petrol ya need, hand over that much of the folding stuff to whoever's on the till, and tell 'em which pump yer on (a Yank might say, "I need 50 bucks on pump number 1"). The pump'll cut out on its own once the price of the petrol it's put through hits the amount ya handed over, or if ya guessed too high ya can go back in and ask for the rest of yer dosh back.

The U.S. octane scale's different from the one they use in Europe; a regular gallon of U.S. petrol has an octane rating of 87, which is about the same as 92 in Europe. In most states, servos give ya a choice of three grades of octane: 87 (regular), 89 (midgrade or plus), and 91 (premium). Unless yer hirin' a flash car, yer car'll most likely only need 87 (regular). If yer drivin' round the Rocky Mountains, ya might come across servos sellin' petrol at lower octane ratings, mostly 85. That's fine for 87-octane cars up high, but if yer headin' down lower, make sure ya fill up with 87.

Diesel's not as common, but it's still used heaps and ya can get it at most servos, especially the ones that look after truckies. Untaxed "offroad diesel", sold out in the country for farm use, is dyed red (and sometimes called "red diesel") and shouldn't go in cars, cos there's hefty fines if ya get caught.

In the United States, the usual colour code at servos is that petrol comes out of nozzles with a black handle, and diesel comes out of nozzles with a green handle. That's the opposite of the colour code in the United Kingdom. Don't mix 'em up.
