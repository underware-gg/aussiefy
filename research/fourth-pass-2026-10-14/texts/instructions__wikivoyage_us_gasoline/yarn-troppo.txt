Off to the States, are ya? Reckon fillin' up'll be easy as? Hang on a sec. Petrol over there's "gas", for starters, and they sell it by the gallon, at servos that are mostly self-service (ya pump yer own, same as home), except in New Jersey, where self-service is flat-out illegal, and Oregon, where the law says servos in the cities have to keep at least 50% of their pumps full-service. And don't go doin' the maths with the gallon yer nan used to talk about: the American gallon's smaller than the imperial one, and comes out at 3.785 litres.

Now here's where it gets fiddly. Back in the crime waves of the 1990s and 2000s, so many blokes were fillin' up and doin' a runner (the "drive offs", they call 'em) that nearly every American servo now makes ya pay before ya pump. It used to be the other way round, like back home, where ya fill up first and then go in and pay, eyein' off a pie that's been in the warmer since Tuesday. These days ya preauthorise a credit or debit card at the pump. Or, if yer payin' with the folding stuff, ya have a guess at how much petrol ya need, hand that much over to whoever's on the till, and tell 'em which pump yer on. A Yank'd say somethin' like, "I need 50 bucks on pump number 1." The pump cuts out on its own once the petrol it's put through hits what ya paid, or if ya guessed too high, ya just trot back in and ask for the rest of yer dosh back. No dramas.

Then there's the octane, and this'll do yer head in. The U.S. scale's different from the one in Europe: a regular gallon of U.S. petrol is 87 octane, which is about 92 in European money. Most states give ya three grades: 87 (regular), 89 (midgrade or plus), and 91 (premium). Unless ya've hired somethin' flash, yer car'll most likely only need 87 (regular), so don't go splashin' out. Up round the Rocky Mountains ya might come across servos sellin' lower octane, mostly 85. That's fine for 87-octane cars up high, but if yer headin' back down to lower country, make sure ya fill up with 87.

Diesel's not as common over there, but it's still used heaps and most servos have it, especially the ones lookin' after the truckies. Now, out in the country you'll find untaxed "offroad diesel" for farm use. It's dyed red (some call it "red diesel") and it's not to go in cars, full stop, cos there's hefty fines if ya get caught.

And the big one. In the United States the usual colour code at servos is black handle for petrol and green handle for diesel. That's the opposite of the colour code in the United Kingdom. Don't mix 'em up.
