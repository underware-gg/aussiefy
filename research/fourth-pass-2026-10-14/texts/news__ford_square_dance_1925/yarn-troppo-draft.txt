DETROIT, Dec. 13 (AP).—Here's one for ya. Henry Ford, the motor car bloke himself, has already announced he's on a campaign to teach the American public the old-fashioned square dances, and his first big crack at it comes off Tuesday night at one of the biggest dance halls in Detroit. For the big do, Mr. Ford's puttin' ads out for couples to dance the square dance and make up the core of the party, so the thing's not just him and the band.

Asked tonight whether he really reckoned the old-fashioned steps'd make a comeback, Mr. Ford came straight back at 'em:

"Come back? They're here! We simply are falling back in line. What we are trying to do in getting out a book of dances is to standardise them. The first edition is being revised in the direction of simplicity."

So there ya go. Out of the twenty-one quadrilles, Mr. Ford explained, they'll whittle it down to four, to make 'em easier to teach and get people a bit keener on 'em.

Now, "Mellie" Dunham, the champion fiddler of Maine, has been stayin' with Mr. Ford for a week, sawin' away for an old-fashioned dance party and chippin' in a few old-time tunes for the Ford "Americana" of such pieces that's bein' got ready for publication. He was meant to lob at the Tuesday night do. But the plans went out the window, and "Grandpa Mellie," with the missus in tow, is on his way tonight to New York, where he might just answer the call of the footlights and sign on for a vaudeville contract. Not bad for an old fiddler, eh?

"Mellie" Dunham fronts up in New York this mornin'. Word got around last night that he'll be under the management of Tex Rickard, who's said to be cookin' up a barn dance in his honour at the brand-spankin'-new Madison Square Garden. And the story goes that puttin' on this dance was meant to give Henry Ford's push a leg-up, to get square dances goin' again.
