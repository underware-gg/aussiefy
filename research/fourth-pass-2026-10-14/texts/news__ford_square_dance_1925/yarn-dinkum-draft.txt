DETROIT, Dec. 13 (AP).—Henry Ford's first big crack at the campaign he's already announced, to teach the American public the old-fashioned square dances, comes off on Tuesday night at one of the biggest dance halls in Detroit. For the do, Mr. Ford's advertisin' for couples to dance the square dance and make up the core of the party.

Asked tonight whether he really reckoned the old-fashioned steps'd come back, Mr. Ford sang out:

"Come back? They're here! We simply are falling back in line. What we are trying to do in getting out a book of dances is to standardise them. The first edition is being revised in the direction of simplicity."

Out of the twenty-one quadrilles, Mr. Ford explained, they'll pick four, to make 'em easier to teach and get people keener on 'em.

"Mellie" Dunham, the champion fiddler of Maine, who's been stayin' with Mr. Ford for a week, playin' for an old-fashioned dance party and chippin' in a few old-time tunes for the Ford "Americana" of such pieces that's now bein' got ready for publication, was meant to turn up at the Tuesday night do. But the plans got changed, and "Grandpa Mellie," with the missus, is on his way tonight to New York, where he might answer the call of the footlights and sign up for a vaudeville contract.

"Mellie" Dunham'll front up in New York this mornin'. Word last night was he'll be under the management of Tex Rickard, who's said to be plannin' a barn dance in his honour at the brand-spankin'-new Madison Square Garden. It was added that puttin' on this dance was hoped to help Henry Ford's push to get square dances goin' again.
