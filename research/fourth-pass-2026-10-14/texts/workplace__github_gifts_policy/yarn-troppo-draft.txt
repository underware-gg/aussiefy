## Examples of acceptable and unacceptable gifts

So what's “of value” when it's at home? First thing: no gifts are OK for government officials. None. (Righto, maybe stickers.) Always ask them what their own rules let them take and what they don't. And if you're not sure, please run it past Legal to make sure. For example, plenty of U.S. congresspeople won't let you take them out for dinner and pick up the bill, no matter how nicely you ask.

As for other customers or would-be customers, something that seems like small potatoes to you can be worth a packet to someone else. Say you get your mate’s daughter in the door as an intern. Might not seem like a big deal to you, but it could mean heaps to them. And if she's not qualified and you pull strings to make it happen, then it's not OK. On top of that, if the whole idea is to improperly influence someone, anything at all, however small, can be counted as "of value."

🙅 _Here's some more stuff that's not ok:_

* First class airfare to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️+🍾
* Winery tours 🍷
* Putting in a good word to get a mate's kid into some flash private school 🎓
* Tickets to Disneyland 👸, the World Cup 🥅, the Olympics 🏅
* A soccer ball ⚽️ signed by some big-name player
* A donation to a charity

🙆 _And here's some stuff that's actually fine for customers or prospective customers:_

* Swag
* Economy class airfare to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️
* Cheap seats at a sporting event ⚾️
* A dinner in town that won't break the bank 🍽
* A small box of chocolates as an end of year thank you 🍫

And the lot of it cuts both ways: it all goes for _taking_ bribes too. So if a vendor or would-be vendor sends you something “of value,” don’t take it—or at the very least run it past Legal before you do.

## Process

If you're planning on giving or taking an informal gift (anything that isn't one of our own promo bits like t-shirts and sticker swag), please make sure you stick to the process below.

Keep in mind that what counts as an OK amount can change depending on where you are. For example, $50 on dinner might be a fair bit of dosh in some countries and not much at all in others. So, if you're not sure, ask!

1. Generally speaking, for any expenses over $150, you'll need approval (every time) from Legal in writing. And for any gifts you get that are worth over $150, you'll need to report them (every time) to Legal in writing. (Legal's only weighing in on the bribery side of things. You might still need to check with Finance for other reasons.)

1. When you put your expense report in to get your money back, please:
     * Say who got the gift and what it was for in the notes; and
     * Include a copy of the email approval

## Consequences

So what happens if you don't follow the policy, and GitHub ends up shouting a prospective customer and his whole family first class flights to Japan and a trip to the Tokyo Olympics? Or if you drop off a suitcase full of Euros for a government official in a train station locker you'd sorted out beforehand, like something out of a spy flick?

FINES and JAIL. And it's not just GitHub copping it, either. You could be the one doing a few years in jail, and it won't matter a bit whether the customer ever put an order in with GitHub after their trip to Japan, or whether the government official even picked up the suitcase of cash. Basically, it doesn't matter whether you actually _made_ a bribe. Yoda or no Yoda, it's the trying that counts.
