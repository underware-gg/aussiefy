## Examples of acceptable and unacceptable gifts

So what does “of value” actually mean? Keep in mind that no gifts are OK for government officials (well, maybe stickers). You should always ask them what their policies allow and don't allow. But if you're not sure, please run it past Legal to make sure. For example, plenty of U.S. congresspeople won't let you take them out for dinner and pick up the bill.

As for other customers or would-be customers, some things might seem like nothing much but be worth a lot to someone else. For example, getting your mate’s daughter in as an intern might not seem like a big deal to you, but it could be worth heaps to them. And if she's not qualified and you pull strings to make it happen, then it's not OK. On top of that, if the idea is to improperly influence someone, anything, however small, might be counted as "of value."

🙅 _Here are some other things that aren't ok:_

* First class airfare to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️+🍾
* Winery tours 🍷
* Putting in a good word for a mate's kid to get into a flash private school 🎓
* Tickets to Disneyland 👸, the World Cup 🥅, the Olympics 🏅
* A soccer ball ⚽️ signed by a famous player
* A donation to a charity

🙆 _Here is some stuff that is actually ok for customers or prospective customers:_

* Swag
* Economy class airfare to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️
* Cheap-seat tickets to a sporting event ⚾️
* A dinner in town that doesn't break the bank 🍽
* A small box of chocolates as an end of year thank you 🍫

All of the above goes for _taking_ bribes too. So if a vendor or would-be vendor sends you something “of value,” don’t accept it—or at least run it past Legal before you do.

## Process

If you're planning to give or accept an informal gift (one that isn't one of our promo items like t-shirts and sticker swag), please make sure you follow the process below.

Keep in mind that what counts as an acceptable amount can vary. For example, spending $50 on dinner might be a lot of money in some countries but not so much in others. So, if you're not sure, ask!

1. Generally speaking, for any expenses over $150, you'll need approval (each time) from Legal in writing. And for any gifts you receive over $150, you'll need to report them (each time) to Legal in writing. (Legal is only weighing in on the bribery question. You might still need to check with Finance for other reasons.)

1. When you put in your expense report to get paid back, please:
     * Note who got the gift and what it was for in the notes; and
     * Include a copy of the email approval

## Consequences

What happens if you don’t follow the policy and GitHub ends up shouting a prospective customer and his family first class flights to Japan and a trip to the Tokyo Olympics? Or if you drop off a suitcase of Euros for a government official in a train station locker you'd organised beforehand?

FINES and JAIL. And not just for GitHub, either. You might end up doing a few years in jail yourself, and it won’t matter whether the customer ever put in an order with GitHub after their trip to Japan, or whether the government official even picked up the suitcase of cash. Basically, it doesn’t matter whether you actually _made_ a bribe. Yoda or no Yoda, it’s the trying that counts.
