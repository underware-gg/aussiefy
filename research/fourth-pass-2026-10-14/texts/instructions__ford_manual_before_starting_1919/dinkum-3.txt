What's gotta be done before ya start the Car?

First things first: before ya even try to start her, fill the radiator (whip the cap off the top) with clean fresh water. If ya can't get yer mitts on spotless water, strain it through muslin or some cloth like that, so no muck gets in and bungs up the radiator's little tubes. The system takes about three gallons (around 11 litres). And it's important: don't run the car under its own steam till the water system's been filled. Keep pourin' till yer sure the radiator and the cylinder water jackets are both full. You'll know the whole water system's filled proper when the water starts runnin' out the overflow pipe onto the ground. For the first few days yer drivin' a brand-spankin'-new car, it's a beaut idea to check the radiator often and keep it topped up. Top up the water as often as ya find it needs it. Soft rainwater, if ya can get it clean, beats hard water hands down, cos hard water can have alkalies and other salts in it that tend to leave sediment behind and clog the radiator. (See chapter on Cooling System.)

What about Petrol?

Fill the ten-gallon (around 38-litre) petrol tank—nearly full—and never let it run low. When yer fillin' the tank make sure there's no naked flames within several feet, cos the fumes are dead volatile and they travel quick. And always watch yerself strikin' a match anywhere near spilt petrol: the air for several feet round about is full of the fumes, and they're highly explosive. Don't let the little vent hole in the petrol tank cap get bunged up either, cos then the petrol won't flow properly to the carburettor. To drain the petrol tank, open the pet cock in the sediment bulb down the bottom.

How about the Oilin' System?

Soon as ya get the car, make sure some medium light, top-quality petrol engine oil goes into the crank case through the breather pipe at the front of the engine (there's a metal cap over it). Get down under the car and in the flywheel casing (the tank that holds this oil) you'll find two pet cocks. Pour the oil in slowly till it runs out the top cock. Leave that cock open till it stops runnin'—then shut it. Once the engine's been well and truly run in, you'll get the best out of it keepin' the oil halfway between the two cocks—but whatever ya do, never let it drop below the bottom cock. Everything else on the car's oiled properly before it leaves the factory. Still, it's worth checkin' all the grease cups are full and the parts that need oil are gettin' it. (See Cut No. 18, also chapter on Lubrication.)

How are Spark and Throttle Levers used?

Under the steerin' wheel ya've got two little levers. The right-hand one (the throttle) controls how much mixture (petrol and air) goes into the engine. With the engine runnin', the further ya pull this lever down toward ya (that's called "openin' the throttle") the faster the engine goes and the more grunt ya get. The left-hand lever controls the spark, which is what sets off the petrol mix in the engine's cylinders. Movin' this lever "advances the spark," and ya bring it down a notch at a time till the motor seems to hit its top speed. Push the lever past that point and you'll hear a dull knock in the engine. (See chapter on Ignition.)

Where should these levers be when startin' the Engine?

Usually ya want the spark lever in about the third or fourth notch of the quadrant (the notched half-circle the levers work on), and the throttle opened about five or six notches. A bit of practice'll soon teach ya where to set these levers to start her properly. Just take care not to advance the spark lever too far, cos the engine can "back kick."
