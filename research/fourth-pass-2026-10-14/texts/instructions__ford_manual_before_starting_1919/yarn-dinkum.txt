What's to be done before ya start the Car?

Before you have a crack at startin' the car, fill the radiator (take the cap off the top) with clean fresh water. If ya can't get water that's perfectly clean, you're best off strainin' it through muslin or some cloth like it, so no muck gets in and blocks up the little tubes of the radiator. The system holds about three gallons (near enough 11 litres). It's important you don't run the car under its own steam unless the water circulatin' system's been filled. Keep pourin' the water in till you're sure both the radiator and the cylinder water jackets are full. Once the whole water system's filled up proper, the water'll run out the overflow pipe onto the ground. For the first few days you're drivin' a brand-spankin'-new car, it's a good idea to check the radiator often and make sure it's kept topped up. Top up the water as often as it needs it. Soft rainwater, when you can get it clean, beats hard water, which can have alkalies and other salts in it that tend to leave sediment and clog up the radiator. (See chapter on Cooling System.)

What about the Petrol?

The ten-gallon (about 38-litre) petrol tank should be filled (nearly full) and you should never let it run low. When you're fillin' the tank, make bloody sure there's no naked flames within several feet, cos the vapour is dead volatile and gets around quick smart. Always be careful strikin' matches anywhere near where petrol's been spilt, cos the air for several feet round about is full of the highly explosive vapour. Don't let the little vent hole in the petrol tank cap get bunged up, cos that'd stop the petrol flowin' proper to the carburettor. You can drain the petrol tank by openin' the pet cock in the sediment bulb at the bottom.

What about the Oilin' System?

When you get the car, make sure a good dose of medium light, top-quality petrol engine oil gets poured into the crank case through the breather pipe at the front of the engine (there's a metal cap over it). Down under the car in the flywheel casin' (the reservoir that holds this oil) you'll find two pet cocks. Pour the oil in slow till it runs out the upper cock. Leave the cock open till it stops runnin', then shut it. Once the engine's well and truly limbered up, you'll get the best results keepin' the oil at a level halfway between the two cocks, but whatever you do, don't ever let it get below the lower cock. All the other parts of the car are oiled proper when it leaves the factory. Still, it's worth checkin' that all the grease cups are full and that oil's gettin' to the parts that need it. How do the Spark and Throttle Levers work?

Under the steerin' wheel there's two little levers. The right-hand (throttle) lever controls how much mixture (petrol and air) goes into the engine. When the engine's goin', the further you move this lever down toward the driver (that's called "opening the throttle"), the faster the engine runs and the more grunt it puts out. The left-hand lever controls the spark, which sets off the gas in the engine's cylinders. Pushin' this lever along "advances the spark," and you should move it down notch by notch till the motor seems to hit its top speed. If you advance the lever past that point, you'll notice a dull knock in the engine. (See chapter on Ignition.)

Where should these levers be when ya start the Engine?

The spark lever usually wants to go in about the third or fourth notch of the quadrant (the notched half-circle the levers work on). The throttle usually wants openin' about five or six notches. You'll soon get the hang of where these levers go for a proper start. Be careful not to advance the spark lever too far, cos the engine may "back kick."
