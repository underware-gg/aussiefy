Why'd me banana catch fire in the microwave?

Hooley dooley, I know that sounds like a daft question, but fair dinkum! The missus froze some bananas in the freezer with the peel still on. You can't peel 'em when they're frozen. So I bunged one in the microwave for one minute at 50% power. After 30 seconds the thin end of the peel (where a bunch join up to each other) was on fire, with a flame you could see plain as day (about the size of a lighter). What on earth happened?

* * *

It's down to all the potassium in the banana. Microwaves react with metals: they bounce off and cause arcing. You can even put on a ripper little light show by bunging a raw peeled banana in the microwave. No worries, it won't explode, but it'll make a dog's breakfast of the thing. It's harmless too.

This can happen with some frozen veggies as well, depending on the soil they grew in. Like the linked article says, heaps of other metals such as magnesium, iron and zinc can be the mineral to blame.

A quick bit of sparking is harmless and won't hurt your microwave, but the USDA reckons you should turn your microwave off if you see sparks all the same. If it does start a fire, unless it's only a very small fire, do not open the microwave door. The safest thing is to turn the microwave off and let the fire burn itself out once it's used up all the oxygen. Open the door and you could get a dangerous backdraft fireball.
