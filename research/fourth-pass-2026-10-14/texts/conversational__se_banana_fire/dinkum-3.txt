Why'd me banana catch fire in the microwave?

Hooley dooley, I know it sounds like a daft question, but fair dinkum! The missus chucked a heap of bananas in the freezer with the skins still on, and you can't peel the buggers once they're frozen. So I bunged one in the microwave for one minute at 50% power. 30 seconds in, the thin end of the skin (where a bunch all join up) was alight, with a flame you could actually see, about the size of a lighter. What the bloody hell happened there?

* * *

It's all the potassium, mate: bananas have heaps of it. Microwaves react with metals, bouncing off 'em and causing arcing. You can even put on a ripper little light show if you bung a raw peeled banana in the microwave. No worries, it won't blow up, but it'll make a dog's breakfast of the inside, and it's harmless enough.

Same thing can happen with some frozen veggies too, depending on what kind of soil they were grown in. Like the linked article says, it can be high amounts of other metals doing it, such as magnesium, iron and zinc.

A quick spark or two is harmless and won't hurt your microwave, but the USDA still reckons you should switch the microwave off if you see sparks. If it does go up in flames, unless it's only a real small fire, don't open the microwave door. Safest thing is to switch the microwave off and let the fire burn itself out once it's used up all the oxygen. Open the door and you could get a dangerous fireball from the backdraft.
