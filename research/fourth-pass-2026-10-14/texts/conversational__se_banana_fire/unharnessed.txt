Why the bloody hell did me banana catch fire in the microwave?

Strewth, I know that sounds like a dopey question, but fair dinkum! The missus chucked some bananas in the freezer with the skins still on. You can't peel the buggers when they're frozen. So I bunged one in the microwave for a minute on 50% power. Thirty seconds in, the skinny end of the peel (where the bunch joins up) was on fire, a proper flame and all (about the size of a lighter's). What on earth went on there?

* * *

Righto, it's down to all the potassium in a banana. Microwaves muck about with metals, bouncing off 'em and making 'em arc. You can even put on a bonzer little light show by sticking a raw peeled banana in the microwave. No dramas, it won't blow up, but it'll make a bit of a mess. It's harmless as.

Same thing can happen with some frozen veggies too, depending on what sort of dirt they were grown in. Like the linked article says, other big lots of metals like magnesium, iron and zinc can be the mineral to blame.

A quick little spark here and there won't hurt anyone or wreck your microwave, but the USDA reckons you should switch it off if you see sparks anyway. If it does catch fire, and it's anything more than a tiny one, don't you dare open the microwave door. Safest bet is to switch the thing off and let the fire burn itself out once it's chewed through all the oxygen. Open the door and you could cop a nasty backdraft fireball in the face.
