Why'd me banana go up in flames in the microwave?

Listen to this. Hooley dooley, I know it sounds like a daft question, but fair dinkum, it happened! The missus froze a heap of bananas in the freezer with the peel still on, and you can't get the peel off 'em when they're frozen solid, can ya? So I bung one in the microwave for one minute at 50% power, nice and gentle. 30 seconds in, the skinny end of the peel (the bit where a bunch hang on to each other) is on fire. Not smoking, mate: an actual flame you could see, about the size of a lighter. What on earth's going on there?

* * *

Blame the potassium. There's a fair bit of it in a banana, and microwaves don't get on with metals: they bounce off 'em and cause arcing. You can even put on a ripper little light show by bunging a raw peeled banana in the microwave. No dramas, it won't explode, but it'll make a right dog's breakfast of the inside of the thing. Harmless, though.

Same thing can happen with some frozen veggies too, depending on what sort of soil they grew in. Like the linked article says, heaps of other metals such as magnesium, iron and zinc can be the mineral to blame, so it's not always the potassium copping it.

Now, a quick bit of sparking is harmless and won't hurt your microwave, but the USDA reckons you should turn your microwave off if you see sparks all the same. And if it does start a fire, unless it's only a very small fire, do not open the microwave door. The safest thing is to turn the microwave off and let the fire burn itself out once it's used up all the oxygen in there. Open that door and you could cop a dangerous backdraft fireball in the face. Leave it shut.
