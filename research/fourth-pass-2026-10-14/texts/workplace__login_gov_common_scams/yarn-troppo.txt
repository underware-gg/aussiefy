Strewth, where do I start? Scammers never give it a rest. They're always cookin' up some brand-spankin'-new way to con people into handin' over their personal details or forkin' out dodgy payments. Knowin' their tricks is the best defence you've got. So here's a rundown of the ones doin' the rounds most, and how to keep yerself out of their clutches.

## Fake emails and texts

Scammers fire off emails or texts that look official as anything, often with government logos slapped on and the government lingo laid on thick. This is called phishing. Phishing attacks might:

- Rush ya into clickin' a link to “verify your account”
- Carry on like there's some urgent drama with yer benefits or tax return
- Ask for yer personal details, like Social Security numbers or bank details

### How to protect yourself

- Don't click on links or download attachments from anyone ya don't know. Not a one.
- Check the message is fair dinkum by gettin' onto the agency yerself, usin' their official contact details.
- Report dodgy messages to whatever agency the scammer's tryin' to pass themselves off as

## Impersonation phone calls

Fraudsters might ring ya up pretendin' to be from the government. They'll often:
- Threaten ya with arrest, losin' yer licence, or losin' yer benefits
- Lean on ya to pay up right this minute with gift cards, crypto, or wire transfers

### How to protect yourself

- Government agencies will never ask ya to pay over the phone. Never. So that's yer giveaway.
- Hang up on 'em and ring the official number listed on the Login.gov or agency website.

## Fake websites and online portals

Some scams use dodgy websites done up to look like official government pages, a wolf in sheep's clothing. These sites might:

- Ask ya to log in with yer personal details
- Sting ya fees for services that cost nothin' on the official sites

### How to protect yourself

- Always check the website URL ends in .gov.
- Don't go trustin' a search engine for sign-in links—type the official URL in yerself and double-check the spellin'.

## Social media scams

Scammers knock up fake social media profiles makin' out they're government officials. They might:
- Dangle grants or a bit of financial help in front of ya, so long as ya cough up fees first
- Ask for yer personal or bankin' details through direct messages

### How to protect yourself

- Bear in mind government agencies don't send ya messages out of the blue about benefits.
- Report suss profiles on social media to the social media platform.
