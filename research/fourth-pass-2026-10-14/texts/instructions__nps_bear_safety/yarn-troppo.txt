So a bear's clocked you, and now it's watching you. Here's what you can do to stop things going from bad to worse. These are general tips, mind. Every park's got its own advice, so check it for each park you visit: the local bears don't all carry on the same way, so the advice changes from park to park.

First up, let the bear know what you are by talking to it nice and calm, so it twigs you're a human and not something to eat. Stay still. Stand your ground, but slowly wave your arms. You're helping the bear work out you're a person. It might come closer, or get up on its hind legs for a better look or a better whiff of you. Don't panic if it does: a bear standing up is usually just having a stickybeak, not squaring up to you.

Keep your cool, and remember most bears don't want a bar of attacking you; mostly they just want to be left alone. A bear might bluff its way out of it by charging at you and then peeling off at the last second. It might also get defensive: woofing, yawning, drooling, growling, snapping its jaws and laying its ears back. Keep talking to it in a low voice. That'll help keep you calmer, and it won't rattle the bear. A scream or a sudden move could set off an attack. And never, ever try doing bear noises at it, or let out a high-pitched squeal.

Got littlies with you? Pick them up straight away. Don't make any loud noises or screams: the bear might think it's the sound of a prey animal. Slowly wave your arms above your head and tell the bear to back off. Do NOT run or make any sudden moves. And again, because it matters: don't make any loud noises or screams, cos the bear might think it's the sound of a prey animal.

Hike and travel in a mob. A group of people is usually noisier and smellier than one person on their own (no offence to your mates). That means bears tend to pick up on a group from heaps further off, and with all of you put together, a group's big enough to put a bear off as well.

Make yourselves look as big as you can, say by getting up onto higher ground.

Do NOT let the bear get at your tucker. If it scores a feed off you, that only encourages it, and it makes things worse for whoever comes along after you.

Do NOT drop your pack. It can protect your back, and it stops the bear getting into your food.

If the bear's staying put, move away slowly and sideways. That way you can keep an eye on it and you won't trip over. Moving sideways doesn't threaten a bear either.

Do NOT run. If the bear follows you, stop and hold your ground. A bear can go as fast as a racehorse, uphill or down, so you're not outrunning it. And like dogs, they'll chase anything that bolts.

Do NOT climb a tree. Grizzlies and black bears can both climb, so up a tree is no escape.

Get out of the area or take a detour. If you can't, wait until the bear moves off. Always leave the bear a way out.

Be extra careful if you see a female with cubs. Never get between a mum and her cub, and never try to go up to them. The odds of an attack go through the roof if she reckons you're a danger to her cubs.

Bear Attacks

Bear attacks are rare. Most bears only care about protecting their food, their cubs or their patch. Still, having your head sorted beforehand helps you react the best way you can. Every situation's different, but below is how a brown bear attack can differ from a black bear one. Help look after the next lot by reporting every bear incident to a park ranger straight away. And above all: keep your distance from bears!

Brown/Grizzly Bears: If you're attacked by a brown/grizzly bear, leave your pack on and PLAY DEAD. Lie flat on your stomach with your hands clasped behind your neck. Spread your legs so it's harder for the bear to roll you over. Stay still until the bear's left the area. Fighting back usually makes these attacks worse. But if the attack keeps going, fight back with everything you've got. Grab whatever's on hand and hit the bear in the face.

Black Bears: If you cop an attack from a black bear, DO NOT PLAY DEAD. That's the opposite of the grizzly, so get it straight. Try to get away to somewhere secure, like a car or a building. If you can't get away, fight back with whatever you can lay your hands on. Aim your kicks and punches at the bear's face and muzzle.

And if any bear attacks you in your tent, or stalks you and then attacks, do NOT play dead: fight back!
