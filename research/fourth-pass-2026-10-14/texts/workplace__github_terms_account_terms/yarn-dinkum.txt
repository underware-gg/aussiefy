## B. Account Terms

**Short version:** _Personal Accounts and Organizations get different admin controls; a human has to create your Account; you must be 13 or over; you need to give us a valid email address; and you can't have more than one free Account. You alone are responsible for your Account and anything that happens while you're signed in to it or using it. Keeping your Account secure is your job._

### 1. Account Controls

* Users. Subject to these Terms, you keep ultimate admin control over your Personal Account and the Content in it.

* Organizations. The "owner" of an Organization that was set up under these Terms has ultimate admin control over that Organization and the Content in it. Within the Service, an owner can manage User access to the Organization’s data and projects. An Organization can have more than one owner, but at least one Personal Account has to be named as an owner of an Organization. If you're the owner of an Organization under these Terms, we hold you responsible for whatever gets done on or through that Organization.

### 2. Required Information

You must give us a valid email address to finish signing up. Anything else we ask for, like your real name, is up to you, unless you're accepting these terms on behalf of a legal entity (in which case we need more information about that legal entity) or you go for a [paid Account](#l-payment), in which case we'll need a bit more information for billing.

### 3. Account Requirements

We've got a few simple rules for Accounts on GitHub's Service.
* Only a human can create an Account. Accounts registered by "bots" or other automated methods aren't allowed. We do allow machine accounts, though:
* A machine account is an Account set up by an individual human who accepts the Terms on behalf of the Account, gives a valid email address, and is responsible for what it does. A machine account is used only for running automated tasks. More than one user can direct what a machine account does, but at the end of the day the owner of the Account is responsible for what the machine does. You can keep no more than one free machine account on top of your free Personal Account.
* One person or legal entity can keep no more than one free Account (if you want to run a machine account as well, no worries, but it can only be used for running a machine).
* You've got to be 13 or older. We're stoked to see bright young coders get keen on learning to program, but we have to comply with United States law. GitHub doesn't aim our Service at kids under 13, and we don't allow any Users under 13 on our Service. If we find out about any User under the age of 13, we'll [terminate that User’s Account immediately](#m-cancellation-and-termination). If you live in a country outside the United States, your country's minimum age might be older; if so, it's on you to comply with your country's laws.
* Your login can only be used by one person — i.e., a single login can't be shared by a few people. A paid Organization can only give access to as many Personal Accounts as your subscription allows.
* You can't use GitHub in breach of the export control or sanctions laws of the United States or any other applicable jurisdiction. You can't use GitHub if you are, or are working on behalf of, a [Specially Designated National (SDN)](https://www.treasury.gov/resource-center/sanctions/SDN-List/Pages/default.aspx) or a person subject to similar blocking or denied party prohibitions administered by a U.S. government agency. GitHub may let persons in certain sanctioned countries or territories access certain GitHub services under U.S. government authorisations. For more info, have a squiz at our [Export Controls policy](/site-policy/other-site-policies/github-and-trade-controls).
