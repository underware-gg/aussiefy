“Rev. Leonidas W. H'm, Reverend Le--well, there was a feller here once, name of Jim Smiley, in the winter of '49--or maybe it was the spring of '50--I can't rightly remember, though what makes me reckon it was one or the other is I remember the big flume wasn't finished when he first rolled up to the camp; but anyway, he was the curiousest cove you ever saw for bettin' on anythin' that lobbed, if he could get anyone to take the other side; and if he couldn't, he'd switch sides. Any way that suited the other bloke suited HIM--any way at all, so long as he got a bet, he was happy as Larry. And still he was a lucky bugger, flamin' lucky; he nearly always come out on top. He was always up for it and waitin' for his chance; there couldn't be one solit'ry thing mentioned but that feller'd offer to bet on it, and take whatever side ya like, like I was just tellin' ya. If there was a horse race, you'd find him cashed up or you'd find him stony broke at the end of it; if there was a dog fight, he'd bet on it; if there was a cat fight, he'd bet on it; if there was a chook fight, he'd bet on it; strewth, if there was two birds sittin' on a fence, he'd bet ya which one'd fly off first; or if there was a camp meeting, he'd be there reg'lar to bet on Parson Walker, who he reckoned was the best preacher round here, and so he was too, and a good man. If he so much as saw a straddle-bug set off anywhere, he'd bet ya how long it'd take him to get to--to wherever he was headed, and if ya took him on, he'd foller that straddle-bug all the way to Mexico to find out where he was bound for and how long he was on the frog and toad. Heaps of the blokes here have seen that Smiley, and can tell ya about him. Well, it never made a skerrick of difference to him--he'd bet on anything--the dangdest feller. Parson Walker's missus was laid up bloody crook once, for a fair while, and it looked like they weren't gonna pull her through; but one mornin' he come in, and Smiley up and asked him how she was goin', and he goes she was a good deal better--thank the Lord for his inf'nite mercy--and pickin' up that quick that with the blessin' of Prov'dence she'd come good yet; and Smiley, without thinkin', goes, 'Well, I'll risk two-and-a-half she don't, anyway.'

“Now, this Smiley had a mare--the boys called her the fifteen-minute nag, but that was only a joke, ya know, cos of course she was quicker than that--and he used to win the foldin' stuff on that horse, for all she was slow as a funeral and always had the asthma, or the distemper, or the consumption, or somethin' like that. They used to give her two or three hundred yards' start, and then go past her on the way; but always at the tail end of the race she'd get all het up and desperate, like, and come cavortin' and straddlin' up, flingin' her legs every which way, sometimes up in the air, and sometimes out to one side among the fences, and kickin' up m-o-r-e dust and makin' m-o-r-e of a racket with her coughin' and sneezin' and blowin' her nose--and always pull up at the stand just about a neck in front, near as you could work it out.
