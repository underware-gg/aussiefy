yarn-troppo: A rich, natural level-5 retelling that keeps Twain's dialect and every detail; a couple of nudging asides and stock phrases are its only weak spots.
  minor: "To the parson's face! True story." (added): Added aside spells out the joke; Twain's narrator never notices it is funny.
  minor: 'on the frog and toad' (for 'on the road'): Cockney rhyming slang reads as stock Aussie pastiche; rarely heard in speech.
  minor: 'no worries' (for "and if he couldn't he'd change sides"): Stock phrase bolted onto the line.

yarn-dinkum: A faithful, natural retelling that keeps Twain's voice; slightly light on the vulgarity the brief allows.
  minor: 'how long he was on the frog and toad' (for 'how long he was on the road'): Rhyming slang reads as stock pastiche.
  minor: "lucky bugger, flamin' lucky" (for 'lucky, uncommon lucky'): Swearing stays light for a vulgarity-2 brief; fine, but under the weight asked.

unharnessed: Fluent, properly vulgar modern Aussie retelling with every point kept, but it strips Twain's dialect and run-on shape, and slips in an Americanism.
  major: 'he was lucky as all get-out' (for 'uncommon lucky'): 'All get-out' is American, not Australian.
  minor: 'Nearly always came out on top.' (for 'he most always come out winner'): Clipped, standardised grammar drops the narrator's dialect; representative of the whole text.
  minor: 'the mad bastard' (for 'the dangdest feller'): Replaces the signature dialect word.

dinkum-3: Clean and faithful with natural Aussie, but plainer than the brief and it irons out Twain's dialect.
  minor: "strangest bloke goin'" (for 'the dangdest feller'): Loses the signature dialect word and repeats 'strangest bloke' from earlier.
  minor: "she doesn't anyway" (for "she don't anyway"): Standardises the narrator's grammar, flattening the dialect in the punchline.
  minor: 'thank the Lord for his endless mercy' (for "thank the Lord for his inf'nite mercy"): Needless change to a fixed pious phrase.
  minor: 'a good bloke with it' (for 'and a good man'): 'bloke' leaned on throughout.

engine: Twain's text with Aussie tags pasted in; several land in the wrong sense or place, and the tone jars.
  major: "ya flamin' mongrel" (for "and if he couldn't he'd change sides"): An insult addressed to no one; changes the friendly narrator into an abusive one.
  major: 'and a dead-set legend, mate' (for 'and a good man'): Wrong register for praising a preacher; changes 'good man' into a youth-slang compliment.
  major: "for a fuckin' good while" (for 'for a good while'): Swearing about the parson's wife's illness; an Australian would wince at it here.
  major: 'before he reckoned' (for 'before he thought'): Reckon means to suppose; the sense of acting before thinking is lost.
  major: "she get rapt'n desperate like" (for 'she get excited and desperate like'): Rapt means delighted, not excited; wrong sense.
  minor: 'the young fellas called her' (for 'the boys called her'): Shifts 'the boys' (the camp men) to young men.
  major: "cobber. Stone the flamin' crows." (added): Stock stage-Australian tags bolted on at the end.
  minor: 'the bloody big flume' (for 'the big flume'): Swear dropped in where it adds nothing.
