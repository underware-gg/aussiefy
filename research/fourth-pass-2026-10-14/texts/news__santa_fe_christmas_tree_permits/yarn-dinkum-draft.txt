Chrissy tree permits will be up for grabs online through Recreation.gov and in person at all Santa Fe National Forest District Offices and the Supervisor's Office from November 13, 2025. Details on the designated cutting areas, dates, and the types of trees you're allowed to cut can be found here.

Getting your Christmas Tree permit:

Online through Recreation.gov: search for Santa Fe National Forest Christmas Tree Permits. It's important to have a careful read of the overview and the need-to-know information before you buy the permit. Visitors will also need to set up or log in to a Recreation.gov account to finish the transaction.

In person at the Supervisor's Office in Santa Fe, N.M., or the Jemez, Cuba, Coyote, Española, and Pecos/Las Vegas District Offices. Get in touch with us for current hours.

Permits will be sold in person at the Walatowa Visitor Center on Saturdays only, from 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., from November 22 through to December 20, 2024.

Pre-cut Christmas Trees might be available to buy at district offices. That depends on the weather and on supply, and priority goes to people who can't cut their own tree.

The SFNF's Christmas tree permits are $10 and non-refundable. Permits bought online through recreation.gov come with an extra $2.50 service fee. One tag covers a tree up to 5 inches (13 cm) across and 10 feet (3 m) tall. Trees taller than 10 feet and/or wider than 5 inches need an extra tag. For example, a 15-foot (4.6 m) tree needs two tags.

Fourth graders (Year 4 kids) with an Every Kid Outdoors pass can get a free Christmas tree permit, and can apply by entering the pass or voucher number when they buy a permit (note: a $2.50 service fee will still apply).

Cutting a Chrissy tree also does the forest's health a favour. The permit system helps thin out crowded stands of small-diameter trees. Local forest health experts pick out areas that'd benefit from thinning, and those trees tend to be just the right size for Christmas trees. Taking them out of the designated areas helps the other trees grow bigger and can open up areas with feed for wildlife.
