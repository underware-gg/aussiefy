# FOLD Token

So, what's FOLD actually for in the Interfold network? That's what this FAQ is here to sort out.
It's for token holders, and for anyone who reckons they'd like to operate a ciphernode. The questions
come in three lots: the role of FOLD, transferability, and ciphernode operation. If it's supply and
allocation data you're after, that lives over at [FOLD Tokenomics](/tokenomics).

## The role of FOLD

### What role does FOLD play in the Interfold network?

Righto, the big picture first. The Interfold is a distributed network for confidential coordination.
Applications put their hand up for Encrypted Execution Environments (E3s), and the ciphernodes are the
ones that coordinate and secure each computation.

Where FOLD comes in is the money side: it keeps the economic operation of the network running.

### What is the utility of the FOLD token?

FOLD's got three main jobs, and they're all tangled up with each other: ciphernode bonding, protocol
incentives, and governance.

**Ciphernode bonding.** If you're a ciphernode operator, you bond FOLD to get a shot at network duties. Bonded
FOLD sits tight in the `BondingRegistry` contract. When the bond owner (the wallet that funds the bond)
unbonds it, the FOLD has to wait its turn in an exit queue before the bond owner can claim it. Bonded
FOLD may be subject to protocol-defined withdrawal periods. It can also cop penalties for provable
misbehaviour, or for not pulling your weight on the responsibilities you've been assigned. The whole
point: if you're the one running the show, it's your own dosh on the line if you muck it up.

**Protocol incentives.** Now for the good bit. Ciphernode operators get a cut of the fees from
completed E3s. And on top of that, they may score FOLD incentives for successfully getting through
protocol-defined duties as members of an E3 committee.

**Governance.** FOLD is expected to get holders a say in governance through the Interfold DAO.
That could mean calls on network parameters, protocol upgrades, and how the treasury gets run. The
governance processes may even include secret-ballot mechanisms powered by the Interfold network
itself, so the network's backing its own gear. For the current voting rules, see
[Governance](/governance).

## Transferability

### When did FOLD become generally transferable?

The gate could open, so to speak, at or after Aug 19 at **14:00 UTC (10 AM ET)**: that's when general FOLD
transferability could be enabled.

Here's how it worked. At or after Unix timestamp **`1787148000`**, anyone at all could call the
`tge()` function on the FOLD contract to enable general transferability. First one to do it flicked
the switch: once that transaction executed, FOLD became generally transferable.

And somebody was quick off the mark. That transaction ran on Aug 19, 2026 at 14:00:23 UTC, a whole
23 seconds in. `tgeTimestamp()` on the FOLD contract returns `1787148023`, so yep, FOLD is generally
transferable now.

Just don't mix it up with Network Alpha. General FOLD transferability and Network Alpha are two
separate milestones in the bigger Interfold launch sequence. For the full rundown, see
[FOLD Transferability](/faq/transferability).

## Ciphernode operation

### How can I use FOLD to operate a ciphernode?

Good news: operating a ciphernode is designed to be permissionless, so you don't need anyone's
tick of approval. The setup's got four parts:

1. Provision cloud-based or personal hardware.
2. Run the ciphernode software.
3. Bond FOLD to the ciphernode.
4. Complete on-chain registration.

Once your ciphernode's registered, it can get stuck into E3 committees whenever the protocol rules
pick it. But being registered isn't the whole story: to be active for committee selection, a
registered ciphernode also needs a minimum ticket balance. See
[Tickets and sortition](/operate/tickets-and-sortition).

For the current setup requirements, bonding instructions, and operator documentation, the place to
go is [Ciphernode Operators](/operate) and [Registration and bonding](/operate/registration).

### Can I delegate my FOLD to a ciphernode operator?

Nah, sorry. The Interfold's bonding model is operator-only, and that's no accident. Every FOLD that's
bonded to a ciphernode is capital at risk for the operator that runs it: their skin in the game.

Let people delegate, and operators could end up running ciphernodes they only half own, playing
with other people's money. That'd water down accountability and weaken the network's economic
security guarantees.

So in the `BondingRegistry` contract, each ciphernode has one bond owner, and only the bond owner
can bond FOLD to it. The operator decides who the bond owner is. It can be the operator address, or a
separate wallet if you'd rather keep things apart, say a cold wallet or a Safe.
