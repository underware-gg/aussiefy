# FOLD Token

This FAQ runs through what FOLD does in the Interfold network. It's written for token holders, and
for anyone keen to run a ciphernode. The questions fall into three groups: the role of FOLD,
transferability, and ciphernode operation. If you're after supply and allocation data, see
[FOLD Tokenomics](/tokenomics).

## The role of FOLD

### What role does FOLD play in the Interfold network?

The Interfold is a distributed network for confidential coordination. Applications ask for Encrypted
Execution Environments (E3s), and ciphernodes coordinate and secure each computation.

FOLD is what keeps the economic side of the network ticking over.

### What is the utility of the FOLD token?

FOLD does three main jobs, and they all hang together: ciphernode bonding, protocol incentives, and
governance.

**Ciphernode bonding.** Ciphernode operators bond FOLD so they're eligible for network duties. Bonded
FOLD sits in the `BondingRegistry` contract. When the bond owner (the wallet that funds the bond)
unbonds it, the FOLD waits its turn in an exit queue before the bond owner can claim it. Bonded FOLD
may be subject to protocol-defined withdrawal periods. It can also cop penalties for provable
misbehaviour, or for not pulling its weight on assigned responsibilities. Put together, these
mechanisms tie operational responsibility to economic accountability.

**Protocol incentives.** Ciphernode operators get a share of the fees from completed E3s. On top of
that, they may pick up FOLD incentives for successfully completing protocol-defined duties as members
of an E3 committee.

**Governance.** FOLD is expected to back governance participation through the Interfold DAO.
Governance might cover decisions on network parameters, protocol upgrades, and treasury
administration, and its processes may include secret-ballot mechanisms that the Interfold network
itself powers. For the current voting rules, see [Governance](/governance).

## Transferability

### When did FOLD become generally transferable?

General FOLD transferability could be switched on at or after Aug 19 at **14:00 UTC (10 AM ET)**.

From Unix timestamp **`1787148000`** on, anyone could call the `tge()` function on the FOLD
contract to switch on general transferability. Once that transaction went through, FOLD was
generally transferable.

As it turned out, that transaction ran on Aug 19, 2026 at 14:00:23 UTC. `tgeTimestamp()` on the FOLD
contract returns `1787148023`, so FOLD is generally transferable now.

Mind you, general FOLD transferability and Network Alpha are two separate milestones in the wider
Interfold launch sequence. For more on that, see [FOLD Transferability](/faq/transferability).

## Ciphernode operation

### How can I use FOLD to operate a ciphernode?

Running a ciphernode is meant to be permissionless. There are four parts to the setup:

1. Provision cloud-based or personal hardware.
2. Run the ciphernode software.
3. Bond FOLD to the ciphernode.
4. Complete on-chain registration.

Once your ciphernode's registered, it can take part in E3 committees whenever the protocol rules pick
it. To be active for committee selection, though, a registered ciphernode also needs a minimum ticket
balance. See [Tickets and sortition](/operate/tickets-and-sortition).

For the current setup requirements, bonding instructions and operator documentation, have a squiz at
[Ciphernode Operators](/operate) and [Registration and bonding](/operate/registration).

### Can I delegate my FOLD to a ciphernode operator?

Nah, nah. The Interfold's bonding model is operator-only, and that's on purpose. Every FOLD bonded to
a ciphernode is capital at risk for the operator that runs it.

Delegated capital would let operators run part-owned ciphernodes, which would water down
accountability and weaken the network's economic security guarantees.

In the `BondingRegistry` contract, each ciphernode has one bond owner, and only the bond owner can
bond FOLD to it. The operator sets the bond owner. The bond owner can be the operator address or a
separate wallet, say a cold wallet or a Safe.
