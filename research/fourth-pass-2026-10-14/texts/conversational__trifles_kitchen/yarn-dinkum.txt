SHERIFF: Sweet bugger-all here but kitchen stuff.

(_The_ COUNTY ATTORNEY, _after another squiz round the kitchen, opens the door of a cupboard closet. He gets up on a chair and has a look on a shelf. Pulls his hand away, sticky_.)

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Here's a beaut mess.

(_The women come in a bit closer_.)

MRS PETERS: (_to the other woman_) Oh, her fruit; it did freeze, (_to the_ LAWYER) She was worried about that when it turned so cold. Said the fire'd go out and her jars'd crack.

SHERIFF: Well, can you beat the women! Locked up for murder and fussin' about her preserves.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: I'd say before we're done she might have something a fair bit heavier than preserves to worry about.

HALE: Well, women are used to stewin' over trifles.

(_The two women shift a little closer together_.)

COUNTY ATTORNEY: (_with all the charm of a young pollie_) And yet, for all their worries, what would we do without the ladies? (_the women don't thaw. He goes to the sink, takes a dipperful of water from the pail and, tipping it into a basin, washes his mitts. Starts to wipe them on the roller-towel, turns it for a cleaner bit_) Filthy towels! (_kicks his foot against the pans under the sink_) Not much chop as a housekeeper, would you say, ladies?

MRS HALE: (_stiffly_) There's heaps of work to be done on a farm.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Fair enough. And yet (_with a little bow to her_) I know there's some Dickson county farmhouses that don't have towels like that. (_He gives it a tug to show the length of it again_.)

MRS HALE: Those towels get grotty awful quick. Men's hands aren't always as clean as they might be.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Ah, sticking up for your own, I see. But you and Mrs Wright were neighbours. I reckon you were mates, too.

MRS HALE: (_shaking her head_) I've not seen much of her these last few years. I've not been in this house--it's more than a year.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: And why's that? You didn't like her?

MRS HALE: I liked her well enough. Farmers' wives have their hands full, Mr Henderson. And then--

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Yeah--?

MRS HALE: (_looking about_) It never seemed a very cheerful sort of place.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: No--it's not cheerful. I wouldn't say she had a knack for keeping house.

MRS HALE: Well, I dunno as Wright had, either.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: You mean they didn't get on too flamin' well?

MRS HALE: Yeah, but nah, I don't mean anything. But I don't reckon a place'd be any cheerier for having John Wright in it.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: I'd like to have a bit more of a chat about that later on. I want to get the lay of things upstairs now. (_He goes to the left, where three steps lead up to a stair door_.)

SHERIFF: S'pose anything Mrs Peters does'll be right. She was to take in some clothes for her, you know, and a few little bits and pieces. We cleared out in such a hurry yesterday.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Yeah, but I'd like to see what you take, Mrs Peters, and keep an eye out for anything that might be some use to us.

MRS PETERS: Yes, Mr Henderson.

(_The women listen to the men's footsteps on the stairs, then look about the kitchen_.)

MRS HALE: I'd hate to have blokes traipsing into my kitchen, sticking their noses in and picking holes in everything.

(_She straightens up the pans under the sink that the_ LAWYER _had shoved out of place_.)

MRS PETERS: Course, it's no more than their duty.

MRS HALE: Duty's all right, but I'd bet that deputy sheriff that came out to light the fire might've got a bit of this on. (_gives the roller towel a tug_) Wish I'd thought of that sooner. Seems a bit rough to have a go at her for not having things spick and span when she had to clear out in such a hurry.
