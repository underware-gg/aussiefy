SHERIFF: Sweet bugger-all here but kitchen things.

(_The_ COUNTY ATTORNEY, _after again looking around the kitchen, opens the door of a cupboard closet. Crikey, he gets up on a chair and looks on a shelf. Anyway, Pulls his hand away, sticky_.)

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Here's a beaut mess.

(_The women draw nearer_.)

MRS PETERS: (_to the other woman_) Oh, her fruit; it did freeze, (_to the_ LAWYER) She worried about that when it turned so cold. She said the fire'd go out and her jars would break.

SHERIFF: Well, can you beat the women! Held for murder and worryin' about her preserves.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: I guess before we're through she may have something more serious than preserves to worry about.

HALE: Well, women are used to worrying over trifles.

(_The two women move a little closer together_.)

COUNTY ATTORNEY: (_with the gallantry of a young pollie_) And yet, for all their worries, what would we do without the ladies, mate? (_the women don't unbend. He goes to the sink, takes a dipperful of water from the pail and pouring it into a basin, washes his mitts. Starts to wipe them on the roller-towel, turns it for a cleaner place_) Dirty towels! (_kicks his foot against the pans under the sink_) bugger all of a housekeeper, would you say, ladies, ay?

MRS HALE: (_stiffly_) There's heaps of work to be done on a farm.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: To be sure. And yet (_with a little bow to her_) I know there are some Dickson county farmhouses which do not have such roller towels. (_He gives it a pull to expose its length again_.)

MRS HALE: Those towels get grotty bloody awful quick. Men's mitts aren't always as clean as they might be.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Ah, loyal to your sex, I see. But you and Mrs Wright were neighbours. I reckon you were mates, too.

MRS HALE: (_shaking her head_) I've not seen much of her of late years. Anyway, I've not been in this house--it's more than a year.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: And why was that? You didn't like her?

MRS HALE: I liked her all well enough. Farmers' wives have their mitts full, Mr Henderson. And then--

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Yes--?

MRS HALE: (_looking about_) It never seemed a very cheerful place.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: No--it's not cheerful. I shouldn't say she had the homemaking instinct.

MRS HALE: Well, I dunno as Wright had, either.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: You mean that they didn't get on flamin' well?

MRS HALE: Yeah, but nah, I don't mean anything. But I don't reckon a place'd be any cheerfuller for John Wright's being in it.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: I'd like to talk more of that a little later. I want to get the lay of things upstairs now. (_He goes to the left, where three steps lead to a stair door_.)

SHERIFF: I reckon anything Mrs Peters does'll be all right. She was to take in some clothes for her, you know, and a few little things. Now, we left in such a hurry yesterday.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Yeah, but I would like to see what you take, Mrs Peters, and keep an eye out for anything that might be of use to us.

MRS PETERS: Yeah, Mr Henderson.

(_The women listen to the men's steps on the stairs, then look about the kitchen_.)

MRS HALE: I'd hate to have blokes coming into my bloody kitchen, snooping around and criticising.

(_She arranges the pans under sink which the_ LAWYER _had shoved out of place_.)

MRS PETERS: Of course it's no more than their duty.

MRS HALE: Duty's all right, but I guess that deputy sheriff that came out to make the fire might have got a little of this on. (_gives the roller towel a pull_) Wish I'd thought of that sooner. Anyway, seems mean to talk about her for not having things slicked up when she had to come away in such a hurry.
