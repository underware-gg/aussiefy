So get this. Friday's whopping winter storm over the northeastern United States has gone and flattened a 227-year-old tree on George Washington's patch. That's Mount Vernon, in Virginia, the estate of the first US President George Washington himself. As of yesterday, the mob looking after Mount Vernon reckoned the Canadian hemlock's been picked up and kept, for preservation and a few special projects. Not a splinter wasted.

Now, this hemlock went in back in 1791, and it stood there through the lot until Friday, when gusts of reportedly up to 70 mph (about 110 kph) knocked the old fella flat.

Bit of history for ya. Mount Vernon was Washington's own place, and he ran most of his two terms as president out of it between 1789–1797, cos the first White House wasn't even finished yet. Imagine running the country from the back paddock. According to the Mount Vernon officials, the hemlock's the best documented tree on the whole estate. It was a pressie from New York Governor George Clinton, and it got delivered in a whisky barrel, of all things. And nah, the New York governor's no relation to the 42nd President Bill Clinton, before ya ask. Mind you, George Clinton did go on to become the United States's Vice President, under the Thomas Jefferson and James Madison administrations, between 1805–1812. Not a bad run, that.

And it's not the only famous old tree to cark it lately, either. The seventh President Andrew Jackson's southern magnolia at the White House has come down, and so has the Pioneer Cabin giant sequoia in California's Calaveras Big Trees State Park, the one known for the car-sized tunnel cut right through the trunk. Rough few years for the big old trees over there.
