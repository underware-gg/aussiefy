yarn-dinkum: The most even rendering: faithful, natural and varied, keeping the obituary's measured tone.
  minor: 'He wrote heaps on moral questions.' (for 'He was a prolific writer on moral questions.'): A little flat beside the rest; fine but plain.

dinkum-3: A natural, faithful obituary in an Aussie voice; restrained, slightly light for level 4.
  minor: 'well known right across the country' (for 'widely known'): Adds a scope the original does not state.

yarn-troppo: A lively, faithful yarn, but its gossipy frame sits uneasily on an obituary.
  major: 'Listen to this. Dr. Wilbur F. Crafts has died.' (for 'Dr. Wilbur F. Crafts ... died at a hospital here today of pneumonia.'): A gossipy opener on a death notice; tone made flippant.
  minor: 'Racy! From him!' (added): Plays up the dry note into a gag.
  minor: 'Fair dinkum, among the reform leaders' (for 'Among reform leaders'): Stock opener bolted on.

unharnessed: Vivid and varied, but heavier than an obituary bears and dismissive in places.
  major: 'barracking for prohibition and that sort of carry-on' (for 'his activities on behalf of prohibition and similar movements'): "carry-on" is dismissive; the obituary takes no side.
  major: 'He wrote a bloody mountain of stuff on moral questions.' (for 'He was a prolific writer on moral questions.'): Swearing and flippancy in an obituary's factual line.
  minor: 'who was his offsider in heaps of his many goings-on' (for 'his partner in many of his numerous activities'): "offsider" makes his partner an assistant; "goings-on" is dismissive.
  minor: 'His pen and his gob barely got a breather' (for 'His pen and his voice were allowed little rest'): "gob" is crude for an obituary.

engine: Essentially the original with three swaps, one of them misleading; far under the brief.
  major: 'Sunday baseball, darts were a few of the objects' (for 'Sunday baseball, cigarettes were a few of the objects'): In a list of amusements "darts" reads as the pub game, not cigarettes.
  minor: "he spent in his old man's church" (for "he spent in his father's church"): Slang dropped into formal 1922 prose; jarring.
  major: 'Dr. Crafts, charged by opponents of legislation he sponsored with being a "reform fanatic,"' (for 'Dr. Crafts, charged by opponents of legislation he sponsored with being a "reform fanatic,"'): Left untranslated, far under level 4.
