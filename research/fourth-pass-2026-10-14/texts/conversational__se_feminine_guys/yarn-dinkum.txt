So what's the feminine version of 'guys'?

I use the word 'guys' all the time for a mob of blokes, casual like. It's casual, yeah, but it's not rude or off-puttin', and it's not condescendin' or patronisin' either (mind you, I wouldn't use it on a group of blokes at a board meetin' (hypothetically, of course) unless I knew 'em). So the ones I wouldn't count as swaps are

- buddies: too old-fashioned

- dudes: too informal

- boys: too patronisin'

- men, gentlemen: too formal, or the respect's a bit put on

Then I twigged I'd been usin' it for any mob of people at all, males 'n females, even just females. It worked for me 'n I never gave the gender side of it a thought, that women mightn't be keen on it. No bugger ever whinged, not to me face and not behind me back (rule of life: sometimes people don't whinge about stuff they bloody well should).

But it dawned on me (maybe I'd read somethin' that got me thinkin') that the people I was sayin' it to mightn't all be keen on it.

So what'd be a female gendered or non-gendered version of 'guys'?

Here's some I've had a think about that don't sit right with me (though fair enough, they might be decent answers):

- dolls: too old-fashioned

- babes: too informal

- ladies: too formal, or, so I've heard from women, too creepy

- women: too factual

- girls: too patronisin' (as much as 'boys')

- everyone, you all: too bland

- y'all: not sexist one bit, but too regional (might work, but not everywhere)

Hopefully I haven't ruled out every option -- got any ideas?

“Guys” can be used in English as gender neutral, for a mixed mob.

You'll even hear women callin' other women “guys.”

The closest thing goin' with a feminine tilt would be “gals.” “Guys and gals” is a fair bit more casual take on “ladies and gentlemen.” (Note it's back to front.)

---

Edit: As @kitukwfyer pointed out in the question comments, “gals” can come across as a put-down when it's used on its own, so go easy. It's not likely to be a problem in the pairin' above. Used carefully it's prob'ly safe, but on its own it could be taken as a piss-take.

Sayin' this is unlikely to get anyone's back up:

> “The gals talked in the kitchen while the guys went out to see Fred’s brand-spankin'-new truck.”

But if you rock up to a couple of women on a street corner and say this, you might get a funny look:

> “Excuse me gals, where is the supermarket?”

Say this to a couple of girls and they might reckon you're dodgy:

> “Hey gals come with me.”

But if you're explainin' the rules of some game and say this, it'll prob'ly be fine:

> “Alright, guys on this side of the room, gals on the other.”
