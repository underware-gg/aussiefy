Okay, so. What's the feminine version of 'guys'?

I chuck 'guys' around all day long for a mob of blokes, casual as. It's casual, yeah, but it's not rude, it's not off-puttin', and it's not condescendin' or patronisin' neither (mind you, I wouldn't front up to a pack of blokes at a board meetin' and go "G'day guys" (hypothetically, course), not unless I knew 'em). So here's the ones I wouldn't have a bar of as swaps:

- buddies: too old-fashioned, like somethin' outta your grandad's war stories

- dudes: too informal

- boys: too patronisin'

- men, gentlemen: too formal, or the respect's laid on with a trowel

Then it hit me like a cricket ball to the noggin: I'd been usin' it for any mob of people at all, males 'n females, even a mob of just females. Worked a treat for me, 'n I never gave the gender side of it a second's thought, that women mightn't be all that keen on it. Mind you, no bugger ever whinged, not to me face and not behind me back (rule of life: sometimes people don't whinge about stuff they bloody well should).

But it dawned on me (maybe I'd read somethin' that switched the light on up top) that the people I was sayin' it to mightn't all be keen on it.

So what's the go? What'd be a female gendered or non-gendered version of 'guys'?

Here's the ones I've had a crack at that just don't sit right with me (though fair enough, they might be decent answers):

- dolls: too old-fashioned

- babes: too informal

- ladies: too formal, or, so I've heard from women, too creepy by half

- women: too factual

- girls: too patronisin' (every bit as bad as 'boys')

- everyone, you all: too bland, like a sausage sizzle with no sauce

- y'all: not sexist one bit, but too regional (might work, but not everywhere)

Hopefully I haven't knocked out every last option -- anyone got any ideas?

Right, here's the thing. “Guys” can be used in English as gender neutral, for a mixed mob.

You'll even hear women callin' other women “guys.” Happens all the time.

The closest thing goin' with a feminine tilt would be “gals.” “Guys and gals” is a fair bit more knockabout version of “ladies and gentlemen.” (Note it's back to front, too.)

---

Edit: As @kitukwfyer flagged in the question comments, “gals” can come across as a put-down when it's used on its own, so tread careful, mate. It's not likely to be a problem in the pairin' above. Used carefully it's prob'ly safe as houses, but on its own it could land as a piss-take.

Say this and you're not gonna get anyone's back up:

> “The gals talked in the kitchen while the guys went out to see Fred’s brand-spankin'-new truck.”

But rock up to a couple of women on a street corner and say this, and you might cop a very funny look:

> “Excuse me gals, where is the supermarket?”

Say this to a couple of girls and they'll reckon you're dodgy as:

> “Hey gals come with me.”

But if you're explainin' the rules of some game and you say this, you'll be right:

> “Alright, guys on this side of the room, gals on the other.”
