Where do I book a bed in Yellowstone?
So get this: there's nine lodges in the park, and Yellowstone National Park Lodges runs the lot. They're all open from late spring right through autumn, but come winter only two keep the lights on: Old Faithful Snow Lodge and Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel. Get your booking in early, mate, or you'll miss out!

Do I need a booking just to get in the gate?
Nah, you don't need a vehicle reservation to get into the park – just a park entrance pass. Do yourself a favour and buy your pass online before you lob, and you'll skip a fair bit of mucking about at the entry stations. And keep in mind summer's flat chat, so be ready for crowds at the popular spots and queues at the entrance gates, through the roadworks, and anywhere a bison's wandered up to the road and every car's pulled over for a squiz. Please be patient and enjoy your visit. It's worth the wait, I reckon.

How did Yellowstone get its name?
Now, this one's a ripper. Yellowstone National Park's named after the Yellowstone River, the main river running through the park. Back in the 1800s, according to some French-Canadian trappers, they asked the Minnetaree tribe, who live in what's now eastern Montana, what the river was called. The answer came back “Mi tse a-da-zi,” which word for word is “Rock Yellow River.” The trappers turned that into French as “Roche Jaune” or “Pierre Jaune.” Then in 1797 explorer-geographer David Thompson went with the English version—“Yellow Stone.” Lewis and Clark had a bob each way and called the Yellowstone River by both the French and the English names. After that, folks just kept saying it till it was official: “Yellowstone.”

Is Yellowstone the biggest national park?
Nah, nah, not even close. More than half of Alaska’s national park units are bigger, including Wrangell–St. Elias National Park and Preserve, the biggest unit in the whole National Park System at a whopping 13 million acres. Until 1994, Yellowstone (at 2.2 million acres) was the biggest national park in the contiguous United States. Then that year Death Valley National Monument got a bit added on and became a national park—it's got more than 3 million acres. Pipped at the post.

Is Yellowstone the most visited national park?
Yellowstone's up there in the top five national parks for number of recreational visitors, but Great Smoky Mountains National Park often pulls the biggest crowd. Have a squiz at https://irma.nps.gov/Stats/ for up-to-date figures on all the national parks.

What's the difference between a national park and a national forest?
National parks are run by the Department of the Interior, and national forests by the Department of Agriculture. The National Park Service has to keep resources unimpaired, by law: look but don't touch. The Forest Service, by law, has to manage resources wisely for plenty of sustainable uses. And there's six national forests all round Yellowstone National Park.

How many rangers work in Yellowstone?
About 750 people work for the National Park Service in the peak summer season, and about 392 of them are permanent, year-round staff. Park rangers do the whole box and dice: education, resource management, law enforcement, emergency medical services, and backcountry operations. Other staff specialise in research, maintenance, management, administration, trail maintenance, fire management, and fee collection.

What's the highest peak in the park?
Eagle Peak, down in the southeast of Yellowstone, is the highest, at 11,358 feet.

Why is Yellowstone called a biosphere reserve and a world heritage site?
The United Nations named Yellowstone National Park a biosphere reserve and a world heritage site in recognition of how much its natural and cultural resources matter to the whole world. Not a bad gong, that.

What's the Continental Divide?
Think of the Continental Divide as the ridgepole of the continent, like the peak of a tent roof. In theory, when rain or snow falls on the west side of the Divide, it ends up in the Pacific Ocean. When it falls on the east side of the Divide, it ends up in the Atlantic Ocean. In Yellowstone (same as everywhere else), this ridgeline's not straight, it wiggles about. You cross the Continental Divide three times between the South Entrance and the Old Faithful area. Craig Pass is the highest crossing, at 8,262 feet.

How did Mt. Washburn form?
At 10,219 feet, this peak can be seen from heaps of spots in the park. It's what's left of an extinct volcano from the Absaroka Volcanics of about 50 million years ago. And get this: the volcano was literally cut in half by a volcanic eruption 631,000 years ago. Only the northern half of the original volcano's still standing where you can see it.

Does Yellowstone include a federally designated wilderness?
