KEY MESSAGE 2...Isolated shower possible Wednesday, but drier overall. Dry conditions return for Thursday with seasonably cool temperatures.

A trailing cold front shifts across the region Wednesday with surface high pressure building in from the west through the day. Model soundings show increased mixing in the boundary layer supporting drier conditions and also westerly winds with gusts 20-30 mph. Westerly, downslope flow combined with 925mb temperatures +17- 18C will support a warm fall day with high temperatures in the 70s in many areas. There are indications of a stray shower or two as a weak wave moves through, but this will be fairly limited in coverage and at most a light shower/sprinkle due to the drier lower levels.

As high pressure settles in further into Thursday, the airmass dries out even more. Thursday will likely be dry, although cooler than Wednesday. A cooler airmass will also have moved in with 925mb temperatures around 10C cooler than Wednesday. This will yield seasonably cool temperatures mainly in the lower 60s.

KEY MESSAGE 3...Low pressure system expected to bring rain Friday into early Saturday. Gusty northwest winds and below normal temperatures.

Deterministic and ensemble guidance show a stronger/deeper northern creek shortwave trough push across the Great Lakes and the northeast Friday into Saturday. There's synoptic support with this system as an upper level jet positions near southern New England yielding large scale ascent while also advecting in a plume of above normal moisture northward into the region. This will set the stage for a widespread rain. Despite plentiful moisture, instability appears to be limited thus minimising the potential for heavy rain impacts. Ensemble guidance ranges amounts generally from 0.25 to 0.60" with the ECMWF and Canadian leaning wetter than the GFS solutions. Across those particular models, amounts closer to an inch are possible. These higher amounts aren't out of the question if we can tap into better forcing with the warm front. Finer details such as exact timing of arrival and exit of rain are less certain as there are difference across guidance still. Rain more likely arrives in the Friday morning timeframe, continuing overnight. Most guidance shows this system to be fairly progressive meaning it won`t be lingering the whole weekend. The bulk of the rain likely exits early Saturday morning. In addition, the initial southerly LLJ with this system nudges in Friday which if mixed sufficiently, will bring breezy to gusty southerly winds (gusts 20-30 mph).

A strong cold front pushes across the region by early Saturday morning putting the northeast within a classic cold air advection pattern with gusty NW winds, cooler temperatures, and isolated showers. A NW 40-50 kt LLJ positions across the region Saturday. The tighter gradient and CAA to help steepen lapse rates will support deeper mixing and thus gusty NW winds. The degree or mixing and strength of the LLJ are still less certain this far out, but ensembles show a signal for gusts 35-45 mph possible, potentially higher across the coastal waters. 925mb temperatures range between - 2C to +2C Saturday yielding likely the coolest temperatures of the season so far. Highs will likely do it tough to get out of the 40s across the interior with low 50s in the valleys and coastal plain. RH fields show residual moisture within the cyclonic flow pattern. This may be enough to support some isolated showers. Some of the higher elevation points across the Berkshires may only hit the 40 degree mark on Saturday. It`s a bit too early to say the "S word" just yet (snow). However...if the timing aligns, a morning flurry or shower can`t be ruled out in those higher terrain spots out west. Expect a cold night Saturday night with high probabilities for the interior to at least drop to freezing. Higher elevation locations may drop into the upper 20s.
