Once, out in a little country town, there was this bloke with a crook back.

Mind you, he could chuck a grindstone up into a farm wagon if anyone wanted to bet him the cigars, but every time he picked up an axe, something grabbed him right in the spine and he had to go inside and have a lie-down. So his missus took in boarders and did all the cooking herself.

Fair's fair, though, he was happy to split the work, so he did the shopping. Only, once he'd bought the tucker, he'd park himself on a shoe-box with the basket between his knees and carry on about how he couldn't for the life of him work out what Congress was bloody thinkin'.

He had his theories about the Alaskan boundary, and he was dead against any Anglo-American alliance, 'cos Uncle Sam could look after himself at any bend in the road, when you get right down to it, and the American people were better than every other nation going in every way, shape, manner and form, as far as that goes. Then his missus would have to send word for him to get a move on with the groceries so she could get dinner on.

Just about the whole town felt for her, stuck as she was with a great useless lump of a husband. Everyone reckoned she was a martyr.

Then one day the husband copped sunstroke, being too bone-idle to shift into the shade, and the next day he carked it without lifting a finger. The widow gave him the best send-off of the year, then scraped together every last zack she could and stuck it into a marble column with "At Rest" carved on it.

Plenty of folks reckoned she was better off without the lazy bugger, and it was a bloody good riddance of bad rubbish.

They hoped if she ever got hitched again she'd pick a bloke who wasn't scared of hard yakka and had enough nous to pound sand down a rat-hole.

So everyone was pretty chuffed when she married Mr Gladden, who owned the general store. He built her a new house, got a girl in to help, and had the washing sent out. She could wander into the store and grab whatever took her fancy, and every evening he took her for a spin in his new runabout.

So naturally she was miserable as sin, pining for the treasure she'd lost.

MORAL: _If the sheila reckons he's all right, you stay on your own side of the fence._
