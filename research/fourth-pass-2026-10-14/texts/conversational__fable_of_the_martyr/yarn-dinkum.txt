So get this. Out in a country town there lived a bloke with a crook back.

He could hoist a grindstone up into a farm wagon if anyone wanted to bet him the cigars, but every time he picked up an axe, something grabbed him right in the spine and he had to go inside and have a lie-down. So his missus took in boarders and did all the cooking herself.

Mind you, he was willing to go halves on the work, so he did the shopping. Only, once he'd bought the tucker, he'd squat on a shoe-box with the basket between his knees and carry on that he couldn't for the life of him see what Congress was thinkin'.

He had his theories about the Alaskan boundary, too, and he was dead against any Anglo-American alliance, cos Uncle Sam could look after himself at any bend in the road, when you come right down to it, and the American people were a cut above every other nationality in every way, shape, manner and form, as far as that goes. Then his missus would have to send word for him to hurry up with the groceries so she could get the tea on.

Just about everyone felt sorry for her, stuck with a great big useless lump of a husband like that. Folks reckoned she was a proper martyr.

Then one day the husband copped sunstroke, being too bone idle to shift into the shade, and the next day he carked it without so much as an effort. The widow gave him the best funeral of the year, then put every bit of folding stuff she could rake and scrape together into a marble shaft marked "At Rest."

Heaps of people said she was better off without him, and it was defo good riddance to bad rubbish.

They hoped that if she ever got hitched again she'd pick a bloke who wasn't scared of hard yakka, and had the nous to pound sand into a rat-hole.

Well, the whole town was rapt when she married Mr. Gladden, who owned the general store. He built a brand-spankin'-new house, got a girl in and sent the washing out. She could wander into the store and pick out anything she fancied, and every evening he took her for a spin in his new runabout.

So, naturally, she was down in the dumps, pining for the jewel she'd lost.

MORAL: _If the woman reckons he's all right, you keep on your own side of the fence._
