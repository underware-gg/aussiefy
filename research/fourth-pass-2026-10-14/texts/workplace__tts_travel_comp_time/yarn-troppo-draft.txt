## Travel comp time, for everyone

Here's the go: you can put in for travel comp time when you've been approved to travel for work and that travel happens outside your regular working hours. Travel comp time is for “temporary duty,” also known as “TDY” travel, _not_ local travel or your daily trek to and from work. Sorry, the commute doesn't count. TDY travel runs more than 12 hours and needs a pre-approved authorisation in Concur.

And there's no cap on earning travel comp time, whatever your salary level or schedule type. Everyone's in.

Have a squiz at OPM’s [Travel Comp Time Fact Sheet](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/pay-administration/fact-sheets/compensatory-time-off-for-travel/) for more detail.

### Understanding travel comp time

Travelling during your usual work day counts as regular hours, and that includes the run from home out to the airport.

Generally, travel comp time works as a “gap filler” for when you're on the move outside your regular work hours on a regular work day, but not officially “performing work.” Think sitting at the gate, or stuck in a plane seat with your knees up round your ears.

For the nitty-gritty, current GSA employees can check the [Compensation for Government Travel slidedeck](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RFSq_4KdMza_pkcHGp9Hy2RNR2ryLs0b/view?usp=sharing) to work out how your hours break down. Got questions? Sing out to your Timekeeper or an HR Specialist at your agency. That's what they're there for.

### Calculating travel comp time

When you're tallying up travel comp time hours, travel ends when you check in at the hotel or walk in your front door. Travel comp time hours are based on elapsed time, and you use the time zone where you left from to work out the elapsed time. No fiddling with the clocks.

To report your travel time accurately, we recommend keeping a log of:

- When you left home and when you got to the airport
- When your flights actually took off and landed
- When you left the destination airport and when you got to your hotel

### Using travel comp time

Now, the catch. Travel comp time runs out one year (26 pay periods) after you earn it. Hit the expiry date and you lose the leave, simple as that. And if you leave GSA, any unused travel comp time is forfeited. So use it or lose it.
