## Travel comp time, for everyone

You can ask for travel comp time when you've been approved to travel for work and that travel happens outside your regular working hours. Travel comp time is for “temporary duty,” also known as “TDY” travel, _not_ local travel or getting to and from work. TDY travel runs more than 12 hours and needs a pre-approved authorisation in Concur.

There's no cap on earning travel comp time based on your salary level or schedule type.

Have a squiz at OPM’s [Travel Comp Time Fact Sheet](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/pay-administration/fact-sheets/compensatory-time-off-for-travel/) for more detail.

### Understanding travel comp time

Travelling during your usual work day counts as regular hours, and that includes getting from home to the airport.

Generally, travel comp time works as a “gap filler” for when you're travelling outside your regular work hours on a regular work day, but not officially “performing work.”

For the finer details, current GSA employees can check the [Compensation for Government Travel slidedeck](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RFSq_4KdMza_pkcHGp9Hy2RNR2ryLs0b/view?usp=sharing) to work out how your hours break down. If you've got questions, sing out to your Timekeeper or an HR Specialist at your agency.

### Calculating travel comp time

When you're working out travel comp time hours, travel ends when you check in at the hotel or get home. Travel comp time hours are based on elapsed time. Use the time zone where you left from to work out the elapsed time.

To report your travel time accurately, we recommend keeping a log of:

- When you left home and when you got to the airport
- When your flights actually took off and landed
- When you left the destination airport and when you got to your hotel

### Using travel comp time

Travel comp time runs out one year (26 pay periods) after you earn it. Once you hit the expiry date, you lose the leave. If you leave GSA, any unused travel comp time is forfeited.
