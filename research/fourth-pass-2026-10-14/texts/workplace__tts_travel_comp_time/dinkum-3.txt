## Travel comp time, for everyone

If you've been approved to travel for work and the travel happens outside regular working hours, you can put in for travel comp time. It's for “temporary duty,” also known as “TDY” travel, _not_ local travel or your commute. TDY travel runs more than 12 hours and needs a pre-approved authorisation in Concur.

Anyone can earn travel comp time: there are no limits based on salary level or schedule type.

For more detail, have a squiz at OPM’s [Travel Comp Time Fact Sheet](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/pay-administration/fact-sheets/compensatory-time-off-for-travel/).

### Understanding travel comp time

Travel during your usual work day counts as regular hours, and that includes getting from home to the airport.

As a rule, travel comp time is a “gap filler” for when you're travelling outside your regular work hours on a regular work day, but you're not officially “performing work.”

If you're current GSA staff and want more detail, the [Compensation for Government Travel slidedeck](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RFSq_4KdMza_pkcHGp9Hy2RNR2ryLs0b/view?usp=sharing) will help you work out how your hours break down. Got questions? Give your Timekeeper or an HR Specialist at your agency a shout.

### Calculating travel comp time

When you're working out travel comp time hours, travel ends once you check in at the hotel or get home. The hours are based on elapsed time, and you work that out using the time zone you left from.

To get your travel time right, we recommend keeping a log of:

- When you left home and got to the airport
- When your flights actually took off and landed
- When you left the destination airport and got to your hotel

### Using travel comp time

Travel comp time runs out one year (26 pay periods) after you earn it. Once you hit the expiry date, you forfeit the leave. And if you leave GSA, any travel comp time you haven't used is forfeited too.
