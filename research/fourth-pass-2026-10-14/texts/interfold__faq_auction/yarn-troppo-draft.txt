## About the auction

### What is FOLD Auction 2?

FOLD Auction 2 was the second public FOLD auction, the sequel, if you like. It ran on the Uniswap
Continuous Clearing Auction mechanism.

It followed on from the first FOLD Auction, which was held July 8–10, 2026.

Auction 2 was kept small on purpose: just **2% of total FOLD supply**, not the whole box and dice. It
was held right around the changeover to general FOLD transferability. The point of it was to spread
FOLD round a few more hands and help get deeper, longer-term on-chain liquidity off the ground.

### Why was there a second FOLD Auction?

Think of Auction 2 as a limited distribution step, one last hand-out before general FOLD
transferability on Aug 19.

It was small on purpose, at **2% of total FOLD supply**. And it kicked off with a floor that matched
the clearing price of the first FOLD Auction, so nobody started lower than last time.

The auction gave everyone an open, public way to get more FOLD out the door before it became
generally transferable. It also put its shoulder behind long-term on-chain liquidity.

**50% of proceeds were committed to long-term on-chain liquidity support.**

Auction 2 shut up shop just before general FOLD transferability was switched on.

### What happened in the first FOLD Auction?

The first FOLD Auction ran from **July 8–10, 2026**. It used the same Uniswap Continuous Clearing
Auction mechanism.

That one's done and dusted. Its clearing price was **0.02154816 USDC / FOLD**, and that's also the
floor for FOLD Auction 2.

If you were in the first auction, you can still get to claiming and the post-auction info through the
official [Interfold auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction).

### What is a Uniswap Continuous Clearing Auction?

A Continuous Clearing Auction (CCA) is a way of sussing out the price when tokens get handed out. It's
built on Uniswap infrastructure. Here's how it goes:

- The auction runs over a string of separate periods.
- Participants put their bids in through the official Uniswap CCA interface.
- Successful bids within a period all clear at the same clearing price, no favourites.
- The minimum price for each later period is whichever's higher: the original floor price or the
  clearing price of the period before.
- The minimum price can creep up in later periods. It never drops below the applicable earlier clearing
  price or the original floor.
- Any tokens not handed out earlier are still up for grabs in the final period.

So the CCA dishes tokens out bit by bit, with one price for everyone in each period. It's built to cut
down the gas competition, make life hard for snipers, and let the clearing prices find their own level
as people take part in the auction.

[Read more about Uniswap CCAs](https://cca.uniswap.org).

### Did the auction determine the price of FOLD after transferability?

Nah, nah. The auction sorted out the allocations and a clearing price inside the auction itself, and
that's where it stopped. It didn't set the market price of FOLD when general transferability started.

And where market prices go from here? Nobody can tell ya. Anyone who reckons they can is dreamin'.

## Participation

<Callout type='warning'>
  Auction 2 closed on Aug 19, 2026 at 13:06 UTC. The steps in this section show how the auction
  worked, for the record. You can't put in a new bid. That horse has bolted.
</Callout>

### How did participation work?

1. Participants headed over to the official [FOLD Auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction).
2. They followed the verified link through to the Uniswap CCA interface.
3. They got through the required verification process via the official flow.
4. They put their bids in while the auction window was open.
5. Once the auction closed, they could claim any allocated FOLD and unspent USDC through the
   verified auction interface.

Participants needed USDC on Ethereum Mainnet. And before they had a go, they had to read over the
auction terms and eligibility requirements. Homework first.

### Who could participate?

Participation was open to eligible participants. It was subject to jurisdiction, identity
verification, AML and sanctions screening, and other applicable restrictions.

The official verification process decided who was eligible, and the auction terms governed eligibility.

Mind you, getting through verification didn't guarantee you were eligible, or that you'd score an
allocation.

### Was KYC required?

Yep. Participants had to get through identity verification via the official verification process
before they took part. No ID, no go.

The verification process decided eligibility and ran the applicable AML and sanctions screening.

Participation was subject to the auction terms and applicable restrictions.

## Claiming and transferability

### When can participants claim FOLD?

Claiming was set to open straight after the auction closed.

Participants claim allocated FOLD and any unspent USDC through the verified auction interface.

Now, claiming and general FOLD transferability are two separate things. Don't mix 'em up.

### When did general FOLD transferability begin?

FOLD Auction 2 closed Aug 19 at **13:06 UTC (9:06 AM ET)**.

General FOLD transferability could be switched on not long after, at or after **14:00 UTC (10 AM
ET)**.

At or after Unix timestamp **`1787148000`**, anyone at all could call the `tge()` function on the FOLD
contract to switch on general transferability. The TGE transaction went through on Aug 19, 2026 at
14:00:23 UTC. `tgeTimestamp()` returns `1787148023`. So FOLD's now generally transferable. Done.

FOLD Auction 2, claiming, general FOLD transferability, and Network Alpha are separate milestones in
the bigger Interfold launch sequence. For the nitty-gritty, see
[FOLD Transferability](/faq/transferability).

## Safety

### How do I know I’m using the official auction?

This bit matters. Only use auction, claiming, verification, and support links put out through
official Interfold channels.

The official Interfold channels are:

- [Interfold auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction)
- [Interfold website](https://www.theinterfold.com/)
- [Interfold X account](https://x.com/theinterfold)
- [Interfold documentation](https://docs.theinterfold.com/)

Don't trust:

- Auction, claiming, or support links sent to you in DMs out of the blue.
- Token or contract addresses copied from unofficial sources.
- Accounts pretending to be someone they're not.
- Private wallet-connection links.
- Anyone asking for your seed phrase, private key, or wallet backup.
- Anyone asking you to pay for support.

If it looks suss, it probably is.
