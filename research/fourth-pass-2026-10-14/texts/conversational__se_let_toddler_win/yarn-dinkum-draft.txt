Should you let a littlie win?

We've started playing a few simple games with my 2-year-old son, including an easy matching game that's right for his age.

He loves a game, but he seems (to me, anyway) more interested in the playing than the winning. I reckon that's spot on.

Thing is, I noticed last night that he always wins.

Turns out my wife has literally been stacking the deck in his favour. When the game gets near the end, she starts having a squiz at the tiles to check whether the next one dealt (she's the dealer) would be the winning card. If it'd end the game with either her or me winning, she puts it aside and picks the next one. In at least one game she apparently went and put every tile that matched her card right at the bottom on purpose, so they'd never get drawn.

When I asked her about it, she said she reckoned he should win, and that winning would be more fun for him.

Leaving aside what it teaches him about cheating (let's say it was sneaky enough, and my son was distracted enough, that he had no idea the deck was being rigged), is there anything wrong with making sure he wins every time?

What bugs me, and I told the missus as much, is that I want him to enjoy games for the sake of playing, not for the sake of winning. I don't want him expecting to win every time and then getting upset when he doesn't. Is that a fair worry at his age, or is he just too young to care about the competitive side of games, so letting him win is nothing more than a harmless way to get him enjoying playing at this age?

And if it's not a problem at this age, when does it become one?

What makes a kids' game a kids' game is that luck usually counts for a whole lot more than strategy. In other words, it doesn't take much for a kid to win fair and square.

I recently had the exact opposite experience to yours with my five-year-old son. He got a new game for Christmas where you flip over two cards to find a match. I made up my mind not to let him win, and he didn't for the first 10 games or so. When he finally did win, he was over the moon, cos he knew I hadn't just handed it to him. Now he wins about every third game, and he's learning to win and lose with good grace.

When he's old enough for games that take more strategy, I still won't let him win, but I'll give myself a handicap to even things up. For example, when he learns chess I'll start out playing without a queen, but still do my best to win. That way winning means something for either of us, and seeing my handicap shrink over time will be a real long-term achievement for him.

There's also the fact that the game has to be fun for the grown-up too, or they won't stay keen enough to keep playing. When the deck's stacked against you, it stops being fun. For example, when I play games with them, my stepdad often throws away his own position to help my mum win. I dunno if she doesn't notice or just doesn't care, but it sure takes the fun out of it for me.

I just read an interesting article on this. Basically it says that trying to keep your kid happy all the time often ends up with them being unhappy adults, cos they're not equipped to deal with disappointment. It's a long article, but well worth the read.
