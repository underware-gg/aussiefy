Should you let a littlie win?

So we've started playing a few simple games with my 2-year-old son, one of them a little matching game made for kids his age.

He has a ball with it, but from where I'm sitting he's more into the playing than the winning. Which, if you ask me, is exactly how it should be.

Then last night I twigged: he wins. Every. Single. Time.

Turns out the missus has been stacking the deck in his favour. Literally. Once the game's getting near the end, she starts sneaking a squiz at the tiles to see if the next one off the top (she's the dealer, naturally) would be the winner. If it'd finish the game with her or me winning, she slips it aside and deals the next one instead. In at least one game she apparently went the whole hog and buried every tile that matched her card right at the bottom of the pile so they'd never come up. Talk about a card sharp.

When I pulled her up on it, she said she reckoned he should win, and that winning would be more fun for him.

Now, leave aside what it teaches him about cheating (let's say she was sneaky enough, and the little fella was off with the fairies enough, that he had no idea the deck was being rigged). Is there anything actually wrong with making sure he wins every time?

What gets me, and I told my wife as much, is I want him to love games for the playing, not the winning. I don't want him rocking up expecting to win every time and then being gutted when he doesn't. Is that a fair worry at his age? Or is he just too little to care about the competitive side of things, so letting him win is nothing more than a harmless bit of encouragement to enjoy a game at this age?

And if it's not a problem now, when does it turn into one?

* * *

Here's the thing: what makes a kids' game a kids' game is that luck usually counts for a heap more than strategy. In other words, it doesn't take much for a kid to win fair and square.

I've just had the exact opposite run to yours with my five-year-old son. He got a new game for Christmas where you flip over two cards looking for a match. I made up my mind I wasn't going to let him win, and he didn't, not for the first 10 games or so. When he finally got one, he was over the moon, cos he knew I hadn't just handed it to him on a plate. These days he wins about every third game, and he's learning to win and lose like a good sport.

When he's old enough for games that need more strategy, I still won't let him win, but I'll handicap myself to even things up. Say he learns chess: I'll start out without my queen, but still give it everything I've got. That way a win means something for either of us, and watching my handicap shrink over time will be a real long-term win for him.

There's also the fact that the game's got to be fun for the grown-up as well, or they won't stay keen enough to keep turning up to the table. Once the deck's stacked against you, the fun goes out the window. For example, when I play games with them, my stepdad's forever throwing away his own position to help my mum win. Dunno if she doesn't notice or just doesn't care, but it fair takes the fun out of it for me.

I just read an interesting article on all this. The gist of it is that trying to keep your kid happy all the time often ends up giving you an unhappy adult, cos they've never learnt how to cop a disappointment. It's a long one, but well worth the read.
