Snags

Pull up a stool, this is how the old girls did it back in the day. Cut yer string of snags apart. Then grab a carving fork and stab each snag a few times, give it what for. Into the frying pan with 'em, cover 'em with boiling water, and let 'em cook fifteen minutes; drain 'em, back in the frying pan they go, and fry till they're good and brown. Serve 'em up with fried apples, which is a combo ya don't see at a sausage sizzle. Snags often get grilled same as bacon, too, with the apples bakin' in the pan underneath catchin' all the drips. Beauty.

Boston Baked Beans

Now this one's a labour of love, so settle in. Pick over one quart of pea beans, chuck any duds, cover 'em with cold water and leave 'em soak overnight. Come mornin', drain 'em, cover with fresh water, heat 'em slow (keep that water under boiling point, mind), and cook till the skins are ready to burst. And here's the trick for tellin' when: take a few beans on the tip of a spoon and blow on 'em, and if they're cooked enough the skins'll split right open. Them test beans are done for, mind, so they get chucked out, no arguments. Drain the beans, and tip the bean water out the back door, not down the sink. Then the pork: scald the rind of three-quarters of a pound of fat salt pork, scrape it, cut off a one-quarter inch slice and lay it in the bottom of the bean pot. Score through the rind of the rest of the pork every half inch, makin' the cuts one inch deep. Beans go in the pot, and ya bury the pork in 'em with just the rind pokin' out the top like a little island. Mix one tablespoon salt, one tablespoon molasses and three tablespoons sugar; add one cup of boiling water and pour it over the beans; then top up with more boiling water till the beans are covered. Lid on, whack it in the oven, and bake it low and slow for six or eight hours, takin' the lid off for the last hour so the rind goes brown and crackly. Top up the water as ya need to. Heaps of folks swear blind that if ya add half a tablespoon of mustard with the seasonings, the beans sit easier in yer belly, which, let's be honest, with beans, is no small thing. If ya'd rather pork with a bit of lean through it, go easier on the salt.

Now, Boston Baked Beans have got themselves a bloody good name, and the story goes it's all down to the earthen bean pot they're meant to be cooked in, the one with the little top and the big pot belly. But here's the thing: beans every bit as good have been eaten plenty of times where a five-pound lard pail got roped in cos the bean pot had gone bung. So don't stress if ya haven't got the fancy pot.

And yellow-eyed beans? Dead good baked. Give 'em a crack.
