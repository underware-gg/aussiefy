No word of a lie, the average household's leaks can add up to more than 9,300 gallons (about 35,200 litres) of water down the gurgler every year. And nine percent of homes have leaks wasting 50 gallons (around 190 litres) or more a day. Every single day! The usual suspects round the house are worn dunny flappers, drippy taps and other leaky valves. Good news, but: these sorts of leaks are often dead easy to fix. You only need a few tools and a bit of hardware, and that pays for itself in the water you save. Fix the easy household leaks and you can knock about 10 percent off your water bill. Not bad for an arvo's tinkering.

So, to check your place for leaks, first you've got to work out whether you're wasting water at all, then go hunting for where the leak's coming from. Here are some tips for sniffing out leaks:

Have a squiz at your water use during a colder month, such as January or February. If a family of four's going through more than 12,000 gallons (about 45,400 litres) a month, there could be some serious leaks going on.

Check your water meter before and after a two-hour stretch when nobody's using any water. If that meter's budged at all, chances are you've got a leak.

Catch dunny leaks out by putting a few drops of food colouring in the cistern. If any colour turns up in the bowl after 10 minutes, gotcha: you've got a leak. (Make sure you flush straight after the test so the cistern doesn't end up stained.)

Give the tap gaskets and pipe fittings a good once-over for any water on the outside of the pipe, to check for surface leaks.

Dunny Leaks

Old or worn-out dunny flappers (e.g., valve seal) can cause leaks. This simple little rubber gizmo holds the water in the cistern, then lets it go into the bowl when you flush. Trouble is, rubber wears out, so the flapper wants checking every so often and replacing at least every five years, so it seals properly and doesn't leak. An old or worn flapper can make your dunny flush all on its own, which is a bit spooky at two in the morning, or quietly leak thousands of gallons a year without a peep. Swapping a flapper is a quick, easy fix that'll save you water and the folding stuff. Showerhead Leaks

A showerhead leaking at 10 drips per minute wastes more than 500 gallons (about 1,900 litres) a year. That's the water it takes to wash 60 loads of dishes in your dishwasher. Some leaky showerheads can be fixed by making sure the showerhead's on nice and snug to the pipe stem and using pipe tape to hold it there. Pipe tape, also called teflon tape, you can get at most hardware shops, it's easy as to put on, and it can help keep leaks in check. For trickier valve leaks in showers that drip when they're not in use, though, get onto an experienced handyperson or licensed plumber.

Tip: It's also a good idea to check and, if it needs it, replace the washer or "o" ring inside the showerhead while you're doing this repair.
