yarn-troppo: The liveliest retelling, fully faithful with litre conversions; asides make it a little jokier than an EPA guide.
  minor: 'Good news, but:' (for 'These types of leaks are often easy to fix'): Sentence-final Aussie "but" forced into a mid-sentence slot.
  minor: 'you can also ask at your local hardware shop, home improvement, retailer, or a licensed plumber' (for 'consult your local hardware store, home improvement, retailer, or a licensed plumber'): Keeps the original's garbled list instead of smoothing it.

dinkum-3: Clean, natural and faithful; keeps the public-service tone with an easy Aussie voice.
  minor: 'plenty of how-to videos online' (for 'Many tutorials are available online'): Narrows tutorials to videos.
  minor: "Don't forget to turn the water off at the line before you start!" (for "Don't forget to turn off the water line before you start!"): "at the line" is an odd phrasing; "at the mains" would be the local one.

unharnessed: Vivid and varied with natural dad-level swearing; a few British/US picks.
  minor: 'a bit of coin' (for 'save water and money'): American slang for money; "a few bucks" or "the folding stuff" would be local.
  minor: 'a doddle to fix' (for 'often easy to fix'): British rather than Australian.
  minor: "That's enough water for more than 180 showers, strewth!" (for "That's the amount of water needed to take more than 180 showers!"): Exclamation bolted on at the end of the line.

yarn-dinkum: Faithful and natural but on the light side of level 4.
  minor: 'down the drain' (for 'wasted'): A generic phrase where a local one ("down the gurgler") would lift it to level 4.
  minor: 'you can also ask at your local hardware shop, home improvement, retailer, or a licensed plumber' (for 'consult your local hardware store, home improvement, retailer, or a licensed plumber'): Keeps the original's garbled list.

engine: A light word-swap pass that barely moves off the original, with two wrong-sense swaps.
  major: 'Give a stuff about the width of the flapper' (for 'Pay attention to the width of the flapper'): "give a stuff" means to care (mostly in "couldn't give a stuff"); wrong sense and a jarring register.
  major: 'wash 60 heaps of dishes in your dishwasher' (for 'wash 60 loads of dishes in your dishwasher'): "heaps" as a count noun garbles the measure.
