The Metropolitan Council of Nashville and Davidson County, over in the southeastern U.S. state of Tennessee, has made it official: it's marked 50 years since the first mob of Kurdish families landed in the U.S., and given 'em a big wrap for all they've put into the city's culture, community and economy.

Accordin' to an official proclamation VOA got hold of, the first lot of Kurdish families touched down in the United States on May 1, 1976. Heaps of 'em ended up settlin' in Nashville, Tennessee, while others set up communities in cities and states right across the country.

The proclamation gives a shout-out to the Kurdish community for hangin' on to its language, culture and traditions over the last five decades, all while pullin' its weight in Nashville's growth and development.

Accordin' to the document, Nashville's now home to more than 20,000 Kurds, which makes it one of the biggest Kurdish communities in the United States. The place has turned into a real hub for Kurdish culture and community life.

The proclamation also tips its hat to the long-runnin' relationship between the United States and Kurdistan, pointin' to the Kurdish Peshmerga forces, U.S. troops and their allies workin' shoulder to shoulder in the fight against terrorism.

Metropolitan Nashville Council member Mike Cortese, who looks after District 4, led the push for the official recognition of the anniversary, workin' with other councillors.

The proclamation was signed on October 6, 2026.
