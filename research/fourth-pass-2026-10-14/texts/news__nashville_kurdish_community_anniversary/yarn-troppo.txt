Over in Tennessee, in the southeastern U.S., the Metropolitan Council of Nashville and Davidson County has gone and made it official: it's been 50 years since the very first mob of Kurdish families landed in the U.S., and the council's given 'em a big wrap for everythin' they've put into the city, the culture, the community and the economy, the whole box and dice.

Now, accordin' to an official proclamation VOA got its mitts on, that first lot of Kurdish families touched down in the United States on May 1, 1976. Heaps of 'em ended up puttin' down roots in Nashville, Tennessee, and the rest spread out and set up communities in cities and states right round the country.

The proclamation reckons the Kurdish community's done a cracker of a job hangin' on to its language, its culture and its traditions for five decades straight, and pulled its weight in Nashville growin' up into what it is today while they were at it. Not a bad effort, that.

And here's the bit that gets ya: accordin' to the document, Nashville's now home to more than 20,000 Kurds. Twenty thousand. That makes it one of the biggest Kurdish communities in the whole United States. The town's turned into a proper hub for Kurdish culture and community life.

The proclamation also gives a nod to the long-standin' friendship between the United States and Kurdistan, and to the Kurdish Peshmerga forces, U.S. troops and their allies goin' shoulder to shoulder in the fight against terrorism.

The bloke behind it all is Metropolitan Nashville Council member Mike Cortese, who looks after District 4. He led the push for the official recognition of the anniversary, workin' with other councillors.

And the proclamation got signed on October 6, 2026. Fifty years, near enough to the day... well, near enough to the year, anyway.
