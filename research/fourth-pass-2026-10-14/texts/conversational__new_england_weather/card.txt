dinkum-3: Natural, faithful and smooth; the best balance of Aussie colour and Twain's deadpan.
  minor: "Here's one for ya." (added): Yarn opener added to a speech; a little over the level 2 weight.
  minor: 'one of the shiniest gems' (for 'one of the brightest gems'): Needless change of a phrase with no Aussie gain.

yarn-dinkum: Light, faithful and at the brief's weight; a stock opener and a repeated reckon.
  minor: "Now, ya might've heard this one." (added): Stock yarn opener bolted onto a speech.
  minor: 'but I reckon it must be' (for 'but I think it must be'): Second 'reckon' in two sentences.
  minor: 'Crikey, what awful thunder' (for 'Why, what awful thunder'): Stock exclamation.

unharnessed: A light, faithful retelling at the right weight; a couple of forced swaps.
  minor: 'I reverently reckon' (for 'I reverently believe'): 'Reverently' and 'reckon' clash in register; forced swap.
  minor: 'muck about and learn the trade' (for 'experiment and learn how'): Mucking about means idle fooling, not experimenting.
  minor: 'likely patches of rain, snow, hail and drought, followed or preceded by earthquakes' (for 'probable areas of rain, snow, hail, and drought, succeeded or preceded by earthquakes'): Rewords the forecaster's quoted bulletin, whose officialese is the joke.

yarn-troppo: Lively and mostly natural, but heavier than the level 2 brief and given to asides that explain Twain's jokes.
  minor: "Here's one for ya." (added): A yarn opener added to a speech; part of the over-weighting for a level 2 brief.
  minor: 'Hedging his bets, the cheeky beggar.' (added): Added aside that spells out the joke and tilts the dry tone towards matey.
  minor: 'chuffed with himself' (for 'in the joy and pride of his power'): British rather than Australian idiom.
  minor: "how dazzlingly you can't pick it" (for 'the dazzling uncertainty of it'): Awkward grafting of 'dazzlingly' onto 'can't pick it'.
  minor: "fair dinkum, he's earned every bit" (for 'thoroughly well deserves it'): Stock phrase bolted on.

engine: Faithful because it is nearly the original; a few word swaps, far too light for the brief.
  major: 'I reverently believe that the Maker who made us all makes everything in New England but the weather.' (for 'I reverently believe that the Maker who made us all makes everything in New England but the weather.'): Left untranslated throughout; the brief's light Aussie touch is barely there.
  minor: 'that bloke that had that marvellous collection' (for 'that man that had that marvellous collection'): One slang word dropped into archaic prose reads as a patch.
