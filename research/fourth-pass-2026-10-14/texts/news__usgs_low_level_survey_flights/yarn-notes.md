## dinkum

- Accepted on the first submit. Declined the only planned phrase, "dead" for "completely" ("dead passive
  with no emissions"): "dead" goes on casual adjectives (dead easy, dead quiet), and on a technical term in a
  safety claim it reads wrong. With one planned phrase for a 550-word piece, the plan gave little to work with
  at level 3; most of the Aussie had to come from phrasing ("choppers", "a chunk of", "signed up", "sizing up").
- Lint suggestions in the wrong sense: "turn up" (minerals that could be found) -> "lob", "front up", "rock
  up" (a person arriving); "sticks out" (the stinger) -> "stick out like a sore thumb"; "completely" (safety
  claim) -> "flamin'". "plenty" -> "heaps" was right and taken; "the public" -> "punters" skipped for a
  government notice.
- Quotes kept verbatim as the genre says; no lint or submit noise on them.

## troppo

- Accepted on the first submit; same "dead" decline. Lint again offered "completely" -> "flamin'" on the
  no-emissions safety line, which troppo's "a hazard is never the joke" rules out; lint could skip intensifier
  swaps on safety claims. "don't panic" -> "she'll be right" was a good suggestion and taken.
- OPENINGS ("Fair dinkum, this happened.", "Ever hear the one about ...?") don't fit a datelined notice; I
  kept the dateline and opened the prose as a local talking to locals.
- Troppo for news meant a talker framing the facts (lead-ins to the verbatim quotes, "read like a chemistry
  exam", "So how's it work?", "Now the important bit." before the safety facts, "Not a cent to pay."), with the
  quotes, counties, numbers and safety claims unchanged. I caught myself softening "still have significant
  potential" to "there's reckoned to be" and put the claim back.
