Here's what the job involves at the GS-12. If you're picked at a lower grade level, you'll get to learn the whole box and dice, and you'll get training to help you grow into the role.

As a TECHNICAL WRITER-EDITOR, you'll:

Handle the contents of documents and of Federal Register publications properly, so they're published in a way that's legally effective, on time and accurate.

Manage the work, whether you're doing it on your own, as part of a team or leading others, so all documents get published on time and accurately and all program goals get ticked off.

Understand what customers need across every part of the Federal Register publication process and look after it properly, as part of carrying out the Office of the Federal Register's program responsibilities.

Understand and properly use the technical resources on hand for, and tied in with, the Federal Register publication processes, including information technology, legacy systems and other technical resources.

Specialized Experience: For the GS-09, you need one year of specialised experience at a level of difficulty and responsibility equal to the GS-07 grade level in the Federal service. Specialised experience for this job includes:

Editing legal or regulatory documents written by other people to make sure they stick to the style and formatting requirements before they're developed for publication; AND

Spotting and working through production problems and coming up with fixes for them; AND

Getting information across well and tactfully to internal and/or external audiences to help meet program goals.

OR

EDUCATION: A master's or equivalent graduate degree, or 2 full years of graduate study, each year at a higher level, leading to that sort of degree, or an LL.B. or J.D., if related.

OR

COMBINATION OF EDUCATION AND EXPERIENCE: Note: Only graduate study beyond the first year (18 semester hours) counts towards the GS-9.

NOTE: Undergraduate and Graduate Education: Must have included a total of 15 semester hours in an appropriate scientific, technical or social science field(s), and at least one course above the introductory level in the field(s) the job covers. For technical manuals and specifications writers or editors, the equivalent of 15 semester hours can have come from vocational or educational training above high school level at a public, private or Armed Forces school.
