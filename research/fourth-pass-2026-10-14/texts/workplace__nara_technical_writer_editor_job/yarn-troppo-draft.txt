Righto, here's what you'd actually be doing in this job at the GS-12. And if they pick you at a lower grade level, no dramas: you'll get to learn the lot, and they'll train you up so you can grow into the role.

As a TECHNICAL WRITER-EDITOR, you'll:

Wrangle the contents of documents and of Federal Register publications properly, so they come out legally effective, on time and spot on.

Run the work, whether you're going it alone, pulling your weight in a team or leading the pack, so every document gets published on time and right, and every program goal gets ticked off.

Get your head around what customers need at every step of the Federal Register publication process, and look after them properly, as part of the Office of the Federal Register doing its job by its program responsibilities.

Know your way around the technical resources on hand for, and tied in with, the Federal Register publication processes, and use them properly: information technology, legacy systems (the old clunkers that still have to keep running) and other technical resources.

Qualifications

You've got to meet these requirements by the closing date of this announcement. Not the day after.

Specialized Experience: For the GS-09, you need one year of specialised experience at a level of difficulty and responsibility equal to the GS-07 grade level in the Federal service. For this job, specialised experience means:

Editing legal or regulatory documents someone else has written, to make sure they toe the line on style and formatting before they're developed for publication; AND

Spotting production problems, nutting out what's behind them and coming up with fixes; AND

Getting information across well, and with a bit of tact, to internal and/or external audiences, to help get the program goals over the line.

OR

EDUCATION: A master's or equivalent graduate degree, or 2 full years of graduate study, each year at a higher level, heading towards that sort of degree, or an LL.B. or J.D., if related.

OR

COMBINATION OF EDUCATION AND EXPERIENCE: Note: Only graduate study past the first year (18 semester hours) counts towards the GS-9.

NOTE: Undergraduate and Graduate Education: Must have included a total of 15 semester hours in an appropriate scientific, technical or social science field(s), and at least one course above the introductory level in the field(s) the job covers. For technical manuals and specifications writers or editors, the equivalent of 15 semester hours can come from vocational or educational training above high school level at a public, private or Armed Forces school.
