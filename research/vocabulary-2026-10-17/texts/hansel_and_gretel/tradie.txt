Near a great forest lived a poor woodcutter, his wife and his two kids: Hansel was the boy, Grethel the girl. There was bugger-all to eat or drink at the best of times. Then a terrible shortage hit the country, and the bloke couldn't even earn enough for the day's bread. One night he lay tossing and turning, worrying a hole through the mattress. He let out a great sigh and said to his wife,

“What's going to happen to us? We can't even feed the kids. There's nothing left for ourselves.”

“I'll tell you what we'll do,” his wife said. “Early tomorrow we'll take the kids deep into the forest, where it's thickest. Make them a fire, give them a piece of bread each, then head off to work and leave them there. They'll never find their way home, and that'll be them off our hands.”

“No, love,” he said. “I can't bloody do that. I haven't got it in me to leave my kids alone out there. The wild animals would come and eat them before you could say Jack Robinson.”

“You bloody fool,” she said. “Then all four of us starve. Might as well start knocking the coffins together.” She kept at him like a loose sheet of tin in the wind until he gave in.

“But I feel so sorry for the poor little buggers,” he said.

The kids hadn't been able to sleep, their empty bellies seeing to that. They'd heard everything their stepmother said to their father. Grethel cried her eyes out and said to Hansel,

“We're done for.”

“Settle down, Grethel,” Hansel said. “Don't fret yourself sick. I'll work something out.” Once the parents were asleep, he got up, pulled on his little coat, opened the back door and slipped outside. The moon was bright, and the white flints out in front of the house shone like freshly cut silver. Hansel bent down and packed his little coat pocket with as many as it would hold. Then he went back in and told Grethel,

“Easy now, little sister. Get some sleep. God won't leave us in the lurch.” Then he lay down in his bed again.

At daybreak, before the sun was even up, the wife came and woke the kids.

“Up you get, you lazy bones. We're off into the forest to cut wood.”

She handed them a piece of bread each and said,

“That's your dinner. Don't go eating it beforehand, because you're getting no more.”

Grethel carried the bread under her apron because Hansel's pockets were chockers with flints. Off the four of them went towards the forest. A little way along, Hansel stopped and looked back at the house. He kept doing it, until his father said,

“Hansel, what are you gawking at? Don't forget what your legs are for.”

“Dad, I'm looking at my little white kitten. It's sitting up on the roof saying goodbye to me.”

“You young goose,” the woman said. “That's no kitten. It's the sun on the chimney-pot.”

Of course Hansel hadn't been looking at his kitten at all. Every so often he'd been taking a flint out of his pocket and dropping it on the road.

When they got to the middle of the forest, their father told the kids to gather wood for a fire to keep warm. Hansel and Grethel piled up brushwood like a little mountain, and it was lit. Once the flames were properly going, the wife said,

“Lie down by the fire and have a rest, you two. We'll go and cut wood, then come and get you when we're ready.”

So Hansel and Grethel sat by the fire, and at noon they ate their bread. They thought their dad was out cutting timber the whole time, because they could hear what sounded like an axe chopping away. But it was only a dry branch hanging off a withered tree, knocking back and forth in the wind. They waited and waited until they could hardly hold their eyelids up, then fell sound asleep. When they finally woke, it was night. Grethel started crying.

“How the hell are we ever going to get out of this forest?” But Hansel put her at ease.

“Hang on a bit longer, till the moon comes up. Then finding our way home'll be a piece of cake.”

Once the full moon rose, Hansel took his little sister's hand and followed the flints. They gleamed silver and showed the road plain as anything. The pair walked all night and reached their father's house at first light. They knocked. When the wife opened the door and saw Hansel and Grethel, she said,

“You naughty kids! What did you sleep so long in the forest for? We thought you were never bloody coming home!”

But their father was glad to see them. Leaving those two alone in the woods had cut him to the quick.
