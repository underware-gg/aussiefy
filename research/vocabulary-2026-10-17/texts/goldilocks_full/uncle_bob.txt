There was once a little girl called Goldilocks. One morning she went walking in the woods. Kept going so long that before much longer she was buggered and hungry enough to eat the crotch out of a low-flying duck.

Eventually she came across a little house in the middle of the forest. Knocked on the door. No answer. So she opened it and walked straight in. Cheek on her like a bull's backside.

On the kitchen table sat three bowls of porridge. Goldilocks was starving, so she reckoned she'd have a taste.

Tried the first bowl. "Bloody hell, that's too hot!"

Tried the second. "That's too cold!"

Then she tried the third. "That's just right!" she said, happy as Larry, and polished off the lot.

After eating she wanted to park her bum. There were three chairs in the living room. She sat on the first. "Too big!" Tried the second. "Too hard!" Then down she went on the third. "Just right!" But she'd barely settled herself before the bloody thing broke to pieces and dumped her on the floor.

By now she was absolutely stuffed, so upstairs she went to the bedroom. Three beds there. First one was too hard. Second one was too soft. Third one was just right. Up came the blanket, and out she went like a light.

Soon the three bears got home from their walk.

"Someone's been eating my porridge!" said Father Bear in that great booming voice of his.

"Someone's been eating my porridge!" said Mother Bear.

"Someone's been eating my porridge, and they've scoffed every bloody bit!" cried Baby Bear.

Into the living room they went.

"Someone's been sitting in my chair!" said Father Bear, getting cranky.

"Someone's been sitting in my chair!" said Mother Bear.

"Someone's been sitting in my chair, and they've smashed it to bits!" cried Baby Bear.

Now the bears were properly pissed off. Upstairs to the bedroom.

"Someone's been sleeping in my bed!" said Father Bear.

"Someone's been sleeping in my bed!" said Mother Bear.

"Someone's been sleeping in my bed, and she's still bloody here!" cried Baby Bear.

That woke Goldilocks. Three bears standing over her—she nearly jumped clean out of her skin.

"Help!" she screamed. Out of bed, down the stairs and out the door as fast as her legs would carry her. Didn't even stop to put her shoes on.

She never went back to that little house in the woods. Once was enough to cure her of that caper.
