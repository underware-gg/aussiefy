Eighty-seven years ago, our fathers brought a new nation into being on this continent, built on liberty and committed to the idea that all men are created equal.

Now we're in the thick of a bloody great civil war, finding out whether that nation—or any nation built on those principles—can stay standing for the long haul. We've gathered on one of that war's great battlefields. We're here to set aside part of it as a final resting place for the blokes who gave their lives here so the nation could live. It's only right and proper that we do.

But in the bigger picture, we can't dedicate this ground, can't consecrate it, can't make it holy. The brave men, living and dead, who fought here have already done that, far beyond anything our poor efforts could add or take away. The world won't pay much attention to our words here, or remember them long, but it can never forget what those men did here. It's us still living who need to commit ourselves to the unfinished work the men who fought here carried forward so nobly. We need to put our shoulders to the great job still ahead: take from these honoured dead a stronger dedication to the cause they gave absolutely everything for; make a firm bloody promise that they won't have died for nothing; see that this nation, under God, gets a fresh birth of freedom; and see that government of the people, by the people, for the people doesn't disappear off the face of the earth.
