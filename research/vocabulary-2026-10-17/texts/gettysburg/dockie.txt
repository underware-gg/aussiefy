Eighty-seven years ago our fathers brought a new nation into the world here on this continent. Liberty was the idea that gave it life, and it was built on this plain proposition: all men are created equal.

Now we're in the thick of a great civil war, finding out whether that nation—or any nation founded on those terms—can last the distance. We're gathered on one of the great battlefields of that war. We've come to set aside part of it as the final resting place of the men who gave their lives here so that their nation could go on living. It's right and proper that we do that.

But in the bigger reckoning, we can't dedicate this ground, can't consecrate it, can't make it holy with a few words. The brave men, living and dead, who fought here have already done that, far beyond anything our poor efforts could add or take away. The world won't pay much heed to what we say here, or remember it long; but it can never forget what they did here. It's us still living who ought to dedicate ourselves to the unfinished work those men carried forward so nobly. We've got to shoulder the great job still before us: let the honoured dead give us greater devotion to the cause they gave their very last ounce for; make up our minds, here and now, that they didn't die for bloody nothing; see that this nation, under God, has a fresh birth of freedom; and see that government of the people, by the people, for the people doesn't disappear off the face of the earth.
