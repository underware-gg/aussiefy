Once a cheerful swagman dossed beside a billabong,
In the coolibah's shade, out of the glare,
And he sang while he watched and waited for his billy to boil,
“You'll come a-waltzing Matilda with me, I swear.”

Waltzing Matilda, waltzing Matilda,
You'll come a-waltzing Matilda with me,
And he sang while he watched and waited for his billy to boil,
“You'll come a-waltzing Matilda with me.”

Down came a jumbuck for a drink at that billabong,
Up shot the swagman, pleased as could be,
And he sang as he stuffed that jumbuck in his tucker bag,
“You're bloody coming a-waltzing Matilda with me.”

Up rode the squatter on his fancy thoroughbred,
Down came the troopers—one, two, three,
“Where's that bloody jumbuck you've stashed in your tucker bag?
You're coming a-waltzing Matilda with me.”

Up leapt the swagman and plunged into the billabong,
“You'll never bloody take me alive!” said he,
And his ghost can still be heard when you pass that billabong,
“You'll come a-waltzing Matilda with me.”
