Once there was a little country girl living in a village, pretty as a picture. Her mum thought the sun shone out of her, and her grandma doted on her even more. Grandma made her a little red riding-hood, and it suited her so well everybody called her Little Red Riding-Hood.

One day her mum had made some custards, and she said, “Off you go, love, and see how your grandma's doing. I hear she's been very crook. Take her a custard and this little pot of butter.”

Little Red Riding-Hood set off straightaway for her grandma's place in another village.

Going through the forest, she met Gaffer Wolf. He was itching to gobble her up, but he didn't dare: there were blokes nearby gathering bundles of firewood. He asked where she was off to. The poor kid didn't know there was any danger in stopping to listen to a wolf's spiel, so she said, “Going to see my grandma, and take her a custard and a little pot of butter from Mum.”

“She live far away?” asked the Wolf.

“Oh, yes,” said Little Red Riding-Hood. “Past that mill you can see there, first house in the village.”

“Righto,” said the Wolf. “I'll go and see her too. I'll go this way, you go that way, and we'll see who gets there first.”

The Wolf took the shortest track and ran like the devil was on his tail. The little girl went the long way round, picking nuts, chasing butterflies and making little bunches of flowers she found. Before long the Wolf reached the old woman's house. Tap, tap, he knocked.

“Who's there?”

“Your granddaughter, Little Red Riding-Hood,” said the Wolf, putting on the girl's voice. “I've brought you a custard and a little pot of butter from Mum.”

Grandma was in bed, feeling poorly, and called out, “Pull the bobbin and the latch'll lift.”

The Wolf pulled the bobbin, opened the door and fell on the good old woman. Ate her in a flash, the bastard: he hadn't had a bite in more than three days. Then he shut the door, got into Grandma's bed and waited for Little Red Riding-Hood. Some time later she arrived. Tap, tap, she knocked.

“Who's there?”

The Wolf's great big voice gave her a fright at first, but she thought Grandma must have a cold and be hoarse. “It's your granddaughter, Little Red Riding-Hood. I've brought you a custard and a little pot of butter Mum sent.”

The Wolf made his voice as soft as he could. “Pull the bobbin and the latch'll lift.”

She pulled it, and the door opened.

Seeing her come in, the Wolf hid under the bedclothes. “Put the custard and butter on the stool, love, and come and lie down beside me.”

Little Red Riding-Hood took off her clothes and got into bed. She was mighty surprised by the way her grandma looked in her nightclothes.

“Grandma, what great big arms you've got!”

“All the better to give you a cuddle, love.”

“Grandma, what great big legs you've got!”

“All the better for running, my girl.”

“Grandma, what great big ears you've got!”

“All the better for hearing, my girl.”

“Grandma, what great big eyes you've got!”

“All the better to see you with, my girl.”

“Grandma, what great big teeth you've got!”

“All the better to eat you up.”

And with that, the wicked Wolf sprang on Little Red Riding-Hood and ate the poor girl, every last bit.
