Once, in a village, there lived a little country girl as pretty as anything you'd ever seen. Her mum thought the world of her, and her grandmother doted on her even more. Grandma made her a little red riding-hood, and it suited her so well that everyone called her Little Red Riding-Hood.

One day her mum had made some custards and said,

“Off you go, love, and see how Grandma's doing. I hear she's been very crook. Take her a custard and this little pot of butter.”

Little Red Riding-Hood set off straightaway for her grandmother's place in another village.

On her way through the woods she met Gaffer Wolf, who was keen as mustard to eat her. He didn't dare, though: some blokes were making bundles of firewood close by in the forest. He asked where she was heading. The poor kid had no idea standing round listening to a wolf was asking for trouble, so she told him,

“I'm going to Grandma's with a custard and a little pot of butter from Mum.”

“She live far away?” the Wolf asked.

“Oh, yeah,” said Little Red Riding-Hood. “Past that mill you can see, in the first house in the village.”

“Righto,” said the Wolf. “I'll go and see her too. I'll go this way, you go that way, and we'll see who gets there first.”

The Wolf took the short way and ran flat out. The little girl took the long way round, picking nuts, chasing butterflies and making little bunches of whatever flowers she found. The Wolf got to the old woman's house in no time. Tap, tap, he knocked.

“Who's there?”

“Your granddaughter, Little Red Riding-Hood,” said the Wolf, putting on her voice. “I've brought a custard and a little pot of butter Mum sent you.”

The good grandmother was in bed because she was a bit crook. She called,

“Pull the bobbin and the latch'll lift.”

The Wolf pulled the bobbin, the door opened, and he was onto her like a dog on a dropped sausage. He ate the poor woman in a moment—he hadn't had a bite for more than three days. Then he shut the door, climbed into Grandma's bed and waited for Little Red Riding-Hood. After a while she arrived and knocked: tap, tap.

“Who's there?”

The Wolf's big voice gave Little Red Riding-Hood a fright at first. But she figured Grandma had a cold and sounded hoarse, so she answered,

“It's your granddaughter, Little Red Riding-Hood. I've brought you a custard and a little pot of butter from Mum.”

The Wolf made his voice as soft as he could and called,

“Pull the bobbin and the latch'll lift.”

Little Red Riding-Hood pulled the bobbin and the door opened.

The Wolf saw her come in, kept himself hidden under the bedclothes and said,

“Put the custard and that little pot of butter on the stool, then come and lie down with me.”

Little Red Riding-Hood took off her clothes and got into bed. She was gobsmacked at the way Grandma looked in her nightclothes and said,

“Grandma, what bloody big arms you've got!”

“All the better to hug you, love.”

“Grandma, what big legs you've got!”

“All the better for running, kid.”

“Grandma, what big ears you've got!”

“All the better to hear you, kid.”

“Grandma, what big eyes you've got!”

“All the better to see you, kid.”

“Grandma, what big teeth you've got!”

“They're for eating you up.”

And with that, the rotten bloody Wolf pounced on Little Red Riding-Hood and ate every bit of her.
