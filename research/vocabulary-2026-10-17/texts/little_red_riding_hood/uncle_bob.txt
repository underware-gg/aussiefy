Once there was a little country girl living in a village, pretty as a picture. Her mum thought the world of her, and her grandmother doted on her even more. Granny made her a little red riding-hood, and it suited her so well that everyone called her Little Red Riding-Hood.

One day her mother made some custards and said, "Off you go and see how your grandmother's doing, love. I hear she's been very ill. Take her a custard and this little pot of butter."

Little Red Riding-Hood set off straightaway. Granny lived in another village.

On her way through the woods, she met Gaffer Wolf. He was dead keen to eat her, but he didn't dare: there were blokes gathering bundles of firewood close by. So the sneaky bastard asked where she was heading. The poor child didn't know standing around listening to a wolf was asking for trouble.

"I'm going to see my grandmamma," she said, "and take her a custard and a little pot of butter from my mamma."

"She live far away?" asked the Wolf.

"Oh, yes. Past that mill you can see, in the first house in the village."

"Righto, I'll visit her too. I'll go this way, you go that way, and we'll see who gets there first."

Wolf took the short way and ran like the clappers. The little girl took the long way round, gathering nuts, chasing butterflies and making little bunches of flowers she found. So the Wolf got to Granny's house in no time. Tap, tap on the door.

"Who's there?"

"Your grandchild, Little Red Riding-Hood," he said, putting on her voice. "I've brought you a custard and a little pot of butter from mamma."

Granny was in bed, feeling poorly. "Pull the bobbin and the latch will lift," she called.

He pulled the bobbin, the door opened, and he was on her in a flash. Ate the poor old woman up in a moment. Hadn't had a bite for more than three days, the ravenous bastard. Then he shut the door and climbed into Granny's bed to wait for Little Red Riding-Hood.

She arrived a while later. Tap, tap.

"Who's there?"

That big voice gave her a fright at first. But she thought Granny must have a cold and a croaky throat.

"It's your grandchild, Little Red Riding-Hood. I've brought you a custard and the little pot of butter mamma sends."

The Wolf softened his voice as much as he could. "Pull the bobbin and the latch will lift."

She pulled, and the door opened.

He hid under the bedclothes as she came in. "Put the custard and the little pot of butter on the stool, and come and lie down with me."

Little Red Riding-Hood took off her clothes and got into bed. But she was astonished at how odd Granny looked in her nightclothes.

"Grandmamma, what enormous arms you've got!"

"All the better to hug you with, my dear."

"Grandmamma, what enormous legs you've got!"

"All the better to run with, my child."

"Grandmamma, what enormous ears you've got!"

"All the better to hear with, my child."

"Grandmamma, what enormous eyes you've got!"

"All the better to see with, my child."

"Grandmamma, what enormous teeth you've got!"

"All the better to eat you up."

And with that, the wicked bloody Wolf sprang on Little Red Riding-Hood and ate her all up.
