Righto, this lawyer gets to his feet, looking to put him through the wringer. “Teacher,” he says, “what do I have to do to inherit eternal life?”

He says back, “What's written in the law? How do you read it?”

The lawyer says, “Love the Lord your God with your whole heart, your whole soul, all your strength and all your mind; and love your neighbour as you love yourself.”

“That's the ticket,” he tells him. “Do that, and you'll live.”

But the lawyer wants to come out smelling of roses, so he asks Jesus, “Right, but who's my neighbour?”

Jesus says, “There was a bloke heading down from Jerusalem to Jericho when a mob of robbers got hold of him. They stripped him, bashed the living daylights out of him and cleared off, leaving him half dead. As luck had it, a priest came down that road. Saw the poor bugger and gave him a berth as wide as a railway cutting. A Levite did much the same: got there, had a look, crossed over and kept going. Then a Samaritan travelling that way came upon him. The sight of the bloke cut him to the quick. He went over, poured oil and wine on his wounds and bandaged him up. Put him on his own animal, took him to an inn and looked after him there. Next day, when he had to push on, he took out two denarii and gave them to the innkeeper. ‘Look after him,’ he said. ‘If it costs you any more, I'll square up when I come back.’ Now, which of those three reckon's a neighbour to the bloke the robbers got?”

“The one who showed him mercy,” says the lawyer.

“Then off you go,” Jesus tells him, “and do the same.”
