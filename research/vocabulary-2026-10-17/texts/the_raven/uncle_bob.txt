One miserable midnight, I was knackered, head full of worries,
Bent over odd old books of knowledge most had long forgotten,
Nearly nodded off, when tapping came and caught me napping,
Someone softly rapping, rapping at my bedroom door.
"Some visitor," I muttered, "having a knock at my bedroom door—
Just that. Nothing bloody more."

I remember it dead clearly: bleak December, cold and dreary,
Every dying ember throwing ghostly shapes across the floor.
Christ, I wanted morning; all those books had got me nowhere,
Couldn't read away the ache of losing lovely, lost Lenore—
That rare, radiant girl the angels call Lenore—
No name here for her evermore.

Then the purple curtains rustled, silky, sad and bloody eerie,
Put a fright through me unlike a thing I'd felt before;
Heart was going like a rabbit trapped behind the skirting,
So I stood repeating, "Someone wants to come in at the door,
Some late visitor asking to come in at the bedroom door—
That's the lot. Nothing more."

Then I found a bit of backbone, quit my dithering and called out,
"Sir—or Madam—sorry, I do beg your pardon, that I do;
Thing is, I was nearly sleeping, and your knocking was so gentle,
Such a faint and quiet tapping, tapping at my bedroom door,
I wasn't even sure I'd heard you." Flung the bloody door right open—
Darkness. Not a sausage more.

Into that black dark I stared, puzzled, frightened, bloody wary,
Dreaming things no living soul had dared to dream before;
Not a sound to break the silence, not a sign out in the darkness,
Till the only word was one I breathed myself: "Lenore!"
Whispered it, and back the echo whispered softly, "Lenore!"
Only that, and nothing more.

Turned back in, my soul on fire, then another bloody tapping,
Louder this time than the tapping I'd been listening to before.
"Surely that's the window lattice. Something out there making mischief.
Let's have a look and sort this little mystery out, for sure—
Heart, just settle for a minute while I sort the mystery out—
Only wind. Nothing more!"
