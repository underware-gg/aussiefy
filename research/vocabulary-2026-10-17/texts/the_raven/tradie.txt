One rotten midnight, tired as hell, I sat there trying to work things out,
Bent over odd old books of lore most people'd forgotten long before;
My head was dropping, nearly kipping, when I heard a little tapping,
Like someone ever so gently rapping, rapping at my bedroom door.
“Some visitor,” I muttered, “having a tap there at my door—
Just that. Nothing bloody more.”

I remember clear as daylight: bleak December, cold and joyless,
Each dying ember throwing its own ghost across the floor.
God, I wanted morning badly; I'd been trying, but getting nowhere,
To lose my grief among those books—my grief for missing Lenore—
That rare, bright girl the angels call by her own name, Lenore—
Nameless here for evermore.

And the silk of purple curtains, with its sad, uncertain rustle,
Put the wind up me like nothing ever bloody had before;
So to stop my heart from hammering like a loose roof in a southerly,
I stood there saying, “Just a visitor who wants in at my door,
Some late visitor who's asking to come in through my bedroom door—
That's the lot, and nothing more.”

Then I got a bit of backbone; stopped the hemming and the hawing.
“Sir,” I said, “or Madam, sorry—please forgive me, I implore;
Thing is, I was nearly sleeping, and you knocked so bloody gently,
Barely made a sound there tapping, tapping at my bedroom door,
I wasn't even sure I'd heard you”—then I threw open the door;
Pitch-black dark, and nothing more.

I stood staring into darkness, wondering, scared and full of questions,
Dreaming things no living soul had dared to dream like that before;
But the silence didn't budge, and all that dark gave me no answer,
Till one whispered word slipped out of me, and that word was “Lenore!”
I whispered it; an echo murmured back the same word, “Lenore!”
Only that, and nothing more.

Back into the room I turned, my insides burning like a furnace;
Soon I heard that tapping start again, a little louder than before.
“Surely,” I said, “surely something's knocking at my window lattice.
Let's have a squiz at what's out there and get this mystery sorted;
Let my bloody heart lie still a tick and get this mystery sorted—
It's the wind, and nothing more!”
