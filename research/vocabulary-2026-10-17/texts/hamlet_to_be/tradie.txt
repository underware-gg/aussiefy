Keep living, or pack it in—that's the bloody question:
Is it a finer thing to stand there and cop
Every sling and arrow rotten luck fires at you,
Or front up to a whole sea of trouble
And finish it by fighting back? To die—to sleep,
That's all; and with that sleep to put an end
To heartache and the thousand everyday hammerings
Our flesh gets lumbered with: that's a finish
You'd pray for with both hands. To die, to sleep.
To sleep, maybe to dream—yeah, there's the snag,
For what dreams might turn up in death's sleep,
Once we've climbed out of this mortal clobber,
Has to pull us up short. That's the sticking point
That lets misery drag its boots through such a long life.
Who'd keep copping time's lash and piss-taking,
The bully's crooked treatment, the big-head's abuse,
The hurt of love thrown back, the law dragging the chain,
The jumped-up official, and the kicks
Decent, patient merit gets from useless pricks,
If a bloke could settle his own account
With one bare dagger? Who'd lug these loads,
Grunting and sweating through a life that's flogged him out,
Except that fear of whatever comes after death,
That country nobody's mapped, whose border
No traveller crosses back from, jams the will
And makes us cop the troubles we've already got
Rather than bolt to others we know bugger-all about?
So conscience makes cowards of the whole bloody lot of us,
And resolution's healthy, proper colour
Gets washed out by that pale coat of thinking,
And plans with real grunt and weight behind them
Get turned off course by all this chewing it over
And never make it out of the shed into action.
