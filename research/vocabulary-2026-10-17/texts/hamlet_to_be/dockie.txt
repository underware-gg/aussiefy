Keep living, or pack it in—that's the bloody question:
Is it nobler in your mind to cop
Every rotten sling and arrow fortune flings,
Or stand and fight a whole sea of trouble,
And finish it by fighting? Die—sleep,
That's all; and say that sleep puts paid
To heartache, and the thousand natural knocks
Our flesh is born to cop: that's an ending
A bloke could wish for with his whole heart. Die, sleep.
Sleep, maybe dream—ah, there's the snag,
Because in death's sleep, what dreams might find us,
Once we've shrugged off this mortal tangle,
Ought to pull us up. That's the consideration
That makes us drag calamity through such a long life.
Who'd put up with time's lashings and sneers,
The bully's wrongs, the stuck-up bastard's insults,
The hurt of love thrown back, the law dragging its feet,
The cheek of people in office, and the kicks
Worthwhile, patient people get from those worth less,
When he could settle his own account
With just a bare dagger? Who'd haul these loads,
Grunting and sweating through a life that's worn him out,
Except for fear of something after death,
That country nobody's mapped, whose boundary
No traveller comes back across? It ties the will in knots,
And makes us stick with the troubles we've got
Instead of bolting into others we know nothing about.
So conscience makes a bunch of cowards of us all,
And the strong, healthy colour of resolve
Gets washed sickly pale by thinking,
And undertakings with real guts and consequence,
On that account, get shunted off their course
And never make it into action.
