Keep living, or chuck it in? That's the bloody question:
Is it nobler to stand there and cop
Every rotten sling and arrow fortune sends your way,
Or take up arms against a whole sea of trouble
And finish it by fighting? To die—to sleep,
That's all; and in that sleep put paid
To heartache and the thousand kicks in the guts
That come with having flesh: there's an ending
You could wish for with your whole soul. To die, to sleep.
To sleep, maybe to dream—ah, there's the bastard,
Because what dreams might turn up in death's sleep,
Once we've shrugged off this mortal business,
Has to pull us up. That's the thought
That makes us drag calamity through so many years.
Who'd cop time's lash and all its sneering,
The bully's injustice, the stuck-up prick's contempt,
The sting of love thrown back at you, the law dragging its feet,
The jumped-up official's cheek, and all the boots
That decent, patient merit gets from useless bastards,
If you could settle your own account
With one bare dagger? Who'd shoulder those bloody loads,
Grunting and sweating through a life that wears you out,
Except that fear of something after death,
That undiscovered country past a border
No traveller comes back across, ties our will in knots
And has us putting up with the misery we've got
Instead of bolting into misery we know nothing about?
So conscience makes cowards of the lot of us,
And the healthy colour of our get-up-and-go
Turns sick and pale under all that thinking,
And plans with real weight and consequence,
When looked at that way, veer off course
And never make it into doing a bloody thing.
