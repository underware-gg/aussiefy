Back when pigs could spin a rhyme
And monkeys chewed tobacco,
Hens took snuff to toughen up,
And ducks went quack, quack, quack-o!

There was an old sow with three little pigs. She didn't have enough to keep them, so she sent them out to make their own way. The first pig met a bloke carrying a bundle of straw and said,

“Any chance of that straw, mate? I could build myself a house.”

The bloke handed it over and the little pig knocked up a house with it. Before long a wolf turned up, knocked on the door and said,

“Little pig, little pig, let me in.”

The pig answered,

“Not bloody likely, by the hair of my chinny-chin-chin.”

The wolf said,

“Then I'll huff and I'll puff and I'll blow your bloody house in.”

So he huffed and puffed, blew the house in and ate the little pig. Straw wasn't up to that job.

The second little pig met a bloke with a bundle of furze and said,

“Could you spare that furze, mate? I need it to build a house.”

The bloke did, and the pig built his house. Along came the wolf again.

“Little pig, little pig, let me in.”

“Not bloody likely, by the hair of my chinny-chin-chin.”

“Then I'll puff and I'll huff and I'll blow your bloody house in.”

He huffed and puffed, puffed and huffed, and eventually down it came. Then he ate the second little pig too.

The third little pig came across a bloke with a load of bricks and said,

“Please, mate, could I have those bricks to build a house?”

The bloke gave him the bricks, and the pig built his place out of them. Sure enough, the wolf turned up just as he had at the other pigs' places.

“Little pig, little pig, let me in.”

“Not bloody likely, by the hair of my chinny-chin-chin.”

“Then I'll huff and I'll puff and I'll blow your bloody house in.”

Well, he huffed and puffed, huffed and puffed, puffed and huffed till he was blowing like a compressor with a split hose. But he couldn't bring that house down. Once he realised all his huffing and puffing wasn't shifting it, he tried another tack.

“Little pig, I know where there's a lovely field of turnips.”

“Where?” said the pig.

“Mr. Smith's Home-field. Be ready tomorrow morning and I'll pick you up. We'll go together and get some for dinner.”

“Righto,” said the pig. “I'll be ready. What time are we going?”

“Six o'clock.”

The little pig was up at five. He fetched the turnips and was home before the wolf turned up, about six, and called,

“Little pig, you ready?”

“Ready? I've bloody been and come back. Got a lovely potful for dinner.”

The wolf was filthy about that, but reckoned he'd get one over the little pig somehow. So he said,

“Little pig, I know where there's a lovely apple tree.”

“Where?” asked the pig.

“Down at Merry-garden,” said the wolf. “If you don't pull a swifty on me, I'll come for you at five tomorrow and we'll get some apples.”

Next morning the little pig bustled out of bed at four and headed off for the apples, hoping to beat the wolf home. But it was farther to go and he had to climb the tree. Just as he was coming down, he saw the wolf heading his way. That put his heart in his mouth, as you can imagine. The wolf came up and said,

“Little pig! Here before me, are you? Apples any good?”

“Bloody beautiful,” said the pig. “I'll throw you one.”

He lobbed an apple such a long way that while the wolf went off to pick it up, the little pig jumped down and legged it home. Next day the wolf came back and said,

“Little pig, there's a fair at Shanklin this afternoon. Coming?”

“Oh, yeah,” said the pig. “What time will you be ready?”

“Three.” So, as usual, the little pig set off early. He got to the fair and bought a butter-churn. He was bringing it home when he spotted the wolf coming. For a moment he didn't know what the hell to do. Then he climbed into the churn to hide. That tipped it over, and down the hill it rolled with the pig inside. The great round thing gave the wolf such a fright he took off home without ever going to the fair. He went to the pig's place and told him about this huge round thing that had rolled down the hill past him and scared the living daylights out of him. The little pig said,

“Ha! So I put the shits up you, did I? I'd been to the fair and bought a butter-churn. When I saw you, I climbed in and rolled down the hill.”

Now the wolf was absolutely spewing. He swore he'd eat that little pig and come down the chimney to get him. When the pig saw what he was up to, he hung a pot full of water over the fire and got the flames roaring. Just as the wolf came down, he whipped off the lid. In fell the wolf. The pig slapped the lid straight back on, boiled him up, ate him for supper and lived happily ever after.
