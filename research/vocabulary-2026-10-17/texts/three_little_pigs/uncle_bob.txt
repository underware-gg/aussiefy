Back when pigs could spin a rhyme,
And monkeys chewed their baccy,
Hens snorted snuff to toughen up,
And ducks went quack, quack, quack-y!

There was an old sow with three little pigs, and she couldn't afford to keep feeding the lot. So off they went to make their own fortunes. First pig met a bloke carrying a bundle of straw.

"Please, can I have that straw to build myself a house?"

Bloke gave it to him, and up went the house. Along came a wolf, knocking at the door.

"Little pig, little pig, let me in."

"No bloody fear—not by the hair on my chinny-chin-chin."

"Then I'll huff, and I'll puff, and I'll blow your house in."

And he did. Huffed, puffed, blew the bloody place in, and ate the little pig. Not a promising start.

Second pig met a bloke with a bundle of furze, that prickly scrub.

"Please, can I have that furze to build a house?"

The bloke handed it over, and the pig made his place. Then here's the wolf again.

"Little pig, little pig, let me in."

"No bloody fear—not by the hair on my chinny-chin-chin."

"Then I'll puff, and I'll huff, and I'll blow your house in."

He huffed and puffed, puffed and huffed, and down went the house at last. Then he ate the second little pig. The greedy bastard was going through them like a whipper-snipper through weeds.

Third little pig met a man with a load of bricks.

"Please, can I have those bricks to build a house?"

The man gave him the bricks, and the pig built himself a proper solid place. Along came old hungry guts, same routine.

"Little pig, little pig, let me in."

"No bloody fear—not by the hair on my chinny-chin-chin."

"Then I'll huff, and I'll puff, and I'll blow your house in."

Well, he huffed and puffed, huffed and puffed, puffed and huffed till you'd have thought his arse would whistle. Didn't shift the house. Once he worked out all that blowing wasn't worth a pinch of shit, he tried another angle.

"Little pig, I know a lovely field of turnips."

"Where?"

"Mr. Smith's Home-field. Be ready tomorrow morning and I'll come round. We'll go together and get some for dinner."

"Righto. What time?"

"Six o'clock."

Pig was out of bed at five. Went and got his turnips before the wolf turned up, which he did about six.

"Little Pig, ready to go?"

"Ready? I've been and come back, and there's a lovely potful here for dinner."

The wolf was as crooked as a dog's hind leg about that, but reckoned he'd get one over on the pig somehow.

"Little pig, I know a lovely apple tree."

"Where?"

"Down at Merry-garden. If you don't trick me this time, I'll come for you tomorrow at five o'clock and we'll get some apples."

Next morning the pig was on his feet at four and off to get the apples, hoping to beat the wolf home again. But it was further away, and he had to climb the tree. Just as he was coming down, he spotted the wolf. Gave him a fright, I can tell you.

Up comes the wolf. "Little pig! You're here before me! Are the apples any good?"

"Beaut," says the pig. "I'll chuck you one."

He lobbed it such a bloody distance that while the wolf went to fetch it, the pig jumped down and scarpered home.

Next day the wolf came back. "Little pig, there's a fair at Shanklin this afternoon. Fancy going?"

"Oh, yes. What time will you be ready?"

"Three."

Naturally the pig went early again. Got to the fair, bought a butter-churn and was carting it home when he saw the wolf coming. Now he didn't know whether to shit or go blind. He climbed into the churn to hide, tipped it in the process, and away it rolled down the hill with him inside. The wolf saw that great round thing thundering towards him, got the wind up something fierce, and bolted home without even making it to the fair.

Later he went to the pig's house and told him all about this enormous round thing that had rolled past and frightened the living daylights out of him.

"Ha! So I put the wind up you!" said the pig. "I'd already been to the fair and bought a butter-churn. Saw you coming, climbed in it and rolled down the hill."

That properly tore it. The wolf was furious. Swore he'd eat the pig, and said he was coming down the chimney to do it.

Pig saw what was coming, hung a pot full of water over the fire and got a proper blaze going. Just as the wolf came down, he whipped off the lid. In dropped the wolf. Pig slapped the lid straight back on, boiled the bastard up and ate him for supper.

And he lived happily ever after. Sensible brickwork, that.
