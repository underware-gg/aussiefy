## deadset
- Wrong sense: "scared stiff" for "afraid" in "I am afraid your ideas ... have swelled up". That "afraid" is the polite hedge (I suspect), not fear. Declined; used "I reckon".
- "a beaut" for "a good" was planned on "a good education". It's passable there, but the same trigger would also hit "a good deal like", "a good merchant" and "a good business man", where it overpraises or is the wrong sense. I kept it on "education" only.
- "a bloke" for "a man": "the first traveling man" is a term of art (a commercial traveller), and the engine draft had "the first traveling bloke", which loses that. I wrote "commercial traveller", and used it for "drummers" too.
- Lint suggestion "nous" for "common sense" was spot on.

## dinkum
- The engine's dinkum draft dropped swears into neutral spots: "a bloody good deal like feeding his weight in corn" (a fixed phrase meaning "much like"), and "Bloody hell, I simply mention these things". Neither reacts to anything in the letter. It also produced "very, dead careful" (only one of the "very"s replaced), "a beaut business man", and "a quid is worth ... and he never spends the dollar", switching quid and dollar in one sentence.
- The openings ("True story, this.", "You'll like this one.") don't fit a letter that has to start with its dateline and greeting; I didn't use one.
- Lint suggestion "a skerrick" for "a scrap" was good.

## troppo
- Lookups: "sunshine" (the ironic address, "not that way, sunshine") returned only weather phrases ("a cracker of a day"); "so-and-so" came back empty; "two hoots" only knew "give two hoots about", so I dropped "doesn't matter two hoots".
- Lint offered "every bugger"/"every bastard" for "everyone" at vulgarity 1. Within the cap, but for a father writing to his son "every man and his dog" was the one to use, and I did.
- Troppo on a letter: I kept the dateline and greeting and read "perform" as the father getting worked up (repeats, "Galahs, the lot of 'em", "the creative so-and-sos"). The brief doesn't say whether a troppo letter should stay a letter or become a retelling of one; I kept it a letter.
