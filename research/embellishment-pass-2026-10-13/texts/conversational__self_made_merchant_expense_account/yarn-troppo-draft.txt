CHICAGO, May 4, 189-

_Dear Pierrepont:_ Well. The cashier's just handed me your expense account for the month, and I'll be honest with ya, son, one look at it and me shoulders went up round me ears. It fair makes a bloke hunch over. When I said I wanted you to get a broad education, I didn't mean I wanted to buy the whole of Cambridge. Now, the bills won't send me broke, don't you worry about that. But they'll send you broke, and quick smart, unless you're dead careful. Dead, dead careful.

Two years now I've watched these accounts of yours get heavier every single month, and in all that time I haven't seen one sign of you taking honours to justify the extra running costs. That's bad business, that is--a fair bit like feeding a no-hoper steer its own weight in corn, and the useless thing still won't fatten up.

I've kept me trap shut about it till now, cos I was leaning a fair bit on your own nous to stop you making a mug of yourself, the way some of these young blokes carry on who've never had to work a day for it. But don't you go thinking, just because I've sat tight, that the old man's rolling in it and can wear it. He can't, and he won't wear it, not once you've left uni. The sooner you bring your spending into line with what you'll actually be earning, the easier the two of them will find it to live under the one roof.

Here's the truth of it. The only sure way a bloke gets rich quick is to have it handed to him or to inherit it. And you're not getting rich that way, sunshine--not till you've shown you can hold down a pretty important job with the firm, anyway. And there's only the one place a man can start from for that job with Graham & Co. Makes no difference whether he's the son of the old man or the son of the cellar boss--that place is the bottom. And the bottom in the office end of this business is a seat at the mailing-desk, with eight quid every Saturday night. Eight quid. Not a lot, but it's a start.

I can't hand you success on a plate. It'd do you no good, and it'd do the firm harm. There's heaps of room at the top here, mind, but there's no lift in the building. Ya take the stairs, same as every man and his dog. Now, starting off with a beaut education like you've got, you ought to climb quicker than the bloke who hasn't got one. But there'll come a day, when you start at the factory, when the other young fellas at the desk will lick stamps faster than you can. And yet the man who hasn't licked stamps isn't fit to write letters. That's the day you'll find that knowing whether the pie comes before the ice-cream, and how to drive a motor car, isn't worth a skerrick.

I'm only bringing all this up cos I reckon your ideas about the terms you're coming into the firm on have puffed up a bit, out there back East. I'll give you a start, but after that you'll have to blast your own way to the front, all on your own. It's all up to the man. Give some blokes a talent wrapped in a napkin to start out in business with, and they'll swap the talent for a gold brick and lose the napkin into the bargain. Galahs, the lot of 'em. And then there's others you could start off with nothing but the napkin, and they'd set up in the drapery business in a small way with it, and before ya know it they've sweet-talked the other bloke's talent into it as well.

Now, I'm proud enough to believe you've got the right sort of stuff in you. But I want to see some of it come out. You'll never make a good merchant of yourself by flipping round the order the Lord laid down for us--learning the spending end of business before the earning end. For the spendthrift, payday's always a month off, and he never gets more than sixty cents out of any quid that comes his way. But a good businessman? To him a quid's worth one hundred and six cents, and he never spends the quid. It's the man who keeps his savings up and his expenses down who ends up buying a share in the business. And that's where you'll come up short, son, if your expense accounts don't lie. Mind you, they generally don't lie in that particular way, though Baron Munchausen was the first commercial traveller, and my commercial travellers' bills still show his influence, the creative so-and-sos.
