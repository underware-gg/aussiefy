No word of a lie, the average household's leaks can send more than 9,300 gallons (around 35,000 litres) of water down the gurgler every year, and nine percent of homes have leaks wasting 50 gallons (around 190 litres) or more a day. The usual suspects round the house are worn dunny flappers, dripping taps and other leaky valves. Good news is they're often easy to fix: you only need a few tools and a bit of hardware, and that pays for itself in the water you save. Sort out the easy household leaks and you can knock about 10 percent off your water bills.

To check your place for leaks, first you've got to work out whether you're wasting water at all, then track down where it's coming from. Here's a few tips for finding leaks:

Have a squiz at how much water you use in one of the colder months, like January or February. If a family of four's going through more than 12,000 gallons (around 45,000 litres) a month, you could have some big leaks.

Check your water meter before and after a two-hour stretch when nobody's using any water. If the meter's moved at all, chances are you've got a leak.

To find a dunny leak, pop a few drops of food colouring in the cistern. If any colour turns up in the bowl after 10 minutes, you've got a leak. (Make sure you flush straight after, or it'll stain the cistern.)

Give the tap washers and pipe fittings a once-over for any water on the outside of the pipe, to check for surface leaks.

Dunny Leaks

Old or worn-out dunny flappers (e.g., valve seal) can cause leaks. It's a simple rubber gizmo that holds the water in the cistern, then lets it go into the bowl when you flush. Rubber wears out, so check the flapper every so often and replace it at least every five years so it seals properly and doesn't leak. An old or worn flapper can make your dunny flush all on its own, or quietly leak thousands of gallons a year. Swapping one over is a quick, easy fix that saves you water and the folding stuff. Tap Leaks

Showerhead Leaks

A showerhead leaking at 10 drips a minute wastes more than 500 gallons (around 1,900 litres) a year. That's the water it takes to run 60 loads through your dishwasher. You can fix some leaky showerheads by making sure the showerhead's on nice and tight to the pipe stem, with some pipe tape to hold it. Pipe tape, also called teflon tape, you can get at most hardware shops; it's easy to put on and can help stop leaks. For the trickier valve leaks, where the shower drips when it's not in use, get onto an experienced handyman or a licensed plumber.

Tip: While you're at it, it's a good idea to check the washer or "o" ring inside the showerhead too, and replace it if it needs it.
