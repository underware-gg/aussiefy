Strewth, where do I start? Your average household's leaks can send more than 9,300 gallons (near enough 35,000 litres) of water straight down the gurgler every year. Every year! And nine percent of homes have got leaks wasting 50 gallons (about 190 litres) or more a day. The usual culprits round the house? Worn dunny flappers, dripping taps and a few other leaky valves. Now, the good news is these are often easy as to fix: a few tools, a bit of hardware, and the lot pays for itself in the water you stop chucking away. Sort out the easy ones and you can knock about 10 percent off your water bills. Not bad for an arvo's tinkering.

So, to check your place for leaks, first you've got to work out if you're wasting water at all, and then you go hunting for where the bugger's coming from. Here's a few tips for finding leaks:

Have a squiz at how much water you're using in one of the colder months, January or February, say. If a family of four's getting through more than 12,000 gallons (about 45,000 litres) a month, you could have some proper leaks on your hands.

Check your water meter, then leave it two hours with nobody using a drop, and check it again. If that meter's budged at all, chances are you've got a leak.

Reckon the dunny's leaking? Pop a few drops of food colouring in the cistern. If any of that colour turns up in the bowl after 10 minutes, you've got a leak, mate. (And flush straight after, or you'll have a stained cistern to explain.)

Give the tap washers and pipe fittings a good once-over, too, for any water on the outside of the pipe. That's your surface leaks.

Dunny Leaks

Old or worn-out dunny flappers (e.g., valve seal) can cause leaks. The flapper's a simple little rubber gizmo: it holds the water in the cistern, then lets it go into the bowl when you flush. Trouble is, rubber wears out, so check the flapper every so often and replace it at least every five years, so it seals up properly and doesn't leak. An old, worn flapper can have your dunny flushing all on its own like it's haunted, or quietly leaking thousands of gallons a year without a peep. Swapping one over is a quick, easy fix that saves you water and the folding stuff both. Need a hand? Ask at your local hardware shop, home improvement place, retailer, or get a licensed plumber in. Tap Leaks

Showerhead Leaks

A showerhead leaking at 10 drips a minute wastes more than 500 gallons (about 1,900 litres) a year. That's the water it takes to put 60 loads through your dishwasher. You can fix some leaky showerheads just by making sure the head's on nice and tight to the pipe stem, with a bit of pipe tape to hold it. Pipe tape, also called teflon tape, you'll find at most hardware shops; it's dead easy to put on and it can help stop leaks. But for the trickier valve leaks, where the shower keeps dripping when nobody's using it, don't muck about: get onto an experienced handyman or a licensed plumber.

Tip: While you've got it apart, it's a good idea to check the washer or "o" ring inside the showerhead as well, and replace it if it needs it.
