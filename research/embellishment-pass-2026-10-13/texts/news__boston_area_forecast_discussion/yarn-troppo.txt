KEY MESSAGE 2...Isolated shower possible Wednesday, but drier overall. Then Thursday dries right out, and it's a bit nippy for this time of year.

Pull up a stool, here's the weather. Wednesday, a trailing cold front shifts across the region, and surface high pressure builds in from the west through the day behind it. The model soundings have more mixing in the boundary layer, and that means drier weather and a westerly with a bit of go in it, gusts 20-30 mph (30-50 km/h). Chuck that westerly, downslope flow in with 925mb temperatures of +17- 18C and you've got yourself a warm autumn day, tops in the 70s (21-26°C) in a fair few spots. Shorts weather, nearly. There's a hint of a stray shower or two as a weak wave wanders through, but don't go running for the brolly: it won't cover much ground, and with the lower levels that dry it'll be a light shower or a few spits at most.

Into Thursday the high pressure settles in further and the airmass dries out even more. Thursday's meant to be dry, but cooler than Wednesday, cos a cooler airmass will have moved in by then, with 925mb temperatures about 10C down on Wednesday. So, a bit on the cool side for this time of year, mostly in the lower 60s (15-17°C). Dig the jumper out.

KEY MESSAGE 3...Low pressure system expected to bring rain Friday into early Saturday. Gusty northwest winds and below normal temperatures.

Now here's where it gets interesting. The deterministic and the ensemble guidance, the whole lot of 'em, have a stronger/deeper northern stream shortwave trough shoving across the Great Lakes and the northeast Friday into Saturday. And it's got synoptic support, this system: an upper level jet parks itself near southern New England, giving large scale ascent and advecting a plume of above normal moisture north into the region. That sets up rain right across the board. Heaps of moisture, mind, but instability looks limited, so the chance of heavy rain impacts isn't great. Ensemble guidance has amounts mostly between 0.25 and 0.60" (6-15 mm), with the ECMWF and the Canadian leaning wetter than the GFS solutions. Believe those particular models and you're looking at closer to an inch (25 mm). And those bigger totals aren't out of the question if we can tap into better forcing with the warm front. Exactly when the rain rocks up and when it clears off, that's still up in the air, cos the guidance can't agree yet. Best bet is it lobs in the Friday morning timeframe and keeps on overnight. Good news is most guidance has this system fairly progressive, so it won't be hanging about all weekend like a bad smell. The bulk of it likely clears off early Saturday morning. On top of that, the initial southerly LLJ with this system nudges in Friday, and if it mixes down enough you'll get breezy to gusty southerly winds (gusts 20-30 mph, 30-50 km/h).

Then the real cold stuff. A strong cold front pushes across the region by early Saturday morning and drops the northeast into a classic cold air advection pattern: gusty NW winds, cooler temperatures and isolated showers, the whole box and dice. A NW 40-50 kt LLJ sets up across the region Saturday. The tighter gradient, and CAA to help steepen lapse rates, will back deeper mixing and so gusty NW winds. How much mixing and how strong that LLJ gets are still less certain this far out, but the ensembles show a signal for gusts 35-45 mph (55-70 km/h), maybe more across the coastal waters. Hang onto your hat. 925mb temperatures range between - 2C to +2C Saturday, likely the coldest of the season so far. The highs'll be doing it tough, battling to get out of the 40s (4-9°C) across the interior, with low 50s (10-12°C) in the valleys and on the coastal plain. RH fields show leftover moisture in the cyclonic flow pattern, and that may be enough for some isolated showers. Up across the Berkshires, some of the higher spots may only hit the 40 degree mark (4°C) on Saturday. Now, it's a bit early to say the "S word" just yet (snow). But...if the timing lines up, a morning flurry or shower can't be ruled out in those higher spots out west. And Saturday night'll be brass monkeys, with good odds of the interior getting down to freezing at least, and the higher spots maybe dropping into the upper 20s (-2 to -3°C). Rug up.
