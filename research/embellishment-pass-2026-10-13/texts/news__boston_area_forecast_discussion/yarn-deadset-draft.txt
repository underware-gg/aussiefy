KEY MESSAGE 2...Isolated shower possible Wednesday, but drier overall. Dry weather's back for Thursday with temperatures on the cool side for this time of year.

A trailing cold front shifts across the region Wednesday with surface high pressure building in from the west through the day. Model soundings show more mixing in the boundary layer, backing drier weather and westerly winds with gusts 20-30 mph (30-50 km/h). Westerly, downslope flow combined with 925mb temperatures +17- 18C will make for a warm autumn day with tops in the 70s (21-26°C) in a fair few spots. There are signs of a stray shower or two as a weak wave comes through, but it won't cover much ground and it'll be a light shower/a few spits at most, cos the lower levels are drier.

As high pressure settles in further into Thursday, the airmass dries out even more. Thursday's meant to be dry, though cooler than Wednesday. A cooler airmass will have moved in as well, with 925mb temperatures around 10C cooler than Wednesday. That'll give temperatures on the cool side for this time of year, mostly in the lower 60s (15-17°C).

KEY MESSAGE 3...Low pressure system expected to bring rain Friday into early Saturday. Gusty northwest winds and below normal temperatures.

Deterministic and ensemble guidance show a stronger/deeper northern stream shortwave trough pushing across the Great Lakes and the northeast Friday into Saturday. There's synoptic support with this system, as an upper level jet sits near southern New England, giving large scale ascent while also advecting a plume of above normal moisture northward into the region. That'll set things up for widespread rain. Despite heaps of moisture, instability looks limited, which cuts down the chance of heavy rain impacts. Ensemble guidance has amounts generally from 0.25 to 0.60" (6-15 mm), with the ECMWF and Canadian leaning wetter than the GFS solutions. Across those particular models, amounts closer to an inch (25 mm) are possible. Those higher amounts aren't out of the question if we can tap into better forcing with the warm front. The finer details, like exactly when the rain rocks up and clears out, are less certain, as the guidance still differs. Rain's more likely to lob in the Friday morning timeframe, keeping on overnight. Most guidance has this system fairly progressive, meaning it won't be hanging about the whole weekend. Most of the rain likely clears out early Saturday morning. On top of that, the initial southerly LLJ with this system nudges in Friday, which, if it's mixed enough, will bring breezy to gusty southerly winds (gusts 20-30 mph, 30-50 km/h).

A strong cold front pushes across the region by early Saturday morning, putting the northeast in a classic cold air advection pattern with gusty NW winds, cooler temperatures and isolated showers. A NW 40-50 kt LLJ sets up across the region Saturday. The tighter gradient and CAA to help steepen lapse rates will back deeper mixing and so gusty NW winds. The degree of mixing and strength of the LLJ are still less certain this far out, but ensembles show a signal for gusts 35-45 mph (55-70 km/h) possible, maybe higher across the coastal waters. 925mb temperatures range between - 2C to +2C Saturday, likely giving the coldest temperatures of the season so far. Tops will likely battle to get out of the 40s (4-9°C) across the interior, with low 50s (10-12°C) in the valleys and coastal plain. RH fields show leftover moisture within the cyclonic flow pattern. That may be enough to back some isolated showers. Some of the higher spots across the Berkshires may only hit the 40 degree mark (4°C) on Saturday. It's a bit early to say the "S word" just yet (snow). But...if the timing lines up, a morning flurry or shower can't be ruled out in those higher spots out west. Expect a cold night Saturday night, with good odds of the interior dropping to at least freezing. Higher spots may drop into the upper 20s (-2 to -3°C).
