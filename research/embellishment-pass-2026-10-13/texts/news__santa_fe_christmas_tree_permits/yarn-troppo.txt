Here's one for ya. If you're after a real Chrissie tree this year, one you cut yourself, the permits go on sale from November 13, 2025. You can get 'em online through Recreation.gov, or in person at any of the Santa Fe National Forest District Offices and the Supervisor's Office. All the details are here: the designated cutting areas, the dates, and which trees you're allowed to have a go at.

How to get your Chrissie tree permit:

Online through Recreation.gov. Search for Santa Fe National Forest Christmas Tree Permits. And do yourself a favour, have a proper read of the overview and the need-to-know info before you hand over any dosh. You'll need to set up or log in to a Recreation.gov account to finish paying, too, so have your password handy.

In person at the Supervisor's Office in Santa Fe, N.M., or at the Jemez, Cuba, Coyote, Española, and Pecos/Las Vegas District Offices. Give 'em a bell for current opening hours.

The Walatowa Visitor Center sells permits in person too, but Saturdays only, mind, from 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., from November 22 to December 20, 2024.

Can't swing an axe? No worries. District offices might have pre-cut Chrissie trees for sale. Depends on the weather and how many they've got, and the folks who can't cut their own tree get first dibs, which is only fair.

Now, the damage. The SFNF's Chrissie tree permits are $10, and there's no refunds, so don't go changing your mind. Buy online through recreation.gov and they'll sting you an extra $2.50 service fee on top. One tag covers a tree up to 5 inches (13 cm) across and 10 feet (3 m) tall. Got your eye on something bigger, taller than 10 feet and/or wider than 5 inches? That needs an extra tag. So a whopping 15-foot (4.6 m) tree takes two tags.

And here's a good one for the families: Year 4 kids with an Every Kid Outdoors pass get a Chrissie tree permit free. Just pop the pass or voucher number in when you're buying the permit (fair warning, though: the $2.50 service fee still gets slapped on).

Best bit? Cutting a Chrissie tree actually does the bush a favour. The permit system helps thin out the stands where the skinny little trees are packed in like sardines. Local forest health experts pick the areas that need thinning, and wouldn't ya know it, the trees there tend to be just the right size for a Chrissie tree. Clear those out of the designated areas and the other trees get room to grow bigger, and it can open up patches of feed for the wildlife. Everyone's a winner.
