Chrissie tree permits go on sale from November 13, 2025, online through Recreation.gov and in person at all the Santa Fe National Forest District Offices and the Supervisor's Office. You'll find the details on the designated cutting areas, the dates, and which kinds of trees you're allowed to cut here.

How to get your Chrissie tree permit:

Online through Recreation.gov: just search for Santa Fe National Forest Christmas Tree Permits. Make sure you have a proper read of the overview and need-to-know info before you buy the permit. You'll also need to set up or log in to a Recreation.gov account to finish paying.

In person at the Supervisor's Office in Santa Fe, N.M., or at the Jemez, Cuba, Coyote, Española, and Pecos/Las Vegas District Offices. Get in touch with us for current opening hours.

The Walatowa Visitor Center will sell permits in person on Saturdays only, from 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., from November 22 to December 20, 2024.

District offices might have pre-cut Chrissie trees for sale too. That depends on the weather and how many there are, and people who can't cut their own tree get first dibs.

The SFNF's Chrissie tree permits are $10, and you can't get a refund. Buy one online through recreation.gov and there's an extra $2.50 service fee on top. One tag covers a tree up to 5 inches (13 cm) across and 10 feet (3 m) tall. Anything taller than 10 feet and/or wider than 5 inches needs an extra tag, so a 15-foot (4.6 m) tree, say, needs two.

Year 4 kids with an Every Kid Outdoors pass can get a Chrissie tree permit for free: just pop in the pass or voucher number when you're buying the permit (heads up: the $2.50 service fee still applies).

And cutting a Chrissie tree does the bush a good turn, too. The permit system helps thin out thick stands of skinny little trees. Local forest health experts pick the areas that'll benefit from thinning, and the trees there tend to be just the right size for a Chrissie tree. Clearing them out of the designated areas gives the other trees room to grow bigger and can open up patches of feed for the wildlife.
