Snags

Cut a string of snags apart. Give each snag a few jabs with a carving fork. Put 'em in the frying pan, cover with boiling water, and cook fifteen minutes; drain 'em, back in the frying pan they go, and fry till they're nice and brown. Serve 'em up with fried apples. Snags are often grilled same as bacon, with the apples baked in a pan underneath.

Boston Baked Beans

Pick over one quart pea beans, cover 'em with cold water, and leave 'em to soak overnight. Come mornin', drain, cover with fresh water, heat 'em up slowly (keep the water under boiling point, mind), and cook till the skins are ready to burst,—and the best way to tell is to take a few beans on the tip of a spoon and blow on 'em: if they're cooked enough, the skins'll burst. The beans ya test like that have to be chucked, of course. Drain the beans, and chuck the bean water outside, not down the sink. Scald the rind of three-quarters pound fat salt pork, scrape it, cut off a one-quarter inch slice and put it in the bottom of the bean pot. Cut through the rind of the rest of the pork every one-half inch, makin' the cuts one inch deep. Put the beans in the pot and bury the pork in 'em, leavin' the rind showin'. Mix one tablespoon salt, one tablespoon molasses, and three tablespoons sugar; add one cup boiling water, and pour it over the beans; then top up with enough boiling water to cover 'em. Put the lid on the bean pot, bung it in the oven, and bake it slow for six or eight hours, takin' the lid off for the last hour so the rind goes nice and brown and crisp. Add water as ya need it. Heaps of people reckon if ya throw in one-half tablespoon mustard with the seasonings, the beans go down easier on the gut. If ya'd rather pork with a bit of lean through it, go easier on the salt.

Now, Boston Baked Beans have got a good name for themselves, and people put it down to the clay bean pot with the small top and the bulgin' sides they're meant to be cooked in. Mind you, just as beaut beans have often been eaten where the bean pot's broken and a five-pound lard bucket's done the job instead.

Yellow-eyed beans are dead beaut baked, too.
