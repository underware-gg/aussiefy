Snags

Cut apart a string of snags. Poke each snag several times with a carving fork. Put in frying pan, cover with boiling water, and cook fifteen minutes; drain, put back in frying pan, and fry till nicely browned. Serve with fried apples. Snags are often grilled same as bacon, with apples baked in a pan under 'em.

Boston Baked Beans

Pick over one quart pea beans, cover with cold water, and soak overnight. In the mornin', drain, cover with fresh water, heat slowly (keepin' the water below boiling point), and cook till the skins'll burst,—which is best worked out by takin' a few beans on the tip of a spoon and blowin' on 'em, when the skins'll burst if they're cooked enough. Beans tested like this have to be chucked out, of course. Drain beans, chucking the bean water outside, not down the sink. Scald the rind of three-quarters pound fat salt pork, scrape, take off a one-quarter inch slice and put it in the bottom of the bean pot. Cut through the rind of the rest of the pork every one-half inch, makin' cuts one inch deep. Put beans in pot and bury pork in beans, leavin' the rind showin'. Mix one tablespoon salt, one tablespoon molasses, and three tablespoons sugar; add one cup boiling water, and pour over beans; then add enough more boiling water to cover beans. Cover bean pot, bung it in the oven, and bake slowly six or eight hours, takin' the lid off the last hour of cookin', so the rind goes brown and crisp. Add water as needed. Heaps of people reckon that addin' one-half tablespoon mustard with the seasonings makes the beans easier on the gut. If ya'd rather pork mixed with lean, use less salt.

The good name Boston Baked Beans have got has been put down to the clay bean pot with the small top and bulgin' sides they're meant to be cooked in. Just as beaut beans have often been eaten where a five-pound lard bucket stood in for the broken bean pot.

Yellow-eyed beans are dead beaut baked.
