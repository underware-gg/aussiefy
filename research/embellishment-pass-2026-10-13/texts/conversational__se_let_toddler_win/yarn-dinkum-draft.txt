Should you let a littlie win?

So we've started playing a few easy games with my 2-year-old son, one of them a simple matching game that's right for his age.

He loves a game, but (to me, anyway) he seems more into the playing than the winning. Which I reckon is perfect.

Thing is, I noticed last night that he wins every single time.

Turns out the missus has literally been stacking the deck for him. Once the game's getting near the end, she starts having a squiz at the tiles, checking if the next one dealt (she's the dealer) would be the winning card. If it'd end the game with her or me winning, she pops it to one side and picks the next one. In at least one game, apparently, she'd gone and put every tile that matched her card right down the bottom on purpose, so they'd never get drawn.

When I asked her about it, she said she reckoned he should win, and that winning would be more fun for him.

Now, leaving aside what that teaches him about cheating (let's say it was subtle enough, and he was off with the fairies enough, that he had no idea the deck was rigged), is there anything wrong with making sure he wins every time?

My worry, and I told her this, is that I want him to enjoy games for the sake of playing, not for the sake of winning. I don't want him expecting to win every time and then being spewin' when he doesn't. Is that a fair worry at his age? Or is he just too little to care about the competitive side of things, and letting him win is nothing more than a harmless way to get him enjoying a game at this age?

And if it's not a problem now, when does it become one?

* * *

What makes a kids' game a kids' game is that luck mostly counts for heaps more than strategy. Put another way, it doesn't take much for a kid to win fair and square.

I've just had the exact opposite go of it with my five-year-old son. He got a new game for Chrissie where you flip over two cards to find a match. I made up my mind I wasn't going to let him win, and he didn't, not for the first 10 games or so. When he finally did win, he was over the moon, cos he knew I hadn't just handed it to him. These days he wins about every third game, and he's learning to win and lose like a good sport.

Once he's old enough for games that take more strategy, I still won't let him win, but I'll give myself a handicap to even things up. Say, when he learns chess, I'll start out without my queen but still have a red-hot go at winning. That way a win's a real achievement for either of us, and watching my handicap shrink over time will be a real long-term achievement for him.

There's also the fact that the game's got to be fun for the grown-up too, or they won't stay keen enough to keep playing. When the deck's stacked against you, it stops being fun. Like, when I play games with them, my stepdad's always giving up his own position to help my mum win. I dunno if she doesn't notice or just doesn't care, but it sure takes the fun out of it for me.

I just read an interesting article on this. Basically it says that trying to keep your kid happy all the time often turns them into unhappy adults, cos they've got no way of dealing with disappointment. It's a long one, but well worth the read.
