Should you let a littlie win?

Now, this one's been doin' my head in. We've started playing a few easy games with my 2-year-old son, little matching game, the sort they make for kids his age. Nothing flash.

And he loves it, but (to me, anyway) he's more into the playing than the winning. Just happy to be there, bless him. Which I reckon is perfect.

Except. Last night it dawns on me: the kid never loses. Not once.

So I start watching, and turns out the missus has literally been stacking the deck for him. Card sharp, she is. Once the game's getting near the end, she starts having a sneaky squiz at the tiles, checking if the next one dealt (she's the dealer, course she is) would be the winning card. If it'd end the game with her winning, or me, she quietly pops it to one side and picks the next one. And in at least one game, apparently, she'd gone and buried every tile that matched her card right down the bottom on purpose, so they'd never come up. Cold-blooded, that.

So I ask her about it, and she goes, she reckons he should win, and winning'd be more fun for him.

Now, forget for a sec what that teaches him about cheating (let's say she was smooth enough, and he was off with the fairies enough, that he had no idea the deck was rigged). Is there actually anything wrong with making sure he wins every single time?

Here's my worry, and I told her this: I want him to love a game for the playing, not for the winning. I don't want him rocking up expecting to win every time, then spewin' the first time he doesn't. Is that a fair worry at his age? Or is he just too little to care about who wins, and letting him win is nothing more than a harmless way to get him hooked on a game at this age?

And if it's not a problem now, when does it become one, ay?

* * *

Then someone else pipes up: Mate, what makes a kids' game a kids' game is that it's mostly luck, and luck counts for heaps more than strategy. Put another way, it doesn't take much for a kid to win fair and square.

I've just had the exact opposite go of it with my five-year-old son. Young fella got a new game for Chrissie, the one where you flip over two cards to find a match. And I decided, right, I'm not letting him win. And he didn't, not for the first 10 games or so. Poor little bloke. But when he finally did win, he was over the moon, cos he knew I hadn't just handed it to him. Earned it. These days he wins about every third game, and he's learning to win and lose like a good sport.

Once he's old enough for games with more strategy, I still won't let him win, but I'll give myself a handicap to even things up. Say, when he learns chess, I'll start out without my queen and still have a red-hot go at winning. That way a win's a real achievement for either of us, and watching my handicap shrink over the years'll be a real long-term achievement for him.

Then there's the other side of it: the game's got to be fun for the grown-up too, or they won't stay keen enough to keep playing. Get the deck stacked against you and it stops being fun, quick smart. I've seen it myself: when I play games with them, my stepdad's forever giving up his own position to help my mum win. Dunno if she doesn't notice or just doesn't care, but it sure takes the fun out of it for me.

And I just read an interesting article on this, too. Basically says if you try to keep your kid happy all the time, they often grow up to be unhappy adults, cos they've got no idea how to cop disappointment. Bit of a long one, but well worth the read.
