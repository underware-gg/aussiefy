Now, this one's a ripper, and it might just save your bacon. Bushwalking in the Grand Canyon is no stroll down the beach. You've got the heat, you've got the height, and you've got country that'll give your legs a proper workout. If you want to come out the other end having had a good time, and in one piece, you've got to sort out your water and your tucker before anything else.

Stay Hydrated and Keep Your Energy Up

Here's the thing. While you're walking, and especially when you're slogging uphill in the full sun, you have to keep your fluids up. It's that hot and dry down there that your sweat's gone before you notice it, so you mightn't realise how much fluid you're losing. The giveaway is salt stains or rings on your gear. See those, and that's a common sign you're getting dehydrated.

So sip water regularly the whole way, thirsty or not. And don't forget the electrolytes you're sweating out: you've got to top them up, and salty snacks are the go, cos they help your body hang on to fluids and stay in balance. Now, ya want to know what the inner canyon rangers, the ones who'd know, chuck in their daypacks? Pretzels, Pringles, chips and cheese crackers are some of their favourite salty snacks. Tasty tucker, ready for the track. Can't argue with that!

Fuel Your Body for the Trip
Make no mistake, a Grand Canyon walk is hard yakka, so eat enough to keep the engine running. Keep the snacks coming regularly, a mix of carbs, protein and electrolytes, and your energy'll hold up. Eat and drink even when you're not thirsty or peckish, cos your body won't always tell you when the tank's getting low. Get the balance of water and tucker right and you won't end up buggered, and your body'll keep working at its best.

Look after your water, your tucker and your rest, and you'll have a heaps better time out on the tracks. Worth every step.

But here's the catch. Even if you're eating and drinking like a champion, you still need to stay out of the full sun in the hottest part of the day. It's 15F to 20F (9C-11C) degrees hotter in the sun than the posted shade temperatures say. And keep in mind, the further down into the canyon you go, the hotter it gets! It's like walking into an oven, a bit at a time.

So plan your day so you're not walking between the hours of 10am and 4pm. Find a spot near shade and water, have a break, and sit out the worst of the heat. Be up and off before dawn, and finish late in the arvo when it's cooled down a bit. The experienced desert bushwalkers will tell ya: when you walk is the biggest thing in staying out of strife. And here's a number to think about. Most of the people who end up needing emergency medical help in the canyon because of heat illness are walking between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. Don't be one of them.

KEEP YOURSELF SOAKING WET TO STAY COOL.

Now this is the trick I reckon most people miss. One of the best ways to keep your body temperature in check on a Grand Canyon bushwalk is to stay soaking wet. Wringing wet, the whole way. The heat's fierce, especially in the inner canyon, and it can send your core body temperature up fast, so grab every chance you get to cool down. Any time you're near water, a creek or a water station, stop and give your clothes and yourself a good soaking.

Wear clothes that hold water well (cotton is ripper in the desert!), and if you can, wear a hat or a bandanna you can dunk often. Simple as, and it works a treat for staying cool and keeping your strength up all day. Down in the canyon, where the temperatures can really climb, take every chance to get wet, cos it'll make your walk a heap more comfortable. Come the end of the day you'll feel the difference, cooler and fresher than you'd ever expect.

And don't go thinking staying soaking wet is just about feeling nice. It's a critical tool for holding your energy levels and steering clear of heat exhaustion, which is no joke. So don't skip this one. It's simple, but it's vital, and it's how you cool off and walk out happy!
