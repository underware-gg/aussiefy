Bushwalking in the Grand Canyon throws up its own challenges, because of the heat, the height, and how hard the country is on your body. To have a good, safe walk, you need to stay on top of your water and your tucker.

Stay Hydrated and Keep Your Energy Up

As you walk, especially uphill and in full sun, you've got to keep your fluids up. In the Grand Canyon's hot, dry climate, sweat dries off quick, so you mightn't realise how much fluid you're losing. Keep an eye out for salt stains or rings on your gear, a common sign of dehydration.

To stay properly hydrated, sip water regularly the whole way, even if you don't feel thirsty. It's just as important to top up the electrolytes you've lost by eating salty snacks, which help your body hang on to fluids and stay in balance. Wondering what inner canyon rangers chuck in their daypacks? Some of their favourite salty snacks are pretzels, Pringles, chips and cheese crackers: tasty tucker that's ready for the track!

Fuel Your Body for the Trip
Bushwalking in the Grand Canyon can be hard yakka, so make sure you eat enough to keep your body going. Having snacks regularly, with a mix of carbs, protein and electrolytes, will help keep you going. Drink and eat even if you're not feeling thirsty or hungry: your body won't always tell you when it's time to refuel. Getting the balance of water and tucker right is the key to not getting buggered and to keeping your body working at its best.

By staying on top of your water, your tucker and your rest, you'll help make your time on the tracks heaps more enjoyable and worth it.

Even if you are eating and drinking right, you still need to stay out of the full sun during the hottest part of the day. Temperatures in the sun are 15F to 20F (9C-11C) degrees hotter than the posted shade temperatures. And keep in mind, the further into the canyon you go, the hotter it gets!

Plan your day so you are not walking between the hours of 10am and 4pm. Take a break near shade and water to dodge the worst of the heat. Get going before dawn and finish up late in the arvo. Experienced desert bushwalkers know that when they walk is the most important thing in staying out of strife. Most of the people who need emergency medical help in the canyon because of heat illness are walking between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m.

KEEP YOURSELF SOAKING WET TO STAY COOL.

One of the best ways to hold your body temperature in check while bushwalking in the Grand Canyon is to stay soaking wet. The fierce heat, especially in the inner canyon, can push your core body temperature up fast, so you've got to grab every chance to cool down. Whenever you're near water, whether it's a creek or a water station, take the time to soak your clothes and yourself.

Wear clothes that hold water well (cotton is ripper in the desert!), and if you can, wear a hat or bandanna that you can soak often. This is a simple but handy way to stay cool and keep your strength up all day. When walking in the canyon, where temperatures can shoot up, take every chance to get wet, as this will make your walk a heap more comfortable. By the end of the day, you'll feel the difference, with your body staying cooler and fresher.

Remember, staying soaking wet isn't just about feeling better: it's a critical tool to help you hold your energy levels and avoid heat exhaustion, so don't skip this simple but vital way of cooling off!
