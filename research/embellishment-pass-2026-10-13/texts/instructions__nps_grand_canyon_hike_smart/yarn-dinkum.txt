Bushwalking in the Grand Canyon's a different beast, what with the heat, the height, and how tough the country is on your body. If you want a beaut walk and a safe one, you've got to look after your water and your tucker properly.

Stay Hydrated and Keep Your Energy Up

While you're walking, especially uphill and out in the full sun, keeping your fluids up is a must. The Grand Canyon's hot and dry, and your sweat dries off that quick you mightn't even realise how much fluid you're losing. Keep an eye out for salt stains or rings on your gear: that's a common sign you're getting dehydrated.

So sip water regularly the whole way, even if you don't feel thirsty. Topping up the electrolytes you've sweated out matters just as much, and salty snacks do the job, cos they help your body hang on to fluids and stay in balance. Wondering what the inner canyon rangers chuck in their daypacks? A few of their favourite salty snacks are pretzels, Pringles, chips and cheese crackers. Tasty tucker, and ready for the track!

Fuel Your Body for the Trip
A walk in the Grand Canyon can be hard yakka, so make sure you're eating enough to keep the engine running. Have snacks regularly, with a mix of carbs, protein and electrolytes, and your energy will hold up. Eat and drink even when you're not thirsty or peckish, cos your body won't always let you know when it's time to refuel. Get the balance of water and tucker right and you won't get buggered, and your body will keep working at its best.

Stay on top of your water, your tucker and your rest, and you'll have a heaps better time out on the tracks, and it'll be worth the effort.

Mind you, even if you're eating and drinking right, you still want to stay out of the full sun in the hottest part of the day. It's 15F to 20F (9C-11C) degrees hotter in the sun than the posted shade temperatures. And keep in mind, the further down into the canyon you go, the hotter it gets!

So plan your day so you're not walking between the hours of 10am and 4pm. Take a break somewhere near shade and water to sit out the worst of the heat. Get an early start before dawn and finish up late in the arvo. Experienced desert bushwalkers know that when you walk is the biggest thing in staying out of strife. Most of the people who end up needing emergency medical help in the canyon because of heat illness are walking between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m.

KEEP YOURSELF SOAKING WET TO STAY COOL.

One of the best ways to keep your body temperature in check on a Grand Canyon bushwalk is to stay soaking wet. The heat's fierce, especially in the inner canyon, and it can send your core body temperature up fast, so grab every chance you get to cool down. Any time you're near water, whether it's a creek or a water station, take the time to give your clothes and yourself a good soaking.

Wear clothes that hold water well (cotton is ripper in the desert!), and if you can, wear a hat or bandanna you can wet down often. It's a simple trick but a good one for staying cool and keeping your strength up all day. Down in the canyon, where the temperatures can really climb, take every chance to get wet, cos it'll make your walk a heap more comfortable. By the end of the day you'll feel the difference, with your body cooler and fresher.

And remember, staying soaking wet isn't just about feeling better. It's a critical tool for holding your energy levels and avoiding heat exhaustion, so don't skip this simple but vital way of cooling off!
