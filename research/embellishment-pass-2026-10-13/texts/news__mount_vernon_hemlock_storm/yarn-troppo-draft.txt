Now, ya might've heard this one. That whopper of a winter storm that belted the northeast of the United States on Friday? It only went and flattened a 227-year-old tree at Mount Vernon in Virginia, the estate of the first US President George Washington, no less. As of yesterday, the mob in charge of the Mount Vernon estate say they've gathered up the whole Canadian hemlock to keep it, and they've got special projects lined up for it. Not going in the woodheap, this one.

Mind you, the old girl had a good innings. Planted in 1791, she finally got knocked flat on Friday by gusts of reportedly up to 70 mph (about 110 km/h). That's a bit breezy, I reckon.

Now here's the bit I like. Mount Vernon was Washington's own patch, and he ran most of his two terms as president from there, between 1789–1797, cos the first White House wasn't even finished yet. Imagine running the joint from your back paddock. And according to the Mount Vernon mob, this hemlock's the best documented tree on the whole place. Turns out it was a pressie from New York Governor George Clinton, and how do ya reckon he sent it? In a whisky barrel. Fair dinkum.

And before anyone asks: no, the New York governor's no relation to the 42nd President Bill Clinton. But old George Clinton did all right for himself, mind. He ended up as the United States's Vice President, under the Thomas Jefferson and James Madison governments, between 1805–1812.

It's been a rough trot for the famous old US trees lately, too. The seventh President Andrew Jackson's southern magnolia at the White House has come down, and so has the Pioneer Cabin giant sequoia in California's Calaveras Big Trees State Park, the big fella famous for the car-sized tunnel cut right through its trunk. Ya could drive through it, apparently. Not anymore, but.
