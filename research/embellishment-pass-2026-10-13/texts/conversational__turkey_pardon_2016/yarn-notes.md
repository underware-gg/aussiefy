## deadset

- Wrong-sense planned phrases, declined:
  - "chook" for "chicken" in "proved that they weren't chicken": that's the coward sense and a pun; "weren't chook" means nothing.
  - "Yep" for "Yes" in "Yes, We Cran": a pun on the "Yes, We Can" slogan; the genre says keep a speech's puns intact. Lint then suggested "yeah"/"goodo" for the same "Yes" again.
  - "every bastard" for "everybody who's trying to get a second chance": it closes a list of the poor, the sick and refugees, and turns compassion into contempt.
  - "little buggers" for "children" in a sober statistic (20 million insured, "including millions of children"). Lint then suggested it again for my "kids".
- Kept "the battlers" for "the poor" (Aussie political speech does say that), "reckon" for the first "I believe", "every man and his dog has a feed", "some bugger at yer table", "arvo", "heaps", "Beaut one".
- Lint suggestion false positive: "come on" (to a crying baby, "Now, come on.") read as BE_FAIR and offered "fair suck of the sav", "fair crack of the whip". It's soothing, not protesting.
- Lint flagged "chuffed" only as a HAPPY option list; it's more British than Australian, so I swapped to "rapt". The dictionary might mark "chuffed" as UK.
- Accepted first submit.
