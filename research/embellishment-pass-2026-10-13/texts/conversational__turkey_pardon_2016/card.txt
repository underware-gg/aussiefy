yarn-troppo: The fullest and most natural yarn, right on the level-5 brief with jokes that suit the speech; a couple of additions edge into new claims.
  minor: "not to be one bad day from a hospital bill they can't pay" (for 'who finally know the security of health insurance'): Adds a new claim about medical bills rather than colour on the original line.
  minor: "hopes they'll have it better than we did" (for 'we all have hopes for their better future'): Adds a comparison with the speaker's generation not in the original.
  minor: 'the crook and the oldies' (for 'the sick and the elderly'): "crook" as a noun strains.

yarn-dinkum: Polished, natural and faithful, with the jokes and the earnest middle intact; slightly lighter than a full level-5 yarn.
  minor: 'the crook and the oldies' (for 'the sick and the elderly'): "crook" as a noun for sick people is strained.
  minor: "There's heaps more we've got in common than what divides us." (for 'We have a lot more in common than divides us.'): "heaps" repeated several times across the text.

yarn-deadset: A faithful, natural level-4 retelling that keeps every joke and the earnest middle; a touch light for level 5 and leans on "heaps".
  minor: 'the crook and the oldies' (for 'the sick and the elderly'): "crook" is an adjective; "the crook" as a noun for sick people reads awkwardly and could suggest criminals.
  minor: "we've got heaps to be thankful for" (for 'we have a lot to be thankful for'): "heaps" is leaned on across the speech.
  minor: 'this is me last presidential turkey pardon' (for 'this is my final presidential turkey pardon'): "me" for "my" is used inconsistently alongside "my great privilege", so it reads as applied rather than voiced.

unharnessed: Faithful but light for level 5, with one awkward swear and thin, repeated slang.
  major: 'after a long arse of a campaign season' (for 'after a long campaign season'): Unidiomatic form of long-arse, and an Australian would wince at it in the blessings line.
  minor: 'the number of folks without health cover' (for 'our uninsured rate'): "folks" is American.
  minor: "can't pull his weight on the day" (for "can't fulfill his duties"): "pull his weight" implies slacking, not being unable to serve.
  minor: 'I reckon that to really live up to those ideals' (for 'I believe that in order to truly live up to those ideals'): Repeats "I reckon" from the sentence before.

engine: Mostly untranslated with interjections and swears bolted on; breaks the key puns and makes the solemn passages flippant.
  critical: "the brave turkeys who weren't lucky as fuck" (for "the brave turkeys who weren't so lucky"): Garbles the meaning; reads as if the turkeys were extremely unlucky or lucky, and is crude in the mock-solemn line.
  major: "proved that they weren't chook" (for "proved that they weren't chicken"): "chicken" means cowardly; "chook" loses the pun and the sense.
  major: 'Yep, We Cran' (for 'Yes, We Cran'): Breaks the pun on the "Yes, We Can" slogan, the punchline.
  critical: 'Oh, shit, and there are many families of servicemembers' (for 'And there are many families of servicemembers'): Swear tacked onto the most sombre line inverts its tone.
  major: "every bastard who's tryin' to get a second chance" (for "everybody who's trying to get a second chance"): Sounds contemptuous of the vulnerable people the line is about.
  major: 'millions of little buggers' (for 'including millions of children'): Wrong register for children covered by health insurance.
  minor: 'no matter what some drongo reckons' (added): Adds a jab not in the original.
  major: 'Jeez Louise, Malia and Sasha' (for 'Malia and Sasha, by the way'): Stock interjections bolted onto sentence starts throughout; stage-Australian.
  minor: 'Anyway, every year.' (for 'Every year.'): "Anyway" deflates the deadpan repetition the joke depends on.
