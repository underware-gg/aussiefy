Story goes, the Metropolitan Council of Nashville and Davidson County, way over in Tennessee in the southeastern bit of the U.S., has gone and made it official: fifty years, the 50th anniversary, since the first mob of Kurdish families stepped off into the U.S. And they've not just ticked a box, either. They've given the whole community a bloody good pat on the back for everythin' it's chipped in to the city, culture, social life, the economy, the whole works.

Now, VOA's got its mitts on the official proclamation, and accordin' to that, the first lot of Kurdish families landed in the United States on May 1, 1976. Heaps of 'em ended up puttin' down roots in Nashville, Tennessee. The rest scattered and set up their own communities in cities and states right round the country.

What the proclamation really rates is how the Kurds have hung on to their lingo, their culture and their traditions for five decades straight. Fifty years a long way from home and they haven't let it go. And all the while they've been pullin' their weight in Nashville growin' up and gettin' on.

Here's the kicker. Accordin' to the document, Nashville's home to more than 20,000 Kurds now, which makes it one of the biggest Kurdish communities in the whole United States. Not bad for a town most of us only know for country music, ay? The Big Smoke's turned into a proper hub for Kurdish culture and community life.

The proclamation gives a nod as well to the long-runnin' relationship between the United States and Kurdistan, and it singles out the Kurdish Peshmerga forces, U.S. troops and their allies for pitchin' in together in the fight against terrorism. Mates in a scrap, that lot.

So who got the ball rollin'? Metropolitan Nashville Council member Mike Cortese, who represents District 4. He led the official recognition of the anniversary, workin' hand in glove with the other councillors.

And the proclamation was signed, sealed and done on October 6, 2026. Good on 'em.
