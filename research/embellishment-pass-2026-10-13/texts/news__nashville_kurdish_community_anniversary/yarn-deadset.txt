The Metropolitan Council of Nashville and Davidson County, down in the southeastern U.S. state of Tennessee, has officially marked the 50th anniversary of the first mob of Kurdish families landin' in the U.S., givin' 'em a pat on the back for all they've chipped in to the city's cultural, social and economic development.

Accordin' to an official proclamation VOA got its mitts on, the first mob of Kurdish families landed in the United States on May 1, 1976. Heaps of 'em later put down roots in Nashville, Tennessee, while others set up communities in cities and states right across the country.

The proclamation gives a big rap to the Kurdish community for hangin' on to its language, culture and traditions over the past five decades, while pullin' its weight in Nashville's growth and development.

Accordin' to the document, Nashville's now home to more than 20,000 Kurds, which makes it one of the biggest Kurdish communities in the United States. The Big Smoke's become a big hub for Kurdish cultural and community life.

The proclamation also gives a nod to the long-runnin' relationship between the United States and Kurdistan, singlin' out how Kurdish Peshmerga forces, U.S. troops and their allies have pitched in together in the fight against terrorism.

Metropolitan Nashville Council member Mike Cortese, who represents District 4, led the official recognition of the anniversary, workin' hand in glove with other councillors.

The proclamation was signed on October 6, 2026.
