The Metropolitan Council of Nashville and Davidson County, over in the southeastern U.S. state of Tennessee, has gone and officially marked the 50th anniversary of the first mob of Kurdish families landin' in the U.S., and it's given 'em a proper pat on the back for everythin' they've chipped in to the city's cultural, social and economic development.

Accordin' to an official proclamation VOA got its mitts on, that first mob of Kurdish families landed in the United States on May 1, 1976. Heaps of 'em ended up puttin' down roots in Nashville, Tennessee, while the rest set up communities in cities and states right round the country.

The proclamation gives the Kurdish community a big rap for hangin' on to its lingo, culture and traditions these past five decades, all while pullin' its weight in Nashville's growth and development.

Now, the document reckons Nashville's home to more than 20,000 Kurds these days, which makes it one of the biggest Kurdish communities in the United States. The Big Smoke's turned into a real hub for Kurdish cultural and community life.

The proclamation gives a nod as well to the long-runnin' relationship between the United States and Kurdistan, and it singles out how Kurdish Peshmerga forces, U.S. troops and their allies have pitched in together in the fight against terrorism.

The bloke behind it was Metropolitan Nashville Council member Mike Cortese, who represents District 4. He led the official recognition of the anniversary, workin' hand in glove with other councillors.

The proclamation was signed on October 6, 2026.
