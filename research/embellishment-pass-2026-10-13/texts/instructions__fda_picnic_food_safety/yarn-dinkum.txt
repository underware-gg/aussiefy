Picnic and barbie season's got heaps going for it: plenty of chances to get outdoors and have a bit of fun with the family and your mates. Trouble is, all that warm weather gives foodborne bacteria plenty of chances to thrive as well. Once tucker heats up in summer temperatures, bacteria multiply quick smart.

So if you want to keep yourself, your family and your mates clear of foodborne illness through the warm-weather months, handling your tucker safely when you're eating outdoors is dead important. Read on for a few simple food safety guidelines for getting your tucker out to the picnic spot, and for preparing and serving it safely once you've rocked up.

Pack and Transport Your Tucker Safely

Keep your tucker safe the whole way: from the fridge/freezer — right out to the picnic table.

Keep cold tucker cold. Pop your cold tucker in an esky with ice or frozen ice bricks. Cold tucker needs to be kept at 40 °F (4 °C) or below so bacteria don't get a chance to grow. Meat, chook and seafood can go in while they're still frozen, so they stay colder for longer.

Sort out the esky. It's worth packing the drinks in one esky and the tucker that goes off in another. That way, when everyone's in and out of the drinks esky topping up, the tucker that goes off isn't copping the warm outdoor air.

Keep the eskies shut: Once you're there, open the esky as few times as you can. It'll keep what's inside colder for longer.

Don't cross-contaminate. Keep raw meat, chook and seafood well wrapped up, so their juices don't get onto prepared/cooked tucker or onto stuff that'll be eaten raw, like fruit and veg.

Give your fruit and veg a wash. Rinse fresh fruit and veg under running tap water before they go in the esky — even the ones with skins and rinds nobody eats. Firm-skinned ones want a rub under running tap water, or a scrub with a clean veggie brush while you rinse them under running tap water. Dry them off with a clean tea towel or a paper towel. If the packet says "ready-to-eat," "washed," or "triple washed," you don't need to wash it.

Quick Tips for Setting Up at the Picnic Spot

Food safety starts with a proper clean of your mitts — outdoors as well. Before you start laying out the spread, make sure your mitts and the surfaces are clean.

Cleaning Your Mitts Outdoors: No running water handy? Use a water jug, a bit of soap and some paper towels. Or grab some wet wipes to clean your mitts with.

Utensils and Serving Dishes: Keep all your utensils and platters clean while you're getting the tucker ready.

Follow Safe Barbie Tips

A barbie and a picnic often go hand in hand. And same as cooking indoors, there are a few important guidelines you should stick to so your barbecued tucker gets to the table safely.

Marinate safely. Marinate your tucker in the fridge — never on the kitchen bench or outdoors. And if you're planning to use some of the marinade as a sauce on the cooked tucker, put a bit aside separately before the raw meat, chook or seafood goes in. Don't reuse marinade.

Cook it straight away after "partial cooking." Partly cooking stuff before it goes on the barbie is only safe if the partly cooked tucker can go straight onto the hot barbie, like at home with the barbie out on the patio or deck.

Cook your tucker right through. When it's time to cook, have your food thermometer ready, and always use it to make sure the tucker's cooked right through. (See Safe Food Temperature Chart)

Keep the "ready" tucker hot. Once it's done, shift it to the side of the barbie grill, just away from the coals, and it'll stay hot till it's served. That keeps it hot without overcooking it.

Don't reuse platters or utensils. If you use the same platter or utensils that had raw meat, chook or seafood on them, bacteria from the raw tucker's juices can spread to the cooked tucker. Have a clean platter and utensils ready next to the barbie to serve up instead.
