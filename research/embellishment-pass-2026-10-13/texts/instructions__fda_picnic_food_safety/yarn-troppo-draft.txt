Picture this: picnic and barbie season, snags burnin' and the flies havin' a go, heaps of chances for a bit of outdoor fun with the family and your mates. Beauty. Trouble is, the foodborne bacteria reckon it's their season too. All that warm weather's a picnic for them as well, and as your tucker heats up in summer temperatures, the little mongrels multiply quick smart.

So if you want to keep yourself, the family and your mates off the dunny and clear of foodborne illness through the warm-weather months, handling your tucker safely when you're eating outdoors is dead important. No two ways about it. Here's the good oil on simple food safety guidelines for getting your tucker out to the picnic spot, and preparing and serving it safely once you've rocked up.

Pack and Transport Your Tucker Safely

Your tucker's got to stay safe the whole trip: from the fridge/freezer — right out to the picnic table.

Keep cold tucker cold. Sounds obvious, I know. Cold tucker goes in an esky with ice or frozen ice bricks, and it's got to sit at 40 °F (4 °C) or below, or the bacteria start growing. Meat, chook and seafood can go in still frozen, mind, so they stay colder for longer.

Sort out your eskies. Best plan is drinks in one esky, the one full of tinnies and half a bag of ice, and the tucker that goes off in another. That way, every man and his dog can be in and out of the drinks esky topping up all arvo, and the tucker that goes off isn't copping the warm outdoor air every time.

Keep the lid shut: Once you're there, open the esky as few times as you can. Every time that lid comes up, the cold goes out. Keep it shut and what's inside stays colder for longer.

Don't cross-contaminate. Raw meat, chook and seafood stay wrapped up tight, full stop. Otherwise their juices get onto your prepared/cooked tucker, or onto the stuff that'll be eaten raw, like your fruit and veg, and that's how you end up crook.

Give your fruit and veg a wash. Rinse fresh fruit and veg under running tap water before they go in the esky — even the ones with skins and rinds nobody eats, like your watermelon. Firm-skinned ones want a good rub under running tap water, or a scrub with a clean veggie brush while you rinse them under running tap water. Dry the lot off with a clean tea towel or a paper towel. And if the packet says "ready-to-eat," "washed," or "triple washed," you're right, no need to wash it.

Quick Tips for Setting Up at the Picnic Spot

Food safety starts with your mitts, and a proper clean of 'em — outdoors too, no excuses. Before you start laying out the spread, make sure your mitts and the surfaces are clean.

Cleaning Your Mitts Outdoors: No tap out there? Use a water jug, a bit of soap and some paper towels. Or grab some wet wipes and give your mitts a going-over with those.

Utensils and Serving Dishes: Keep all your utensils and platters clean while you're getting the tucker ready.

Follow Safe Barbie Tips

A picnic without a barbie is like a pie without sauce, so the two go hand in hand. And same as cooking in the kitchen, there are a few important guidelines you've got to stick to so your barbecued tucker gets to the table safely.

Marinate safely. The tucker marinates in the fridge — never on the kitchen bench, and never outdoors. And if you're planning to use some of the marinade as a sauce on the cooked tucker, put a bit aside separately before the raw meat, chook or seafood goes anywhere near it. Don't reuse marinade. Not once.

Cook it straight away after "partial cooking." Partly cooking stuff before it hits the barbie is only safe if the partly cooked tucker can go straight onto the hot barbie, like at home with the barbie right there on the patio or deck. Not half-cooked at home and then an hour in the car.

Cook your tucker right through. When it's time to cook, have your food thermometer ready, and always use it to make sure the tucker's cooked right through. Don't just eyeball it. (See Safe Food Temperature Chart)

Keep the "ready" tucker hot. Once it's done, shove it over to the side of the barbie grill, just away from the coals, and it'll stay hot till it's served. Keeps it hot without cooking it to a crisp.

Don't reuse platters or utensils. Use the same platter or tongs that had the raw meat, chook or seafood on them and the bacteria from those raw juices hitch a ride straight onto your cooked tucker. So have a clean platter and utensils ready by the barbie to serve up instead.
