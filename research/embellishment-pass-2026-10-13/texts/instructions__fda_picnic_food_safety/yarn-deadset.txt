Picnic and barbie season offers heaps of chances for outdoor fun with family and mates. But these warm-weather do's also give foodborne bacteria heaps of chances to thrive. As tucker heats up in summer temperatures, bacteria multiply quick smart.

To protect yourself, your family and your mates from foodborne illness in the warm-weather months, handling tucker safely when you're eating outdoors is dead important. Read on for simple food safety guidelines for getting your tucker to the picnic spot, and preparing and serving it safely once you've rocked up.

Pack and Transport Tucker Safely

Keep your tucker safe: from the fridge/freezer — all the way to the picnic table.

Keep cold tucker cold. Put cold tucker in an esky with ice or frozen ice bricks. Cold tucker should be kept at 40 °F (4 °C) or below to stop bacteria growing. Meat, chook and seafood can be packed while still frozen so they stay colder for longer.

Sort out what goes in the esky. Think about packing drinks in one esky and tucker that goes off in another. That way, as the picnickers open and reopen the drinks esky to top up their drinks, the tucker that goes off won't be exposed to warm outdoor air temperatures.

Keep eskies shut: Once you're there, cut down the number of times the esky gets opened as much as you can. This helps keep what's inside cold for longer.

Don't cross-contaminate. Make sure you keep raw meat, chook and seafood wrapped up tight. This stops their juices contaminating prepared/cooked tucker or tucker that'll be eaten raw, such as fruit and veg.

Clean your fruit and veg. Rinse fresh fruit and veg under running tap water before packing them in the esky — including ones with skins and rinds that aren't eaten. Rub firm-skinned fruit and veg under running tap water or scrub them with a clean veggie brush while rinsing with running tap water. Dry fruit and veg with a clean tea towel or paper towel. Packaged fruit and veg that's labelled "ready-to-eat," "washed," or "triple washed" doesn't need washing.

Quick Tips for Setting Up at the Picnic Spot

Food safety starts with giving your mitts a proper clean — outdoors too. Before you start setting out your picnic spread, make sure mitts and surfaces are clean.

Cleaning Your Mitts Outdoors: If you can't get to running water, use a water jug, some soap and paper towels. Or think about using wet wipes for cleaning your mitts.

Utensils and Serving Dishes: Take care to keep all utensils and platters clean when you're preparing tucker.

Follow Safe Barbie Tips

Barbies and picnics often go hand in hand. And just like cooking indoors, there are important guidelines you should follow to make sure your barbecued tucker gets to the table safely.

Marinate safely. Marinate tucker in the fridge — never on the kitchen bench or outdoors. Also, if you plan to use some of the marinade as a sauce on the cooked tucker, put some aside separately before adding the raw meat, chook or seafood. Don't reuse marinade.

Cook straight away after "partial cooking." Partial cooking before barbecuing is only safe when the partly cooked tucker can go on the hot barbie straight away, for example at a home with a barbie on the patio or deck.

Cook tucker right through. When it's time to cook the tucker, have your food thermometer ready. Always use it to make sure your tucker is cooked right through. (See Safe Food Temperature Chart)

Keep "ready" tucker hot. Barbecued tucker can be kept hot until it's served by moving it to the side of the barbie grill, just away from the coals. This keeps it hot but stops it overcooking.

Don't reuse platters or utensils. Using the same platter or utensils that held raw meat, chook or seafood before lets bacteria from the raw tucker's juices spread to the cooked tucker. Instead, have a clean platter and utensils ready next to the barbie to serve your tucker.
