_Terre Haute, Indiana, September 6._

MATE AL: Well Al old mate I spose you seen in the paper where I been sold to the White Sox. Fair dinkum Al it come as a shock to me and I bet it did to all you good old mates back home too. You could of knocked me over with a feather when the old man come up to me and says Jack I've sold you to the Chicago Americans.

I didn't have no idea nothing like that was on the cards. For five minutes I was just struck dum and couldn't get a word out.

He says We aren't getting what you're worth but I want you to go up to that big league and show them blokes there's a Central League on the map. He says Go and pitch the ball you been pitching down here and there won't be nothing to it. He says All you need is the guts and Walsh or no one else won't have nothing on you.

So I says I'd do me best and I thanked him for how I been treated in Terre Haute. They always done right by me here and even though I done more than my share I always felt my work was appresiated. We're finishing second and I done most of it. I can't help being proud of me first year's record in professional baseball and you know I'm not big-noting meself when I say that Al.

Well Al it'll feel funny being up there in the big show when I never really been in a big city before. But I reckon I seen enough of life not to be scared of the tall buildings eh Al?

I'll just give 'em what I got and if they don't like it they can send me back to the old Central and I'll be happy as Larry.

I didn't know nobody was running the eye over me, but one of the boys told me Jack Doyle the White Sox scout was down here having a squiz at me when Grand Rapids was in town. I beat them twice in that serious. You know Grand Rapids never had a hope against me when I was on song. I shut them out in the first game and they got one run in the second on account of Flynn misjuging that fly ball. Anyhow Doyle liked my work and he wired Comiskey to buy me. Comiskey come back with an offer and they excepted it. I dunno how much they got but anyway I'm sold to the big league and believe me Al I'll make good.

Well Al I'll be home in a few days and we'll have some of the good old times. Say g'day to all the boys and tell 'em I'm still their mate and not all up meself over this big league business.

Your mate, JACK.
