yarn-dinkum: A natural, faithful level 3 that keeps Jack's voice; the best balance of the set.
  minor: 'He goes Just pitch the ball you been pitching down here' (for 'He says Go and pitch the ball you been pitching down here'): "He goes" three times in a row reads as a tic.

unharnessed: Natural, well-pitched and varied; only the forced salutation jars.
  minor: 'MATE AL:' (for 'FRIEND AL:'): A letter heading is not slangified this way; it reads as forced.

yarn-deadset: Faithful and natural but light for level 3.
  minor: "I didn't have no idea that anything like that was coming off." (for "I didn't have no idea that anything like that was coming off."): Much of the letter is left unchanged, so the brief's weight is not met.

yarn-troppo: Lively and idiomatic, but over-embellished for level 3, with a tag-sentence tic.
  minor: "show them city fellas there's a Central League on the map" (for 'show those birds that there is a Central League on the map'): "city" is added; the original's birds are just big-leaguers.
  minor: 'Just telling it how it is.' (added): One of many tag sentences that render nothing; the pattern is a tic.
  minor: 'Not a bad wrap, ay?' (added): Added aside; "rap" is the usual spelling for praise.

engine: Largely untranslated with swears scattered in; some breaks of sense.
  major: 'all you bloody good old pals down home' (for 'all you good old pals down home'): "bloody" dropped in as filler; one representative quote for the swearing bolted on through the letter.
  minor: "there won't be bugger all to it" (for "there won't be nothing to it"): Strains the sense: "bugger all to it" can read as nothing in it, not that it will be easy.
  major: 'I will make bloody good' (for 'I will make good'): Breaks the idiom "make good"; an Australian would wince.
  minor: 'one of the young fellas told me' (for 'one of the boys told me'): "the boys" are his teammates; "young fellas" shifts who told him.
