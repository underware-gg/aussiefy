## B. Account Terms

**Short version:** _Personal Accounts and Organizations get different admin controls; a real human has to create your Account; you've gotta be 13 or over; you need to give a valid email address; and you can't have more than one free Account. You and only you are responsible for your Account and anything that happens while you're signed in to it or using it. It's on you to keep your Account secure._

### 1. Account Controls

* Users. Subject to these Terms, you keep the final say on admin control over your Personal Account and the Content in it.

* Organizations. The "owner" of an Organization that was set up under these Terms has the final say on admin control over that Organization and the Content in it. Within the Service, an owner can manage User access to the Organization's data and projects. An Organization can have more than one owner, but there has to be at least one Personal Account named as an owner of an Organization. If you're the owner of an Organization under these Terms, we reckon you're responsible for whatever gets done on or through that Organization.

### 2. Required Information

You've gotta give a valid email address to finish signing up. Any other info we ask for, like your real name, is optional, unless you're accepting these terms on behalf of a legal entity (in which case we need more info about the legal entity) or if you go for a [paid Account](#l-payment), in which case we'll need extra info for billing.

### 3. Account Requirements

We've got a few simple rules for Accounts on GitHub's Service.
* You've gotta be a human to create an Account. Accounts registered by "bots" or other automated methods aren't allowed. We do allow machine accounts:
* A machine account is an Account set up by an individual human who accepts the Terms on behalf of the Account, gives a valid email address, and is responsible for what it does. A machine account is used only for doing automated tasks. Multiple users can direct what a machine account does, but the owner of the Account is the one ultimately on the hook for the machine's actions. You can keep no more than one free machine account on top of your free Personal Account.
* One person or legal entity can keep no more than one free Account (if you want to run a machine account as well, that's fine, but it can only be used for running a machine).
* You've gotta be 13 or older. We're stoked to see bright young coders getting fired up about learning to program, but we have to follow United States law. GitHub doesn't aim our Service at kids under 13, and we don't allow any Users under 13 on our Service. If we find out about any User under 13, we'll [terminate that User's Account immediately](#m-cancellation-and-termination). If you live in a country outside the United States, your country's minimum age might be higher; if so, it's on you to follow your country's laws.
* Your login can only be used by one person — i.e., a single login can't be shared by a bunch of people. A paid Organization can only give access to as many Personal Accounts as your subscription allows.
* You can't use GitHub in breach of export control or sanctions laws of the United States or any other applicable jurisdiction. You can't use GitHub if you are, or are working on behalf of, a [Specially Designated National (SDN)](https://www.treasury.gov/resource-center/sanctions/SDN-List/Pages/default.aspx) or a person subject to similar blocking or denied party bans run by a U.S. government agency. GitHub may let people in certain sanctioned countries or territories get at certain GitHub services under U.S. government authorisations. For more info, have a look at our [Export Controls policy](/site-policy/other-site-policies/github-and-trade-controls).
