## B. Account Terms

**Short version:** _Personal Accounts and Organizations come with different admin controls; a human has to create your Account; you have to be 13 or over; you have to give a valid email address; and you can't have more than one free Account. You and only you are responsible for your Account and anything that happens while you're signed in to or using your Account. It's on you to keep your Account secure._

### 1. Account Controls

* Users. Subject to these Terms, you keep the final say on admin control over your Personal Account and the Content in it.

* Organizations. The "owner" of an Organization that was set up under these Terms has the final say on admin control over that Organization and the Content in it. Within the Service, an owner can sort out User access to the Organization's data and projects. An Organization can have more than one owner, but there has to be at least one Personal Account named as an owner of an Organization. If you're the owner of an Organization under these Terms, we count you as responsible for whatever is done on or through that Organization.

### 2. Required Information

You have to give a valid email address to finish signing up. Any other info we ask for, like your real name, is optional, unless you're accepting these terms on behalf of a legal entity (in which case we need more info about the legal entity) or if you go for a [paid Account](#l-payment), in which case we'll need extra info for billing.

### 3. Account Requirements

We've got a few simple rules for Accounts on GitHub's Service.
* You have to be a human to create an Account. Accounts signed up by "bots" or other automated means aren't allowed. We do allow machine accounts:
* A machine account is an Account set up by an individual human who accepts the Terms on behalf of the Account, gives a valid email address, and is responsible for what it does. A machine account is used only for doing automated jobs. More than one user can direct what a machine account does, but the owner of the Account is, at the end of the day, responsible for what the machine does. You can keep no more than one free machine account on top of your free Personal Account.
* One person or legal entity can keep no more than one free Account (if you choose to run a machine account as well, that's all good, but it can only be used for running a machine).
* You have to be 13 or older. While we're stoked to see switched-on young coders get excited about learning to program, we have to comply with United States law. GitHub doesn't aim our Service at kids under 13, and we don't allow any Users under 13 on our Service. If we find out about any User under the age of 13, we will [terminate that User’s Account immediately](#m-cancellation-and-termination). If you live in a country outside the United States, your country's minimum age may be older; if so, it's on you to comply with your country's laws.
* Your login can only be used by one person — i.e., a single login can't be shared by more than one person. A paid Organization can only give access to as many Personal Accounts as your subscription allows.
* You can't use GitHub in breach of the export control or sanctions laws of the United States or any other applicable jurisdiction. You can't use GitHub if you are, or are working on behalf of, a [Specially Designated National (SDN)](https://www.treasury.gov/resource-center/sanctions/SDN-List/Pages/default.aspx) or a person subject to similar blocking or denied party prohibitions run by a U.S. government agency. GitHub may let people in certain sanctioned countries or territories get into certain GitHub services under U.S. government authorisations. For more info, have a squiz at our [Export Controls policy](/site-policy/other-site-policies/github-and-trade-controls).
