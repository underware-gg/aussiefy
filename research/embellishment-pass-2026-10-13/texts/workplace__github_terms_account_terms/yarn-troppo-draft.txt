## B. Account Terms

**Short version:** _Here's one for ya, the whole box and dice on the back of a coaster. Personal Accounts and Organizations each get their own admin controls; a human has to make your Account, not a robot; you need to be 13 or over; you've got to hand over a valid email address; and you can't have more than one free Account, so no doubling up. Your Account is your lookout, full stop, and so is anything that happens while you're signed in to it or using it. And keeping your Account secure? Also your job, mate._

### 1. Account Controls

* Users. Subject to these Terms, you're the boss: you keep the final say on admin control over your Personal Account and every bit of Content in it.

* Organizations. Now, an Organization that was set up under these Terms has an "owner", and that owner gets the final say on admin control over that Organization and the Content in it. Inside the Service, the owner's the one who sorts out which Users can get at the Organization's data and projects. You can have a few owners on the one Organization if you like, but there's got to be at least one Personal Account named as an owner of an Organization. No owner, no Organization. And here's the kicker: if you're the owner of an Organization under these Terms, we reckon you're responsible for whatever gets done on or through that Organization. Whatever. So keep an eye on it.

### 2. Required Information

To finish signing up, all you need to give us is a valid email address. Simple as. Anything else we ask for, like your real name, is up to you, unless you're accepting these terms on behalf of a legal entity (in which case we'll need more info about the legal entity, fair enough) or you go for a [paid Account](#l-payment), in which case we'll need a bit of extra info for billing. Can't send a bill to nobody.

### 3. Account Requirements

GitHub's Service has only got a few simple rules for Accounts, and here they are.
* Only a human can create an Account. Accounts signed up by "bots" or other automated means? Tell 'em they're dreamin'. Machine accounts, though, those we're happy with:
* A machine account is an Account set up by an individual human, a real live one, who accepts the Terms on behalf of the Account, gives a valid email address, and is responsible for whatever it gets up to. A machine account is only for doing automated jobs, nothing else. Any number of users can boss a machine account around, but at the end of the day the owner of the Account carries the can for what the machine does. And on top of your free Personal Account, you can keep no more than one free machine account. One. That's your lot.
* One person or legal entity can keep no more than one free Account (if you want to run a machine account as well, no worries, but it can only be used for running a machine).
* You must be 13 or older. Look, we're stoked seeing switched-on young coders get fired up about learning to program, fair dinkum, but we've got to comply with United States law, and that's that. GitHub doesn't aim our Service at kids under 13, and we don't let any Users under 13 on our Service. If we find out there's a User under the age of 13, we will [terminate that User’s Account immediately](#m-cancellation-and-termination). No ifs, no buts. And if you live in a country outside the United States, heads up: your country's minimum age might be older, and if it is, complying with your country's laws is on you.
* Your login's for one person only — i.e., a single login can't be passed round the office between a few people. A paid Organization can only give access to as many Personal Accounts as your subscription allows, and not one more.
* Now the serious bit. You can't use GitHub in breach of the export control or sanctions laws of the United States or any other applicable jurisdiction. You can't use GitHub if you are, or you're working on behalf of, a [Specially Designated National (SDN)](https://www.treasury.gov/resource-center/sanctions/SDN-List/Pages/default.aspx) or a person caught by similar blocking or denied party prohibitions run by a U.S. government agency. GitHub may let people in certain sanctioned countries or territories get into certain GitHub services under U.S. government authorisations. Want the full story? Have a squiz at our [Export Controls policy](/site-policy/other-site-policies/github-and-trade-controls).
