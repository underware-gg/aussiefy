## B. Account Terms

**Short version:** _Personal Accounts and Organizations each come with their own admin controls; your Account has to be made by a human; you need to be 13 or over; you need to give us a valid email address; and you can't have more than one free Account. Your Account is your lookout, and so is anything that happens while you're signed in to it or using it. Keeping your Account secure is up to you, too._

### 1. Account Controls

* Users. Subject to these Terms, you keep the final say on admin control over your Personal Account and whatever Content's in it.

* Organizations. The "owner" of an Organization that was set up under these Terms has the final say on admin control over that Organization and the Content in it. Inside the Service, an owner gets to sort out which Users can get at the Organization's data and projects. An Organization can have a few owners, but there's got to be at least one Personal Account named as an owner of an Organization. And if you're the owner of an Organization under these Terms, we reckon you're responsible for whatever gets done on or through that Organization.

### 2. Required Information

To finish signing up, you'll need to give us a valid email address. Anything else we ask for, like your real name, is up to you, unless you're accepting these terms on behalf of a legal entity (in which case we'll need more info about the legal entity) or you go for a [paid Account](#l-payment), in which case we'll need a bit of extra info for billing.

### 3. Account Requirements

There's only a few simple rules for Accounts on GitHub's Service.
* Only a human can create an Account. No Accounts signed up by "bots" or other automated means, ta. Machine accounts are fine, though:
* A machine account is an Account set up by an individual human who accepts the Terms on behalf of the Account, gives a valid email address, and is responsible for what it gets up to. A machine account is only for doing automated jobs. More than one user can tell a machine account what to do, but at the end of the day the owner of the Account is responsible for what the machine does. On top of your free Personal Account, you can keep no more than one free machine account.
* One person or legal entity can keep no more than one free Account (if you want to run a machine account as well, all good, but it can only be used for running a machine).
* You must be 13 or older. Look, we're stoked to see switched-on young coders getting excited about learning to program, but we've got to comply with United States law. GitHub doesn't aim our Service at kids under 13, and we don't let any Users under 13 on our Service. If we find out there's a User under the age of 13, we will [terminate that User’s Account immediately](#m-cancellation-and-termination). If you live in a country outside the United States, your country's minimum age might be older; if it is, complying with your country's laws is on you.
* Your login's for one person only — i.e., a single login can't be shared round between a few people. A paid Organization can only give access to as many Personal Accounts as your subscription allows.
* You can't use GitHub in breach of the export control or sanctions laws of the United States or any other applicable jurisdiction. And you can't use GitHub if you are, or you're working on behalf of, a [Specially Designated National (SDN)](https://www.treasury.gov/resource-center/sanctions/SDN-List/Pages/default.aspx) or a person caught by similar blocking or denied party prohibitions run by a U.S. government agency. GitHub may let people in certain sanctioned countries or territories get into certain GitHub services under U.S. government authorisations. For the full story, have a squiz at our [Export Controls policy](/site-policy/other-site-policies/github-and-trade-controls).
