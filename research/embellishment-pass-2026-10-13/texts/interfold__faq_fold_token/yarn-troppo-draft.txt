# FOLD Token

Here's the lowdown on what FOLD actually does in the Interfold network. This one's for the token
holders, and for anyone who fancies running a ciphernode of their own. The questions come in three
lots: the role of FOLD, transferability, and ciphernode operation. And if it's the supply and
allocation numbers you're after, they live over at [FOLD Tokenomics](/tokenomics).

## The role of FOLD

### What role does FOLD play in the Interfold network?

So, the Interfold. It's a distributed network for confidential coordination. Applications sing out
for Encrypted Execution Environments (E3s), and the ciphernodes are the ones doing the yakka:
coordinating and securing every computation.

And FOLD? FOLD is the money side of it, what keeps the whole network's economics ticking over.

### What is the utility of the FOLD token?

FOLD's got three main jobs, and they're all tangled up in each other: ciphernode bonding, protocol
incentives, and governance.

**Ciphernode bonding.** Ciphernode operators bond FOLD to get eligible for network duties. Think of it
as putting your money where your mouth is. Bonded FOLD sits in the `BondingRegistry` contract. When the
bond owner (the wallet that funds the bond) unbonds it, the FOLD doesn't just walk out the door: it
lines up in an exit queue before the bond owner can claim it. Bonded FOLD may be subject to
protocol-defined withdrawal periods. And it can cop penalties too, for provable misbehaviour, or for
not pulling its weight on assigned responsibilities. Muck up, and it's your bond that pays. That's the
point of these mechanisms: they tie operational responsibility to economic accountability.

**Protocol incentives.** Now the good bit. Ciphernode operators get a share of the fees from completed
E3s. And on top of that, they may pick up FOLD incentives for successfully completing protocol-defined
duties as members of an E3 committee. Do the work, get a cut.

**Governance.** FOLD is expected to back governance participation through the Interfold DAO. That
could mean having a say on network parameters, protocol upgrades and treasury administration, the
whole box and dice. Governance processes may even include secret-ballot mechanisms that the Interfold
network powers itself, which is a neat trick when you think about it. For the current voting rules,
see [Governance](/governance).

## Transferability

### When did FOLD become generally transferable?

General FOLD transferability could be switched on at or after Aug 19 at **14:00 UTC (10 AM ET)**.

Here's how it worked: from Unix timestamp **`1787148000`** on, anyone at all could call the `tge()`
function on the FOLD contract to switch on general transferability. First in, best dressed. Once that
transaction went through, FOLD was generally transferable.

And someone was quick off the mark: that transaction ran on Aug 19, 2026 at 14:00:23 UTC, a whole 23
seconds after the gates opened. `tgeTimestamp()` on the FOLD contract returns `1787148023`, so FOLD is
generally transferable now.

Don't get the two mixed up, mind: general FOLD transferability and Network Alpha are two separate
milestones in the wider Interfold launch sequence. For more on that, see
[FOLD Transferability](/faq/transferability).

## Ciphernode operation

### How can I use FOLD to operate a ciphernode?

Running a ciphernode is meant to be permissionless: nobody's standing at the door with a clipboard.
There are four parts to the setup:

1. Provision cloud-based or personal hardware.
2. Run the ciphernode software.
3. Bond FOLD to the ciphernode.
4. Complete on-chain registration.

Once your ciphernode's registered, it can get a guernsey on E3 committees whenever the protocol rules
pick it. But to be active for committee selection, a registered ciphernode also needs a minimum ticket
balance. No ticket, no ride. See [Tickets and sortition](/operate/tickets-and-sortition).

For the current setup requirements, bonding instructions and operator documentation, have a squiz at
[Ciphernode Operators](/operate) and [Registration and bonding](/operate/registration).

### Can I delegate my FOLD to a ciphernode operator?

Nah, nah. The Interfold's bonding model is operator-only, and that's on purpose. Every FOLD bonded to
a ciphernode is the operator's own capital at risk, skin in the game for whoever runs it.

Let delegated capital in and operators could run part-owned ciphernodes. That would water down
accountability and weaken the network's economic security guarantees. Everyone's on the hook, so
nobody is.

In the `BondingRegistry` contract, each ciphernode has one bond owner, and only the bond owner can
bond FOLD to it. The operator picks the bond owner. The bond owner can be the operator address or a
separate wallet, say a cold wallet or a Safe.
