Microwaves are made inside the oven by an electron tube called a magnetron. The microwaves bounce around the metal inside of the oven, where they're soaked up by the tucker. Microwaves make the water molecules in food vibrate, and that makes the heat that cooks the tucker. That's why foods with heaps of water in them, like fresh veggies, cook quicker than other foods. The microwave energy turns into heat as the food soaks it up, and it doesn't make food “radioactive” or "contaminated."

Even though the heat's made right in the tucker, microwave ovens don't cook food from the "inside out." When thick foods are cooked, the outer layers get heated and cooked mostly by the microwaves, while the inside gets cooked mainly by the conduction of heat from the hot outer layers.

Cooking in the microwave can be more energy efficient than cooking the usual way, because the food cooks faster and the energy heats only the food, not the whole oven compartment. Microwave cooking doesn't cut the nutritional value of food any more than ordinary cooking does. In fact, tucker cooked in a microwave oven may hang onto more of its vitamins and minerals, because microwave ovens can cook quicker and without adding water.

Glass, paper, ceramic or plastic containers are used in microwave cooking because microwaves go straight through these materials. Even though these containers can't be heated by the microwaves, they can get hot from the heat of the food cooking inside. Some plastic containers shouldn't go in a microwave oven, because the heat of the food inside can melt them. Generally, metal pans or alfoil shouldn't go in a microwave oven either, as the microwaves bounce off these materials, which makes the food cook unevenly and can damage the oven. The instructions that come with each microwave oven say what sorts of containers to use. They also cover how to test containers to see whether or not they can go in microwave ovens.

Avoiding Injuries from Super-Heated Water in Microwave Ovens

The FDA has had reports in the past of serious skin burns or scalding injuries around people's hands and faces from hot water erupting out of a cup after it was overheated in a microwave oven. Super-heated water (water heated past its boiling temperature) doesn't look like it's boiling, and it happens when water is heated on its own in a clean cup. If the water's super-heated, a slight bump or movement, like picking up the cup or tipping in a spoonful of instant coffee, can set off a violent eruption, with the boiling water exploding out of the cup. Adding things like instant coffee or sugar before heating cuts this risk right down.

Users should closely follow the precautions and recommendations in the microwave oven's instruction manual, especially on heating times. Users should make sure they don't go over the recommended heating times when working out the best time settings to heat water to the temperature they want.
