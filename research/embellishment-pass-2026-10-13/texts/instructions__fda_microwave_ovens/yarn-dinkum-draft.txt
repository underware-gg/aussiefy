Now, the microwaves get made inside the oven by an electron tube called a magnetron. They bounce round the metal inside of the oven till the tucker soaks them up. What the microwaves do is set the water molecules in the food vibrating, and that makes the heat that cooks your tucker. That's why stuff with heaps of water in it, like fresh veggies, cooks quicker than other foods. The microwave energy turns into heat as the food soaks it up, and nah, it doesn't make your food “radioactive” or "contaminated."

The heat's made right there in the food, but that doesn't mean a microwave cooks it from the "inside out." With thick foods, the outside layers get heated and cooked mostly by the microwaves, and the middle gets cooked mainly by the conduction of heat coming in from those hot outside layers.

Cooking in the microwave can be more energy efficient than doing it the usual way, cos the food cooks faster and the energy only heats the food, not the whole oven compartment. And it doesn't knock the nutritional value out of your tucker any more than ordinary cooking does. In fact, food done in a microwave oven may hang onto more of its vitamins and minerals, cos the microwave cooks it quicker and without adding water.

You use glass, paper, ceramic or plastic containers in the microwave because the microwaves go straight through them. The microwaves can't heat those containers themselves, but they can still get hot from the heat of the food cooking in them. Some plastic containers shouldn't go in a microwave oven at all, because the heat of the food inside can melt them. As a rule, keep metal pans and alfoil out of the microwave too: the microwaves bounce off them, so the food cooks unevenly and you might damage the oven. The instructions that come with each microwave oven tell you what sorts of containers to use, and how to test a container to see whether or not it's right to go in.

Avoiding Injuries from Super-Heated Water in Microwave Ovens

The FDA has had reports in the past of serious skin burns and scalds around people's hands and faces, from hot water erupting out of a cup after it was overheated in a microwave oven. Super-heated water (water heated past its boiling temperature) doesn't look like it's boiling, and it happens when you heat water on its own in a clean cup. If the water's super-heated, the slightest bump or movement, like picking the cup up or tipping in a spoonful of instant coffee, can set off a violent eruption, with the boiling water exploding out of the cup. Put the instant coffee or sugar in before you heat it and that cuts the risk right down.

So follow the precautions and recommendations in your microwave's instruction manual closely, especially on heating times. When you're working out the best time settings to get the water to the temperature you want, make sure you don't go over the recommended heating times.
