Righto, here's one that's been doin' me head in: what's the sheila version of 'guys'?

Thing is, I chuck 'guys' around all day for a mob of blokes. It's casual, yeah, but it's not rude, it doesn't put anyone off, and it's not up itself or talkin' down to anyone. Mind you, I wouldn't front a bunch of coves at a board meetin' with it (hypothetically, course) unless I knew 'em. So here's the ones I've already crossed off as swaps:

- buddies: too old-fashioned, sounds like yer grandad

- dudes: too casual by a mile

- boys: too much talkin' down

- men, gentlemen: too formal, or the respect's as fake as a three-dollar note

Then it fair knocked me flat: I'd been usin' it on any mob of people at all, blokes 'n sheilas, even a room full of just sheilas. It worked for me, so I never gave the gender side of it a second thought, that the sheilas mightn't be all that keen on it. And no bugger ever whinged, not to me face and not behind me back either. Rule of life, that: sometimes people don't whinge about stuff they bloody well should.

But it dawned on me (I reckon I read somethin' that got the old noggin tickin') that the people I was sayin' it to mightn't all be keen on it.

So what's a sheila's version of 'guys', or one with no gender in it at all?

Here's the ones I've had a crack at that don't sit right with me (fair enough, some of 'em might be decent answers):

- dolls: too old-fashioned, straight out of an old black-and-white flick

- babes: too casual

- ladies: too formal, or, so I've heard from sheilas, too creepy, like a bloke leanin' on the bar

- women: too matter-of-fact

- girls: too much talkin' down (same as 'boys')

- everyone, you all: too plain, boring as

- y'all: not a skerrick of sexism in it, but too regional (might work, just not everywhere)

Hope I haven't knocked out every bloody option -- got any ideas, anyone?

* * *

So a bloke wanders in with an answer. Thing is, he goes, "guys" can be used in English with no gender at all, for a mixed mob of blokes 'n sheilas.

You'll even hear sheilas call other sheilas "guys."

The closest word with a bit of a sheila's lean to it'd be "gals." "Guys and gals" is a pretty casual take on "ladies and gentlemen." (Note the order's flipped. Sheilas first for the posh one, blokes first for the casual one.)

---

Then he's back with an edit: like @kitukwfyer pointed out in the comments on the question, "gals" can come across as a put-down when it's used on its own, so watch yerself. In the pairin' above it's not likely to be a problem. Use it carefully 'n it's prob'ly safe, but on its own it could be heard as takin' the piss.

Say this, and no one's gonna get their back up:

> "The gals had a yak in the kitchen while the guys went out to check out Fred's brand-spankin'-new ute."

But rock up to a couple of sheilas on a street corner with this one and ya might cop a funny look:

> "Excuse me gals, where's the supermarket?"

Try this on a couple of girls and they'll be wary of ya quick smart, and fair enough too:

> "Hey gals, come with me."

But if yer explainin' the rules of a game and ya come out with this, she'll be right:

> "Righto, guys on this side of the room, gals on the other."
