So what's the sheila version of 'guys'?

I chuck the word 'guys' around all the time for a mob of blokes, casual like. It's casual, yeah, but it's not rude or off-puttin', and it's not up itself or talkin' down to anyone (mind you, I wouldn't use it on a bunch of coves at a board meetin' (hypothetically, course) unless I knew 'em). So the ones I wouldn't count as swaps are

- buddies: too old-fashioned

- dudes: too casual

- boys: too much talkin' down

- men, gentlemen: too formal, or the respect's a bit fake

Then I twigged I'd been usin' it for any mob of people at all, blokes 'n sheilas, even just sheilas. Worked for me, 'n I never gave the gender side of it a thought, that sheilas mightn't be keen on it. No bugger ever whinged, not to me face and not behind me back either (rule of life: sometimes people don't whinge about stuff they bloody well should).

But it dawned on me (maybe I read somethin' that got me thinkin') that the people I was sayin' it to mightn't all be keen on it.

So what'd be a sheila's version of 'guys', or one with no gender in it at all?

Here's some I've had a think about that don't sit right with me (though fair enough, they might be decent answers):

- dolls: too old-fashioned

- babes: too casual

- ladies: too formal, or, so I've heard from sheilas, too creepy

- women: too matter-of-fact

- girls: too much talkin' down (same as 'boys')

- everyone, you all: too plain

- y'all: no sexism in it whatsoever, but too regional (might work, just not everywhere)

Hopefully I haven't knocked out every option -- got any ideas?

Thing is, "guys" can be used in English with no gender at all, for a mixed mob of blokes 'n sheilas.

You'll even hear sheilas call other sheilas "guys."

The closest word with a bit of a sheila's lean to it'd be "gals." "Guys and gals" is a pretty casual take on "ladies and gentlemen." (Note the order's flipped.)

---

Edit: Like @kitukwfyer pointed out in the comments on the question, "gals" can come across as a put-down when it's used on its own, so watch yerself. It's not likely to be a problem in the pairin' above. Use it carefully 'n it's prob'ly safe, but on its own it could be heard as takin' the piss.

Sayin' this is unlikely to get anyone's back up:

> "The gals had a yak in the kitchen while the guys went out to check out Fred's brand-spankin'-new ute."

But rock up to a couple of sheilas on a street corner and say this, and ya might get a funny look:

> "Excuse me gals, where's the supermarket?"

Say this to a couple of girls and they might get a bit wary of ya:

> "Hey gals, come with me."

But if yer explainin' the rules of a game and ya say this, it'll likely be fine:

> "Righto, guys on this side of the room, gals on the other."
