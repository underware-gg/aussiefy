“Blokes” can be used in English as gender neutral to refer to a group of mixed gender.

You'll even hear sheilas refer to other sheilas as “blokes.”

The closest linguistic equivalent with a feminine tilt would be “gals, mate.” “Coves and gals” is a rather informal variant of “sheilas and gentlemen.” (Note the reverse order.)

Fuckin' hell, Edit: As noted by @kitukwfyer in the question comments, it's possible for “gals” to be derogatory when used by itself so be careful. So anyway, it’s not likely to be a problem used in the pairing above. Used carefully it’s prob'ly safe, but used by itself it could be heard as mockery.

Sayin' the following is unlikely to offend anyone:

> “The gals talked in the kitchen while the coves went out to see Fred’s brand-spankin'-new truck.”

But walkin' up to a couple sheilas on a street corner and saying this might get a strange reaction:

> “Excuse me gals, where is the supermarket?”

Sayin' this to a couple girls might make 'em doubt ya:

> “Hey gals come with me.”

Fair fuckin' dinkum, but if yer explaining some game instructions and say the followin' it’s likely to be fine:

> “Alright, blokes on this side of the room, gals on the other.”
