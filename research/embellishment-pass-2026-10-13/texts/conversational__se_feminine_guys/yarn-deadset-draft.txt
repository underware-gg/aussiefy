What's a sheila's version of 'guys'?

I use the word 'guys' all the time for a mob of blokes, casual like. It's casual but not rude, off-puttin', up itself or talkin' down to anyone (though I wouldn't use it on a bunch of coves at a board meetin' (hypothetically, course), unless I knew 'em). So, some I wouldn't count as swaps would be

- buddies: too old-fashioned

- dudes: too casual

- boys: too much talkin' down

- men, gentlemen: too formal or fake respect

I twigged I'd also been usin' it for any mob of people, blokes 'n sheilas, even just sheilas. It worked for me 'n I never thought about the gender side of it, that sheilas mightn't be keen on it. No bugger ever whinged, straight out or on the quiet (rule of life: sometimes people don't whinge about stuff they should).

But it dawned on me (maybe from readin' somethin' that got me thinkin') that the people I meant it for mightn't all be keen on it.

What might be a sheila's or a no-gender version of 'guys'?

Some I've thought of that don't sit right (though these might be fair enough answers) are:

- dolls: too old-fashioned

- babes: too casual

- ladies: too formal or, I've heard from sheilas, too creepy

- women: too matter-of-fact

- girls: too much talkin' down (as much as 'boys')

- everyone, you all: too plain

- y'all: no sexism in it at all but too regional (might work, but not everywhere)

Hopefully I haven't knocked out every option -- any ideas?

"Guys" can be used in English with no gender to mean a mob of mixed blokes 'n sheilas.

You'll even hear sheilas call other sheilas "guys."

The closest word with a bit of a sheila's lean would be "gals." "Guys and gals" is a pretty casual take on "ladies and gentlemen." (Note the order's flipped.)

---

Edit: As @kitukwfyer pointed out in the comments on the question, "gals" can come across as a put-down when it's used on its own, so watch yerself. It's not likely to be a problem in the pairin' above. Used carefully it's prob'ly safe, but on its own it could be heard as takin' the piss.

Sayin' this is unlikely to get anyone's back up:

> "The gals had a yak in the kitchen while the guys went out to check out Fred's brand-spankin'-new ute."

But wanderin' up to a couple of sheilas on a street corner and sayin' this might get ya a funny look:

> "Excuse me gals, where's the supermarket?"

Sayin' this to a couple of girls might make 'em wary of ya:

> "Hey gals, come with me."

But if yer explainin' the rules of a game and say this, it's likely to be fine:

> "Righto, guys on this side of the room, gals on the other."
