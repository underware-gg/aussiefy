What's gotta be done before ya start the Car?

Before ya have a go at startin' the car, fill the radiator (take the cap off the top) with clean fresh water. If ya can't get water that's spotless, ya'd best strain it through muslin or some other cloth like it, to stop any muck gettin' in and bungin' up the little tubes of the radiator. The system holds about three gallons (around 11 litres). It's important the car isn't run under its own steam unless the water system's been filled. Pour the water in till yer sure both the radiator and the cylinder water jackets are full. The water'll run out the overflow pipe onto the ground once the whole water system's been filled properly. For the first few days ya drive a brand-spankin'-new car, it's a beaut idea to check the radiator often and make sure it's kept topped up. Top the water up as often as ya find ya need to. Soft rainwater, when ya can get it clean, beats hard water, which can have alkalies and other salts in it that tend to leave a gunk of sediment and clog the radiator. (See chapter on Cooling System.)

What about Petrol?

The ten-gallon (around 38-litre) petrol tank should be filled—nearly full—and never let run low. When fillin' the tank make sure there's no naked flames within several feet, cos the fumes are flamin' volatile and travel quick. Always watch yerself strikin' matches anywhere near spilt petrol, cos the air for several feet around is full of the fumes, and they go up like a bomb. Don't let the little vent hole in the petrol tank cap get bunged up, cos that'd stop the petrol flowin' properly to the carburettor. The petrol tank can be drained by openin' the pet cock in the sediment bulb at the bottom.

How about the Oilin' System?

When ya take delivery of the car, make sure some medium light, top-quality petrol engine oil gets poured into the crank case through the breather pipe at the front of the engine (there's a metal cap over it). Down under the car in the flywheel casing (the tank that holds this oil) you'll find two pet cocks. Pour the oil in slowly till it runs out the top cock. Leave the cock open till it stops runnin'—then shut it. Once the engine's been well and truly run in, you'll get the best results keepin' the oil at a level halfway between the two cocks—but no matter what, never let it get below the bottom cock. Every other part of the car is oiled properly when it leaves the factory. Still, it's worth checkin' that all the grease cups are full and that oil's gettin' to the parts that need it. (See Cut No. 18, also chapter on Lubrication.)

How are Spark and Throttle Levers used?

Under the steerin' wheel there's two little levers. The right-hand (throttle) lever controls how much mixture (petrol and air) goes into the engine. When the engine's runnin', the further this lever's pushed down toward the driver (called "openin' the throttle") the faster the engine goes and the more grunt it gives. The left-hand lever controls the spark, which sets off the petrol mix in the cylinders of the engine. Pushin' this lever forward "advances the spark," and it should be moved down notch by notch till the motor seems to hit its top speed. If the lever's pushed past this point you'll hear a dull knock in the engine. (See chapter on Ignition.)

Where should these levers be when startin' the Engine?

The spark lever should usually go in about the third or fourth notch of the quadrant (the notched half-circle the levers work on). The throttle should usually be opened about five or six notches. A bit of practice'll soon teach ya where these levers should sit for startin' properly. Take care not to advance the spark lever too far, cos the engine can "back kick."
