What's gotta be done before you start the Car?

Before you even try starting the thing, fill the radiator (take the cap off the top) with clean fresh water. If you can't get your hands on perfectly clean water, it's a good idea to strain it through muslin or some such, so no muck gets in and blocks up the little tubes in the radiator. The system holds about three gallons. Don't run the car under its own steam unless the water system's been filled, full stop. Keep pouring water in till you're dead sure the radiator and the cylinder water jackets are both full. When the whole water system's properly filled, the water'll start running out the overflow pipe onto the ground. For the first few days you're driving a new car, it's a good plan to check the radiator often and keep it properly topped up. Top up the water as often as you find you need to. Soft rainwater, if you can get it clean, beats hard water hands down, 'cause hard water can have alkalies and other salts in it that leave sediment and clog the radiator. How do you use the Spark and Throttle Levers?

Under the steering wheel there's two little levers. The right-hand one (the throttle) controls how much mixture (petrol and air) goes into the engine. When the engine's running, the further you pull this lever down towards you (that's called "opening the throttle"), the faster the engine goes and the more grunt you get. The left-hand lever controls the spark, which sets off the gas in the engine's cylinders. Pushing this lever on "advances the spark," and you move it down notch by notch till the motor seems to hit its top speed. Push it past that and you'll hear a dull knock in the engine. (See chapter on Ignition.)

Where do these levers go when you're starting the Engine?

The spark lever should usually go in about the third or fourth notch of the quadrant (the notched half-circle the levers work on). The throttle should usually be opened about five or six notches. You'll soon pick up where these levers ought to sit for a proper start once you've had a bit of a go. Be careful not to push the spark lever too far, though, or the engine might "back kick" and give you a hell of a whack.
