December 19th.

_Dear Daddy-Long-Legs_,

You never answered my question, and it was a really important one.

ARE YOU BALD?

I've got you all worked out--down to a tee--right up to the top of your head, and then I _am_ stuck. I can't make up my mind whether you've got white hair or black hair or sort of sprinkly grey hair, or maybe none at all.

Here's your portrait:

But here's the thing: do I give you some hair?

Want to know what colour your eyes are? They're grey, and your eyebrows stick out like a verandah roof (beetling, they call them in novels), and your mouth's a straight line that tends to turn down at the corners. Oh, see, I know! You're a cranky old thing with a temper, I reckon.

(Chapel bell.)

9.45 P. M.

I've got a new rule, and there's no breaking it: never, ever study at night, no matter how many written reviews are due in the morning. I read plain old books instead--I have to, you know, cos there are eighteen blank years behind me. You wouldn't believe it, Daddy, what a great big hole of ignorance my mind is; I'm only just working out how deep it goes myself. The stuff most girls with a proper family and a home and mates and a library just soak up, I've never even heard of. For example:

I never read "Mother Goose" or "David Copperfield" or "Ivanhoe" or "Cinderella" or "Blue Beard" or "Robinson Crusoe" or "Jane Eyre" or "Alice in Wonderland" or a single word of Rudyard Kipling. I didn't know Henry the Eighth was married more than once, or that Shelley was a poet. I didn't know people used to be monkeys, or that the Garden of Eden was a beautiful myth. I didn't know R.L.S. stood for Robert Louis Stevenson, or that George Eliot was a lady. I'd never seen a picture of the "Mona Lisa", and (ya won't believe this, but it's true) I'd never heard of Sherlock Holmes.

Now I know all that and heaps more besides, but you can see how much catching up I've got to do. And oh, it's good fun! I look forward to the evening all day, and then I hang an "engaged" on the door, get into my nice red dressing gown and furry slippers, pile all the cushions up behind me on the couch, light the brass student lamp at my elbow, and read and read and read. One book's not enough. I've got four on the go at once. Right now it's Tennyson's poems and "Vanity Fair" and Kipling's "Plain Tales" and--don't laugh--"Little Women." Turns out I'm the only girl at uni who wasn't brought up on "Little Women." I haven't let on to anybody, though (that _would_ mark me as a bit odd). I just quietly went and bought it with $1.12 of last month's pocket money; and next time somebody brings up pickled limes, I'll know what she's on about!

(Ten o'clock bell. This letter keeps getting interrupted.)

Saturday.

_Sir_,

I have the honour to report fresh explorations in the field of geometry. On Friday last we abandoned our former works in parallelopipeds and proceeded to truncated prisms. We are finding the road rough and very uphill.

Sunday.

The Christmas holidays start next week and the trunks are up. The corridors are so chockers you can hardly get through, and everyone's so excited that nobody's doing any study. I'm going to have a beaut time over the holidays; there's another first-year from Texas staying behind, and we're planning to go on long walks and--if there's any ice--learn to skate. Then there's still the whole library to get through--and three empty weeks to do it in!

Hooroo, Daddy, I hope you're feeling as happy as I am.

Yours ever,

JUDY.

P. S. Don't forget to answer my question. If you can't be bothered writing, get your secretary to send a telegram. He can just say:

Mr. Smith is quite bald, or Mr. Smith is not bald, or Mr. Smith has white hair.

And you can take the twenty-five cents out of my pocket money.

See ya in January--and a merry Chrissie!
