December 19th.

_Dear Daddy-Long-Legs_,

You never answered my question, and it was a big one. Don't think I didn't notice.

ARE YOU BALD?

I've got the whole picture of you sorted--and pretty pleased with it I am, too--right up until I hit the top of your head, and then I _am_ stuck. Stumped. Is it white hair? Black hair? That sort of sprinkly grey, like salt and pepper on a chop? Or maybe nothing up there at all, shiny as a billiard ball?

Here's your portrait:

But here's the problem, ay: do I give the poor bloke some hair or not?

Want to know what colour your eyes are? Grey. And your eyebrows stick out like a verandah roof, the kind you could shelter a cattle dog under (beetling, they call that in novels, if you don't mind), and your mouth's a dead straight line that wants to droop at the corners. Oh, I know, see! You're a cranky old thing with a short fuse, aren't ya?

(Chapel bell.)

9.45 P. M.

New rule, and it's set in concrete: never, ever study at night, no matter how many written reviews are coming in the morning. I read plain old books instead--I've got no choice, you know, cos there are eighteen blank years behind me. You wouldn't believe it, Daddy, what a bottomless pit of not-knowing my mind is; I'm only just finding out how far down it goes meself. All the stuff most girls with a proper family and a home and mates and a library just soak up without trying, I've never even heard of. For instance:

I never read "Mother Goose" or "David Copperfield" or "Ivanhoe" or "Cinderella" or "Blue Beard" or "Robinson Crusoe" or "Jane Eyre" or "Alice in Wonderland", or one single word of Rudyard Kipling. Not a word. I didn't know Henry the Eighth got married more than once (more than once! the man was a serial bridegroom), or that Shelley was a poet. I didn't know people used to be monkeys, or that the Garden of Eden was a beautiful myth. I didn't know R.L.S. stood for Robert Louis Stevenson, or that George Eliot was a lady. I'd never laid eyes on a picture of the "Mona Lisa", and--ya won't believe this, but it's true--I'd never heard of Sherlock Holmes. Sherlock Holmes!

Now I know the lot, and heaps more besides, but you can see how much ground I've got to make up. And oh, it's fun though! I look forward to the evening all day long, and then I whack an "engaged" on the door, climb into my nice red dressing gown and furry slippers, pile every cushion I own up behind me on the couch, light the brass student lamp at my elbow, and read and read and read. One book? Not enough. I've got four on the go at once. Right now it's Tennyson's poems and "Vanity Fair" and Kipling's "Plain Tales" and--don't you laugh--"Little Women." Turns out I'm the only girl at uni who wasn't raised on "Little Women." Haven't let on to a soul, mind (that _would_ mark me down as a real oddball). I just snuck off and bought it with $1.12 of last month's pocket money, quiet as a mouse; and next time somebody starts carrying on about pickled limes, I'll know exactly what she's on about!

(Ten o'clock bell. This letter gets interrupted more than a footy commentator.)

Saturday.

_Sir_,

I have the honour to report fresh explorations in the field of geometry. On Friday last we abandoned our former works in parallelopipeds and proceeded to truncated prisms. We are finding the road rough and very uphill. (Like a bush track after rain, Sir, and about as much fun.)

Sunday.

The Christmas holidays kick off next week and the trunks are up. The corridors are that chockers you can barely squeeze through, and everyone's so wound up with excitement that nobody's cracking a book. I'm going to have a ripper time over the holidays; there's another first-year from Texas staying behind too, and we're planning long walks and--if there's any ice going--learning to skate. Then there's still the whole library to get through--and three empty weeks to do it in! Bliss.

Hooroo, Daddy, I hope you're feeling as happy as I am, which is very.

Yours ever,

JUDY.

P. S. Don't forget to answer my question! If you can't be bothered writing, get your secretary to send a telegram. He can just say:

Mr. Smith is quite bald, or Mr. Smith is not bald, or Mr. Smith has white hair.

And you can take the twenty-five cents out of my pocket money. Fair's fair.

See ya in January--and a merry Chrissie!
