yarn-dinkum: The best balance: a natural, light Aussie layer that keeps every point and Judy's voice; a couple of small shifts.
  minor: "You're a cranky old thing with a temper, I reckon." (for "Oh, you see, I know! You're a snappy old thing with a temper."): The tag hedges a line whose joke is Judy's mock certainty.
  minor: 'down to a tee' (for 'very satisfactorily'): Usually spelt 'to a T'; and it shifts 'very satisfactorily' (pleased with her picture) to 'exactly'.
  minor: "everyone's so excited that nobody's doing any study" (for 'everybody is so bubbling over with excitement that studying is getting left out'): Overstates: studying is neglected, not abandoned, and 'bubbling over' is lost.

unharnessed: A well-judged light Aussie retelling, faithful and natural, with a couple of clunky phrases.
  minor: 'a sort of sprinkly grey sort of hair' (for 'sort of sprinkly gray hair'): 'sort of' doubled: clunky.
  minor: "the study's getting left by the wayside" (for 'studying is getting left out'): 'the study' reads as a room or an object, not studying.
  minor: "I've got it all worked out exactly what you look like" (for 'I have it planned exactly what you look like'): Awkward construction kept from the original's syntax after changing the verb.
  minor: 'Bye till January--and a merry Christmas!' (for 'Good-by till January--and a merry Christmas!'): Sign-off left plain after 'Hooroo' earlier; a missed spot, not an error.

yarn-troppo: Lively and varied but over-embellished for a level-2 brief, with some stage-bush colour and a misfiring simile.
  major: 'This letter gets interrupted more than a footy commentator.' (for 'This is a very interrupted letter.'): The simile doesn't work: commentators interrupt, they aren't interrupted.
  major: '(Like a bush track after rain, Sir, and about as much fun.)' (added): Added aside breaks the joke of the deadpan, formal 'Sir' report.
  minor: 'the kind you could shelter a cattle dog under' (added): Stock bush colour bolted on to a simile that was already complete.
  minor: "I'm only just finding out how far down it goes meself" (for 'I am just realizing the depths myself'): 'meself' is stage-Aussie for this voice.
  minor: "Don't think I didn't notice." (added): Added line; plus many similar asides make the text heavier than a level-2 brief.

  minor: 'Good-by till January--and a merry Chrissie!' (for 'Good-by till January--and a merry Christmas!'): Archaic 'Good-by' left beside 'Chrissie': a mixed register.
  minor: 'I find that I am the only girl at uni' (for 'I find that I am the only girl in college'): 'uni' among the original's formal phrasing reads as a word swap, not a voice.
