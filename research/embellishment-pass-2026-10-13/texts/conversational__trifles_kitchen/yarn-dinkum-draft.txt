SHERIFF: Sweet bugger-all in here, just kitchen stuff.

(_The_ COUNTY ATTORNEY _has another squiz round the kitchen, then opens the door of a cupboard. He climbs up on a chair and has a geek along a shelf. Pulls his hand back, all sticky_.)

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Well, here's a beaut mess.

(_The women edge in closer_.)

MRS PETERS: (_to the other woman_) Oh, her fruit; it's gone and frozen, (_to the_ LAWYER) She was worried sick about that when it turned so cold. Said the fire'd go out and her jars'd crack.

SHERIFF: Well, can you beat the women! Locked up for murder and she's fretting about her jam.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Before we're finished, I'd say she'll have a fair bit more than preserves to worry about.

HALE: Ah well, women are always fussing over little things.

(_The two women move in a bit closer together_.)

COUNTY ATTORNEY: (_laying it on like a young pollie_) Mind you, worries and all, what would we do without the ladies, ay? (_the women don't crack a smile. He goes over to the sink, scoops a dipperful of water out of the bucket, tips it into a basin and washes his mitts. Goes to dry them on the roller-towel, turns it round for a cleaner bit_) Dirty towels! (_kicks at the pans under the sink_) Not much chop as a housekeeper, would you say, ladies?

MRS HALE: (_stiffly_) There's heaps to do on a farm.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Fair enough. Still, (_with a little bow to her_) I know a few Dickson county farmhouses where you wouldn't see towels like that. (_He gives it a tug to pull the length of it out again_.)

MRS HALE: Those towels go grotty awful quick. Men's mitts aren't always as clean as they could be.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Ah, sticking up for the girls, I see. But you and Mrs Wright were neighbours. Reckon you were mates, too.

MRS HALE: (_shaking her head_) Haven't seen much of her these last few years. I haven't set foot in this house--it's more than a year now.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: And why's that? Didn't you like her?

MRS HALE: I liked her well enough. Farmers' wives have got their hands full, Mr Henderson. And then--

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Yeah--?

MRS HALE: (_looking about_) It never seemed a very cheery sort of place.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: No--it's not cheery, is it? I wouldn't say she had much of a knack for keeping house.

MRS HALE: Well, I dunno that Wright had much of one either.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: You mean the two of them didn't get on too well?

MRS HALE: Yeah, but nah, I don't mean anything by it. Only I don't reckon having John Wright about would make a place any cheerier.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: I'd like to have a bit more of a yarn about that later on. Right now I want to get the lay of the land upstairs. (_He heads off to the left, where three steps lead up to a stair door_.)

SHERIFF: I s'pose whatever Mrs Peters does'll be right. She was meant to take some clothes in for her, you know, and a few bits and pieces. We shot through in such a hurry yesterday.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Yeah, but I'd like to see what you take, Mrs Peters, and keep your eyes peeled for anything that might be some use to us.

MRS PETERS: Yeah, righto, Mr Henderson.

(_The women listen to the blokes' footsteps going up the stairs, then have a Captain Cook round the kitchen_.)

MRS HALE: I'd hate to have blokes traipsing into my kitchen, stickybeaking around and bagging the lot.

(_She straightens up the pans under the sink that the_ LAWYER _had shoved out of place_.)

MRS PETERS: Well, it's only their job, I s'pose.

MRS HALE: Job's all very well, but that deputy sheriff who came out to get the fire going could've given this a wipe while he was at it. (_gives the roller towel a tug_) Wish I'd thought of that sooner. Seems a bit rough, bagging her for not having the place schmick when she had to clear out in such a hurry.
