SHERIFF: Sweet bugger-all in here, mate. Just kitchen stuff. Pots and pans and that.

(_So the_ COUNTY ATTORNEY _has another squiz round the kitchen, can't leave it alone, and opens up a cupboard. Up he gets on a chair, has a geek along the top shelf, and pulls his hand back sticky as. Serves him right for stickybeakin'_.)

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Well, here's a beaut bloody mess.

(_The women creep in a bit closer for a look_.)

MRS PETERS: (_to the other woman_) Oh, her fruit; it's gone and frozen, the lot of it, (_to the_ LAWYER) She was worried sick about that when it turned so cold. Kept sayin' the fire'd go out and her jars'd crack.

SHERIFF: Well, can you beat the women! Locked up for murder, and what's she fretting about? Her jam!

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Mark my words, before we're finished she'll have a fair bit more than preserves to worry about.

HALE: Ah well. Women are always fussin' over little things, aren't they.

(_The two women shuffle in closer together. Not a word out of 'em_.)

COUNTY ATTORNEY: (_laying it on thick like a young pollie after a vote_) Mind you, worries and all, what would we blokes do without the ladies, ay? (_the women don't crack a smile. Not a flicker. So off he goes to the sink, scoops a dipperful out of the bucket, tips it in a basin and gives his mitts a wash. Goes to dry 'em on the roller-towel, turns it round lookin' for a clean bit_) Dirty towels! (_and he boots the pans under the sink for good measure_) Not much chop as a housekeeper, would you say, ladies?

MRS HALE: (_stiff as a board_) There's heaps to do on a farm, Mr Henderson.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Oh, fair enough. Still, (_givin' her a little bow, the smarmy so-and-so_) I know a few Dickson county farmhouses where you wouldn't catch towels like that. (_And he gives it another tug to show off the whole grotty length of it_.)

MRS HALE: Those towels go grotty awful quick. Blokes' mitts aren't always as clean as they could be, are they.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Ah, stickin' up for the girls, I see. But you and Mrs Wright were neighbours. Reckon you were mates, too.

MRS HALE: (_shakin' her head_) Haven't seen much of her these last few years. Haven't set foot in this house--more than a year now, it's been.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: And why's that, then? Didn't you like her?

MRS HALE: I liked her well enough. Farmers' wives have got their hands full, Mr Henderson. And then--

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Yeah--? Go on.

MRS HALE: (_lookin' about the place_) It never seemed a very cheery sort of joint.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: No--it's not cheery, is it? Wouldn't say she had much of a knack for keepin' house.

MRS HALE: Well, I dunno that Wright had much of one either.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Meaning the two of 'em didn't get on too well?

MRS HALE: Yeah, but nah, I don't mean anything by it. Only I don't reckon havin' John Wright about would make any place cheerier. Put it that way.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: I'd like a bit more of a yarn about that later on. Right now I want to get the lay of the land upstairs. (_And off he trots to the left, where three steps go up to a stair door_.)

SHERIFF: I s'pose whatever Mrs Peters does'll be right. She was meant to take some clothes in for her, you know, and a few bits and bobs. We shot through in such a flamin' hurry yesterday.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Yeah, but I'd like to see what you take, Mrs Peters. And keep your eyes peeled for anything that might be some use to us, will ya?

MRS PETERS: Yeah, righto, Mr Henderson.

(_And the women stand there listenin' to the blokes clompin' up the stairs, then have a good Captain Cook round the kitchen_.)

MRS HALE: I'd hate to have blokes traipsin' into my kitchen, stickybeakin' around and baggin' the whole box and dice.

(_And she straightens up the pans under the sink that the_ LAWYER _had booted out of place_.)

MRS PETERS: Well, it's only their job, I s'pose.

MRS HALE: Job's all very well, but that deputy sheriff who came out to get the fire goin' could've given this a wipe while he was at it. (_gives the roller towel a tug_) Wish I'd thought of that sooner. Seems a bit rough, baggin' her for not havin' the place schmick when she had to clear out in such a hurry, poor thing.
