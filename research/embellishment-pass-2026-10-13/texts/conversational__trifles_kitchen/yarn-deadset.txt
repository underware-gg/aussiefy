SHERIFF: Sweet bugger-all here but kitchen things.

(_The_ COUNTY ATTORNEY, _after another squiz round the kitchen, opens the door of a cupboard. He hops up on a chair and has a geek on a shelf. Pulls his hand away, sticky_.)

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Here's a beaut mess.

(_The women come a bit closer_.)

MRS PETERS: (_to the other woman_) Oh, her fruit; it did freeze, (_to the_ LAWYER) She was worried about that when it turned so cold. She said the fire'd go out and her jars'd crack.

SHERIFF: Well, can you beat the women! Locked up for murder and fretting about her preserves.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: I'd say before we're done she might have something a fair bit worse than preserves to worry about.

HALE: Well, women are used to fretting over little things.

(_The two women move a bit closer together_.)

COUNTY ATTORNEY: (_with the smarm of a young pollie_) And yet, for all their worries, what would we do without the ladies? (_the women don't soften. He goes to the sink, takes a dipperful of water from the bucket and, tipping it into a basin, washes his mitts. Starts to wipe them on the roller-towel, turns it for a cleaner bit_) Dirty towels! (_kicks his foot against the pans under the sink_) Not much chop as a housekeeper, would you say, ladies?

MRS HALE: (_stiffly_) There's heaps of work to be done on a farm.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Fair enough. And yet (_with a little bow to her_) I know there are some Dickson county farmhouses that don't have towels like that. (_He gives it a tug to show the length of it again_.)

MRS HALE: Those towels get grotty awful quick. Men's mitts aren't always as clean as they might be.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Ah, sticking up for the women, I see. But you and Mrs Wright were neighbours. I reckon you were mates, too.

MRS HALE: (_shaking her head_) I've not seen much of her these last few years. I've not been in this house--it's more than a year.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: And why was that? You didn't like her?

MRS HALE: I liked her well enough. Farmers' wives have their hands full, Mr Henderson. And then--

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Yeah--?

MRS HALE: (_looking about_) It never seemed a very cheery sort of place.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: No--it's not cheery. I wouldn't say she had the knack for keeping house.

MRS HALE: Well, I dunno as Wright had, either.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: You mean they didn't get on too well?

MRS HALE: Yeah, but nah, I don't mean anything. But I don't reckon a place'd be any cheerier for John Wright being in it.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: I'd like to talk more about that a bit later. I want to get the lay of the land upstairs now. (_He goes to the left, where three steps lead up to a stair door_.)

SHERIFF: I s'pose anything Mrs Peters does'll be right. She was to take in some clothes for her, you know, and a few bits and pieces. We cleared out in such a hurry yesterday.

COUNTY ATTORNEY: Yeah, but I'd like to see what you take, Mrs Peters, and keep an eye out for anything that might be some use to us.

MRS PETERS: Yeah, Mr Henderson.

(_The women listen to the blokes' steps on the stairs, then have a look round the kitchen_.)

MRS HALE: I'd hate to have blokes coming into my kitchen, stickybeaking around and bagging everything.

(_She straightens the pans under the sink that the_ LAWYER _had shoved out of place_.)

MRS PETERS: Course it's no more than their duty.

MRS HALE: Duty's all right, but I'd say that deputy sheriff that came out to light the fire might've got a bit of this on. (_gives the roller towel a tug_) Wish I'd thought of that sooner. Seems a bit rough to bag her for not having things schmick when she had to clear out in such a hurry.
