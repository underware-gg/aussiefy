## deadset
- Wrong-sense planned phrases:
  - "bugger all" for "Not much" in "Not much of a housekeeper". That idiom means a poor housekeeper, not a small amount, so "bugger all of a housekeeper" doesn't parse. I declined it and used "not much chop as" (from lookup).
  - "flamin'" for "very" in "didn't get on very well". "very" there softens a negative; "flamin'" makes a careful lawyer swear. Declined.
- Borderline planned phrases I kept:
  - "a beaut" for the sarcastic "Here's a nice mess". It still works as irony.
  - "Yeah" for Mrs Peters' deferential "Yes, Mr Henderson". It's a bit casual for her.
- Lookups that came back wrong or empty:
  - "mean" (unkind) returned "gridiron". The match seems to be on the gloss word "codes Australians mean by football".
  - "worry about" returned "no wukkas / no worries / she'll be right", which is the negated sense.
  - "cheerful" returned "sloshed".
  - "serious" returned "muck around".
  - "sticky" and "jars of preserves" came back empty.
- Good finds: "stickybeak around", "bag" (criticise), "schmick", "not much chop".
- The lint "reckon used 5 times" warning was useful. The plan maps every "suppose" and "guess" to reckon, so a faithful deadset piles it up.

## dinkum
- The ENGINE_DRAFT at dinkum drops "Crikey," and "Anyway," into the middle of stage directions ("Crikey, he gets up on a chair"). That's the wrong place for a talker's opener in a script.
- OPENINGS ("You'll like this one.", "No word of a lie,") don't suit a play script that has to keep its layout. "(start straight in with the action)" was the right choice.
- Lint suggested "hit the frog and toad / shoot through" for "heads off" (the lawyer walking to the stairs). Wrong sense: he isn't leaving.
- Same declines as deadset. Accepted on the first submit.
