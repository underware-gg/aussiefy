yarn-deadset: Faithful, natural and well weighted; keeps the play's restraint and its final barb. A couple of small wrinkles.
  minor: 'Sweet bugger-all here but kitchen things.' (for 'Nothing here but kitchen things.'): "Bugger-all ... but" is a clumsy construction; an Australian would say "bugger-all here, just kitchen stuff".
  minor: 'MRS PETERS: Yeah, Mr Henderson.' (for 'MRS PETERS: Yes, Mr Henderson.'): Mrs Peters' deference to the attorney is better kept with "Yes".

unharnessed: Faithful, tonally sound and natural, a touch light for level 4, with one strained exclamation.
  major: "Well, wouldn't it rot your socks, women!" (for 'Well, can you beat the women!'): "Wouldn't it rot your socks" is said of a situation; tacking "women!" on makes an awkward, strained line.
  minor: 'sticking up for the sisterhood, I see' (for 'Ah, loyal to your sex, I see.'): "Sisterhood" is a modern, feminist-era word, odd in the attorney's mouth here.
  minor: 'spick and span' (for 'slicked up'): Neutral English where an Aussie word (schmick) would fit the brief.

yarn-dinkum: Natural, fluent and at the right weight; one reversal in the final towel line costs meaning.
  critical: "could've given this a wipe while he was at it" (for 'might have got a little of this on'): Reverses the point: Mrs Hale suggests the deputy dirtied the towel, not that he should have cleaned it; the defence of Mrs Wright is lost.
  minor: 'We shot through in such a hurry yesterday.' (for 'We left in such a hurry yesterday.'): "Shot through" suggests absconding; "cleared out" fits a sheriff leaving in haste better.
  minor: 'have a Captain Cook round the kitchen' (for 'look about the kitchen'): Rhyming slang in a stage direction is a little stock, after "squiz" and "geek".

yarn-troppo: Lively and natural, but heavier than asked, editorialises the stage directions, and reverses the final towel line.
  critical: "could've given this a wipe while he was at it" (for 'might have got a little of this on'): Reverses the point: the deputy is suspected of dirtying the towel, not blamed for not cleaning it.
  major: "Serves him right for stickybeakin'" (for 'Pulls his hand away, sticky.'): Adds an editorial judgement to a stage direction; the play's restraint is lost.
  minor: "givin' her a little bow, the smarmy so-and-so" (for 'with a little bow to her'): Narrator name-calling in the direction spells out subtext.
  minor: 'laying it on thick like a young pollie after a vote' (for 'with the gallantry of a young politician'): "after a vote" adds a detail the original lacks.
  minor: 'Sweet bugger-all in here, mate. Just kitchen stuff. Pots and pans and that.' (for 'Nothing here but kitchen things.'): Padding beyond a level 4 brief.

engine: Mostly the original with tags and swaps dropped in; several wrong-sense swaps and filler tics, uneven weight, and the tone of the scene suffers.
  major: 'bugger all of a housekeeper, would you say, ladies, ay?' (for 'Not much of a housekeeper, would you say, ladies?'): "bugger all" means nothing, not "not much"; the swap is ungrammatical and the trailing "ay?" after the question is redundant.
  major: "You mean that they didn't get on flamin' well?" (for "You mean that they didn't get on very well?"): An intensifier in a careful lawyer's probing question changes its sense and register.
  major: 'Crikey, he gets up on a chair' (for 'He gets up on a chair'): Stage-Australian exclamation inserted into a neutral stage direction.
  major: 'what would we do without the ladies, mate?' (for 'what would we do without the ladies?'): Calling the women "mate" while addressing "the ladies" is a wrong-sense tag.
  minor: 'Anyway, seems mean to talk about her' (for 'Seems mean to talk about her'): Filler "Anyway," used as a tic across the text, adding nothing.
  minor: 'Those towels get grotty bloody awful quick.' (for 'Those towels get dirty awful quick.'): Stacked intensifiers make a pile-up, and the swear sits oddly in Mrs Hale's mouth.
  minor: 'MRS PETERS: Yeah, Mr Henderson.' (for 'MRS PETERS: Yes, Mr Henderson.'): Mrs Peters is deferential; a casual "Yeah" to the attorney loses that.
