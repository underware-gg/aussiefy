Where do I book somewhere to stay in Yellowstone?
Yellowstone National Park Lodges runs the nine lodges in the park. They're all open from late spring through autumn, but only two are open in winter: Old Faithful Snow Lodge and Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel. Make sure you get your booking in early!

Do I need a booking to get into the park?
You don't need a vehicle booking to get into the park – just a park entrance pass. Save yourself some time at the entrance stations by buying your pass online before you lob. Keep in mind summer's flat chat, so be ready for crowds at the popular spots and queues at the entrance gates, at the roadworks, and wherever folks have pulled over to look at wildlife. Please be patient and enjoy your visit.

How did Yellowstone get its name?
Yellowstone National Park is named after the Yellowstone River, the main river running through the park. According to French-Canadian trappers in the 1800s, they asked the Minnetaree tribe, who live in what's now eastern Montana, what the river was called. They answered "Mi tse a-da-zi," which means, word for word, "Rock Yellow River." The trappers put this into French as "Roche Jaune" or "Pierre Jaune." In 1797, explorer-geographer David Thompson used the English version—"Yellow Stone." Lewis and Clark called the Yellowstone River by both the French and English names. Over time the name stuck as "Yellowstone."

Is Yellowstone the biggest national park?
Nah, nah. More than half of Alaska's national park units are bigger, including Wrangell–St. Elias National Park and Preserve, which is the biggest unit (13 million acres, about 5.3 million hectares) in the National Park System. Until 1994, Yellowstone (at 2.2 million acres, about 890,000 hectares) was the biggest national park in the lower 48 of the United States. That year Death Valley National Monument was made bigger and became a national park—it's got more than 3 million acres (about 1.2 million hectares).

Is Yellowstone the most visited national park?
Yellowstone is in the top five national parks for the number of people visiting for a bit of fun outdoors. Great Smoky Mountains National Park often gets the most. Go to https://irma.nps.gov/Stats/ to see the latest figures for all national parks.

What's the difference between a national park and a national forest?
National parks are run by the Department of the Interior and national forests by the Department of Agriculture. The National Park Service's job is to keep resources as they are, unharmed, while the Forest Service's job is to look after resources sensibly for heaps of uses that can keep going. Six national forests surround Yellowstone National Park.

How many rangers work in Yellowstone?
About 750 people work for the National Park Service in the busiest part of summer. About 392 are permanent staff who work all year round. Park rangers work in education, looking after resources, law enforcement, emergency medical services, and work out in the backcountry. Other staff specialise in research, maintenance, management, admin, track maintenance, fire management, and collecting fees.

How do you become a park ranger?
Heaps of National Park Service staff start out as volunteers or as seasonal workers. Getting a job is very competitive and it's handled through the Office of Personnel Management. Find out more about working with us.

Can we swim in rivers and lakes?
Swimming isn't recommended because most lakes and creeks are dangerously cold.

What's the highest peak in the park?
Eagle Peak in the south-east of Yellowstone is the highest at 11,358 feet (3,462 metres).

Why is Yellowstone called a biosphere reserve and a world heritage site?
The United Nations named Yellowstone National Park a biosphere reserve and a world heritage site because its natural and cultural resources matter to the whole world.

What's the Continental Divide?
Think of the Continental Divide as the top of the ridge of the continent. In theory, when rain or snow falls on the west side of the Divide, it ends up in the Pacific Ocean. When it falls on the east side of the Divide, it ends up in the Atlantic Ocean. In Yellowstone (as everywhere else), this ridgeline isn't straight. You cross the Continental Divide three times between the South Entrance and the Old Faithful area. Craig Pass is the highest crossing, at 8,262 feet (2,518 metres).

How did Mt. Washburn form?
At 10,219 feet (3,115 metres), this peak can be seen from lots of spots in the park. It's what's left of an extinct volcano from the Absaroka Volcanics of about 50 million years ago. The volcano was literally cut in half by a volcanic eruption 631,000 years ago. Only the northern part of the original volcano can still be seen.

Does Yellowstone include a federally designated wilderness?
Nah. Most of the park was recommended for this designation in 1972, but Congress hasn't acted on the recommendation.
