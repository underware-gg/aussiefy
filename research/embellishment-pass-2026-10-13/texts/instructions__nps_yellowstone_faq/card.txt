yarn-dinkum: Faithful, natural and at the right weight; the strongest balance of meaning and voice.
  minor: 'The story goes, according to French-Canadian trappers back in the 1800s, that they asked' (for 'According to French-Canadian trappers in the 1800s, they asked'): Doubled framing ("The story goes, according to") makes a slightly tangled sentence.
  minor: 'the biggest national park in the lower 48 of the United States' (for 'the largest national park in the contiguous United States'): "Lower 48" is an American idiom, not an Australian one.

yarn-troppo: Fluent, natural and lively; a shade heavier than asked, with a few additions that edge past colour.
  major: 'Stay out.' (for 'Swimming is not recommended because most lakes and streams are dangerously cold.'): Hardens a recommendation into a ban.
  minor: 'Lewis and Clark, not wanting to pick sides' (for 'Lewis and Clark called the Yellowstone River by the French and English forms.'): Invents a motive the original does not give.
  minor: "wherever someone's spotted a bison and pulled over for a squiz" (for 'at roadside wildlife sightings'): Narrows wildlife to bison; small invented detail.
  minor: 'Two! In a park that size!' (for 'but only two are open in the winter'): Commentary that pushes past the level 4 weight.

unharnessed: Faithful and natural, a touch light for level 4, with one garbled idiom.
  minor: 'cold enough to freeze the bits off a brass monkey' (for 'dangerously cold'): Garbled, softened idiom; a local would say "brass monkey weather" or the full saying.
  minor: 'Park rangers work in education, resource management, law enforcement, emergency medical services and backcountry operations.' (for 'Park rangers work in education, resource management, law enforcement, emergency medical services, and backcountry operations.'): Left as plain English, under the level 4 weight here and elsewhere.

yarn-deadset: Accurate and natural but far too light for a level 4 brief.
  major: 'Please be patient and enjoy your visit.' (for 'Please be patient and enjoy your visit.'): Much of the text is left as the original, well under a level 4 brief.
  minor: 'wherever folks have pulled over to look at wildlife' (for 'at roadside wildlife sightings'): "Folks" is American.
  minor: 'for heaps of uses that can keep going' (for 'for many sustainable uses'): Clumsy paraphrase of "sustainable".

engine: Mostly untranslated, with a few wrong-sense swaps that change facts (national bush, Parliament House).
  critical: "What's the difference between a national park and a national bush?" (for 'What is the difference between a national park and a national forest?'): "National forest" is a named US land category; "bush" destroys the distinction the answer explains.
  critical: 'Does Yellowstone include a federally designated bush?' (for 'Does Yellowstone include a federally designated wilderness?'): "Wilderness" is a legal designation; "bush" makes the question meaningless.
  critical: "but Parliament House hasn't acted on the recommendation" (for 'but Congress has not acted on the recommendation'): Moves a US federal fact to an Australian institution.
  major: 'Not on your nelly.' (for 'No.'): A scornful refusal idiom where a plain factual no is meant.
  major: 'Yellowstone National Park is named after the Yellowstone River, the major river running through the park.' (for 'Yellowstone National Park is named after the Yellowstone River, the major river running through the park.'): Large stretches are left untranslated, far under the level 4 brief.
