Where do I book a place to stay in Yellowstone?
The nine lodges in the park are all run by Yellowstone National Park Lodges. Every one of them's open from late spring right through autumn, but come winter there's only two open: Old Faithful Snow Lodge and Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel. So get your booking in early, ay!

Nah, you don't need to book your car in to get into the park – you just need a park entrance pass. Buy your pass online before you lob and you'll save yourself a bit of time at the entrance stations. How did Yellowstone get its name?
Yellowstone National Park's named after the Yellowstone River, the main river running through the park. The story goes, according to French-Canadian trappers back in the 1800s, that they asked the Minnetaree tribe, who live in what's now eastern Montana, what the river was called. The answer was "Mi tse a-da-zi," which word for word comes out as "Rock Yellow River." The trappers put that into French as "Roche Jaune" or "Pierre Jaune." Then in 1797, explorer-geographer David Thompson went with the English version—"Yellow Stone." Lewis and Clark called the Yellowstone River by both the French and the English names. And over time the name stuck as "Yellowstone."

Is Yellowstone the biggest national park?
Nah, nah. Better than half of Alaska's national park units are bigger, Wrangell–St. Elias National Park and Preserve for one, which is the biggest unit in the whole National Park System (13 million acres, or about 5.3 million hectares). Up until 1994, Yellowstone (at 2.2 million acres, or about 890,000 hectares) was the biggest national park in the lower 48 of the United States. Then that year Death Valley National Monument got made bigger and turned into a national park—it's got more than 3 million acres (about 1.2 million hectares).

Is Yellowstone the most visited national park?
Yellowstone's up there in the top five national parks for the number of people coming for a bit of fun outdoors. It's usually Great Smoky Mountains National Park that gets the most, though. Have a geek at https://irma.nps.gov/Stats/ for the latest figures on all the national parks.

What's the difference between a national park and a national forest?
National parks are run by the Department of the Interior, and national forests by the Department of Agriculture. The National Park Service has to keep resources just as they are, unharmed, whereas the Forest Service has to look after resources sensibly so they can be used heaps of ways for a long time to come. There's six national forests all round Yellowstone National Park.

How many rangers work in Yellowstone?
In the busiest stretch of summer there's about 750 people working for the National Park Service. About 392 of them are permanent staff who are there all year round. Park rangers do education, look after resources, law enforcement, emergency medical services, and work out in the backcountry. The rest of the staff specialise in research, maintenance, management, admin, track maintenance, fire management, and collecting fees.

What's the highest peak in the park?
That'd be Eagle Peak, down in the south-east of Yellowstone, at 11,358 feet (3,462 metres).

Why is Yellowstone called a biosphere reserve and a world heritage site?
The United Nations gave Yellowstone National Park the title of biosphere reserve and world heritage site because its natural and cultural resources matter right round the world.

What's the Continental Divide?
Think of the Continental Divide as the top of the ridge running along the continent. In theory, any rain or snow that falls on the west side of the Divide ends up in the Pacific Ocean, and any that falls on the east side ends up in the Atlantic Ocean. In Yellowstone (same as everywhere else), this ridgeline isn't straight. You'll cross the Continental Divide three times between the South Entrance and the Old Faithful area. The highest crossing's Craig Pass, at 8,262 feet (2,518 metres).

How did Mt. Washburn form?
At 10,219 feet (3,115 metres), you can see this peak from all over the park. It's what's left of an extinct volcano from the Absaroka Volcanics, about 50 million years ago. That volcano got literally cut in half by a volcanic eruption 631,000 years ago, and only the northern part of the original volcano's still there to see.

Does Yellowstone include a federally designated wilderness?
