If you’re a program manager or a federal web developer you’ve probably been handed what looks like a simple job: make a website as part of an initiative at your agency. The hardest bit often isn't writing the content or designing the website, but getting the security and privacy compliance sorted so you can launch and look after the actual website. For that work, you might have to hire a contractor or put extra strain on your agency’s web team.

It shouldn’t be like that.

## Why Pages?

Pages looks after our fellow federal partners by expertly handling the backend and compliance work to launch and run a website, so you can put your know-how into your content.

- **FedRAMP Certified Platform** Pages is hosted on cloud.gov, a platform with security and compliance checked by the FedRAMP Joint Authorization Board, which means you can count on your site being fast, reliable, and ready for unexpected surges in traffic.

- **Easy previews and content approvals** Pages builds draft updates to your site at a special preview URL. That way your team knows their changes will show up right when they're pushed to the main site, and it's easy to get signoff on content updates from stakeholders.

## Why static websites?

Pages generates static websites instead of using a more complicated content management system (CMS) like Drupal or WordPress. Static websites have a few things going for them:

- **Less complexity and vulnerability:** Steering clear of a traditional CMS means steering clear of headaches like maintaining server applications (no PHP or Apache / IIS) on top of database scaling and redundancy. Production-level static sites generally need one simple static file server or service instead of dozens. That means the whole website is easier to look after and has fewer vulnerabilities.

- **Update content without writing code:** Static websites often keep content in simple text files that anyone on the team can edit. The GitHub editor lets any team member make basic edits to their text files, no matter how much code they've written before.

- **Easy to host and maintain:** It’s dead easy to host static website files on Pages. You can easily make use of scaling services like Amazon S3 through Pages, where you only pay for what you use.

- **Fully customisable:** Designers and developers can customise static websites into whatever shape they like without having to strip out CMS-provided features and defaults. Static sites can be used to build all sorts of sites, from organisation homepages or intranets to pre-generated web application program interfaces that display data from a separate server.
