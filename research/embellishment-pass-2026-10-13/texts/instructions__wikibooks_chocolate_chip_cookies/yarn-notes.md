## deadset

- The plan offers four spellings of one word for "cookie(s)": "bickies", "bikkie", "bikkies", "bickie", and the engine draft mixes them with "biscuits" in the same recipe ("chewier bickies. ... crisper biscuits"). I kept "bikkie(s)" throughout and declined "bickie"/"bickies"; the planner should pick one spelling per text.
- Not caught by the plan: "groundnuts" (Australians say peanuts), "oatmeal" (rolled oats), "bar chocolate" (block chocolate), "pre-formed chips" (ready-made). I localised those by hand.
- Lint suggested "beaut" for "good" in "no matter how good it is" and for "nice" in "not nice to eat" — wrong register for a recipe note and a slight sense shift.
- Lint flagged "in place of the" x3, which is the original's own repeated wording.

## dinkum

- The engine's dinkum draft insults the reader in a recipe: "will not spread out heaps, ya flamin' mongrel", and opens a sentence with "Bugger me, the proportion..." — both against the instructions genre's "no insults aimed at the reader" and the guide's "swear inside the sentence". I left swearing out entirely; a recipe doesn't want it.
- Same "bickie"/"bickies" declines for one consistent spelling.
- Lint false positive: "taste a fair bit different" read as TRY_OUT ("give ... a burl"); here "taste" is a linking verb (how it tastes), not tasting something to try it.
- OPENINGS ("Here's one for ya.", "Strewth, where do I start?") don't suit a recipe that starts with its Ingredients heading; I used "First up," at the first step instead.
