Ingredients

2 sticks (½ lb / 225 g / 1 cup) butter, softened

¾ cup (180 ml) white sugar

¾ cup (180 ml) packed brown sugar

¼ teaspoon vanilla extract

2 standard (100 g) eggs

2 ¼ cups (600 ml / 290 g) plain flour

1 teaspoon bicarb soda

1 teaspoon salt

1 teaspoon dry ground lemon or orange peel (optional)

2 cups (480 ml / 12 oz) semi-sweet choc bits / chips

5 oz (150 g) chopped nuts, peanuts or whatever's in the cupboard (optional)

Procedure

Okay, so. First up, crank the oven to 375°F (210°C), or 350°F (195°C) if you're a chewy bikkie person. No judgement.

Get the butter, white sugar and brown sugar into a big mixing bowl and cream the lot till it's all pale and fluffy.

Now the eggs go in one at a time, and you beat each one till it's properly mixed through before the next one goes in.

Add the vanilla extract and give it another beat.

Grab a small bowl and sift the dry ingredients together, but hang onto the choccy and the nuts for later. Tip the dry stuff into the wet mix in two goes and fold it till it's all come together. Go easy here. Beat the daylights out of it and you'll get gluten forming, and then your bikkies turn out tough as old boots.

Now stir in your choc bits (chips), and the nuts if you're using them.

Plonk the dough down by heaped tablespoons onto ungreased baking trays. There's enough fat in that dough that the bikkies shouldn't stick, but if they do, a bit of baking paper or a non-stick tray'll sort you out.

Into the hot oven for 10–12 minutes, or till they're golden brown (every oven's got a mind of its own, so keep an eye on 'em). Let them sit about 1 minute, then get them off that hot tray and onto a wire rack to cool. Careful, the tray's bloody hot.

Notes, tips, and variations

Ingredients

Give cake flour a burl instead of plain, swap the white sugar for brown, and use butter instead of marg. Cake flour and brown sugar mean you can use real butter without your bikkies going all puddly on ya, and puddles are the only reason anyone ever used marg in the first place.

Want less "spread"? Use a bit of vegetable shortening instead of the butter/marg for thicker bikkies.

Want more spread? Swap some of the butter/marg for vegetable oil (no more than 1/5 of the total fat) and they'll come out thinner.

Go for chocolate with more than 65% cocoa and it'll taste a fair bit different from the "traditional" recipe. Rule of thumb: if the chocolate's too bitter or not much chop to eat straight out of the packet, you mightn't like it in the bikkies either, no matter how flash it is.

A few shakes of ground cinnamon in with the dry ingredients gives it a bit more flavour, too.

If you want to use ordinary flour and butter, here's the trick: bump the flour up to 3+ cups. That gives you a fairly stiff dough that won't spread heaps. Works with wholemeal flour and all.

Muck about with the mix of white to brown sugar till it suits ya. More brown sugar, chewier bikkies. More white sugar, crisper ones. Simple as.

Bung in more vanilla extract (between ½ and ¾ of a teaspoon, instead of ¼) and the bikkie gets a stronger flavour.

Can't get your mitts on vanilla extract? Use two teaspoons of vanilla sugar instead of the white sugar.

Chuck in a small handful of rolled oats and the bikkies get more flavour and a better bit of chew to 'em.

Method

Some people swear blind you've gotta chill the dough before baking, but you don't usually need to bother. Chilling it can help keep the spread down, though, so you get thicker bikkies.

An ungreased, non-stick baking tray instead of a greased one helps keep the spread down too, for thicker bikkies. Flip side: if you like 'em thinner (and crispier), grease your tray.

Good flavouring ingredients make a big difference to how they turn out. Top-quality butter, choccy and vanilla make a much better bikkie. Rubbish in, rubbish out.

Chop up a block of chocolate instead of using ready-made chips and you'll get a more rustic texture -- just make sure the bits are small enough to eat comfortably.

Don't stack the bikkies while they're hot, or they'll bend out of shape and take longer to cool. Cooling rack, every time, for the best results.

For a stronger nut flavour, toast the nuts. Spread them out evenly on a baking tray and pop them in the 350°F oven for ten minutes. Take them out and let them cool before they go in the dough.

And if you like a soft, squishy middle in your bikkie, pull them out one minute sooner. Nice soft middle, every time.
