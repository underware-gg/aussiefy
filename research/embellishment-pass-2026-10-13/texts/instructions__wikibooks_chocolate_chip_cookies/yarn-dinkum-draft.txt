Ingredients

2 sticks (½ lb / 225 g / 1 cup) butter, softened

¾ cup (180 ml) white sugar

¾ cup (180 ml) packed brown sugar

¼ teaspoon vanilla extract

2 standard (100 g) eggs

2 ¼ cups (600 ml / 290 g) plain flour

1 teaspoon bicarb soda

1 teaspoon salt

1 teaspoon dry ground lemon or orange peel (optional)

2 cups (480 ml / 12 oz) semi-sweet choc bits / chips

5 oz (150 g) chopped nuts, peanuts or whatever (optional)

Procedure

First up, crank the oven to 375°F (210°C), or 350°F (195°C) if you're after chewy bikkies.

Cream the butter, white sugar and brown sugar together in a big mixing bowl.

Chuck the eggs in one at a time, beating till each one's properly mixed through.

Add the vanilla extract and give it a beat.

In a small bowl, sift the dry ingredients together (not the choccy or the nuts, though). Tip them into the wet mix in two goes and fold till it's all come together. Don't go overboard beating it, or you'll get gluten forming and that'll change how the bikkies turn out.

Stir in your choc bits (chips), and the nuts if you're using them.

Plonk the dough by heaped tablespoons onto ungreased baking trays. There should be enough fat in the dough to stop the bikkies sticking, but if they do, baking paper or non-stick trays'll sort you out.

Bake them in the hot oven for 10–12 minutes or till they're golden brown (every oven's a bit different). Let them sit about 1 minute, then get them off the hot tray and onto a wire rack to cool.

Notes, tips, and variations

Ingredients

Try cake flour instead of plain, swap the white sugar for brown, and use butter instead of marg. With cake flour and brown sugar you can use butter and not stress about your bikkies going all puddly (which is the only reason anyone uses marg in the first place).

Use a bit of vegetable shortening instead of the butter/marg if you want less "spread" (you'll get thicker bikkies).

Swap some of the butter/marg for vegetable oil (no more than 1/5 of the total fat) for more spread and thinner bikkies.

Chocolate with more than 65% cocoa will taste a fair bit different from the "traditional" recipe. Basically, if you reckon the chocolate's too bitter or not much chop to eat on its own, you mightn't like it in the bikkies either (no matter how flash it is).

A few shakes of ground cinnamon in with the dry ingredients gives it a bit more flavour too.

If you want to use ordinary flour and butter, the trick is to bump the flour up to 3+ cups, which should give you a fairly stiff dough that won't spread heaps. It even works with wholemeal flour.

You can muck about with the mix of white to brown sugar to suit your taste. More brown sugar makes chewier bikkies. More white sugar makes crisper ones.

Bung in more vanilla extract (between ½ and ¾ of a teaspoon, instead of ¼) and you'll get a stronger flavour in the bikkie.

If you can't get your hands on vanilla extract, use two teaspoons of vanilla sugar instead of the white sugar.

Throw in a small handful of rolled oats to give the bikkies more flavour and a better mouth-feel.

Method

Some people reckon you should chill the dough before baking, but you don't usually need to. Chilling it can help cut down the spread, so you get thicker bikkies.

Use an ungreased, non-stick baking tray instead of a greased one to help keep the spread down, for thicker bikkies. Then again, if you like them thinner (and crispier), grease your tray.

Good flavouring ingredients make a big difference to how they turn out. Top-quality butter, choccy and vanilla make a much better bikkie.

Chopped-up block chocolate instead of ready-made chips gives you a more rustic texture -- just make sure the bits are small enough to eat comfortably.

Don't stack the bikkies while they're hot, or they'll bend out of shape and take longer to cool. Use a cooling rack instead for the best results.

For a stronger nut flavour, toast the nuts. Spread them out evenly on a baking tray and pop them in the 350°F oven for ten minutes. Take them out and let them cool before you add them to the dough.

If you like a soft, squishy middle in your bikkie, take them out one minute sooner and you'll get a nice soft middle.
