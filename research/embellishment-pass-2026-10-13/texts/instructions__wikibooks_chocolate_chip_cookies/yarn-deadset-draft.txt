Ingredients

2 sticks (½ lb / 225 g / 1 cup) butter, softened

¾ cup (180 ml) white sugar

¾ cup (180 ml) packed brown sugar

¼ teaspoon vanilla extract

2 standard (100 g) eggs

2 ¼ cups (600 ml / 290 g) plain flour

1 teaspoon bicarb soda

1 teaspoon salt

1 teaspoon dry ground lemon or orange peel (optional)

2 cups (480 ml / 12 oz) semi-sweet choc bits / chips

5 oz (150 g) chopped nuts, such as peanuts (optional)

Procedure

Preheat oven to 375°F (210°C), or 350°F (195°C) if you want chewy bikkies.

Cream the butter, white sugar and brown sugar in a big mixing bowl.

Add the eggs one at a time, beating till they're fully mixed in.

Add the vanilla extract and beat it in.

In a small bowl, sift together the dry ingredients (except the choccy and nuts). Add to the wet mix in two lots and fold till combined. If you beat it too long, you can get some gluten forming, which will change the bikkie texture.

Stir in the choc bits (chips), and the nuts if you're using them.

Drop the dough by heaped tablespoons onto ungreased baking trays. There should be enough fat in the bikkie dough to stop it sticking, but baking paper or non-stick trays may help if you find it does stick.

Bake in the preheated oven for 10–12 minutes or till golden brown (cooking times may vary). Let them sit for about 1 minute, then take them off the hot baking tray to cool on a wire rack.

Notes, tips, and variations

Ingredients

Use cake flour instead of plain, swap the white sugar for brown sugar and use butter instead of marg. Using cake flour and brown sugar lets you use butter without worrying your bikkies will turn into puddles (which is the only reason to use marg in the first place).

Use some vegetable shortening in place of the butter/marg to cut down the "spread" (bikkies will be thicker).

Add some vegetable oil (no more than 1/5 of the total fat) in place of the butter/marg to increase spread, for thinner bikkies.

Using chocolate with a cocoa content over 65% will give a noticeably different taste from the "traditional" recipe. Generally, if you find the chocolate too bitter or not nice to eat on its own, you mightn't like it in the bikkies either (no matter how good it is).

Adding a few shakes of ground cinnamon with the dry ingredients also gives more flavour.

If you want to use ordinary flour as well as butter, the trick is to bump the flour up to 3+ cups, which should give you a fairly stiff dough that won't spread heaps. This trick even works with wholemeal flour.

The mix of white to brown sugar can be adjusted to suit your taste. More brown sugar gives chewier bikkies. More white sugar gives crisper bikkies.

Adding more vanilla extract (between ½ and ¾ of a teaspoon, rather than ¼) gives the bikkie a stronger flavour.

If you can't get hold of vanilla extract, use two teaspoons of vanilla sugar in place of the white sugar.

Add a small amount of rolled oats to give the bikkies more flavour and mouth-feel.

Method

Some people also reckon you should chill the dough before baking, though you usually don't need to. Chilling the dough can help reduce spread, for thicker bikkies.

Use an ungreased, non-stick baking tray instead of a greased one to help cut down spread, for thicker bikkies. On the other hand, grease your tray if you like thinner (crispier) bikkies.

The quality of the flavouring ingredients makes a big difference to how they turn out. Top-quality butter, choccy and vanilla will make a better bikkie.

Using chopped-up block chocolate instead of ready-made chips gives a more rustic texture -- just make sure the bits are small enough to eat comfortably.

Don't stack hot bikkies or they'll bend out of shape and won't cool as quick. Use a cooling rack instead for the best results.

For a stronger nut flavour, toast the nuts. Spread them out evenly on a baking tray and put them in the 350°F oven for ten minutes. Take them out and let them cool before adding them to the dough.

If you like a soft and squishy middle in your bikkie, take them out one minute sooner for a nice soft middle.
