## deadset

- Accepted on the first submit.
- Planned phrases were the right sense. "dunny" works, but its gloss "especially an outdoor one" sits oddly in flush instructions; kept, since Australians say it of any toilet. The engine draft used "chemists" for the first "pharmacies" only, and "Post" for the first "Mail" only, leaving "retail pharmacies" and "Mail the envelope" unchanged; I carried them through.
- Lookup "chockers" returned only "chock-a-block"; "kitty litter" was empty (used it, since it's everyday Australian). "yucky" was empty, so I wrote "off-putting".
- Lint "repeated_phrase" on "get rid of" (5 times) was useful: the original's "dispose of" is a refrain, and I'd flattened it all into one phrase.

## dinkum

- Accepted on the first submit, with a clean lint.
- The OPENINGS for an instructions text ("True story, this.", "You'll like this one.") are story openers and don't suit a how-to. I didn't use them.
- The riff "out the back with a redback under the seat" for "dunny" pictures an outdoor pit dunny, which you can't flush medicine down, and it would make light of a safety step. I left it out.

## troppo

- Accepted on the first submit.
- Lint suggestions took the wrong sense: "bush" for "woods" inside the idiom "neck of the woods", and "little fella"/"littlie" for "kids" (kids is already everyday Australian).
- Lookup empty: "the whole box and dice", "neck of the woods", "here's the go"/"the go" (it gave "go off", "fair go" and so on). "get shot of" returned "chuck out {obj}".
- Troppo on instructions: the safety-first genre rule kept it clear. The colour went into connectives and short reactions ("Simple as that.", "Done.", "Serious stuff, that one."), and every step and warning stayed in order. The limit on how far to go was mostly my own judgement; the brief's pub-yarn examples are all for stories.
