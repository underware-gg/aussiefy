MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – Here's one for ya. From Friday, Sept. 4, the last of the fishin' restrictions on the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon (downstream of Norris Campground) rivers, and every little creek that runs into 'em, are comin' off, now the weather's cooled down and the water's finally followed suit. So the fishos can get back out there on these rivers, along with most of the park's other rivers, creeks and lakes, every day from sunup to sundown, like it says in the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet. Rods out.

Bit of background. These rivers got shut to fishin' altogether back on July 15, then the rangers wound it back to part-day restrictions on Aug. 28 once things started lookin' up. And fair enough they shut 'em, too: the water was low and the water temperatures were up over 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius), and that can cook a fish. Kill 'em stone dead.

Mind you, the staff'll be keepin' a close eye on how warm the water gets, and if things turn, they might slap temporary restrictions or closures back on, depending on how it's all lookin'.

Update: Yellowstone lifts full closures for Firehole, Madison and Gibbon rivers

Part-day restrictions still on from 2 p.m. to sunup the next day

MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – Now, the week before. From Friday, Aug. 28, the fishos could have a crack at the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon (downstream of Norris Campground) rivers, and every creek that runs into 'em, from sunup till 2 p.m. each day, then reel in and knock off. The weather and the water had started coolin' off, and there was a bit of rain in the forecast too, so the full closures could come off.

Those full closures had gone on back on July 15, cos low water and water temperatures over 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius) can kill fish. Simple as that. But the water'd been coolin' off overnight, so there was fishin' for part of the day. And ya could still fish most of the park's other rivers, creeks and lakes every day from sunup to sundown, like it says in the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet.

The staff were watchin' the water temperatures the whole time, and dependin' on how things looked, the temporary restrictions might come off, or they might bring in more closures.

And here's the bit for the fishos, straight from the rangers: Do 'em a favour and fish in the coolest part of the day, and land yer fish quick smart. Don't play a hooked trout till the poor bugger's knackered. Handle the fish gently, in the water as much as ya can, and give 'em a chance to come good before ya let 'em go. Do the right thing, and ya look after the park's fisheries, and ya might just save 'em havin' to ban fishin' at all hours on some rivers and creeks if things get worse. Nobody wants that.
