Here's one for ya. There's a job going, and here's what it involves at the GS-12. If they pick you at a lower grade level, don't sweat it: you'll get the chance to learn the whole box and dice, and they'll train you up so you can grow into the role.

As a TECHNICAL WRITER-EDITOR, here's your yakka:

Wrangle the contents of documents and of Federal Register publications properly, so the lot gets published legally effective, on time and spot on. No pressure.

Manage the work, whether you're going it alone, pitching in with the team, or running the show, so every last document gets published on time and accurately and all the program goals get ticked off.

Get your head around what the customers need across every part of the Federal Register publication process, and look after them properly, because that's the Office of the Federal Register's program responsibilities, and somebody's gotta carry them out.

Know your way round the technical resources available for, and tied to, the Federal Register publication processes, the IT, the legacy systems (read: the old clunkers) and the other technical resources, and use them properly.

Qualifications

You've got to tick off these requirements by the closing date of this announcement.

Specialised Experience: For the GS-09, you need one year of specialised experience at a level of difficulty and responsibility equal to the GS-07 grade level in the Federal service. That specialised experience includes:

Editing legal or regulatory documents other people wrote, to make sure they stick to the style and formatting requirements before they're prepared for publication; AND

Spotting and nutting out production problems and coming up with fixes for them; AND

Getting information across well and tactfully to internal and/or external audiences, to help meet program goals. Diplomacy, in other words.

OR

EDUCATION: A master's or equivalent graduate degree or 2 full years of progressively higher level graduate education leading to such a degree or LL.B. or J.D., if related.

OR

COMBINATION OF EDUCATION AND EXPERIENCE: Note: Only graduate study past the first year (18 semester hours) counts towards qualifying for the GS-9.

NOTE: Undergraduate and Graduate Education: Must have included a total of 15 semester hours in a relevant scientific, technical, or social science field(s), and at least one course above the introductory level in the field(s) the position covers. For technical manuals and specifications writers or editors, the equivalent of 15 semester hours can come from vocational or educational training above high school level at a public, private, or Armed Forces school.
