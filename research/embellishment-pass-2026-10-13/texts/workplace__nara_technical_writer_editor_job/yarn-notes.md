## deadset

- Both planned phrases were wrong sense, and the engine draft shows the damage: "the punters" for "the public" gave "gained in either the punters, private sector or volunteer service" (it's the public sector); "paddock" for "field" gave "social science paddock(s)" (a field of study). Declined both. The "field" trigger needs a guard against "field of study / field(s) covered".
- With both declined there was no planned slang at all, so level 3 rested on plain-word swaps (resume -> CV, telework -> work from home, background investigation -> background check, new hires -> new starters). Lint had no suggestions either.
- Accepted first submit; no lint issues.

## dinkum

- Same two wrong-sense planned phrases, declined: "the punters" (public sector) and "paddock" (field of study).
- Lookup wrong: "nut out" returned "nutter", "head case", "off {poss} tree" (the crazy sense of "nut"); "work out a problem" returned "yakka", "no wukkas". "nut (something) out" = work out a problem is everyday Australian and missing. I used it ("nutting out production problems").
- Lint suggested "the whole box and dice / the whole bloody show" for "all of it" in "you'll get the chance to learn to do all of it"; "bloody" in a job ad is out by the genre's own rule.
- OPENINGS ("Ever hear the one about ...?", "Back in the day,") don't fit a job ad at all; ignored.

## troppo

- "the punters" and "paddock" declined a third time (same wrong senses).
- Troppo on official prose was the least clear combination: the genre says keep every obligation and condition exact, troppo says perform it. I read it as a mate at the pub telling you about the job: every condition, number and announcement ID kept, the EDUCATION/COMBINATION/NOTE paragraphs left near-verbatim (they're the qualifying rules), and colour only in tags hung off existing lines ("No pressure.", "(read: the old clunkers)", "Not bad, ay?").
- Lint offered "the whole bloody show" for "the whole lot"/"the whole thing" again; in a job ad the genre forbids it, and "the whole lot" is already Australian.
- Accepted first submit.
