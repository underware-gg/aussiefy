Southern Cornbread, then. It's a hearty cornbread, no sugar in it whatsoever, and right across the Southeastern United States they serve the stuff with just about everything. It's easy as to make, and it usually rocks up as the bread with lunch or tea (supper). And ya won't believe this, but plenty of folks over there will knock up a quick, simple feed out of it now and then (brekkie, even) by crumbling cornbread into a glass of buttermilk and getting stuck in. Cornbread and buttermilk for brekkie. Each to their own, ay.

Ingredients

2 cups yellow or white self-raising cornmeal

1 large egg

1¼–1½ cups buttermilk or full-cream milk

¼ cup olive oil or vegetable oil

Equipment

A well-seasoned 8-inch (20 cm) or 10-inch (25 cm) cast-iron frying pan (or, if you haven't got one of them: an ovenproof frying pan or an 8x8-inch (20 x 20 cm) baking tin)

A few measuring cups, whatever's in the drawer

A medium or large mixing bowl

Method

Crank the oven up to 425°F (220°C). Using the cast-iron pan? Whack it in the oven to heat up while you're at it. But don't preheat any other sort of frying pan or baking tin, mind.

Using a frying pan or tin that isn't cast iron? Give it a good squirt of spray oil.

Beat the egg in the bowl, then stir in the buttermilk and the oil.

Now stir in the cornmeal and mix it till it's only just wet through. The batter's going to be a bit lumpy, and that's no worries. Go easy on it, though: don't overmix it.

Pour the batter into the pan and into the oven she goes.

Bake it till the crust's a nice light golden brown and a toothpick poked into the centre comes out clean. In an 8-inch (20 cm) pan that can be 25–30 minutes; in a 10-inch (25 cm) pan or an 8x8-inch (20 x 20 cm) tin, more like 20–25 minutes.

Then let it sit and cool for 5 minutes in the pan before you dish it up. Keep your mitts off it till then.

Notes, tips and variations

Got cornbread left over? Wrap it in a damp tea towel (keeps it from drying out) and it'll keep in the fridge for several days. You can chuck it in the freezer in freezer bags for several months, too, but fair warning: it'll usually come out a bit more crumbly that way.
