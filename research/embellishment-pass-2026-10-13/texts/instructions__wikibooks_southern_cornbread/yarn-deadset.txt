Southern Cornbread is a hearty, unsweetened sort of cornbread, served all over the Southeastern United States. It's easy to make and usually goes on the table as the bread with lunch or tea (supper). It's not unusual for some people to now and then have a quick, simple feed (even brekkie) by crumbling cornbread into a glass of buttermilk and tucking in.

Ingredients

2 cups yellow or white self-raising cornmeal

1 large egg

1¼–1½ cups buttermilk or full-cream milk

¼ cup olive oil or vegetable oil

Equipment

Well-seasoned 8-inch (20 cm) or 10-inch (25 cm) cast-iron frying pan (or else: an ovenproof frying pan or an 8x8-inch (20 x 20 cm) baking tin)

A few measuring cups

Medium or large mixing bowl

Method

Preheat the oven to 425°F (220°C). If you're using a cast-iron frying pan, whack it in the oven to heat. Don't preheat any other sort of frying pan or baking tin.

If you're using a frying pan or baking tin that isn't cast iron, give it a coat of spray oil.

Beat the egg in the bowl. Stir in the buttermilk and oil.

Stir in the cornmeal and mix till it's just wet through. The batter will be a bit lumpy. Be careful not to overmix it.

Pour the batter into the frying pan and pop it in the oven.

Bake till the crust is a light golden brown and a toothpick poked into the centre comes out clean. For an 8-inch (20 cm) frying pan this can be 25–30 minutes; for a 10-inch (25 cm) frying pan or an 8x8-inch (20 x 20 cm) baking tin about 20–25 minutes.

Leave it to cool for 5 minutes in the pan before serving.

Notes, tips and variations

Any leftover cornbread can be wrapped in a damp tea towel (to keep it moist) and kept in the fridge for several days. It can be frozen in freezer bags for several months, but it'll usually go more crumbly if you keep it that way.
