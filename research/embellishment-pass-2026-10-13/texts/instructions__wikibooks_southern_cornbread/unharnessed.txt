Southern Cornbread is a hearty, no-sugar sort of cornbread that gets dished up all over the south-east of the United States. It's a doddle to make and usually turns up as the bread bit of lunch or dinner (tea, if you like). It's not unheard of for some folks to knock together a quick, simple feed now and then—even brekkie—by crumbling cornbread into a glass of buttermilk and getting stuck into it.

Ingredients

2 cups yellow or white self-raising cornmeal

1 large egg

1¼–1½ cups buttermilk or full-cream milk

¼ cup olive oil or vegetable oil

Equipment

A well-seasoned 8-inch or 10-inch cast-iron frypan (or else: an ovenproof frypan or an 8x8-inch baking tin)

A few measuring cups

Medium or large mixing bowl

Procedure

Crank the oven up to 425°F. If you're using a cast-iron frypan, bung it in the oven to heat up. Don't preheat any other kind of frypan or baking tin.

If you're using a frypan that isn't cast iron, or a baking tin, give it a spray of non-stick.

Beat the egg in the bowl. Stir in the buttermilk and oil.

Stir in the cornmeal and mix till it's just wet through. The batter'll be a bit lumpy. Go easy and don't overmix the bugger.

Tip the batter into the frypan and whack it in the oven.

Bake till the crust is a light golden brown and a toothpick poked into the middle comes out clean. For an 8-inch frypan that can be 25–30 minutes; for a 10-inch frypan or an 8x8-inch baking tin, about 20–25 minutes.

Let it cool for 5 minutes in the pan before you serve it up.

Notes, tips, and variations

Any leftover cornbread can be wrapped in a damp tea towel (to stop it drying out) and kept in the fridge for a few days. You can freeze it in storage bags for a few months, but it'll usually go more crumbly if you keep it that way.
