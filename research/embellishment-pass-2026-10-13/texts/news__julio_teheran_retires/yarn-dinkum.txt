Julio Teherán, a two-time Major League Baseball All-Star, has called time on his baseball career. He announced his retirement on Monday, at 35. It came two days after a crook shoulder saw him scratched from a scheduled start for Team Colombia at the 2026 World Baseball Classic (WBC).

Before he called it, Teherán had helped Team Colombia to a 3-1 win over Team Panama at the WBC.

He had thirteen years in the MLB, most of them with the Atlanta Braves, though he also turned out for the Los Angeles Angels, the Detroit Tigers, the Milwaukee Brewers and the New York Mets. He made his MLB debut for the Braves in 2011 as a 20-year-old and stuck with them for his first nine seasons, picking up All-Star selections in 2014 and 2016 along the way.

All up, Teherán finished his MLB career with an 81-82 record, a 3.85 ERA, a 1.223 WHIP and 1,260 strikeouts from 1,470.2 innings. He'd also spent the 2022 and 2025 seasons over in the Mexican Baseball League.
