Here's one for the baseball tragics. Julio Teherán, two-time Major League Baseball All-Star, has hung up the glove. Announced it Monday, he did, at 35.

Now, the timing. Two days before that, he's meant to be starting for Team Colombia at the 2026 World Baseball Classic (the WBC, if you're keeping score at home), and they scratch him. Crook shoulder. And when the arm that pays the bills starts playing up, well, you start having a think, don't ya?

He didn't go out empty-handed, mind. Before he called it, Teherán had helped Team Colombia knock off Team Panama 3-1 at the WBC. Not a bad way to sign off.

And what a run it was. Thirteen years in the MLB. Most of it with the Atlanta Braves, but he also did stints with the Los Angeles Angels, the Detroit Tigers, the Milwaukee Brewers and the New York Mets. Got around a bit, our Julio. He made his MLB debut for the Braves in 2011, a young fella of 20, and stuck with them for his first nine seasons, picking up All-Star selections in 2014 and 2016.

Now, for the stats blokes up the back: all up, Teherán finished his MLB career with an 81-82 record, a 3.85 ERA, a 1.223 WHIP and 1,260 strikeouts from 1,470.2 innings. That's a fair bit of chucking. And in between, he spent the 2022 and 2025 seasons over in the Mexican Baseball League. A good innings, as they say. Good on ya, Julio.
