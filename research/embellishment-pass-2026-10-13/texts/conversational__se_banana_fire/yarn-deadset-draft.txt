Why did me banana catch fire in the microwave?

Hooley dooley, that sounds like a daft question, but fair dinkum! The missus froze bananas in the freezer with the skin still on. You can't peel 'em frozen. I bunged one in the microwave for one minute at 50% power. After 30 seconds the thin end of the skin (where a bunch join up to each other) was alight, with a flame you could see (about the size of a lighter). What the bloody hell happened?

* * *

It's caused by the banana having heaps of potassium in it. Microwaves react with metals, bouncing off and causing arcing. You can even put on a ripper little light show by bunging a raw peeled banana in the microwave. No worries, it won't blow up, but it'll make a dog's breakfast, and it's harmless too.

This can also happen in some frozen veggies, depending on the soil they grew in. Like the linked article says, high amounts of other metals such as magnesium, iron and zinc can be the mineral to blame.

A quick spark or two for a moment is harmless and won't hurt your microwave, but the USDA still reckons you should turn your microwave off if you see sparks. If it does start a fire, unless it's a real small one, don't open your microwave door. The safest thing is to turn the microwave off and let the fire burn itself out by using up all the oxygen. Opening the door could set off a dangerous fireball from backdraft.
