Why'd me banana go up in flames in the microwave?

So get this. Sounds like a daft question, I know, but fair dinkum, it happened! The missus had gone and frozen a heap of bananas in the freezer with the skins still on, and you can't peel the buggers frozen, they're hard as a cricket ball. So I bung one in the microwave, one minute at 50% power, nice and gentle. 30 seconds in, the thin end of the skin, the bit where the bunch all join up, is alight. A proper flame, mate, you could see it plain as day, about the size of a lighter. What in the bloody hell's going on there, ay?

* * *

Well, here's the go: it's the potassium. Bananas are chockers with the stuff. Microwaves react with metals, see, they bounce off 'em and cause arcing, little sparks jumping about. You can even put on your own light show, a ripper of one, by bunging a raw peeled banana in the microwave. No worries, it won't blow up on ya, but it'll make a right dog's breakfast of the inside, and it's harmless.

And it's not just bananas. Same thing can happen with some frozen veggies, depending on the soil they were grown in. Like the linked article says, it can be high amounts of other metals doing it too, such as magnesium, iron and zinc. Your peas could be packing a bit of iron, who knew.

Now, a quick spark or two is harmless and won't hurt your microwave, but the USDA reckons you should switch the microwave off if you see sparks anyway. And this bit's serious: if it does go up in flames, unless it's only a real small fire, don't open the microwave door. Safest thing is to switch the microwave off and let the fire burn itself out, till it's used up all the oxygen in there. Open that door and you could cop a dangerous fireball from the backdraft, straight in the face. Leave it shut.
