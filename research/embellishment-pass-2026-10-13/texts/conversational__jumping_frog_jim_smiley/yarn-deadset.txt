“Rev. Leonidas W. H'm, Reverend Le--well, there was a bloke here once, name of Jim Smiley, in the winter of '49--or maybe it was the spring of '50--I can't remember exactly, somehow, though what makes me reckon it was one or the other is I remember the big flume wasn't finished when he first lobbed at the camp; but anyway, he was the strangest cove you ever seen for bettin' on anythin' that come up, if he could get anyone to bet on the other side; and if he couldn't he'd swap sides. Any way that suited the other bloke would suit HIM--any way at all, so long as he got a bet, he was happy as Larry. But still he was a lucky bugger, lucky as; he near always come out on top. He was always ready and waitin' for a chance; there couldn't be one single thing mentioned but that bloke'd offer to bet on it, and take whatever side ya like, like I was just tellin' ya. If there was a horse race, you'd find him cashed up or you'd find him stony broke at the end of it; if there was a dog fight, he'd bet on it; if there was a cat fight, he'd bet on it; if there was a chook fight, he'd bet on it; why, if there was two birds sittin' on a fence, he'd bet ya which one'd fly off first; or if there was a camp meetin', he'd be there regular to bet on Parson Walker, who he reckoned was the best preacher round here, and so he was too, and a good bloke. If he even seen a straddle-bug start off anywhere, he'd bet ya how long it'd take him to get to--to wherever he was headed, and if ya took him on, he'd follow that straddle-bug to Mexico till he found out where he was off to and how long he was on the frog and toad. Heaps of the blokes here have seen that Smiley, and can tell ya about him. Why, it was all the same to him--he'd bet on anythin'--the flamin' strangest bloke. Parson Walker's missus was real crook once, for a fair while, and it looked like they weren't gonna save her; but one mornin' he come in, and Smiley up and asked him how she was goin', and he said she was a fair bit better--thank the Lord for his endless mercy--and comin' good so quick that with the blessin' of Providence she'd pull through yet; and Smiley, without thinkin', goes, 'Well, I'll risk two-and-a-half she doesn't anyway.'

“This here Smiley had a mare--the blokes called her the fifteen-minute nag, but that was only a joke, ya know, cos of course she was quicker than that--and he used to win the folding stuff on that horse, for all she was so slow and always had the asthma, or the distemper, or the consumption, or somethin' like that. They used to give her a two or three hundred yard start, and then pass her on the way; but always at the tail end of the race she'd get all het up and desperate like, and come prancin' and sprawlin' up, flingin' her legs round all loose, sometimes in the air, and sometimes out to one side among the fences, and kickin' up m-o-r-e dust and makin' m-o-r-e racket with her coughin' and sneezin' and blowin' her nose--and always pull up at the stand just about a neck in front, near as you could work it out.
