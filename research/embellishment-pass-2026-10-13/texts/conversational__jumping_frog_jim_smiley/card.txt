yarn-dinkum: A polished, faithful and natural Aussie retelling that keeps the deadpan; slightly tame for the brief.
  minor: 'how long it was on the frog and toad' (for 'how long he was on the road'): Rhyming slang is a stock tourist touch and fits a bug's trip awkwardly.

yarn-troppo: The liveliest, most authentic level-5 yarn with well-judged swearing; its knowing asides cost the original's deadpan.
  major: "Talk about not knowin' when to shut his trap." (added): Narrator comments on the joke; the original's comedy depends on the teller's earnest deadpan.
  minor: 'ah, stuff it. Listen to this.' (for 'Reverend Le--well,'): Turns the narrator's polite fumble with the name into dismissive impatience.
  minor: "lobbed at the camp in the winter of '49" (for 'when he first come to the camp'): Arrival given twice ('lobbed' then 'rocked up'), slightly redundant.

yarn-deadset: A careful, faithful Aussie rendering that keeps Twain's shape and deadpan; leans on 'bloke' and stays lighter than the brief.
  minor: "the flamin' strangest bloke" (for 'the dangdest feller'): Awkward stacking; 'flamin'' before a superlative is clumsy.
  minor: 'Heaps of the blokes here have seen that Smiley' (for 'Lots of the boys here has seen that Smiley'): 'Bloke' repeated many times through the text.

unharnessed: A faithful, fluent modern retelling with sound Aussie terms inside the vulgarity limit; a bit light for level 5 and one Americanism.
  minor: 'lucky as all get-out' (for 'uncommon lucky'): 'All get-out' is an American intensifier, not Australian.
  minor: 'Any way at all, so long as he got a bet on, he was happy as Larry. And the lucky bastard,' (for 'But still he was lucky, uncommon lucky'): Swearing thickened beyond the narrator's innocent voice; 'bloody' used four or five times.

engine: The original left in American dialect with stage-Australian tags and wrong-sense swaps bolted on; jarring and off the brief.
  major: "if he couldn't he'd change sides, ya flamin' mongrel" (for "and if he couldn't he'd change sides"): An insult aimed at nobody; changes the narrator's mild voice.
  major: "anythin' that lobbed" (for 'anything that turned up'): 'Lobbed' means a person arriving, not an event arising.
  major: "she get rapt'n desperate like" (for 'she get excited and desperate like'): 'Rapt' means delighted, the wrong sense for a frantic horse.
  minor: 'Smiley, before he reckoned, says' (for 'Smiley, before he thought, says'): 'Reckoned' is opinion, not thinking; wrong sense.
  major: 'and a dead-set legend, mate' (for 'and a good man'): Replaces the narrator's sober praise of the parson with stock slang.
  major: "for a fuckin' good while" (for 'for a good while'): Swearing about a dying woman jars the earnest tone.
  major: 'the curiousest cove about always betting' (for 'the curiousest man about always betting'): Twain's American dialect left in throughout ('warn't', 'dangdest', 'Thish-yer').
  major: "cobber. Stone the flamin' crows." (added): Stock stage-Australian tags tacked on at the end.
  minor: 'Heaps of the young fellas here has seen that Smiley' (for 'Lots of the boys here has seen that Smiley'): 'Young' is invented.
