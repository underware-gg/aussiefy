“Rev. Leonidas W. H'm, Reverend Le--well, there was a cove here once, Jim Smiley his name was, in the winter of '49--or might've been the spring of '50--can't remember exactly, but what makes me reckon it was one or the other is I remember the big flume wasn't finished when he first lobbed at the camp; but anyway, he was the strangest bloke you ever seen for bettin' on anythin' that come up, so long as he could get some bugger to take the other side; and if he couldn't, he'd just swap sides. Whatever suited the other bloke suited HIM--any way at all, long as he got a bet on, he was happy as Larry. But he was a lucky bugger with it, lucky as; near enough always come out on top. He was always ready, just waitin' for his chance; there wasn't a single thing ya could mention but that feller'd offer to bet on it, and take whichever side ya liked, like I was just sayin'. If there was a horse race, he'd either be cashed up or stony broke by the end of it; dog fight, he'd bet on it; cat fight, he'd bet on it; chook fight, he'd bet on it; why, if there was two birds sittin' on a fence, he'd bet ya which one'd take off first; or if there was a camp meetin', he'd be there regular to back Parson Walker, who he reckoned was the best preacher round these parts, and so he was, too, and a good bloke with it. If he so much as seen a straddle-bug startin' off somewhere, he'd bet ya how long it'd take to get to--to wherever it was headed, and if ya took him on, he'd follow that straddle-bug all the way to Mexico to find out where it was goin' and how long it was on the frog and toad. Heaps of the blokes round here have seen that Smiley, and they'll tell ya about him. Why, it was all the same to him--he'd bet on anythin'--strangest bloke goin'. Parson Walker's missus was bloody crook once, for a fair while, and it looked like they weren't gonna save her; but one mornin' he comes in, and Smiley up and asks him how she's goin', and he says she's a fair bit better--thank the Lord for his endless mercy--and comin' good so quick that with the blessin' of Providence she'd pull through yet; and Smiley, without even thinkin', goes, 'Well, I'll risk two-and-a-half she doesn't anyway.'

“Now this Smiley had a mare--the blokes called her the fifteen-minute nag, but that was only a joke, ya know, cos of course she was quicker than that--and he used to win the folding stuff on that horse, slow as she was, and her always crook with the asthma, or the distemper, or the consumption, or somethin' like that. They'd give her a two or three hundred yard start, and then pass her on the way; but every time, at the tail end of the race, she'd get all het up and desperate like, and come prancin' and sprawlin' up, flingin' her legs round all loose, sometimes up in the air, sometimes off to one side among the fences, kickin' up m-o-r-e dust and makin' m-o-r-e racket with her coughin' and sneezin' and blowin' her nose--and she'd always pull up at the stand about a neck in front, near as ya could work it out.
