DETROIT, Dec. 13 (AP).—Henry Ford's already let on he's on a mission to teach the American public the old-fashioned square dances, and his first big crack at it is Tuesday night, at one of the biggest dance halls in Detroit. For the do, Mr. Ford's advertisin' for couples to dance the square dance and make up the core of the party.

Asked tonight whether he really reckoned the old-fashioned steps'd make a comeback, Mr. Ford sang out:

"Come back? They're here! We simply are falling back in line. What we are trying to do in getting out a book of dances is to standardise them. The first edition is being revised in the direction of simplicity."

Out of the twenty-one quadrilles, Mr. Ford explained, they'll pick four, so they're easier to teach and more people get keen on 'em.

Now, "Mellie" Dunham, Maine's champion fiddler, has been stayin' with Mr. Ford as his guest for a week, playin' at an old-fashioned dance party and chippin' in several old-time tunes for the Ford "Americana" of such pieces that's bein' got ready for publishin'. He was meant to rock up at the Tuesday night do as well. But the plans got changed, and tonight "Grandpa Mellie" and the missus are on their way to New York, where he might just answer the call of the footlights and sign a vaudeville contract.

"Mellie" Dunham's due to front up in New York this mornin'. Word last night was he'll be under the management of Tex Rickard, who's said to be plannin' a barn dance in his honour at the brand-spankin'-new Madison Square Garden. And the hope, so they reckon, is that puttin' on this dance'll help Henry Ford along in makin' square dances popular again.
