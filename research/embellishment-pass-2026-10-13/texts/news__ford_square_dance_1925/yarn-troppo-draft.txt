Ya won't believe this, but it's straight off the wire: DETROIT, Dec. 13 (AP). Henry Ford, the motor car bloke himself, has already let on he's out to teach the whole American public the old-fashioned square dances. And his first big crack at it is Tuesday night, at one of the biggest dance halls in Detroit, no less. He's even advertisin' for couples to come and dance the square dance, so there's a core of 'em to get the party goin'.

So someone asks him tonight, does he really reckon the old-fashioned steps are gonna make a comeback? And Mr. Ford goes:

"Come back? They're here! We simply are falling back in line. What we are trying to do in getting out a book of dances is to standardise them. The first edition is being revised in the direction of simplicity."

Simplicity, mind. There's twenty-one of these quadrilles, Mr. Ford explained, and he's whittlin' 'em down to four, so they're easier to teach and more people get keen on 'em. Four out of twenty-one. Fair enough, too.

Now, the bloke with the fiddle. "Mellie" Dunham, the champion fiddler of all Maine, has been Mr. Ford's guest for a week, sawin' away at an old-fashioned dance party and chippin' in several old-time tunes for the Ford "Americana" of such pieces that's bein' got ready for publishin'. He was meant to rock up at the Tuesday night do and all. But the plans got changed, didn't they, and tonight "Grandpa Mellie" and the missus are on their way to New York, where the old fella might just answer the call of the footlights and sign a vaudeville contract. Grandpa on the stage. Good on him.

"Mellie" Dunham's due to front up in New York this mornin'. And word last night was he'll be under the management of Tex Rickard, no less, who's said to be plannin' a barn dance in Mellie's honour at the brand-spankin'-new Madison Square Garden. A barn dance in the Garden. And the hope, so they were sayin', is that puttin' on this dance'll help Henry Ford along in gettin' square dances popular again.
