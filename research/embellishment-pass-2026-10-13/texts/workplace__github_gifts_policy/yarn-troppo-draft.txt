## Examples of acceptable and unacceptable gifts

So what's “of value” mean, then? Well, first up: for government officials, no gifts are okay. None. (Righto, maybe stickers.) Always ask them what their rules let them take and what they don't. And if you're not sure, run it past Legal to make sure, cos that's what they're there for. For example, plenty of U.S. congresspeople won't even let you take them out for dinner and shout them. Not so much as a steak.

Now, the rest of your customers, and the ones you're hoping to land. Some things can seem like nothing to you and be worth a fortune to someone else. Say you get your mate’s daughter a job as an intern. No skin off your nose, but to them it could mean heaps. And if she's not qualified and you pull strings to get her in? That is not on, full stop. What's more, if the idea is to improperly influence someone, anything at all, however small, might count as "of value." Even a snag sanga.

🙅 _Here are some other things that aren't on:_

* First class airfare to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️+🍾
* Winery tours 🍷
* Putting in a good word to get a friend’s kid into a posh private school 🎓
* Tickets to Disneyland 👸, the World Cup 🥅, the Olympics 🏅
* A futbol ⚽️ signed by a famous player
* A donation to a charity

🙆 _Here's some stuff that's actually fine for customers or would-be customers:_

* Swag
* Economy class airfare to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️
* Cheap seats at a sporting event ⚾️
* A reasonably priced dinner in town 🍽
* A small box of choccies as an end of year thank you 🍫

And it cuts both ways: all of that goes for _taking_ bribes too. So if a supplier, or someone angling to be one, sends you something “of value,” don’t take it—or at the very least run it past Legal before you go anywhere near it.

## Process

Planning to give or take an informal pressie (one that isn't our promo gear, like t-shirts and sticker swag)? Then make sure you follow the process below.

Thing is, what counts as an okay amount can change. Fifty bucks ($50) on dinner, say, might be a lot of money in some countries and not so much in others. So, if you're not sure, ask! Nobody's ever copped a fine for asking.

1. Generally speaking, for any expenses over $150, you'll need Legal's approval in writing (each time, not just the once). And for any pressies you get that are over $150, you'll need to report it to Legal in writing (each time). (Legal's only weighing in on the bribery side of things, mind. You might still need to check with Finance for other reasons.)

1. When you put in your expense claim to get paid back, please:
     * Say who got the gift and what it was for in the notes; and
     * Include a copy of the email approval

## Consequences

So what happens if you don’t follow the policy, and GitHub ends up paying for a would-be customer and his whole family to fly first class to Japan and take in the Tokyo Olympics? Or if you drop off a suitcase of Euros for a government official in a train station locker you'd sorted out beforehand, like something out of a spy movie?

FINES and JAIL. That's what. And not necessarily just for GitHub, either. You yourself might end up doing a few years in jail, and it won’t matter one bit whether the customer ever put an order in with GitHub after their trip to Japan, or whether the government official even came and picked up the suitcase of dosh. Basically, it doesn’t matter whether you actually _made_ a bribe. Sorry, Yoda, but in this one it’s the trying that counts.
