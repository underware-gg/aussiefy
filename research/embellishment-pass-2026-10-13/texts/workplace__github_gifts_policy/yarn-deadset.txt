## Examples of acceptable and unacceptable gifts

So what does “of value” mean? Keep in mind that no gifts are acceptable for government officials (alright, maybe stickers). You should always ask them what their policies allow and don't allow. But if in doubt, please run it past Legal to make sure. For example, a lot of U.S. congresspeople won't let you take them out for dinner and shout them.

As for other customers or possible customers, some things might seem minor but be worth a lot to someone else. For example, although it might not seem like a big deal to you to get your mate’s daughter a job as an intern, it might be worth heaps to them. But if she's not qualified and you pull strings to make it happen, then it isn't on. On top of that, if the idea is to improperly influence, anything, however small, might be considered "of value."

🙅 _Here are some other things that aren't on:_

* First class airfare to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️+🍾
* Winery tours 🍷
* Recommending a friend’s kid to a posh private school for entry 🎓
* Tickets to Disneyland 👸, the World Cup 🥅, the Olympics 🏅
* A futbol ⚽️ signed by a famous player
* A donation to a charity

🙆 _Here's some stuff that's actually fine for customers or possible customers:_

* Swag
* Economy class airfare to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️
* General admission tickets to a sporting event ⚾️
* A reasonably priced dinner in town 🍽
* A small box of chocolates as an end of year thank you 🍫

All of the above goes for _taking_ bribes too. So if a supplier or possible supplier sends you something “of value,” don’t accept it—or at least run it past Legal before you do.

## Process

If you're planning to give or accept an informal pressie (that isn't one of our promo items like t-shirts and sticker swag), please make sure you follow the process below.

Keep in mind that what's an acceptable amount can vary. For example, spending $50 on dinner might be thought of as a lot of money in some countries but not so much in others. So, when in doubt, ask!

1. Generally speaking, for any expenses over $150, you'll need approval (each time) from Legal in writing. And for any pressies you receive over $150, you'll need to report that (each time) to Legal in writing. (Legal is just weighing in on the bribery question. You might still need to check with Finance for other reasons.)

1. When you put in your expense claim to be paid back, please:
     * Note who got the gift and what it was for in the notes; and
     * Include a copy of the email approval

## Consequences

What happens if you don’t follow the policy and GitHub ends up paying for a possible customer and his family to fly first class to Japan and go to the Tokyo Olympics? Or if you drop off a suitcase of Euros for a government official in a pre-arranged train station locker?

FINES and JAIL. Not necessarily just for GitHub, either. You might get to spend a few years in jail, yourself, and it won’t matter if the customer ever put in an order with GitHub after their trip to Japan or if the government official even picked up the suitcase of dosh. Basically, it doesn’t matter whether you actually _made_ a bribe. Yoda or no Yoda, it’s the trying that counts.
