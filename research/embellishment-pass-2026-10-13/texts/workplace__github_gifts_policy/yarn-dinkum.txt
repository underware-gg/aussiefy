## Examples of acceptable and unacceptable gifts

So what does “of value” actually mean? Keep in mind there are no gifts that are okay for government officials (righto, maybe stickers). Always ask them what their rules let them take and what they don't. And if you're not sure, run it past Legal to make sure. For example, plenty of U.S. congresspeople won't let you take them out for dinner and shout them.

As for other customers, or anyone who might become one, some things can seem like nothing to you but be worth a lot to someone else. Say you get your mate’s daughter a job as an intern: it mightn't seem like a big deal to you, but it could mean heaps to them. And if she's not qualified and you pull strings to get her in, that's not on. What's more, if the idea is to improperly influence someone, anything, however small, might count as "of value."

🙅 _Here are some other things that aren't on:_

* First class airfare to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️+🍾
* Winery tours 🍷
* Putting in a good word to get a friend’s kid into a posh private school 🎓
* Tickets to Disneyland 👸, the World Cup 🥅, the Olympics 🏅
* A futbol ⚽️ signed by a famous player
* A donation to a charity

🙆 _Here's some stuff that's actually fine for customers or would-be customers:_

* Swag
* Economy class airfare to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️
* Cheap seats at a sporting event ⚾️
* A reasonably priced dinner in town 🍽
* A small box of choccies as an end of year thank you 🍫

All of that goes for _taking_ bribes too. So if a supplier, or someone who'd like to be one, sends you something “of value,” don’t take it—or at the very least run it past Legal first.

## Process

If you're planning to give or take an informal pressie (one that isn't our promo gear, like t-shirts and sticker swag), make sure you follow the process below.

Keep in mind that what counts as an okay amount can change. Spending $50 on dinner, say, might be a lot of money in some countries but not so much in others. So, if you're not sure, ask!

1. Generally speaking, for any expenses over $150, you'll need Legal's approval in writing (each time). And for any pressies you get that are over $150, you'll need to report it to Legal in writing (each time). (Legal's only weighing in on the bribery side of things. You might still need to check with Finance for other reasons.)

1. When you put in your expense claim to get paid back, please:
     * Say who got the gift and what it was for in the notes; and
     * Include a copy of the email approval

## Consequences

So what happens if you don’t follow the policy and GitHub ends up paying for a would-be customer and his family to fly first class to Japan and go to the Tokyo Olympics? Or if you drop off a suitcase of Euros for a government official in a train station locker you'd organised beforehand?

FINES and JAIL. And not necessarily just for GitHub, either. You yourself might end up doing a few years in jail, and it won’t matter whether the customer ever put an order in with GitHub after their trip to Japan, or whether the government official even picked up the suitcase of dosh. Basically, it doesn’t matter whether you actually _made_ a bribe. Sorry, Yoda: it’s the trying that counts.
