Over there petrol's called "gas", and they sell it by the gallon, at servos that are mostly self-serve (ya pump yer own), except in New Jersey, where self-serve's actually against the law, and in Oregon, where city servos have to have at least 50% full-service pumps by law. Mind you, the American gallon's smaller than the imperial one: it comes to 3.785 litres.

Cos of all the "drive offs" in the crime waves of the 1990s and 2000s, near enough every American servo makes ya pay before ya pump now (it used to be the other way round). These days ya have to get yer credit or debit card okayed at the pump first. Or else ya guess how much petrol ya need, hand that much of the folding stuff over to whoever's on the till, and tell 'em which pump yer on (an American might go, "I need 50 bucks on pump number 1"). The pump cuts out by itself once the petrol hits the dosh ya handed over, or if ya guessed too high, ya can go back in and get a refund on whatever dosh ya didn't use.

Now, the U.S. octane scale's different from the one they use in Europe; a regular gallon of U.S. petrol rates 87 octane, which is about the same as 92 in Europe. In most states, servos give ya three grades of octane to pick from: 87 (regular), 89 (midgrade or plus), and 91 (premium). Unless yer hirin' something flash, yer car'll most likely only need 87 (regular). If yer drivin' round the Rocky Mountains, ya might come across servos sellin' petrol with lower octane ratings, mostly 85. That's fine for 87-octane cars up high, but if yer headin' back down lower, make sure ya fill up with 87.

Diesel's not as common, but it's still used a fair bit and ya can get it at most servos, 'specially the ones that cater for truckies. Then there's untaxed "offroad diesel", sold out bush for farm use, which is dyed red (and sometimes called "red diesel") and ya shouldn't put it in a car, cos there's hefty fines if ya get caught.

And in the United States, the old colour code at servos is that petrol comes out of the nozzles with the black handle, and diesel out of the ones with the green handle. That's the opposite of the colour code in the United Kingdom. So don't go mixin' 'em up.
