True story, this. Over in the States they don't call it petrol, they call it "gas", and they flog it by the gallon, at servos that are mostly self-serve, so ya pump yer own. Except in New Jersey, mind, where self-serve's against the bloody law, and in Oregon, where the city servos have to have at least 50% full-service pumps by law. And don't go thinkin' a gallon's a gallon, either: the American gallon's smaller than the imperial one, comes to 3.785 litres. Short-changed from the get-go.

Now, back in the 1990s and 2000s, with the crime waves and that, they had heaps of "drive offs", blokes fillin' up and shootin' through without payin'. So these days near enough every American servo makes ya pay before ya pump (it used to be the other way round). Ya either get yer credit or debit card okayed at the pump first, or ya have a guess at how much petrol ya need, wander inside past the pie that's been in the warmer since Tuesday, hand that much of the folding stuff to whoever's on the till, and tell 'em which pump yer on. Your Yank'll go, "I need 50 bucks on pump number 1." Then the pump cuts out by itself the second the petrol hits the dosh ya handed over, and if ya guessed too high, ya traipse back in and get a refund on the dosh ya didn't use. Bit of a palaver, but there ya go.

Then there's the octane. The U.S. scale's different from the one they use in Europe: a regular gallon of U.S. petrol rates 87 octane, which is about the same as 92 in Europe. Don't ask me. In most states the servo gives ya three grades of octane to pick from: 87 (regular), 89 (midgrade or plus), and 91 (premium). Unless yer hirin' some flash luxury number, yer car'll most likely only need 87 (regular), so don't waste yer money. If yer drivin' round the Rocky Mountains, ya might come across servos sellin' petrol with lower octane ratings, mostly 85. That's fine for 87-octane cars up high where the air's thin, but if yer headin' back down lower, make bloody sure ya fill up with 87.

Diesel's not as common over there, but it's still used a fair bit and ya can get it at most servos, 'specially the ones that cater for the truckies. Now here's one to watch: untaxed "offroad diesel", sold out bush for farm use. It's dyed red (and some call it "red diesel"), and ya don't put that in a car, full stop, cos if ya get caught there's hefty fines. Not worth it.

And this is the one that'll catch ya out. In the United States, the old colour code at servos is that petrol comes out of the nozzle with the black handle, and diesel out of the one with the green handle. That's the opposite of the colour code in the United Kingdom. So check the handle before ya squeeze, and don't mix 'em up.
