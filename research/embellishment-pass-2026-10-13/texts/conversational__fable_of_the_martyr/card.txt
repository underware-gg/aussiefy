unharnessed: A smooth, varied and faithful level-4 retelling with the irony intact; a couple of British/US words slip in.
  minor: 'everyone was pretty chuffed' (for 'There was General Satisfaction'): "Chuffed" is British; an Australian would say "rapt" or "stoked".
  minor: 'Plenty of folks reckoned' (for 'A good many People said'): "Folks" leans American.

yarn-troppo: A lively, well-judged level-4 yarn that keeps every point; the narrator's extra jabs slightly overplay the irony.
  minor: '"At rest! Like he\'d ever done anything else."' (added): The narrator spells out the joke the original leaves to the reader, loosening its deadpan.
  minor: "doesn't she." (for 'So his Wife took Boarders and did the Cooking herself.'): A forced conversational tag that sits oddly on a plain statement.
  minor: 'toddle inside for a lie-down' (for 'he had to go into the House and lie down'): "Toddle" is a cutesy choice that reads more British than Australian here.

yarn-dinkum: A clean, faithful, well-paced retelling that keeps the deadpan irony; slightly light for level 4.
  minor: 'defo good riddance to bad rubbish' (for 'it was certainly a Good Riddance of Bad Rubbish'): "Defo" is a modern youth clipping that sits oddly in an old-time yarn voice.
  minor: 'Just about everyone felt for her' (for 'Nearly Everybody Sympathized with her'): Followed directly by "Everyone had her down", a noticeable repeat.

yarn-deadset: A faithful, tone-perfect word-level translation that keeps the fable frame but sits lighter than level 4.
  minor: 'such a big Lump of a bludger of a Husband' (for 'such a big Hulk of a no-account Husband'): Double "of a" construction piles up awkwardly.
  minor: 'She was reckoned a Martyr.' (for 'She was looked upon as a Martyr.'): Stiff passive use of "reckoned".
  minor: 'defo a Good Riddance' (for 'certainly a Good Riddance of Bad Rubbish'): Youth-slang clipping out of place in a period fable.
  minor: 'There was General Satisfaction when she became the Wife of Mr. Gladden' (for 'There was General Satisfaction when she became the Wife of Mr. Gladden'): Stretches left as written keep it lighter than the level-4 brief.

engine: Mostly the original with scattered bolted-on Aussie words, a wrong "Parliament House" swap and broken joins.
  major: "what Parliament House wuz thinkin' of" (for "he couldn't see what Congress wuz thinkin' of"): Relocates his politics to Australia while Uncle Sam and the Alaskan Boundary remain, a wrong-referent swap.
  major: 'Mind you, Consequently, she was down in the dumps' (for 'Consequently, she was very Miserable'): Broken join of two connectives that also kills the deadpan twist.
  minor: 'Mind you, he was willing to divide the Labor, however;' (for 'He was willing to divide the Labor, however;'): "Mind you" and "however" stack the same connective.
  minor: "Struth, he knocked together a brand-spankin'-new House" (for 'He built a new House'): A bolted-on exclamation, and "knocked together" suggests a rough job, not a fine new house.
  major: 'Once in a Country Town there was a Man with a Weak Back.' (for 'Once in a Country Town there was a Man with a Weak Back.'): Large stretches are left untranslated, far lighter than level 4.
