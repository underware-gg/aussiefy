Once in a Country Town there was a Bloke with a Crook Back.

He could heave a Grindstone into a Farm Wagon if anyone wanted to bet him the Cigars, but every time he picked up an Axe, something got him right in the Spine and he had to go inside and have a lie-down. So his missus took in Boarders and did the Cooking herself.

He was happy enough to split the Work, mind; so he did the Shopping. Only, when he'd bought the Tucker, he'd squat on a Shoe-Box with the Basket between his Legs and say he couldn't see what Congress was thinkin' of.

He had certain Theories about the Alaskan Boundary and he was dead against any Anglo-American Alliance cos Uncle Sam could look after himself at any Bend in the Road, when you got right down to it, and the American People were streets ahead of any other Nationality in every Way, Shape, Manner and Form, as far as that goes. Then his missus would have to send Word for him to get a move on with the Groceries so she could get tea.

Nearly Everybody felt for her, cos she had to put up with such a big Lump of a bludger of a Husband. She was reckoned a Martyr.

One Day the Husband got Sunstroke, being too bone idle to shift into the Shade, and next Day he carked it without an Effort. The Widow gave him the best Funeral of the Year and then put all the folding stuff she could scrape together into a Marble Shaft marked "At Rest."

A good many People said she was Better Off without him, and it was defo a Good Riddance of Bad Rubbish.

They hoped that if she ever got Hitched again she'd pick out a bloke that wasn't scared of Hard Yakka, and had nous enough to pound Sand into a Rat-Hole.

There was General Satisfaction when she became the Wife of Mr. Gladden, who owned the General Store. He built a brand-spankin'-new House, got a Girl in and had the Washing sent out. She could go into the Store and pick out Anything she liked, and he took her for a spin in his new Runabout every Evening.

Consequently, she was down in the dumps, thinking of the Jewel she'd lost.

MORAL: _If the Woman reckons he's All Right, you keep on your own Side of the Fence._
