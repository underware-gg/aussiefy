Ever hear the one about the bloke in a country town with the crook back?

Now, he could heave a grindstone up into a farm wagon if anyone wanted to bet him the cigars on it, but every time he picked up an axe, something got him right in the spine and he had to go inside and have a lie-down. So his missus took in boarders and did all the cooking herself.

Mind you, he was happy to split the work; he did the shopping. Only, once he'd got the tucker, he'd plonk himself on a shoe-box with the basket between his legs and tell anyone who'd listen he couldn't for the life of him see what Congress was thinkin'.

He had his theories about the Alaskan boundary, too, and he was dead against any Anglo-American alliance, cos Uncle Sam could look after himself at any bend in the road, when you got right down to it, and the American people were streets ahead of every other nationality in every way, shape, manner and form, as far as that goes. Then his missus would have to send word for him to get a wriggle on with the groceries so she could get the tea on.

Just about everyone felt for her, stuck with a great big useless lump of a husband like that. Everyone had her down as a proper martyr.

Then one day the husband copped sunstroke, too bone idle to shift into the shade, and the next day he carked it without so much as an effort. The widow gave him the best funeral of the year, and then every bit of folding stuff she could scrape together went into a marble shaft marked "At Rest."

Heaps of people said she was better off without him, and it was defo good riddance to bad rubbish.

They hoped that if she ever got hitched again she'd pick a bloke who wasn't scared of hard yakka, and had the nous to pound sand into a rat-hole.

So the whole town was rapt when she married Mr. Gladden, who owned the general store. He built her a brand-spankin'-new house, got a girl in to help and sent the washing out. She could walk into the store and pick out anything she fancied, and every evening he took her for a spin in his new runabout.

And so, naturally, she was down in the dumps, pining for the jewel she'd lost.

MORAL: _If the woman reckons he's all right, you keep on your own side of the fence._
