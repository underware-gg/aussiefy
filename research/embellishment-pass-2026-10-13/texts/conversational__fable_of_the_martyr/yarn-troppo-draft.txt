Now, this one's a ripper. Out in some country town there's this bloke with a crook back.

Mind you, it's a funny sort of crook back. Bet him the cigars and he'll heave a grindstone up into a farm wagon, no worries. But hand him an axe? Every single time he lifts the thing, something grabs him right in the spine and he's got to toddle inside for a lie-down. So his missus takes in boarders and does all the cooking herself, doesn't she.

Now, fair's fair, he's willing to split the work. He does the shopping. Only, once he's got the tucker, he plonks himself down on a shoe-box with the basket between his knees and starts in on how he can't for the life of him work out what Congress is thinkin', the whole mob of 'em flappin' their gums.

And he's got theories, this bloke. Theories on the Alaskan boundary. And he's dead against any Anglo-American alliance, cos Uncle Sam can look after himself at any bend in the road, when you get right down to it, and the American people are streets ahead of every other nationality in every way, shape, manner and form, as far as that goes. Meanwhile the poor missus has got to send word down the street for him to get a bloody wriggle on with the groceries so she can get the tea on.

Well, just about every man and his dog feels for her, stuck with a great big useless lump of a bludger like that. Everyone's got her down as a proper martyr.

Anyway, one day the husband cops sunstroke, cos he's too bone idle to shift himself into the shade, and the next day he carks it. Doesn't even break a sweat doin' it. The widow gives him the best funeral of the year, then scrapes together every last bit of folding stuff she can find and sinks the lot into a marble shaft with "At Rest" on it. At rest! Like he'd ever done anything else.

Heaps of people reckon she's better off without him, and fair dinkum, good riddance to bad rubbish.

They're all hopin' that if she ever gets hitched again she'll pick a bloke who isn't scared of a bit of hard yakka, and who's got the nous to pound sand into a rat-hole.

So the whole town's rapt when she marries Mr. Gladden, who owns the general store. He builds her a brand-spankin'-new house, gets a girl in to help, sends the washing out, the whole bloody works. She can wander into the store and pick out anything she fancies, and every evening he takes her for a spin in his new runabout.

And is she happy? Is she buggery. She's down in the dumps, pinin' for the jewel she's lost.

MORAL: _If the woman reckons he's all right, you keep on your own side of the fence, mate._
