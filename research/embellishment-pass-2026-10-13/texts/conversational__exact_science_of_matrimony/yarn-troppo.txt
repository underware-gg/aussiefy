Now, this one's a ripper. Jeff Peters leans on the bar and goes, "Like I've told ya before, I've never had much faith in how sneaky women are. Partners, off-siders, whatever ya call 'em, even in the most harmless little swifty going, ya can't trust 'em as far as ya can chuck 'em."

"Fair enough, they've earned that one," I go. "Reckon they've got every right to be called the honest sex."

"Course they have, why wouldn't they be?" goes Jeff. "They've got the rest of us blokes either grafting or staying back at work for 'em. They're right as rain in business till their feelings get stirred up, or they've had the hair done one too many times. Then ya want a flat-footed, wheezing bloke with sandy whiskers, five nippers runnin' rings round their mum and a building society mortgage round his neck, waiting in the wings to grab her desk. Now, there was this widow lady me and Andy Tucker took on to help with a little marriage agency lurk we cooked up out in Cairo.

"See, if you've got the readies for advertising, and I'm talking a roll as fat as the little end of a wagon tongue, there's bloody good dosh in marriage agencies. We had about $6,000 and we were counting on doubling it in two months, and two months is about as long as a lurk like that lasts before ya need a New Jersey charter to keep it legal-looking.

"So we knock up an ad. Went something like this:

"Charming widow, beautiful, home-loving, 32 years, with $3,000 ready money and valuable country property, would remarry. Would prefer a poor man with a loving nature to one with money, as she realises the solid virtues are most often found in the humble walks of life. Doesn't mind an older man or a plain-looking one, if he's faithful and true and can manage property and invest money sensibly. Write, with particulars.

Lonely, Care of Peters & Tucker, agents, Cairo, Ill.

"Beautiful, ay? Pure bait. 'So far, so pernicious,' I go, once we'd finished cooking up this bit of literature. 'Now,' I go, 'where's the lady?'

"And Andy gives me one of his looks. Dead calm, but ya can tell he's had a gutful.

"'Jeff,' he goes, 'I thought you'd got over all them ideas about realism in your art. Why's there gotta be a lady? When they flog heaps of watered stock on Wall Street, do ya go looking for a mermaid in it? What's a marriage ad got to do with a lady, mate?'

"'Now listen here,' I go. 'Ya know my rule, Andy. Any time I'm having a go at the letter of the law, the goods have gotta be real. Ya've gotta be able to see 'em and trot 'em out. That, and keeping a close eye on the council by-laws and the train timetables, is how I've stayed out of any strife with the coppers that a five dollar note and a cigar couldn't sort out. So to run this lurk, we've gotta be able to wheel out a real live charming widow, or near enough, with or without the beauty, the hereditaments and the appurtenances, the whole flamin' catalogue and writ of errors, or else we'll be fronting a justice of the peace.'

"'Well,' goes Andy, and ya can see the cogs turning while he has a rethink, 'maybe it's safer, in case the post office or the peace commission comes sniffing round our agency. But where,' he goes, 'are ya gonna find a widow who'll waste her time on a marriage lurk with no bloody marriage in it?'

"So I tell Andy I reckon I know just the sheila. Old mate of mine, Zeke Trotter, used to pull soda water and teeth in a tent show, both, mind you, out of the same tent. Zeke made his missus a widow a year back by knocking off a bottle of the old doctor's indigestion cure instead of the liniment he always got sloshed on. Wrong bottle, poor bugger. I used to drop in at their place all the time, and I thought we could get her on board.

"It's only sixty miles to the little town where she lives, so I hop on the I. C., and there she is, same cottage, same sunflowers, same roosters standing on the washtub like nothing's changed. Now, Mrs. Trotter fitted our ad first-rate, except maybe for the beauty, the age and what the property was worth. Bit of a stretch on those, I s'pose. But she looked like she'd do the job and she wasn't hard on the eye, and it was a kindness to Zeke's memory to give her the work."
