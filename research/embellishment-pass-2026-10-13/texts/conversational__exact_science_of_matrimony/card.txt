yarn-dinkum: Natural, well-pitched level 3 with every point kept; leans on "lurk".
  minor: "to work this lurk we've got to be able to trot out a real live charming widow" (for "to work this scheme we've got to be able to produce bodily a charming widow"): "lurk" is used four times for the scheme; a reader notices.
  minor: 'I never had much faith in how sneaky women are' (for 'I never had much confidence in the perfidiousness of woman'): Plain paraphrase loses Jeff's grand misused word, part of his character.

unharnessed: A lively, accurate level 3 with good idiom; a few stiff or stock choices.
  minor: 'till their feelings or their hairdos get worked up too much' (for 'until they get their emotions or their hair touched up too much'): "worked up" fits feelings but not hairdos; the zeugma of "touched up" is lost.
  minor: 'as she realizes that the solid virtues are oftenest to be found' (for 'as she realizes that the solid virtues are oftenest to be found'): US spelling left in the ad.
  minor: "'And now,' I says, 'where's the sheila?'" (for "'And now,' says I, 'where is the lady.'"): "sheila" is a dated stock word here.

yarn-deadset: A faithful, light retelling with natural slang; a little under-weight for level 3.
  minor: 'As partners or co-workers in even the most innocent line of shonky business' (for 'As partners or coeducators in the most innocent line of graft'): "co-workers" flattens the comic "coeducators".
  minor: 'a building and loan mortgage' (for 'a building and loan mortgage'): Left as the US form where "building society mortgage" was the easy local swap.

yarn-troppo: A lively full yarn with good idiom, but heavier than the brief, with an invented frame and one muddled idiom.
  major: "Now, this one's a ripper. Jeff Peters leans on the bar and goes" (for '"As I have told you before," said Jeff Peters,'): Invents a pub setting and a stock opener the original lacks.
  major: "ya can't trust 'em as far as ya can chuck 'em" (for 'As partners or coeducators in the most innocent line of graft they are not trustworthy.'): The idiom calls women generally untrustworthy, against the irony that they are too honest to be crooked partners.
  minor: 'both, mind you, out of the same tent' (for 'who used to draw soda water and teeth in a tent show'): A new detail rather than a reaction to the original.
  minor: 'before ya need a New Jersey charter to keep it legal-looking' (for 'without taking out a New Jersey charter'): Adds an explanation that changes the joke slightly.
  minor: "'Jeff,' he goes," (for "'Jeff,' says he,"): "goes" as the speech tag for nearly every line becomes a tic.

engine: Mostly the untouched original with swears and "dosh" sprinkled in; pastiche, and "a goer" takes the wrong sense.
  major: 'Bloody hell, we had about $6,000' (for 'We had about $6,000'): An interjection bolted on where nothing in the original calls for it; reads as pastiche.
  major: 'possessing $3,000 dosh and owning valuable country property' (for 'possessing $3,000 cash and owning valuable country property'): Slang inside a formal newspaper ad breaks the ad's register.
  major: 'But she looked a goer and praiseworthy to the eye' (for 'But she looked feasible and praiseworthy to the eye'): "a goer" said of a woman implies sexual eagerness, not that she was a workable prospect.
  major: 'reconstructing his bloody mind' (for 'reconstructing his mind'): "bloody" scattered as filler throughout; one representative quote.
  minor: 'by a careful study of city ordinances and train schedules I have kept out of all trouble with the police' (for 'by a careful study of city ordinances and train schedules I have kept out of all trouble with the police'): US forms left untouched.
