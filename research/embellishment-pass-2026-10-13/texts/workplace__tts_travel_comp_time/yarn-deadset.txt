## Travel comp time, for everyone

You can ask for travel comp time when you're approved to travel for work and that travel happens outside regular working hours. Travel comp time is for “temporary duty,” also known as “TDY” travel, _not_ local travel or getting to and from work. TDY travel is more than 12 hours long and needs a pre-approved authorisation in Concur.

There are no limits on earning travel comp time based on salary level or schedule type.

Have a squiz at OPM’s [Travel Comp Time Fact Sheet](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/pay-administration/fact-sheets/compensatory-time-off-for-travel/) for more detail.

### Understanding travel comp time

Travelling during your usual work day counts as regular hours, including travelling to the airport from home.

Generally, travel comp time is used as a “gap filler” when you're travelling outside your regular work hours on a regular work day, but not officially “performing work.”

For more details, current GSA staff can check the [Compensation for Government Travel slidedeck](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RFSq_4KdMza_pkcHGp9Hy2RNR2ryLs0b/view?usp=sharing) to work out the breakdown of your hours. Get in touch with your Timekeeper or an HR Specialist at your agency if you've got questions.

### Calculating travel comp time

For working out travel comp time hours, travel ends when you check in at the hotel or get home. Travel comp time hours are based on elapsed time. Use the time zone at departure to work out elapsed time.

To report travel time accurately, we recommend keeping a log of:

- When you left home and got to the airport
- When your flights actually took off and landed
- When you left the destination airport and got to your hotel

### Using travel comp time

Travel comp time expires one year (26 pay periods) after it's earned. When you hit the expiry date, you'll forfeit the leave. If you leave GSA, any unused travel comp time will be forfeited.
