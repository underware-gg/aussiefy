On Saturday, the family of American muso and songwriter Bob Weir announced he'd died surrounded by loved ones. The former guitarist and singer of rock band the Grateful Dead was 78. His family announced his death on Instagram, saying he'd recently fought a cancer diagnosis but died of "underlying lung issues". The announcement didn't say what date Weir died.

Weir played rhythm guitar and sang with American psychedelic rock band the Grateful Dead for decades, and also wrote some of their songs, including the hit "Sugar Magnolia", a staple of Grateful Dead gigs. He also had a long solo career, putting together all sorts of groups with his old Grateful Dead bandmates, and playing live until months before he died.

Weir was born Robert Hall Parber on 16 October 1947 in San Francisco, California, and grew up in the San Francisco Bay Area, spending some time at school in Colorado, where he met future Grateful Dead member John Perry Barlow. Weir started playing guitar at 13 and drifted into a local bluegrass scene, where he first came across Jerry Garcia playing banjo. On 31 December 1965, Garcia and Weir agreed to start their own group called Mother McCree's Uptown Jug Champions, which grew into the Grateful Dead.

Weir played rhythm guitar, and sang for the group, for decades. He also branched out into a solo career and a range of other groups outside the Grateful Dead from the 1970s on, including Kingfish, Bobby and the Midnites, RatDog, Furthur, The Other Ones, The Dead, Wolf Bros, and Dead & Company. All the groups had his old Grateful Dead collaborators in them and tried their hand at genres from country rock to jam band to jazz-influenced roots rock grounded in blues and country. His vocals were mostly just harmonies and backup singing, but he sang lead on several tracks, including Grateful Dead anthem "Truckin'", a song named by the Library of Congress as a national treasure in 1997.

Away from the stage, Weir also set up Tamalpais Research Institute, a virtual performance platform, and was active in politics, backing several causes, including the environment, poverty relief, and issues to do with the arts. He'd stayed active in the Bay Area music scene, playing live with local musos and co-owning the venue Sweetwater Music Hall in Mill Valley, California.
