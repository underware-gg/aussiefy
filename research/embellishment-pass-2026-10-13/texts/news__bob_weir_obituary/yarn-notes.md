## deadset

- Wrong-sense planned phrase: "bloke" for "fellow" in "fellow Grateful Dead alumni" and "fellow Grateful Dead collaborators". That "fellow" is an adjective (= his bandmates), not the noun for a man; the engine draft reads "bloke Grateful Dead alumni". Declined; used "his old Grateful Dead bandmates".
- Lint suggestions offered "give ... a burl" / "have a crack at" for "had a go at a solo career" (already Aussie, and for an obituary "had a go" understates a long career; I changed it to "branched out into").
- Rewriting the dates in Australian order ("16 October 1947", "31 December 1965") passed the number check, which is what I wanted.
- Accepted on the first submit.

- Lint: `tag_in_narration` on "Not bad for a backup singer, ay." Fair enough at the pub, where the talker is speaking to the listener, but I dropped the "ay". `repeated_phrase` "one of them" (×3) counted "one of 'em" usefully; I varied it.
- `name_added` "New Year": from my "New Year's Eve, no less" for 31 December 1965. That's a gloss on the date the original gives, not invented content.
- Lookups that came back empty: "a good innings" (a long life, said of someone who died old), "outfit" (a band or company). "plonk away" returned "nick off", "rack off" and the other "go away" phrases (matched on "away").
