Bob Weir, the American muso and songwriter, has died surrounded by loved ones, his family announced on Saturday. The former Grateful Dead guitarist and singer was 78. The family broke the news on Instagram, saying he'd recently fought a cancer diagnosis but died of "underlying lung issues". They didn't say what date he died.

For decades Weir played rhythm guitar and sang with American psychedelic rock band the Grateful Dead, and he wrote some of their songs too, including the hit "Sugar Magnolia", a fixture at every Grateful Dead gig. He also had a long solo career, starting up all sorts of groups with his old Grateful Dead bandmates, and he was still playing live till just months before he died.

He was born Robert Hall Parber on 16 October 1947 in San Francisco, California, and grew up round the San Francisco Bay Area, with a stint at school in Colorado, where he met John Perry Barlow, later of the Grateful Dead. Weir picked up the guitar at 13 and found his way into the local bluegrass scene, which is where he first came across Jerry Garcia, playing banjo. On 31 December 1965, Garcia and Weir agreed to start their own group, Mother McCree's Uptown Jug Champions, and that grew into the Grateful Dead.

He played rhythm guitar and sang for the band for decades. From the 1970s on he also went out on his own and played in a fair few other groups outside the Grateful Dead, including Kingfish, Bobby and the Midnites, RatDog, Furthur, The Other Ones, The Dead, Wolf Bros, and Dead & Company. Every one of them had his old Grateful Dead collaborators in it, and between them they had a crack at everything from country rock to jam band to jazz-influenced roots rock built on blues and country. Mostly he sang harmonies and backup, but he took the lead on several tracks, including the Grateful Dead anthem "Truckin'", which the Library of Congress named a national treasure in 1997.

Off the stage, Weir also set up Tamalpais Research Institute, a virtual performance platform, and he was active in politics, getting behind several causes, including the environment, poverty relief, and issues to do with the arts. He'd kept his hand in on the Bay Area music scene, too, playing live with local musos and co-owning the venue Sweetwater Music Hall in Mill Valley, California.
