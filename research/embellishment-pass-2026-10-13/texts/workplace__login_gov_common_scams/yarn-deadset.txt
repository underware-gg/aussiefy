Scammers are forever cookin' up brand-spankin'-new ways to con people into handin' over their personal info or makin' dodgy payments. Keepin' up with their tricks is yer best defence. Below are some of the most common rorts, plus ways to look after yerself.

## Fake emails and texts

Scammers send emails or texts that look official, often usin' government logos and lingo. This is called phishing. Phishing attacks might:

- Hurry ya up to click a link to “verify your account”
- Make out there's an urgent problem with yer benefits or tax return
- Ask for personal info like Social Security numbers or bank details

### How to protect yourself

- Don't click on links or download attachments from senders ya don't know.
- Check the message is fair dinkum by gettin' in touch with the agency direct, usin' its official contact details.
- Report dodgy messages to the agency the scammer's pretendin' to be

## Impersonation phone calls

Fraudsters might ring up pretendin' to be from the government. They often:
- Threaten arrest, licence suspension, or losin' yer benefits
- Want payment on the spot by gift cards, cryptocurrency, or wire transfers

### How to protect yourself

- Government agencies will never ask for payment over the phone.
- Hang up and ring the official number listed on the Login.gov or agency website.

## Fake websites and online portals

Some scams use dodgy websites made to look like official government pages. These sites might:

- Ask ya to log in with personal details
- Charge fees for services that are free on the official sites

### How to protect yourself

- Always check the website URL ends in .gov.
- Don't use search engines for sign in links—type the official URL in yerself and double-check the spellin'.

## Social media scams

Scammers set up fake social media profiles makin' out they're government officials. They might:
- Offer grants or financial help in return for fees up front
- Ask for personal or bankin' details through direct messages

### How to protect yourself

- Keep in mind government agencies don't send messages out of the blue about benefits.
- Report suss profiles on social media to the social media platform.
