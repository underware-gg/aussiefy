Strewth, where do I start? Scammers, the grubs, never stop cookin' up brand-spankin'-new ways to con ya into handin' over yer personal info or makin' a dodgy payment. Ya knock one swifty on the head and they've got another one goin' by smoko. So knowin' their tricks is the best defence ya've got. Here's a rundown of the most common ones doin' the rounds, and how to look after yerself.

## Fake emails and texts

First up, scammers send emails or texts done up to look like the real deal, often with government logos and all the official lingo slapped on. Looks the goods. Isn't. This is called phishing, and a phishing attack might:

- Hurry ya along to click a link and “verify your account”, quick, before somethin' terrible happens
- Make out there's some urgent drama with yer benefits or yer tax return
- Ask for yer personal info, like Social Security numbers or bank details

### How to protect yourself

- Don't click links or download attachments from anyone ya don't know. Not a one.
- Check whether the message is fair dinkum by gettin' onto the agency yerself, usin' its official contact details, not whatever's in the message.
- Report dodgy messages to whatever agency the scammer's pretendin' to be

## Impersonation phone calls

Then there's the phone. Fraudsters might give ya a ring makin' out they're from the government, all serious voice and official-soundin' name. Nine times outta ten they'll:
- Threaten ya with arrest, a licence suspension, or losin' yer benefits
- Want payin' on the spot, by gift cards, cryptocurrency, or wire transfers, which is a dead giveaway right there

### How to protect yourself

- Government agencies will never ask ya to pay over the phone. Never.
- So hang up on 'em, then ring the official number listed on the Login.gov or agency website.

## Fake websites and online portals

Some scams go to the trouble of runnin' dodgy websites made up to look just like official government pages. Spittin' image, some of 'em. These sites might:

- Get ya to log in with yer personal details
- Charge ya fees for services that are free on the official sites, the cheek of it

### How to protect yourself

- Always check the website URL ends in .gov.
- Don't go lookin' up sign in links on a search engine—type the official URL in yerself and double-check the spellin', cos one letter out is all it takes.

## Social media scams

And last of all, scammers set up fake social media profiles makin' out they're government officials. They might:
- Offer grants or a bit of financial help, as long as ya cough up some fees first
- Slide into yer direct messages askin' for yer personal or bankin' details

### How to protect yourself

- Bear in mind government agencies don't send messages about benefits out of the blue. If one turns up, it's suss.
- Report suss profiles on social media to the platform they're on, and let 'em sort the grubs out.
