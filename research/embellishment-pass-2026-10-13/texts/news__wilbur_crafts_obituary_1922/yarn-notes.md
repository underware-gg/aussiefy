## deadset

- Wrong-sense planned phrases: "darts" for "cigarettes". In a list of the amusements he attacked (screen vampires, close dancing, Sunday baseball), "darts" reads as the pub game. Lookup "cigarettes" gives "a ciggie" (level 1) first, so the engine picked the riskier level-3 form. "old man" for "father" ("his father's church") is the right sense but too offhand for an obituary, which the genre says is respectful.
- Lint suggestion wrong sense: for "having a go at popular amusements" (criticising), it offered "have a crack at ..." / "give ... a burl", which mean attempt. It also offered "old fella" for "father" again, and "bugger all" for "very little" in an obituary.
- The plan is thin: two phrases for a 400-word text at level 4. The obituary has lots of everyday words with Aussie forms ("started" for founded, "on side", "a fair bit", "ciggies").

## dinkum

- The engine dinkum draft is the worst I saw. It opens "Crikey, WASHINGTON, Dec. 27.—" (an opener before the dateline), "Bloody hell, in 1874 he married...", "his bloody attacks" (swearing in an obituary), and puts a tag-on inside a quoted book title: "Rhetoric Made Racy, ay." The last breaks the genre rule that quotes stay as said, and the respectful-obituary rule throughout.
- Lint is clean apart from the same "old fella" / "have a crack at" suggestions.

## troppo

- Troppo plus a respectful obituary pull against each other, and the brief doesn't say which wins. I kept it warm and wry: "A wowser, we'd say" right after the opponents' "reform fanatic", the "Rhetoric Made Racy! From him!" reaction, and a closing line on him not liking the yarn told over a coldie. None of it is mocking.
- Lint suggestions wrong sense: "the oldies" / "the olds" (parents) for "the folks" meaning people; "bloke" for "man" in "the work this man got through" (fine, but the plan's tone flip in an obituary).
- Lookups wrong or empty: "ran the show" returned "a big-noter" (a show-off); "a breather" returned "an L-plater" and "whinger"; "write up a storm" had no entry (I rephrased to "wrote heaps").
- Lookup "wowser" found exactly the right word ("a puritanical killjoy"); it fits this subject perfectly but wasn't planned, because the original never says "killjoy".
