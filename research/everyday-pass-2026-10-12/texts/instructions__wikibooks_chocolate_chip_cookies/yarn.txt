Ingredients

2 sticks (½ lb / 225 g / 1 cup) butter, softened

¾ cup (180 ml) white sugar

¾ cup (180 ml) packed brown sugar

¼ teaspoon vanilla extract

2 standard (100 g) eggs

2 ¼ cups (600 ml / 290 g) plain flour

1 teaspoon bicarb soda

1 teaspoon salt

1 teaspoon dried ground lemon or orange peel (optional)

2 cups (480 ml / 12 oz) semi-sweet choc chips or choccy bits

5 oz (150 g) chopped nuts, like peanuts (optional)

How ya do it

Crank the oven up to 375°F (210°C), or 350°F (195°C) if ya like your bikkies chewy.

Cream the butter, white sugar and brown sugar together in a big mixing bowl.

Chuck the eggs in one at a time, beating till each one's properly mixed through.

Add the vanilla extract and beat it in.

In a little bowl, sift the dry stuff together (not the choccy or the nuts). Tip it into the wet mix in two goes and fold it through till it's all come together. Don't go mad beating it, or you'll get gluten forming and that'll change how the bikkies turn out.

Stir in the choc chips, and the nuts if you're using 'em.

Drop the dough in rounded tablespoons onto baking trays, no greasing needed. There should be enough fat in the dough to stop it sticking, but if it does, a bit of baking paper or a non-stick tray'll sort ya out.

Bake in the hot oven for 10–12 minutes, or till they're golden brown (ovens vary, so keep an eye on 'em). Let 'em sit for about a minute, then get 'em off the hot tray and onto a wire rack to cool.

Notes, tips and other ways to do it

Ingredients

Use cake flour instead of plain, swap the white sugar for brown sugar, and use butter instead of marg. Cake flour and brown sugar mean you can use butter without your bikkies going all over the tray like puddles (which is the only reason anyone uses marg in the first place).

Use a bit of vegetable shortening instead of the butter or marg to cut down the "spread" (the bikkies come out thicker).

Use a bit of vegetable oil (no more than 1/5 of the total fat) instead of the butter or marg to get more spread, and thinner bikkies.

Choccy with more than 65% cocoa will give ya a fair bit different flavour from the "traditional" recipe. Rule of thumb: if the choccy's too bitter for you to enjoy eating straight, you prob'ly won't like it in the bikkies either (no matter how flash it is).

A few shakes of ground cinnamon in with the dry stuff gives 'em a bit more flavour too.

If ya want to use ordinary flour and butter, the trick is to bump the flour up to 3+ cups, which should give ya a fairly stiff dough that won't spread out much. This trick even works with wholemeal flour.

You can muck about with how much white sugar to brown sugar to suit yourself. More brown sugar makes chewier bikkies. More white sugar makes crispier ones.

More vanilla extract (between ½ and ¾ of a teaspoon instead of ¼) gives the bikkie a stronger flavour.

If ya can't get hold of vanilla extract, use two teaspoons of vanilla sugar in place of some of the white sugar.

Throw in a handful of rolled oats to give the bikkies more flavour and a bit more chew.

Method

Some people reckon you should chill the dough before baking, though ya don't usually need to. Chilling it can help keep the spread down, so you get thicker bikkies.

Use a non-stick tray with no grease on it instead of a greased one to stop them spreading as much, for thicker bikkies. Or grease your tray if ya like 'em thinner (and crispier).

How good your flavour ingredients are makes a heap of difference to how they turn out. Top-shelf butter, choccy and vanilla make a bloody good bikkie.

Chopping up a block of choccy instead of using ready-made chips gives a more rough-and-ready texture. Just make sure the bits are small enough to eat without a struggle.

Don't stack the bikkies while they're hot or they'll go out of shape and won't cool as quick. Use a cooling rack instead for the best results.

For a stronger nutty flavour, toast the nuts first. Spread 'em out even on a baking tray and pop 'em in the 350°F oven for ten minutes. Take 'em out and let 'em cool before ya add 'em to the dough.

If ya like a soft, squishy middle in your bikkie, pull 'em out a minute early.
