_Terre Haute, Indiana, September 6._

FRIEND AL: Well, Al old mate I reckon you seen in the paper where I been sold to the White Sox. Fair dinkum Al it come as a shock to me and I bet it did to all you good old mates down home too. You could of knocked me over with a feather when the old bloke come up to me and says Jack I've sold you to the Chicago Americans.

I didn't have no idea anything like that was on the cards. For five minutes I was just dum and couldn't get a word out.

He says We aren't getting what you are worth but I want you to go up to that big league and show them galahs that there is a Central League on the map. He says Go and pitch the ball you been pitching down here and there'll be bugger all to it. He says All you need is a bit of ticker and Walsh or nobody else won't have nothing on you.

So I says I would do me best and I thanked him for the fair go I got in Terre Haute. They always was beaut to me here and though I done more than my share I always felt my work was appresiated. We are finishing second and I done most of it. I can't help being proud of my first year's record in professional baseball and you know I am not skiting when I say that Al.

Well Al it'll feel funny being up there in the big show when I never was properly in a big city before. But I reckon I seen enough of life not to get spooked by the high buildings eh Al?

I'll just give them what I got and if they don't like it they can send me back to the old Central and I'll be happy as Larry.

I didn't know anybody was sussing me out, but one of the young fellas on the team told me Jack Doyle the White Sox scout was down here having a squiz at me when Grand Rapids was in town. I beat them twice in that serious. You know Grand Rapids never had Buckley's against me when I was on song. I shut them out in the first game and they only got one run in the second on account of Flynn misjuging that fly ball. Anyway Doyle liked my work and he wired Comiskey to buy me. Comiskey come back with an offer and they excepted it. I dunno how much they got but anyway I am sold to the big league and fair dinkum Al I will make good.

Well Al I'll be home in a few days and we'll have some of the good old times. Regards to all the boys and tell them I am still their mate and not all up meself over this big league business.

Your mate, JACK.
