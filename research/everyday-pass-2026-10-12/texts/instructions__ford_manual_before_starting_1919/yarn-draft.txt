What've ya gotta do before ya start the Car?

Before ya even think about crankin' her up, fill the radiator (whip the cap off the top) with clean fresh water. If ya can't get your mitts on properly clean water, strain it through a bit of muslin or some other cloth like it, so no muck gets in and bungs up the little tubes in the radiator. The whole system takes about three gallons. And this is the big one: don't run the car under her own steam unless the water system's been filled. Keep pourin' till you're dead sure the radiator and the cylinder water jackets are both chock-a-block. You'll know it's full when the water starts spillin' out the overflow pipe onto the dirt. For the first few days you're drivin' a brand-spankin'-new car, it's a beaut idea to check the radiator a fair bit and make sure she's kept topped up. Top up the water as often as she needs it. Soft rainwater off the tank, if ya can get it clean, beats hard water hands down, 'cause hard water can carry alkalies and other salts that leave a crust behind and clog the radiator. What about the Petrol?

The ten-gallon petrol tank wants fillin' up, near enough to the top, and never let her run low. When you're fillin' the tank, make bloody sure there's no naked flame within several feet, 'cause the fumes are flamin' volatile and they get about quick smart. And be careful as with strikin' a match anywhere near where petrol's been spilt, 'cause the air for several feet round is thick with that fume, and it'll go up like a bonfire on cracker night. Don't let the little breather hole in the petrol cap get bunged up either, or the petrol won't flow proper to the carburettor. Ya can drain the tank by openin' the pet cock in the sediment bulb at the bottom.

What about the Oilin' System?

Soon as the car rocks up, see to it that some medium light, top-quality gas engine oil goes into the crank case through the breather pipe at the front of the engine (there's a metal cap over it). Down under the car, in the flywheel casing (that's the sump that holds the oil), you'll find two pet cocks. Pour the oil in nice and slow till it runs out the top cock. Leave that cock open till it stops runnin', then shut it. Once the engine's properly loosened up, you'll get the best out of her by keepin' the oil level halfway between the two cocks, but don't you ever, ever let it drop below the bottom one. Everything else on the car's oiled up proper when it leaves the factory. Still, it pays to check all the grease cups are topped up and the bits that need oil have got it. (See Cut No. 18, and the chapter on Lubrication.)

How do ya work the Spark and Throttle Levers?

Under the steerin' wheel there's two little levers. The right-hand one (the throttle) decides how much mixture (petrol and air) goes into the engine. With the engine goin', the further ya pull that lever down towards yourself (they call it "openin' the throttle"), the faster she runs and the more grunt ya get. The left-hand lever looks after the spark, which sets off the gas in the engine's cylinders. Pushin' this lever "advances the spark," and ya bring it down a notch at a time till the motor seems to be goin' flat out. Take it past that and you'll hear a dull knock in the engine, which is her tellin' ya to back off. (See the chapter on Ignition.)

Where do these levers want to be when ya start the Engine?

The spark lever usually goes in about the third or fourth notch of the quadrant (the notched half-circle the levers sit on). The throttle usually wants openin' about five or six notches. Give it a few goes and you'll soon suss out where they need to be for a good start. Just don't shove the spark lever too far, or the engine might "back kick," and that crank handle'll give your wrist a wallop you won't forget in a hurry.
