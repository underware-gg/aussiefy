KEY MESSAGE 2...Maybe the odd shower Wednesday, but drier on the whole. Thursday dries right out and turns a bit fresh, about what you'd expect for this time of year.

Righto, here's the drum. A trailing cold front slides across the region Wednesday, with high pressure at the surface building in from the west through the day. The model soundings show more mixing going on in the boundary layer, which backs up the drier conditions, and also means westerly winds gusting 20-30 mph. That westerly coming down off the hills, plus 925mb temperatures of +17- 18C, will give us a warm autumn day, with highs in the 70s in plenty of spots. There are signs of a stray shower or two as a weak wave wanders through, but it won't cover much ground, and with the lower levels that dry it'll be a light shower or a bit of spitting at most. Not enough to bother bringing the washing in.

Thursday, the high pressure settles in properly and the air dries out even more. Thursday'll most likely stay dry, though it'll be cooler than Wednesday. A cooler airmass will have moved in by then, with 925mb temperatures about 10C down on Wednesday's. That works out to a bit nippy but about normal for the season, mostly in the lower 60s.

KEY MESSAGE 3...Low pressure system expected to bring rain Friday into early Saturday. Gusty northwest winds and colder than usual.

Now, the deterministic and the ensemble guidance both show a stronger, deeper northern stream shortwave trough shoving across the Great Lakes and the northeast Friday into Saturday. This one's got synoptic support behind it, too: an upper level jet sets up near southern New England, which gets the air rising over a big area and at the same time drags a plume of wetter-than-usual air up north into the region. So that sets things up for rain right across the place. There's moisture by the bucketload, mind you, but bugger all instability, so the chance of heavy rain causing any real grief is pretty low. The ensembles have totals mostly from 0.25 to 0.60", with the ECMWF and the Canadian keener on the wet than the GFS runs. On those two, totals closer to an inch are on the cards. Those bigger numbers aren't out of the question either, if we can tap into some better forcing with the warm front. The finer details, like exactly when the rain fronts up and when it clears off, are still a bit up in the air, because the guidance hasn't all agreed yet. Most likely the rain rolls in some time Friday morning and keeps going overnight. Most of the guidance has this system moving through fairly smartly, so it won't hang about all weekend. The bulk of the rain should clear out early Saturday morning. On top of that, the southerly LLJ that comes in ahead of this system nudges in on Friday, and if it mixes down enough, it'll bring breezy to gusty southerlies (gusts 20-30 mph).

Then a strong cold front barrels across the region by early Saturday morning, and that drops the northeast into a classic cold air advection setup: gusty NW winds, cooler temperatures and the odd shower. A NW 40-50 kt LLJ parks itself over the region Saturday. The tighter gradient, plus the CAA steepening the lapse rates, will mean deeper mixing and so gusty NW winds. How much mixing we get and how strong the LLJ ends up are still hard to pin down this far out, but the ensembles are flagging gusts of 35-45 mph, maybe more over the coastal waters. Hang onto your hat. 925mb temperatures run from - 2C to +2C on Saturday, which will most likely give us the coldest temperatures of the season so far. Highs will do it tough to get out of the 40s inland, with the low 50s in the valleys and on the coastal plain. Proper brass monkey weather. The RH fields show some moisture still hanging around in the cyclonic flow. That might be enough for a few scattered showers. Some of the higher spots in the Berkshires might only just scrape the 40 degree mark on Saturday. It's a bit too early to be saying the "S word" just yet (snow). But...if the timing lines up, you can't rule out a morning flurry or shower on that high ground out west. Reckon on a cold night Saturday, with a good chance the interior drops to at least freezing. The higher spots could get down into the upper 20s. Cold enough to leave the esky at home and keep the tinnies on the back verandah.
