## Travel comp time, for everyone

You can ask for travel comp time when you've been given the okay to travel for work and that travel happens outside your normal working hours. Travel comp time is for “temporary duty,” also known as “TDY” travel, _not_ local trips or your daily commute. TDY travel runs for more than 12 hours and needs an authorisation approved ahead of time in Concur.

There's no cap on earning travel comp time based on your pay level or the kind of schedule you work.

Have a squiz at OPM’s [Travel Comp Time Fact Sheet](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/pay-administration/fact-sheets/compensatory-time-off-for-travel/) for the nitty-gritty.

### Understanding travel comp time

Travelling during your usual work day counts as regular hours, and that includes getting from home to the airport.

Generally, travel comp time is a “gap filler” for when you're travelling outside your normal hours on a normal work day, but not officially “performing work.”

For more detail, current GSA staff can check the [Compensation for Government Travel slidedeck](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RFSq_4KdMza_pkcHGp9Hy2RNR2ryLs0b/view?usp=sharing) to work out how your hours break down. If you've got questions, sing out to your Timekeeper or an HR Specialist at your agency.

### Calculating travel comp time

When you're working out travel comp time hours, your travel ends when you check in at the hotel or get home. Travel comp time hours go by elapsed time. Use the time zone you left from to work out the elapsed time.

To get your travel time spot on, it's a good idea to keep a log of:

- When you left home and got to the airport
- When your flights actually took off and landed
- When you left the airport at the other end and got to your hotel

### Using travel comp time

Travel comp time runs out one year (26 pay periods) after you earn it. Once you hit the expiry date, the leave is gone for good, so use it or lose it. If you leave GSA, any travel comp time you haven't used is gone too.
