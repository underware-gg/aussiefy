Julio Teherán, a two-time Major League Baseball All-Star, has hung up the boots, announcing his retirement from baseball on Monday at the age of 35. The call came two days after he was pulled from a scheduled start for Team Colombia at the 2026 World Baseball Classic (WBC) with a crook shoulder.

Before he called it a day, Teherán had helped Team Colombia to a 3-1 win over Team Panama at the WBC.

Teherán had a thirteen-year MLB career, mostly with the Atlanta Braves, but he also had stints with the Los Angeles Angels, Detroit Tigers, Milwaukee Brewers and New York Mets. He made his MLB debut with the Braves in 2011 as a 20-year-old and stuck with them for his first nine seasons, getting picked as an All-Star in 2014 and 2016.

Over his MLB career, Teherán put together an 81-82 record, with a 3.85 ERA, a 1.223 WHIP and 1,260 strikeouts in 1,470.2 innings. Not a bad innings, all up. He'd spent the 2022 and 2025 seasons playing in the Mexican Baseball League.
