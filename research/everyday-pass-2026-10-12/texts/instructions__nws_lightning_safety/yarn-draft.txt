No word of a lie: when there's a thunderstorm about, there's nowhere outside that's safe. Not one spot. If you can hear thunder, you're close enough for the bloody thing to have a crack at ya. So just remember, "When Thunder Roars, Go Indoors!" Too many punters leave it way too late to get somewhere safe when a storm's rollin' in. And sadly, all that dawdlin' is behind a lot of the lightning deaths and injuries over in the U.S.

The best way to keep yourself safe from lightning is to not be anywhere near it in the first place. You do not want to get caught out in a storm, full stop. Have a lightning plan, and call off or put off whatever you're up to early if the weather blokes reckon storms are on the way. Keep an eye on the sky and get yourself somewhere safe before it turns nasty. A proper solid building or a car with a hard roof, they're your safe bets. A rain shelter, a little shed, or anything with no roof on it, like a golf buggy or a convertible, is no flamin' good.

Once you're inside, don't touch anything plugged into a power point, keep your hands off the plumbing, and leave the corded phone alone. Mobiles and cordless phones are fine. Stay away from the outside doors and windows too, and don't go lyin' on the garage floor, ya galah.

Then, to top it off, heaps of people head back out too soon after the storm looks like it's gone, often just waiting for the rain to ease or stop. It's all of that carry-on put together that puts people at risk when there's a thunderstorm about.

Minimizing the Risk

To cut your own chances of copping a lightning strike, plan ahead when you head out so you can get somewhere safe quick smart if a storm starts brewin'. If the sky's lookin' angry or you hear thunder, get inside somewhere safe straight away. Don't muck about. Once you're in, keep away from corded phones, electrical gear, plumbing, and windows and doors. And finally, wait 30 minutes after the last lightning or thunder before you go back out. If every bastard stuck to those simple rules, the number of people hurt or killed by lightning in that country could drop right off.

Remember, it's what you do when there's a storm about that decides your own odds of getting struck. When Thunder Roars, Go Indoors!

Lightning Victims

If someone cops a lightning strike, they might need medical help straight away. A person who's been struck isn't carrying any charge, so it's safe to touch 'em. Call 911 (it's the States, remember, not triple-zero) and keep an eye on 'em. Start CPR or grab an Automated External Defibrillator if they need it.
