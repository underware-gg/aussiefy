December 19th.

_Dear Daddy-Long-Legs_,

You never answered my question, and it was a pretty important one, too.

ARE YOU BALD?

I've got you worked out exactly--and I'm pretty rapt with it--right up until I get to the top of your head, and then I'm _properly_ stuck. I can't decide whether you've got white hair or black hair or sort of sprinkly grey hair, or maybe none at all.

Here's your portrait:

But here's the thing: do I give you some hair or not?

Want to know what colour your eyes are? They're grey, and your eyebrows stick out like a verandah roof (beetling, they call it in novels) and your mouth is a straight line that's keen to turn down at the corners. Oh, see, I know! You're a cranky old thing with a temper.

(Chapel bell.)

9.45 P. M.

I've got a new rule, and there's no breaking it: never, ever study at night, no matter how many written reviews are coming up in the morning. Instead, I just read plain old books--I have to, you see, because I've got eighteen blank years behind me. You wouldn't believe it, Daddy, what a great big hole of not-knowing my brain is; I'm only just working out how deep it goes myself. The things most girls with a proper family and a home and mates and a library just pick up without trying, I've never even heard of. For example:

I'd never read "Mother Goose" or "David Copperfield" or "Ivanhoe" or "Cinderella" or "Blue Beard" or "Robinson Crusoe" or "Jane Eyre" or "Alice in Wonderland" or a single word of Rudyard Kipling. I didn't know Henry the Eighth got married more than once, or that Shelley was a poet. I didn't know people used to be monkeys and the Garden of Eden was a beautiful myth. I didn't know R.L.S. stood for Robert Louis Stevenson or that George Eliot was a lady. I'd never seen a picture of the "Mona Lisa," and (it's true, but you won't believe it) I'd never heard of Sherlock Holmes.

Now I know all of that and heaps more besides, but you can see how much catching up I've got to do. And oh, it's good fun! I look forward to the evening all day, and then I hang an "engaged" sign on the door and get into my nice red dressing-gown and fluffy slippers, pile all the cushions up behind me on the couch, switch on the brass student lamp at my elbow, and read and read and read. One book's not enough. I've got four on the go at once. Right now it's Tennyson's poems and "Vanity Fair" and Kipling's "Plain Tales" and--don't laugh--"Little Women." Turns out I'm the only girl at uni who wasn't brought up on "Little Women." I haven't told a soul, mind you (that _would_ make me look like a real oddball). I just quietly went and bought it with $1.12 out of last month's allowance, and next time someone mentions pickled limes, I'll know what she's on about!

(Ten o'clock bell. This is a very interrupted letter.)

Saturday.

_Sir_,

I have the honour to report fresh explorations in the field of geometry. On Friday last we gave up our earlier work on parallelopipeds and moved on to truncated prisms. We're finding the road rough, and very much uphill.

Sunday.

The Christmas holidays start next week and the trunks are up. The corridors are so chock-a-block you can hardly get through, and everyone's so excited they're fit to burst, so the study's getting left out. I'm going to have a lovely time over the holidays; there's another Freshman from Texas staying back too, and we're planning long walks and--if there's any ice--learning to skate. Then there's still the whole library to read--and three empty weeks to do it in!

Bye, Daddy, I hope you're feeling as happy as I am.

Yours ever,

JUDY.

P. S. Don't forget to answer my question. If you can't be bothered writing, get your secretary to send a telegram. He can just say:

Mr. Smith is quite bald, or Mr. Smith is not bald, or Mr. Smith has white hair.

And you can take the twenty-five cents out of my allowance.

Bye till January--and a merry Chrissie!
