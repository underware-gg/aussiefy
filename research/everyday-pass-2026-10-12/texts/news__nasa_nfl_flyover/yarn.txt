The head honcho at NASA, Administrator Jared Isaacman, is gonna jump in the pilot's seat of one of three F-5 fighter jets and roar over the top to kick off the New York Jets versus Cleveland Browns game in East Rutherford, New Jersey, on Sunday, 11 October. It's all part of the agency's Inspiration Tour flyover. Not a bad way to get to the footy, ay?

Before the game, from 10 a.m. to 1 p.m. EDT, the NASA mob will be out having a chinwag with the fans at the agency's Experience Zone, which is set up at the Jets Tailgate Zone outside the stadium. (Tailgating's what the Yanks call a barbie in the car park before the match.) The punters can find out more about NASA heading back to the Moon under the agency's Artemis dome, have a crack at some hands-on games, and grab a happy snap with a whopping great blow-up SLS (Space Launch System) rocket.

The Inspiration Tour's doing the rounds of the whole country, rounding up the uni crowd, industry and the public service and all the other folks with a stake in it, to hook the agency up with the people, the tech and the outfits that keep America out in front in space.

The tour wraps up with a bang at MAX POWER, a public show-and-tell of American air and space know-how, on Saturday, 7 November, and Sunday, 8 November, on and around the agency's Kennedy Space Center in Florida. It's being put on to mark America's big 250th birthday, and it runs over a few days and is good for the whole family. They'll be showing off the next lot of aircraft, spacecraft, self-driving vehicles and gadgets that'll help shape how we get about in the air and in space down the track. The whole box and dice, by the sound of it.
