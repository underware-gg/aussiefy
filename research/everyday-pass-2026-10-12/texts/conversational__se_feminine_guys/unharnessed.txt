What's a sheila version of 'guys'?

I use the word 'guys' all the time for a mob of blokes, just casual like. It's casual but it's not rude, off-putting, up itself or patronising (though I wouldn't use it on a bunch of blokes in a board meeting (hypothetically, of course) unless I knew 'em). So some I wouldn't count as swaps are:

- buddies: too old-fashioned

- dudes: too casual

- boys: too patronising

- men, gentlemen: too formal, or fake respect

Then it hit me I'd also been using it for any mob of people, blokes and sheilas, even just sheilas on their own. It worked for me and I never gave the gender side of it a second thought, that women mightn't be keen on it. Nobody ever whinged about it, to my face or behind my back (rule of life: sometimes people don't whinge about stuff they bloody well should).

But it occurred to me (maybe I read something that switched the light on) that the people I was talking to mightn't all be keen on it.

So what'd be a female version of 'guys', or one with no gender at all?

Some I've thought about that don't sit right with me (though they might be fair enough answers) are:

- dolls: too old-fashioned

- babes: too casual

- ladies: too formal, or, so I've heard from women, too creepy

- women: too matter-of-fact

- girls: too patronising (same as 'boys')

- everyone, you all: too bland

- y'all: not sexist in the slightest, but too regional (might work, but not everywhere)

Hopefully I haven't knocked out every option. Any ideas?

"Guys" can be used in English as gender neutral for a mixed mob.

You'll even hear women calling other women "guys."

The closest thing with a bit of a feminine lean would be "gals." "Guys and gals" is a pretty casual take on "ladies and gentlemen." (Note it's back to front.)

---

Edit: Like @kitukwfyer pointed out in the comments on the question, "gals" can come across as a put-down when it's used on its own, so watch yourself. It's not likely to be a drama in the pairing above. Used carefully it's probably fine, but on its own it could sound like you're taking the piss.

Saying this is unlikely to get anyone's back up:

> "The gals talked in the kitchen while the guys went out to see Fred's new truck."

But rock up to a couple of women on a street corner and say this, and you might get a funny look:

> "Excuse me gals, where is the grocery store?"

Say this to a couple of girls and they might reckon you're dodgy as fuck:

> "Hey gals come with me."

But if you're explaining the rules of some game and say this, it's likely to be fine:

> "Alright, guys on this side of the room, gals on the other."
