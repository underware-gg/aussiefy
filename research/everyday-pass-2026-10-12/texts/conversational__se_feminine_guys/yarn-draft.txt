Okay, so. What's the sheila version of "guys"?

I chuck the word "guys" round all the time for a mob of blokes. It's casual, but it's not rude, it doesn't put anyone off, and it's not talkin' down to anyone or bein' a smart-arse about it. (Mind you, I wouldn't use it on a table full of suits at a board meeting, hypothetically of course, unless I knew the coves.) So, the ones I wouldn't have a bar of as a swap:

- buddies: too old-fashioned

- dudes: too slack

- boys: too patronisin'

- men, gentlemen: too stiff, or respect you don't really mean

Then it dawned on me I'd been usin' it for any bunch of punters at all, blokes and women mixed, even just women. Worked fine for me, and I never stopped to think about the gender side of it, that the women mightn't be too rapt in it. Not a bugger ever whinged, not to me face and not behind me back either. (Rule of life: sometimes people don't whinge about things they bloody well should.)

But it hit me (prob'ly readin' somethin' that switched the light on) that the folks I'm talkin' to mightn't all be keen on it.

So what's a female, or a no-gender-at-all, version of "guys"?

The ones I've had a go at that don't sit right with me (though fair enough, some of 'em might be decent answers):

- dolls: too old-fashioned

- babes: too slack

- ladies: too stiff, or, so I've heard from women, too creepy

- women: too much like a police report

- girls: too patronisin' (same as "boys")

- everyone, you all: bland as

- the Southern Yank one, "you all" squashed into a single word: not sexist one bit, but too much of a regional thing (might work, but not everywhere)

Hopefully I haven't knocked off every option. Any ideas?

"Guys" can be used in English with no gender to it at all, for a mixed mob.

You'll even hear sheilas callin' other sheilas "guys."

The closest thing goin' with a bit of a feminine lean would be "gals." "Guys and gals" is a pretty casual take on "ladies and gentlemen." (Notice it's the other way round.)

---

Edit: Like @kitukwfyer pointed out in the comments on the question, "gals" can come across as a put-down when it's used on its own, so go easy. It's not likely to be a drama in the pairin' above. Use it with a bit of sense and you're prob'ly right, but on its own it could sound like you're takin' the mickey.

Sayin' this is unlikely to get anyone's back up:

> "The gals had a yarn in the kitchen while the guys went out to check out Fred's brand-spankin'-new ute."

But wander up to a couple of women on a street corner and come out with this, and you might get a funny look:

> "Excuse me gals, where's the supermarket?"

Say this to a couple of girls and they'll reckon you're dodgy as:

> "Hey gals, come with me."

But if you're explainin' the rules of some game and you say this, you'll be right:

> "Righto, guys on this side of the room, gals on the other."
