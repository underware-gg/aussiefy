Christmas tree permits will be up for grabs online through Recreation.gov and in person at all Santa Fe National Forest District Offices and the Supervisor's Office from November 13, 2025. Details about the designated cutting areas, dates, and the types of trees you're allowed to cut can be found here.

Buy your Christmas Tree permit:

Online through Recreation.gov — search for Santa Fe National Forest Christmas Tree Permits. It's important to read the overview and need-to-know information carefully before you buy the permit. Visitors will also need to set up or log in to a Recreation.gov account to finish the purchase.

In person at the Supervisor's Office in Santa Fe, N.M., or the Jemez, Cuba, Coyote, Española, and Pecos/Las Vegas District Offices. Get in touch with us for current opening hours.

Permits will be sold in person at the Walatowa Visitor Center on Saturdays only, from 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., from November 22 through December 20, 2024.

Pre-cut Christmas Trees might be available to buy at district offices. Availability depends on the weather and supply, with first dibs going to people who can't cut their own tree.

The SFNF's non-refundable Christmas tree permits are $10. Permits bought online through recreation.gov come with an extra $2.50 service fee. One tag covers a tree up to 5 inches in diameter and 10 feet tall. Trees taller than 10 feet and/or wider than 5 inches need an extra tag. For example, a 15-foot tree needs two tags.

Fourth graders with an Every Kid Outdoors pass can get a free Christmas tree permit, and can apply by entering the pass or voucher number when they buy a permit (note: a $2.50 service fee will be charged).

Cutting a Christmas tree is good for the health of the forest, too. The permit system helps thin out crowded stands of skinny trees. Local forest health experts pick out areas that'll do better with some thinning, where the trees tend to be just the right size for Christmas trees. Taking these trees out of designated areas helps the other trees grow bigger and can open up spots that give wildlife something to feed on.
