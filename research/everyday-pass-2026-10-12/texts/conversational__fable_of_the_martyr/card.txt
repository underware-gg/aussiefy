unharnessed: The cleanest of the three: faithful, natural and evenly pitched at level 4 and dad-level swearing. Only small word choices to fault.
  minor: 'pretty chuffed' (for 'There was General Satisfaction'): "chuffed" is a Britishism; "rapt" or "stoked" would sound more Australian.
  minor: "If the sheila reckons he's all right" (for "If the Woman thinks he's All Right"): "sheila" is a little coarser and more dated than "the Woman" in a moral; "the missus" would fit better. Also the third "reckon".

yarn: A lively, natural level-4 yarn that keeps every beat and the irony, and adds a few playful asides. The doubled opener and a couple of Britishisms are its only blemishes.
  minor: 'Ever hear the one about the bloke with the crook back?\n\nOnce, out in some little country town, there was this bloke with a weak back.' (for 'Once in a Country Town there was a Man with a Weak Back.'): The added opener introduces the bloke and his back, then the next line introduces them all over again.
  minor: 'pretty chuffed' (for 'There was General Satisfaction'): "chuffed" is more British than Australian; "stoked" or "rapt" would sound more local.
  minor: 'the pollies in Congress' (for "what Congress wuz thinkin' of"): "pollies" glued onto an American institution reads a bit forced.
  minor: 'as far as he was concerned' (for "as fur as that's concerned"): A slight shift: the original's tag is a verbal filler, not a statement of his opinion.

engine: Largely the untouched original with a few Aussie tokens wedged in, several of them in the wrong sense or the wrong country. Well under the brief and clunky where it does intervene.
  major: 'Parliament House' (for "what Congress wuz thinkin' of"): The man is American (Uncle Sam, the American People); swapping in the Australian parliament breaks the story's logic.
  major: 'American punters' (for 'the American People'): "punters" means gamblers or customers, the wrong sense for a nation's people.
  major: 'A beaut many battlers' (for 'A good many People'): "beaut" does not work as a quantity intensifier, and "battlers" narrows "people" to the struggling poor.
  major: 'Mind you, Consequently,' (for 'Consequently,'): Two stacked connectives make broken grammar and undercut the ironic punchline.
  minor: 'Mind you, he was willing to divide the Labor, however;' (for 'He was willing to divide the Labor, however;'): "Mind you" doubles up "however"; a clunky join.
  minor: 'Struth, he built' (for 'He built'): A stock exclamation bolted on where nothing calls for surprise.
  major: 'He could put a Grindstone into a Farm Wagon if any one wanted to bet him the Segars' (for 'He could put a Grindstone into a Farm Wagon if any one wanted to bet him the Segars'): Large stretches are left untranslated, well short of a level-4 brief.
