Ever hear the one about the bloke with the crook back?

Once, out in some little country town, there was this bloke with a weak back. Now, he could chuck a grindstone up into a farm wagon if anyone wanted to bet him the cigars on it, no worries. But every time he picked up an axe, something grabbed him right in the spine and he had to go inside and have a lie-down. Funny, that. So his missus took in boarders and did all the cooking herself.

Mind you, he was happy to split the work, fair's fair, so he did the shopping. Only, once he'd bought the tucker, he'd plonk himself down on a shoe box with the basket between his knees and carry on about how he couldn't see what the pollies in Congress were bloody well thinkin' of.

He had theories about the Alaskan boundary, too, and he was dead against any Anglo-American alliance, 'cause Uncle Sam could look after himself at any turn in the road, when you come right down to it, and the Yanks were better than every other mob on earth in every way, shape, manner and form, as far as he was concerned. And on he'd go, flappin' his gums, till the poor woman had to send word for him to hurry up with the groceries so she could get the tea on.

Just about every man and his dog felt sorry for her, stuck with a great big lump of a no-hoper like that. Folks had her down as a proper martyr.

Well, one day the husband got sunstruck, bein' too bone idle to shift into the shade, and next day he carked it without breakin' a sweat. Last thing he ever did without tryin', I s'pose. The widow gave him the best funeral of the year, then scraped together every bit of dosh she could lay her hands on and stuck it into a marble headstone that said "At Rest." Fair enough, too. He'd had plenty of practice.

Heaps of people said she was better off without him, and it was defo good riddance to bad rubbish.

They hoped that if she ever got hitched again she'd pick some bugger who wasn't scared of a bit of hard yakka, and had the gumption to pound sand down a rat hole.

So the whole town was pretty chuffed when she married Mr. Gladden, who owned the general store. He built a brand-spankin'-new house, hired a girl to help out and sent the washing out. She could wander into the shop and pick out anything she liked, and every evening he took her for a spin in his new runabout.

So of course she was down in the dumps, with a face like a wet week, pining for the gem she'd lost.

MORAL: _If the missus reckons he's all right, you stay on your own side of the fence._
