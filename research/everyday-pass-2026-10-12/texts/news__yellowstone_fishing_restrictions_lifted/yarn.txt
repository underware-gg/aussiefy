MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – Good news for the fishos. From Friday, Sept. 4, the last of the fishin' restrictions on the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon rivers (the Gibbon from Norris Campground downstream) and every creek that runs into 'em are comin' off, 'cause the weather's cooled down and the water with it. So you'll be able to fish those rivers, plus most of the park's other rivers, creeks and lakes, every day from sunup to sundown, same as it says in the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet.

Now, those rivers got shut to fishin' altogether back on July 15, then that got wound back to part-day restrictions on Aug. 28 once things picked up. They had to do it: the rivers were runnin' low and the water got hotter than 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius), and that's enough to cook a trout in its own creek. Poor buggers.

The park staff'll keep an eye on the water temperatures, mind. If the checks say so, they can bring back temporary restrictions or close things again.

Update: Yellowstone lifts full closures for Firehole, Madison and Gibbon rivers

Partial restrictions remain in effect from 2 p.m. to sunrise the following day

MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – Here's the earlier one. From Friday, Aug. 28, you can fish the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon rivers (the Gibbon from Norris Campground downstream) and every creek feedin' 'em, from sunup till 2 p.m. every day. The weather and the water have cooled off a bit, and there's rain in the forecast, so the full closures can come off.

The full closures went on back on July 15, 'cause when the rivers are runnin' low and the water gets above 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius), it can kill the fish. The water's been coolin' down overnight, though, so you can have a fish for part of the day. And on most of the park's other rivers, creeks and lakes you can fish every day from sunup to sundown, same as the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet says.

The staff'll keep checkin' the water temperatures, and dependin' on what they find, the temporary restrictions might come off, or they might shut more of it down.

Fishos, listen up: please fish in the coolest part of the day and get your fish in quick smart. Don't fight a hooked trout till it's absolutely buggered. Handle 'em gently, in the water as much as you can, and let 'em get their breath back before you let 'em go. Play your part and you'll be lookin' after the park's fisheries, and you might save 'em havin' to ban fishin' round the clock on some rivers and creeks if things get worse. Fair enough, too.
