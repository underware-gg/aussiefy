MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – From Friday, Sept. 4, the last of the fishing restrictions on the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon (downstream of Norris Campground) rivers and all their tributaries are getting the flick, thanks to the weather and the water cooling off. Anglers can wet a line on these rivers, plus most of the park's other rivers, creeks and lakes, every day from sunrise to sunset, as set out in the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet.

Full fishing closures on these rivers first came in on July 15, then got wound back to partial restrictions on Aug. 28 as things improved. The restrictions and closures had to happen during a stretch of low water flows and water temps over 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius), which can cook the fish and kill 'em.

Staff'll keep an eye on the water temps. Temporary restrictions or closures might come in depending on how conditions are looking.

Update: Yellowstone lifts full closures for Firehole, Madison and Gibbon rivers

Partial restrictions remain in effect from 2 p.m. to sunrise the following day

MAMMOTH HOT SPRINGS, Wyo. – From Friday, Aug. 28, anglers can fish the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon (downstream of Norris Campground) rivers and all their tributaries from sunrise till 2 p.m. every day. The weather and water cooling off, plus some rain in the current forecast, mean the full closures can be lifted.

The full closures came in on July 15 because low water flows and water temps over 68 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius) can kill fish. The water's been cooling down overnight, so there's fishing for part of the day. Anglers can fish most of the park's other rivers, creeks and lakes every day from sunrise to sunset, as set out in the 2026 Fishing Regulations booklet.

Staff'll keep an eye on the water temps. Temporary restrictions might be lifted, or more closures brought in, depending on how conditions are looking.

Anglers: Fair go, fish in the coolest part of the day and land your fish quick smart. Don't muck about playing a hooked trout till the poor bugger's buggered. Handle fish gently and keep 'em in the water as much as you can, and let 'em get their breath back before you let 'em go. Doing the right thing protects the park's fisheries, and if things get worse it might save us having to ban fishing at all hours of the day on some rivers and creeks.
