Snags

Cut a string of snags apart. Give each snag a few good jabs with a carvin' fork. Bung 'em in the fryin' pan, cover with boilin' water and let 'em cook fifteen minutes; drain, whack 'em back in the pan, and fry till they're nice and brown. Serve with fried apples. Plenty of folks grill their snags same as bacon, too, with the apples bakin' in a pan underneath to catch the drips.

Boston Baked Beans

Pick over a quart of pea beans, toss out the duds, cover 'em with cold water and leave 'em to soak overnight. Come sparrow's fart, drain 'em, cover with fresh water and heat 'em up slow (keep the water under the boil, mind), and cook till the skins are ready to burst. Best way to tell: take a few beans on the tip of a spoon and blow on 'em, and if they're done enough the skins'll split. Those test beans go in the bin, of course; they're done for. Drain the beans, and chuck the bean water outside, not down the sink.

Scald the rind of three-quarters of a pound of fat salt pork, scrape it, cut off a quarter-inch slice and put it in the bottom of the bean pot. With the rest of the pork, score the rind every half an inch, makin' the cuts an inch deep. Tip the beans into the pot and bury the pork in 'em, leavin' the rind pokin' out the top. Mix one tablespoon of salt, one tablespoon of molasses and three tablespoons of sugar; add a cup of boilin' water and pour it over the beans, then top up with more boilin' water till the beans are covered.

Lid on the bean pot, into the oven, and bake it low and slow for six or eight hours. Take the lid off for the last hour so the rind goes brown and crackly. Top up the water as you need. Heaps of people reckon that if you throw in half a tablespoon of mustard with the seasonin's, the beans go down a lot easier on the guts. And if you'd rather have pork with a bit of lean through it, go easy on the salt.

Now, a lot of the fame Boston Baked Beans have got is put down to the clay bean pot, small at the top and pot-bellied in the sides, that they're reckoned to be baked in. But just as beaut a feed of beans has been eaten many a time where the bean pot was cactus and a five-pound lard bucket stood in for it. So don't sweat it if ya haven't got the proper pot.

Yellow-eyed beans come up bloody good baked, too.
