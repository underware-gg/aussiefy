Snags

Cut a string of snags apart. Give each snag a few good stabs with a carving fork. Chuck 'em in the frying pan, cover with boiling water and cook fifteen minutes; drain 'em, back in the pan, and fry till they're nice and brown. Serve with fried apples. Snags are often grilled same as bacon, with apples baked in a pan underneath.

Boston Baked Beans

Pick over one quart of pea beans, cover with cold water and leave 'em soak overnight. In the morning, drain, cover with fresh water, heat slowly (keep the water under the boil), and cook till the skins'll burst. Best way to tell is to grab a few beans on the tip of a spoon and blow on 'em; if they're done, the skins'll split. Beans you've tested like that go in the bin, obviously. Drain the beans, and chuck the bean water outside, not down the sink. Scald the rind of three-quarters of a pound of fat salt pork, scrape it, cut off a quarter-inch slice and whack it in the bottom of the bean pot. Score through the rind of the rest of the pork every half inch, making the cuts an inch deep. Tip the beans in the pot and bury the pork in 'em, leaving the rind poking out. Mix one tablespoon salt, one tablespoon molasses and three tablespoons sugar; add one cup boiling water and pour it over the beans, then top up with enough boiling water to cover 'em. Lid on the bean pot, into the oven, and bake it low and slow for six or eight hours, taking the lid off for the last hour so the rind goes brown and crispy. Add water as you need it. Plenty of folks swear that if you add half a tablespoon of mustard with the seasonings, the beans go down easier on the guts. If you'd rather pork with a bit of lean through it, go easier on the salt.

The bloody good name Boston Baked Beans have got for themselves gets put down to the clay bean pot, small at the top and fat round the sides, they're meant to be cooked in. But beans every bit as good have been eaten plenty of times where some poor bugger broke the bean pot and used a five-pound lard bucket instead.

Yellow-eyed beans are bloody good baked, too.
