Strewth, where do I start? The scammers are forever cookin' up brand-spankin'-new ways to con battlers into handin' over their personal details or forkin' out dodgy payments. Knowin' their tricks is the best defence you've got. So here's a rundown of the ones they pull most, and how to keep the mongrels off ya.

## Fake emails and texts

The shonks send emails or texts that look dead official, often with government logos and all the proper bureaucratic waffle. That's called phishing. A phishing job might:

- Hassle you to click a link to "verify your account"
- Reckon there's some urgent drama with your benefits or your tax return
- Ask for your personal info, like your Social Security number or bank details

### How to protect yourself

- Don't click links or download attachments from anyone you don't know. Simple as.
- Check the message is fair dinkum by gettin' onto the agency yourself, usin' their official contact details.
- Dob in the dodgy messages to whatever agency the scammer's pretendin' to be

## Impersonation phone calls

Fraudsters might ring you up makin' out they're from the government. Usually they'll:
- Threaten to have you arrested, your licence suspended, or your benefits cut off
- Demand you pay up on the spot with gift cards, crypto, or a wire transfer

### How to protect yourself

- A government agency will never, ever ask you to pay over the phone. Not once.
- Hang up on 'em and ring the official number listed on the Login.gov or agency website.

## Fake websites and online portals

Some scams use slanter websites built to look just like the real government pages. Those sites might:

- Ask you to log in with your personal details
- Sting you fees for stuff that's free on the official sites

### How to protect yourself

- Always check the website address ends in .gov.
- Don't go huntin' for sign-in links through a search engine. Type the official URL in yourself and double-check the spellin', 'cause one wrong letter and you're on some crook's patch.

## Social media scams

The scammers set up fake social media profiles makin' out they're government officials. They might:
- Offer you a grant or a bit of financial help, as long as you cough up a fee first
- Ask for your personal or bankin' details in a DM

### How to protect yourself

- Get it into your noggin that government agencies don't send messages about benefits out of the blue.
- Report any suss profiles to the social media platform they're on.
