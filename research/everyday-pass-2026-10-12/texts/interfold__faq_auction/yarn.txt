the auction, or who's got FOLD to claim after it. The questions come in four lots: the auction itself,
taking part, claiming and transferability, and staying safe.

## About the auction

### What is FOLD Auction 2?

FOLD Auction 2 was the second public FOLD auction. It ran on the Uniswap Continuous Clearing Auction
mechanism.

It came after the first FOLD Auction, which ran July 8–10, 2026.

Auction 2 was kept small on purpose, capped at **2% of total FOLD supply**. It was timed around the
switch to general FOLD transferability. The idea was to spread FOLD around a bit wider and help
get deeper, longer-term on-chain liquidity off the ground.

### Why was there a second FOLD Auction?

Auction 2 was a small, limited round of distribution before general FOLD transferability kicked in on Aug 19.

It was deliberately kept small at **2% of total FOLD supply**. It kicked off with a floor that matched the
clearing price of the first FOLD Auction.

The auction gave every man and his dog an open, public way to hand out a bit more FOLD before it became
generally transferable. It also helped prop up long-term on-chain liquidity.

**50% of proceeds were committed to long-term on-chain liquidity support.**

Auction 2 wrapped up not long before general FOLD transferability was switched on.

### What happened in the first FOLD Auction?

The first FOLD Auction ran from **July 8–10, 2026**. It used the Uniswap Continuous Clearing
Auction mechanism too.

The first auction's done and dusted. Its clearing price was **0.02154816 USDC / FOLD**, which is also the
floor for FOLD Auction 2.

If you were in the first auction, you can still get to claiming and the after-auction info
through the official [Interfold auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction).

### What is a Uniswap Continuous Clearing Auction?

A Continuous Clearing Auction (CCA) is a way of working out a token's price while it's being handed out. It's
built on Uniswap's infrastructure.

- The auction runs over a number of separate periods.
- Punters put their bids in through the official Uniswap CCA interface.
- Winning bids in a period all clear at the same clearing price.
- The minimum price for each period after that is whichever's higher: the original floor price or the
  clearing price of the period before.
- The minimum price can go up in later periods. It won't drop below the relevant earlier clearing
  price or the original floor.
- Any tokens not handed out earlier are still up for grabs in the last period.

The CCA hands tokens out bit by bit and charges everyone the same price within each period. It's set up
this way to cut down on gas bidding wars, make sniping harder, and let clearing prices settle through
the bidding itself.

[Read more about Uniswap CCAs](https://cca.uniswap.org).

### Did the auction determine the price of FOLD after transferability?

No. The auction only set the allocations and a clearing price inside the auction itself. It didn't
set FOLD's market price once general transferability started.

Nobody can tell you what the market price will do down the track.

## Participation

<Callout type='warning'>
  Auction 2 closed on Aug 19, 2026 at 13:06 UTC. The steps in this section show how the auction
  worked back then. You can't put in a new bid.
</Callout>

### How did participation work?

Righto, here's how it went:

1. People went to the official [FOLD Auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction).
2. They followed the verified link through to the Uniswap CCA interface.
3. They went through the required verification process via the official flow.
4. They put their bids in while the auction was open.
5. Once the auction closed, they could claim any FOLD they'd been allocated and any USDC they hadn't spent through the
   verified auction interface.

You needed USDC on Ethereum Mainnet. You also had to read through the auction terms and eligibility
requirements before you had a crack.

### Who could participate?

Only eligible people could take part. That depended on where you live, identity
verification, AML and sanctions screening, and any other restrictions that applied.

The official verification process decided who was eligible, and the auction terms set the rules on it.

Getting through verification didn't guarantee you were eligible or that you'd get an allocation.

### Was KYC required?

Yep. You had to get through identity verification via the official verification process
before you could take part.

That verification process decided who was eligible and handled the relevant AML and sanctions screening.

Taking part was subject to the auction terms and any restrictions that applied.

## Claiming and transferability

### When can participants claim FOLD?

Claiming was set to open straight after the auction closed.

You claim your allocated FOLD and any unspent USDC through the verified auction interface.

Claiming and general FOLD transferability are two separate things.

### When did general FOLD transferability begin?

FOLD Auction 2 closed Aug 19 at **13:06 UTC (9:06 AM ET)**.

General FOLD transferability could be switched on not long after, at or after **14:00 UTC (10 AM
ET)**.

At or after Unix timestamp **`1787148000`**, anyone could call the `tge()` function on the FOLD
contract to switch on general transferability. The TGE transaction went through on Aug 19, 2026 at
14:00:23 UTC. `tgeTimestamp()` returns `1787148023`. FOLD is now generally transferable.

FOLD Auction 2, claiming, general FOLD transferability and Network Alpha are separate milestones
in the bigger Interfold launch sequence. For the details, have a squiz at
[FOLD Transferability](/faq/transferability).

## Safety

### How do I know I’m using the official auction?

If you're dead set on staying safe, only use auction, claiming, verification and support links that come through official Interfold
channels.

The official Interfold channels are:

- [Interfold auction page](https://www.theinterfold.com/fold-auction)
- [Interfold website](https://www.theinterfold.com/)
- [Interfold X account](https://x.com/theinterfold)
- [Interfold documentation](https://docs.theinterfold.com/)

Don't trust:

- Auction, claiming or support links that land in your DMs out of the blue.
- Token or contract addresses copied from dodgy, unofficial sources.
- Accounts pretending to be someone they're not.
- Private wallet-connection links.
- Anyone asking for your seed phrase, private key or wallet backup.
- Anyone asking you to pay for support.

<Callout type='warning'>
  Interfold team members and moderators will never, ever ask for your seed phrase, private key, wallet
  backup, or access to your wallet. If they do, they're not who they say they are.
</Callout>
