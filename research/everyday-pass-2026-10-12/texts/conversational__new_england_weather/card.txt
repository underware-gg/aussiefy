unharnessed: A faithful, light, natural level-2 version that keeps every joke. It has a couple of clunky swaps at the start.
  minor: 'I reverently reckon' (for 'I reverently believe'): "Reverently" and "reckon" sit oddly together. It reads like a word swap, not natural speech.
  minor: 'who muck about and learn the trade' (for 'who experiment and learn how'): "Muck about" implies goofing off, not experimenting.
  minor: "I don't know who makes that, but I reckon" (for "I don't know who makes that, but I think"): The second "reckon" in two sentences is noticeable.

yarn: A smooth, natural, well-weighted level-2 retelling. It is let down by breaking the poets punchline and softening "convincing".
  major: 'So, before they know it, the chance to ask how the locals feel has gone for good.' (for 'And so the first thing they know the opportunity to inquire how they feel has permanently gone by.'): "They" were the poets, who have been killed. Making it "the locals" kills the punchline.
  minor: "It's so thorough that when it hits something" (for 'it is so convincing, that when it strikes a thing'): "Convincing" is Twain's ironic word. "Thorough" is literal and loses the irony.
  minor: "Here's one for ya." (added): An added, slightly stock yarn opener.
  minor: '"Crikey, what awful thunder you\'ve got here!"' (for '“Why, what awful thunder you have here!”'): "Crikey" in a stranger's mouth is a bit stage-Australian.

engine: Effectively untranslated, with a handful of word swaps, one of them broken.
  major: 'cannot, too right, know how the natives feel about spring' (for 'cannot, of course, know how the natives feel about spring'): "Too right" means emphatic agreement. As a stand-in for "of course" here it is ungrammatical, and an Australian would wince.
  minor: 'countries that require a beaut article' (for 'countries that require a good article'): The slang sits awkwardly in archaic phrasing.
  major: 'The weather is always doing something there; always attending strictly to business' (for 'The weather is always doing something there; always attending strictly to business'): Left largely untranslated. Representative of the whole text sitting far below the level-2 brief.
