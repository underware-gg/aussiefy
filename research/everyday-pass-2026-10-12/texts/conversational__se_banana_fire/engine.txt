Why did my banana catch fire in the microwave?

Crikey, that sounds like a silly question, but really! Anyway, my bloody wife froze bananas in the freezer with the peel still on. You can't bloody well peel them frozen, ya old bastard. I put it in the microwave for one minute at 50% power. After 30 seconds the thin end of the peel (where a bunch connect to each other) was on fire, with a visible flame (about the size of a lighter). Bloody hell, what on earth happened, mate?

* * *

It's caused by the high amount of potassium in the banana. Microwaves react with metals, bouncing off and cause arcing. You can even create a cool light show by putting a raw peeled banana in the microwave. You're all bloody good, it won't explode, but it will make a dog's breakfast, it's also harmless.

Well, this can also happen in some frozen veggies depending on the soil conditions they grew in. As noted in the linked article, other high concentrations of metals such as magnesium, iron, and zinc can be the responsible mineral.

Momentary brief sparking is harmless and won't harm your microwave, but the USDA recommends turning off your microwave if you see sparks nonetheless, ay. If it does start a fire, unless it's a very small fire, don't open your microwave door. The safest thing is to turn off the microwave and let the fire burn itself out by consuming all the oxygen. Well, Opening the door could create a dangerous backdraft induced fireball.
