Why'd me banana catch fire in the microwave?

Crikey, I know that sounds like a dopey question, but fair dinkum! The missus chucked a bunch of bananas in the freezer with the skins still on. Problem is, you can't peel the buggers when they're frozen solid. So I bung one in the microwave for a minute on 50% power. 30 seconds in, the skinny end of the peel (the bit where they all hang off each other in the bunch) is on fire, with a proper flame you could see, about the size of a cigarette lighter. What the bloody hell's goin' on there?

* * *

It's all the potassium in the banana, mate. Banana's chock-full of the stuff. Microwaves don't get on with metals: they bounce off 'em and you get arcing. You can even put on a bit of a light show by bunging a raw, peeled banana in the microwave. No dramas, it won't blow up on ya. It'll make a dog's breakfast of the inside of the thing, but it's harmless enough.

Same thing can happen with some frozen veggies too, depending on the soil they were grown in. Like the article linked below says, a big dose of other metals, magnesium, iron, zinc and that, can be the culprit as well.

A quick little spark or two won't hurt anyone and won't cook your microwave, but the USDA reckons you should switch the thing off if you see sparks anyway. And if it does catch fire, unless it's only a tiny little one, don't open the microwave door. Safest bet is to turn it off and let the fire burn itself out once it's eaten up all the oxygen in there. Open that door and the fresh air rushin' in can give you a backdraft, and next thing you've got a bloody great fireball in your face. Not the way you want to cook your brekkie.
