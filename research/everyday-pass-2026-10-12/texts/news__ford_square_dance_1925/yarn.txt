DETROIT, Dec. 13 (AP).—Story goes, Henry Ford, the motor car bloke, has got it into his noggin to teach the whole of America the old-fashioned square dances, and he's already told everyone he's on a mission. His first big crack at it is Tuesday night, at one of the biggest dance halls in Detroit. For the occasion Mr. Ford's whacked an ad in the paper for couples to come and do the square dance and be the core of the crowd, so the rest have someone to follow.

Someone asked him tonight whether he reckoned the old steps would ever make a comeback, and Mr. Ford fired straight back, quick as a whip:

"Come back? They're already here! We're just fallin' back into line, that's all. What we're on about, puttin' out a book of the dances, is gettin' 'em all done the same way. The first edition's bein' reworked to make it simpler."

Out of the twenty-one quadrilles, Mr. Ford explained, they'll pick four, so they're easier to teach and more folks take 'em up. Twenty-one down to four. Even Henry knows nobody's learnin' the lot.

Now, "Mellie" Dunham, the champion fiddler of Maine, has been stayin' with Mr. Ford for a week, scrapin' away at an old-fashioned dance party and handin' over a few old-time tunes for Ford's "Americana," a collection of that sort of thing they're gettin' ready to print. He was meant to be playin' the Tuesday night do. But the plans got changed, and "Grandpa Mellie" and his missus are on their way to New York tonight, where the bright lights might just get him and he could sign up for a vaudeville show. Not bad for a grandpa with a fiddle, ay?

"Mellie" Dunham'll front up in New York this mornin'. The word last night was that he'll be under the management of Tex Rickard, who's s'posed to be plannin' a barn dance in his honour at the brand-spankin'-new Madison Square Garden. And they reckon puttin' on that bloody great shindig will help Henry Ford get his square dances goin' again, too.
