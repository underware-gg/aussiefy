DETROIT, Dec. 13 (AP).—Right, so Henry Ford's first big crack at this campaign of his, the one he's already told everyone about, to teach the American public the old-fashioned square dances, is happening Tuesday night at one of the biggest dance halls in Detroit. For the occasion, Mr. Ford's put the word out in the papers for couples to dance the square dance and make up the core of the party.

Asked tonight whether he really reckoned the old-fashioned steps would come back, Mr. Ford fired up:

"Come back? They're here! We're just falling back into line. What we're trying to do putting out a book of dances is standardise the bloody things. The first edition's being reworked to make it simpler."

Out of the twenty-one quadrilles, Mr. Ford explained, they'll pick four, to make 'em easier to teach and get folks keener on 'em.

"Mellie" Dunham, the champion fiddler of Maine, who's been Mr. Ford's guest for a week, playing for an old-fashioned dance party and chipping in a few old-time tunes for the Ford "Americana" collection of those pieces that's being got ready for publishing, was meant to turn up at the Tuesday night do. But the plans got changed, and "Grandpa Mellie," along with his missus, is on his way to New York tonight, where he might answer the call of the bright lights and sign on for a vaudeville contract.

"Mellie" Dunham gets into New York this morning. Word last night was he'll be managed by Tex Rickard, who's apparently planning a barn dance in his honour at the new Madison Square Garden. It was also said that putting on this dance was meant to help Henry Ford's push to get square dances popular again.
