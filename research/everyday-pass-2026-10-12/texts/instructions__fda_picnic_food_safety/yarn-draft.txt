Picnic and barbie season's here, and that means heaps of chances to get outside and have a good time with the family and your mates. Snags burnin' and the flies havin' a go, the whole bit. Trouble is, all that warm weather is a bloody field day for the bugs that give you food poisoning too. Once the tucker warms up in the summer heat, those bacteria breed like rabbits.

So if you want to keep yourself, your family and your mates from a nasty dose of the runs over the warmer months, you've got to handle your food properly when you're eating outdoors. That's not optional. Here's a few simple rules for getting the food out to the picnic spot, and for preparing and serving it safely once you've rocked up.

Pack It and Cart It Safely

Look after your tucker the whole way: from the fridge or freezer right through to the picnic table.

Keep the cold stuff cold. Put cold food in an esky with ice or frozen ice bricks. Cold food needs to sit at 40 °F (about 4 °C) or under, or the bacteria start getting busy. Meat, chook and seafood can go in still frozen, so they stay colder for longer.

Sort out the esky. Think about packing the drinks in one esky and the food that'll go off in another. That way, when everyone's in and out of the drinks esky every five minutes grabbing another one, the perishables aren't copping a blast of warm air each time.

Keep the lid on: once you're at the picnic spot, open the esky as little as you can get away with. It keeps everything in there cold for longer.

Don't cross-contaminate. Make sure the raw meat, chook and seafood are wrapped up good and tight. That stops the juices dripping onto anything that's already been on the barbie or made up, or onto stuff you'll eat raw, like fruit and veggies.

Give the fruit and veg a wash. Rinse fresh fruit and veggies under the tap before they go in the esky, and that includes the ones with skins and rinds you don't eat. Give the firm-skinned ones a good rub under running water, or a scrub with a clean veggie brush while the tap's running. Dry them off with a clean tea towel or a paper towel. Packaged fruit and veg labelled "ready-to-eat," "washed" or "triple washed" don't need washing.

Quick Tips for Setting Up at the Picnic Spot

Safe food starts with clean hands, and that goes for outdoors too. Before you start laying out the spread, make sure your mitts and all the surfaces are clean.

Washing Up Outdoors: if there's no running water, bring a water jug, some soap and paper towels. Or grab a pack of wet wipes for cleaning your hands.

Utensils and Serving Plates: keep all your tongs, utensils and platters clean while you're getting the food ready.

Stick to the Safe Barbecuing Rules

A barbie and a picnic usually go hand in hand. And same as cooking in the kitchen, there are a few important rules you need to follow to make sure what comes off the grill gets to the table safe.

Marinate safely. Marinate in the fridge, never on the kitchen bench or outside. And if you want to use some of the marinade as a sauce once the meat's off the grill, put some aside before the raw meat, chicken or seafood goes in. Don't reuse marinade, full stop.

Cook it straight away after "partial cooking." Part-cooking before it goes on the barbie is only safe if the half-done food can go straight onto a hot grill, like at home with the barbie out on the back deck.

Cook it right through. When it's time to cook, have your meat thermometer handy. Always use it to make sure the food's done properly the whole way through. Guessing doesn't count. (See Safe Food Temperature Chart)

Keep the ready stuff hot. Once it's done, you can keep it hot till serving by shifting it to the side of the grill, just off the coals. That keeps it warm without cooking it to a crisp.

Don't reuse plates or tongs. If you use the same plate or tongs that had the raw meat, chicken or seafood on them, the bacteria from the raw juices get onto the food that's ready to eat. Have a clean plate and clean tongs ready by the barbie to serve up instead.
