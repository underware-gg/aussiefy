yarn: Faithful, natural, level-3 Aussie letter that keeps the father's voice and every point.
  minor: "won't be heaps of use to you" (for "isn't going to be of any real use to you"): 'Heaps of use' is a slightly awkward fit for 'any real use'.
  minor: "it's enough to make a bloke hunch over like he's been carrying a sack of spuds" (for 'it fairly makes a fellow hump-shouldered to look it over'): Colour that slightly loses 'to look it over'.

unharnessed: Natural, faithful level-3 version; one tangled sentence and a couple of British-leaning words.
  major: "feeding a mongrel steer that won't fatten up its own weight in corn" (for "feeding his weight in corn to a scalawag steer that won't fat up"): Reordered so the steer 'fattens up its weight in corn'; the image no longer parses.
  minor: 'the other lads at the desk' (for 'the other boys at the desk'): 'Lads' reads British rather than Australian.
  minor: "there'll come a time when you start at the works when you won't be able" (for "there's going to be a time when you begin at the factory when you won't be able"): Doubled 'when' clunks.

engine: A mechanical word-swap of the original: 'beaut' and 'bloke' everywhere, money garbled into quid, and swearing the letter does not need.
  major: 'a beaut deal like feeding his weight in corn' (for 'a good deal like feeding his weight in corn'): 'A good deal' (much) turned into 'beaut' (excellent): wrong sense.
  major: 'with eight quid every Saturday night' (for 'with eight dollars every Saturday night'): Changes the currency and amount in a Chicago letter.
  major: 'sixty cents on any quid that comes to him. But a dollar is worth one hundred and six cents to a beaut business man, and he never spends the quid' (for 'never able to realize more than sixty cents on any dollar that comes to him. But a dollar is worth one hundred and six cents to a good business man, and he never spends the dollar'): Mixes quid and dollar in one sum; the arithmetic joke breaks.
  major: 'Bloody hell, I simply mention these things because I am scared stiff' (for 'I simply mention these things because I am afraid'): Added swearing and alarm the measured father never shows.
  minor: 'very, dead careful' (for 'very, very careful'): Broken doubling.
  minor: 'It is all with the bloke.' (for 'It is all with the man.'): 'The man' here means the individual; 'bloke' strains.
  minor: 'there is no elevator in the building' (for 'there is no elevator in the building'): US 'elevator' left in while other words are swapped.
  major: 'Starting, as you do, with a beaut education' (for 'Starting, as you do, with a good education'): 'Beaut' leaned on for every 'good', always in the wrong sense.
