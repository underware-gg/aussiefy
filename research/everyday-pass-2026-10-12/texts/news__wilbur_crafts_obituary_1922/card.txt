unharnessed: A faithful, natural level-4 obituary that keeps every fact and quotation and a fitting wry tone. A couple of slightly cheeky word choices.
  minor: 'that sort of carry-on' (for 'prohibition and similar movements'): "carry-on" is mildly derisive; the original is neutral here.
  minor: 'who the blokes fighting the laws he pushed reckoned was a "reform fanatic,"' (for 'charged by opponents of legislation he sponsored with being a "reform fanatic,"'): A tangled relative clause that reads awkwardly aloud.

yarn: Vivid and very Australian, with excellent terms like "wowser" and "stoush", but over-egged, mocking in tone and loose with the quoted words.
  major: "which he said often ended up a ride you'd be sorry and red-faced about for the rest of your days" (for 'which he said "often proved a ride of lifelong shame and woe"'): A direct quotation is paraphrased and stripped of its quote marks; in a news obituary the man's own words matter.
  minor: "Fancy tryin' to call off the biggest fight of the year." (added): Adds a claim (the biggest fight of the year) and a jeer.
  minor: 'Pneumonia got him.' (for 'died at a hospital here today of pneumonia'): Flippant for the cause of death in an obituary.
  minor: "The fun police never had a harder-workin' officer." (added): An added punchline that tips the obituary into a roast.
  minor: "startin' it up himself" (for 'which he founded'): Representative of near-constant g-dropping that reads as put-on.

engine: Mostly untranslated, with interjections and word swaps that corrupt facts (darts for cigarettes, an altered book title) and jar the obituary's tone.
  critical: 'dartses' (for 'cigarettes'): Changes the list of vices: cigarettes become darts, in an ungrammatical plural.
  critical: '"Rhetoric Made Racy, ay."' (for '"Rhetoric Made Racy."'): Alters a quoted book title, the very detail the paragraph turns on.
  major: 'a picturesque reckon' (for 'a picturesque figure'): "reckon" is a verb; the noun swap produces nonsense.
  major: 'Jeez, after funeral services here tomorrow' (for 'After funeral services here tomorrow'): An exclamation in front of the funeral notice; tone-deaf in an obituary.
  minor: 'Well, he was 73 years old.' (for 'He was 73 years old.'): A filler interjection that makes the age sound like an afterthought.
  minor: 'his bloody attacks' (for 'his attacks'): The swear word lands as a gratuitous intensifier in neutral prose.
  minor: 'Divinity School of Boston uni' (for 'Divinity School of Boston University'): Abbreviates a proper name, which breaks it.
