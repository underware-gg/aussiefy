On Saturday, the family of American muso and songwriter Bob Weir announced he'd died surrounded by the people he loved. The former guitarist and singer of rock band the Grateful Dead was 78. His family broke the news on Instagram, saying he'd recently battled a cancer diagnosis but died from "underlying lung issues". The announcement didn't say what day Weir died.

Weir played rhythm guitar and sang with American psychedelic rock band the Grateful Dead for decades, and wrote some of their songs too, including the hit "Sugar Magnolia", a regular at Grateful Dead gigs. He also had a long solo career, putting together a bunch of different groups with other ex-Grateful Dead members, and kept playing live till just months before he died.

Weir was born Robert Hall Parber on October 16, 1947, in San Francisco, California, and grew up round the San Francisco Bay Area, spending a bit of time at school in Colorado, where he met future Grateful Dead member John Perry Barlow. Weir picked up the guitar at 13 and drifted into the local bluegrass scene, where he first came across Jerry Garcia playing banjo. On December 31, 1965, Garcia and Weir agreed to start their own group called Mother McCree's Uptown Jug Champions, which turned into the Grateful Dead.

Weir played rhythm guitar and sang for the band for decades. From the 1970s he also had a crack at a solo career and a whole heap of other groups outside the Grateful Dead, including Kingfish, Bobby and the Midnites, RatDog, Furthur, The Other Ones, The Dead, Wolf Bros, and Dead & Company. All of them had fellow Grateful Dead collaborators in them and covered everything from country rock to jam band to jazz-tinged roots rock grounded in blues and country. Mostly he sang harmonies and backup, but he took the lead on a few tracks, including Grateful Dead anthem "Truckin'", a song the Library of Congress named a national treasure in 1997.

Away from performing, Weir also set up Tamalpais Research Institute, a virtual performance platform, and was into politics, getting behind a few causes including the environment, poverty relief and issues to do with the arts. He'd stayed active in the Bay Area music scene, playing live with local musos and co-owning the venue Sweetwater Music Hall in Mill Valley, California.
