Committee Policy Actions
No word of a lie, here's how it went down. To keep on track with its dual-mandate goals, every member around the table gave the nod to lifting the target range for the federal funds rate by 1/4 percentage point, to 3-3/4 to 4 percent, and they stuck by the FOMC's policy of keeping ample reserves in the banking system. Members reckoned the unemployment rate had barely shifted, and the economy was still chugging along nicely on the back of households and businesses at home that just kept on spending, while inflation was still sitting up a fair bit higher than the Committee's 2 percent goal. The Committee made it crystal clear it's still dead set on hitting its dual-mandate goals, by putting it in black and white in its postmeeting statement that it "will deliver price stability." Members agreed this move would help get inflation back down to the Committee's 2 percent goal a bit quicker.

When the talking was done, the Committee voted to tell the Federal Reserve Bank of New York to carry out trades in the System Open Market Account under the following domestic policy directive, until it's told otherwise, for release at 2:00 p.m.:

"Effective September 17, 2026, the Federal Open Market Committee directs the Desk to:

Do whatever open market operations it takes to keep the federal funds rate in a target range of 3-3/4 to 4 percent.

Run standing overnight repurchase agreement operations at a rate of 4.0 percent.

Run standing overnight reverse repurchase agreement operations at an offering rate of 3.75 percent, with a cap of $160 billion per counterparty per day.

When the time's right, build up the System Open Market Account's holdings of securities by buying Treasury bills and, if need be, other Treasury securities with 3 years or less left to run, to keep reserves at an ample level.

Roll over at auction every principal payment from the Federal Reserve's holdings of Treasury securities. Put every principal payment from the Federal Reserve's holdings of agency securities back into Treasury bills."

The same vote also covered signing off on the statement below, for release at 2:00 p.m.:

"The Federal Open Market Committee said righto to the following statement for release by a 12–0 vote:

The Committee decided to lift the target range for the federal funds rate by 1/4 percentage point to 3-3/4 to 4 percent, in support of the Federal Reserve's dual mandate. The Committee is sticking with its policy of keeping ample reserves in the banking system.

The economy's growing at a solid clip. There's still a fair bit of uncertainty about, partly thanks to what's going on around the world, but spending at home has held up well. Productivity's growing strongly, and businesses are splashing out on investment. Jobs growth has kept up with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has hardly budged.

Inflation's still running high. Today's move will help get it back to the Committee's 2 percent goal sooner. The Committee will deliver price stability."

Voting for this action: Kevin Warsh, John C. Williams, Michael S. Barr, Michelle W. Bowman, Lisa D. Cook, Beth M. Hammack, Philip N. Jefferson, Neel Kashkari, Lorie K. Logan, Anna Paulson, Jerome H. Powell, and Christopher J. Waller.

Voting against this action: None. Not a single knocker in the room.

To match the Committee's call to lift the target range for the federal funds rate to 3-3/4 to 4 percent, the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System voted as one to lift the interest rate paid on reserve balances to 3.90 percent, effective September 17, 2026. The Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System also voted as one to go along with a 1/4 percentage point rise in the primary credit rate to 4.0 percent, effective September 17, 2026.2

It was agreed the Committee would get back together on Tuesday–Wednesday, October 27–28, 2026. The meeting wrapped up at 10:15 a.m. on September 16, 2026. Back to work, the lot of 'em.
