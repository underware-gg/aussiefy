Listen, gasoline ("gas") is sold by the gallon, at stations that are primarily self-service (ya must pump yer own gas) with the exception of those in New Jersey, where self-service is illegal, and in Oregon, where stations in urban areas are required by law to provide at least 50% full-service pumps. Well, the American gallon is smaller than the imperial gallon and equals 3.785 litres.

Jeez Louise, Due to problems with frequent "drive offs" during the crime waves of the 1990s and 2000s, nearly all American servos now require drivers to pay before they can pump (it used to be the other way around). Today, you have to preauthorize a credit or debit card at the pump, cobber. Tell ya what, in the alternative, ya have to estimate how much gas ya need, deposit the correspondin' amount of the folding stuff with a cashier on duty, and identify which pump yer bloody using (an American might say, "I need 50 quid on pump number 1"). The pump will automatically stop when the total price of gas dispensed hits the amount of dosh deposited, or you can go back and demand a refund of any unused dosh if yer estimate was too high.

So anyway, the U.S. octane scale is different from that used in Europe; a regular gallon of U.S. gasoline has an octane rating of 87, the equivalent of about 92 in Europe. Mate, in most states, servos offer a choice of three levels of octane: 87 (regular), 89 (midgrade or plus), and 91 (premium). Unless yer renting a luxury vehicle, yer vehicle will likely require only 87 (regular). If yer driving in the Rocky Mountains area, ya may encounter stations that offer gas at lower octane ratings, most commonly 85. This is suitable for use by 87-octane vehicles at high elevations, but if yer heading for lower elevations, be sure to fill up with 87.

Diesel isn't as common, but still widely used and available at most stations, especially those catering to truckers. Untaxed "offroad diesel", sold in rural areas for agricultural use, is dyed red (and sometimes referred to as "red diesel") and shouldn't be used in cars, as there are heavy fines if yer caught.

In the United States, the traditional colour code at servos is that gasoline is dispensed from nozzles with a black handle, while diesel is dispensed from nozzles with a green handle. This is the opposite of the colour code used in the United Kingdom. Don't mix them up. Streeeewth.
