Ya won't believe this, but the Yanks down south have a bread you can knock up in no time. Southern Cornbread's a hearty, no-sugar sort of cornbread, and they serve it all over the Southeastern United States. It's dead easy to make, and it usually turns up as the bread with lunch or tea (supper, if you're posh about it). And it's not unusual for some buggers to now and then make a quick, simple feed of it, even brekkie, by crumblin' cornbread into a glass of buttermilk and gettin' stuck in. Sounds a bit weird, I reckon, but don't knock it till you've tried it.

Ingredients

2 cups yellow or white self-raising cornmeal

1 large egg

1¼–1½ cups buttermilk or full-cream milk

¼ cup olive oil or veggie oil

That's it. That's the whole shopping list.

Gear

A well-seasoned 8-inch or 10-inch cast-iron frying pan (or else an ovenproof frying pan, or an 8x8-inch baking tin)

A few measuring cups

A medium or big mixing bowl

How to do it

Crank the oven up to 425°F (call it 220°C). If you're using the cast-iron pan, bung it in the oven to heat up. Don't preheat any other kind of pan or tin, mind.

If you're not using cast iron, give the pan or tin a good spray of cooking spray so nothing sticks.

Whisk the egg in the bowl. Stir in the buttermilk and the oil.

Stir in the cornmeal and mix just till it's all wet. The batter'll be a bit lumpy, and that's no worries. Go easy and don't overmix it, or it'll turn out tough as old boots.

Tip the batter into the pan and whack it in the oven.

Bake it till the crust's a light golden brown and a toothpick stuck in the middle comes out clean. In an 8-inch pan that's 25–30 minutes; in a 10-inch pan or an 8x8-inch tin, about 20–25 minutes.

Let it cool in the pan for 5 minutes before you dish it up. Yeah, the whole 5. Keep your mitts off it.

Notes, tips and other ways to do it

If there's any cornbread left over (good luck with that), wrap it in a damp tea towel to keep it moist and it'll keep in the fridge for a few days. You can chuck it in freezer bags and freeze it for a few months, too, but it usually goes a fair bit more crumbly that way.
