No word of a lie, the average household's leaks can add up to more than 9,300 gallons of water down the drain every year, and nine percent of homes have leaks bleedin' 50 gallons or more a day. The usual suspects round the house are worn-out dunny flappers, drippin' taps and other leaky valves. Good news is, most of 'em are easy to fix: a few tools and a bit of hardware, and they pay for themselves in water you're not chuckin' away. Fix the easy leaks and you'll knock about 10 percent off your water bill. Not bad for an arvo's tinkerin', ay?

To check for leaks at your place, first you've got to work out whether you're wastin' water at all, then track down where the bugger's comin' from. Here's a few tips for findin' leaks:

Have a geek at how much water you use in a colder month, like January or February. If a family of four's goin' through more than 12,000 gallons a month, you could have some proper leaks on your hands.

Take a reading off your water gauge, then leave it two hours with nobody usin' a drop, and check it again. If the numbers have moved at all, chances are you've got a leak.

To catch a leaky dunny, put a few drops of food colourin' in the cistern. If any colour rocks up in the bowl after 10 minutes, you've got a leak. (Make sure you flush straight after, mind, or the dye'll stain the tank.)

Have a good look at the tap washers and pipe fittings for any water on the outside of the pipe. That's how you spot a surface leak.

Dunny Leaks

Old or clapped-out dunny flappers (that's the valve seal) can cause leaks. This simple bit of rubber holds the water in the cistern, then lets it go into the bowl when you flush. Rubber wears out, so check the flapper every now and then and swap it for a new one at least every five years, so it seals up nice and tight and doesn't leak. An old or worn flapper can make your dunny flush all on its own, or quietly leak thousands of gallons a year without a peep. Replacin' a flapper's a quick, easy fix that saves you water and the folding stuff. You can also check the owner's manual, if you've still got it, or the manufacturer's website for the right replacement parts.

Showerhead Leaks

A showerhead leakin' 10 drips a minute wastes more than 500 gallons a year. That's the water it takes to run 60 loads through your dishwasher. Some leaky showerheads you can fix by makin' sure the showerhead's screwed on good and tight to the pipe stem, with a bit of plumber's tape round the thread to hold it. Plumber's tape, or teflon tape, you can grab at most hardware shops, it's easy to put on, and it helps keep leaks in check. For trickier valve leaks, where the shower drips when nobody's usin' it, get an experienced handyman or a licensed plumber out.

Tip: While you're in there, it's a beaut idea to check the washer or "o" ring inside the showerhead too, and swap it out if it's had it.
