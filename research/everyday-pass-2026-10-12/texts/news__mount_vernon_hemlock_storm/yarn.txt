So get this. Friday's big winter storm across the north-east of the United States has knocked over a 227-year-old tree at Mount Vernon in Virginia, the estate of the first US President, George Washington. As of yesterday, the folks running the Mount Vernon estate said the Canadian hemlock had been carted off to be preserved and used for special projects.

The hemlock, planted back in 1791, was brought down on Friday by gusts of reportedly up to 70 mph (about 110 km/h). It was blowing dogs off chains, by all accounts.

Mount Vernon was Washington's own place, and he ran most of his two terms as president from there between 1789 and 1797, because the first White House wasn't finished yet. According to the Mount Vernon people, the hemlock is the best documented tree on the whole estate. It was a pressie from New York Governor George Clinton, and it lobbed in a whisky barrel, of all things. And no, the New York governor's no relation to the 42nd President, Bill Clinton, though George Clinton did go on to be Vice President of the United States under Thomas Jefferson and James Madison, between 1805 and 1812.

It's been a rough trot for America's historic trees lately. Others to come down recently include the seventh President Andrew Jackson's southern magnolia at the White House, and the Pioneer Cabin giant sequoia in California's Calaveras Big Trees State Park, the one famous for the car-sized tunnel cut through its trunk.
