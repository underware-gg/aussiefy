Friday's big winter storm across the north-east of the United States has taken out a 227-year-old tree at Mount Vernon, the Virginia estate of the first US President, George Washington. As of yesterday, the people running the Mount Vernon estate said the Canadian hemlock's been collected to be preserved and used for special projects.

The hemlock, planted in 1791, got knocked over on Friday by gusts reportedly up to 70 mph (about 110 kph).

Mount Vernon was Washington's own estate, where he ran most of his two terms as president between 1789–1797 because the first White House wasn't finished yet. According to Mount Vernon officials, the hemlock's the best documented tree on the estate, sent as a gift by New York Governor George Clinton and delivered in a whisky barrel. The New York governor's no relation to the 42nd President Bill Clinton, though George Clinton did go on to become the United States's Vice President under the Thomas Jefferson and James Madison administrations between 1805–1812.

Other historic US trees to come down lately include seventh President Andrew Jackson's southern magnolia at the White House and the Pioneer Cabin giant sequoia in California's Calaveras Big Trees State Park, famous for the car-sized tunnel cut through its trunk.
