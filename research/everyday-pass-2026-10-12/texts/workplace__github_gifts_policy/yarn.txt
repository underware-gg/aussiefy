## Examples of acceptable and unacceptable gifts

So what does “of value” actually mean? Keep in mind that no gifts are OK for government officials (fine, maybe stickers). Always ask them what their rules allow and what they don't. And if you're not sure, run it past Legal to make sure. For example, plenty of U.S. congresspeople won't let you take them out for dinner and pick up the bill.

As for other customers, or people who might become customers, some things might seem like small potatoes to you but mean heaps to someone else. For example, getting your mate's daughter a gig as an intern might not seem like a big deal to you, but it could be worth a lot to them. And if she's not qualified and you pull strings to make it happen, that's not on. Plus, if the idea is to sway someone the wrong way, anything at all, however small, might count as "of value."

🙅 _Here are some other things that aren't ok:_

* First class flights to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️+🍾
* Winery tours 🍷
* Putting in a good word for a friend's kid to get into a flash private school 🎓
* Tickets to Disneyland 👸, the World Cup 🥅, the Olympics 🏅
* A soccer ball ⚽️ signed by a famous player
* A donation to a charity

🙆 _Here is some stuff that is actually ok for customers or prospective customers:_

* Swag
* Economy flights to GitHub offices for Executive Briefings ✈️
* Cheap seats at a sporting event ⚾️
* A fairly priced dinner in town 🍽
* A small box of chocolates as an end of year thank you 🍫

All of the above goes for _taking_ bribes too. So if a supplier, or a business that wants to be one, sends you something “of value,” don't accept it—or at least run it past Legal before you do.

## Process

If you're planning to give or accept an informal pressie (one that isn't our own promo gear, like t-shirts and sticker swag), please make sure you follow the process below.

Keep in mind that what counts as an OK amount can vary. For example, spending $50 on dinner might be a fair bit of money in some countries but not so much in others. So, if in doubt, ask!

1. As a rule of thumb, for any spending over $150, you'll need sign-off (every time) from Legal in writing. And for any pressies you get that are worth over $150, you'll need to tell Legal (every time) in writing. (Legal is only weighing in on the bribery side of it. You might still need to check with Finance for other reasons.)

1. When you put in your expense claim to get paid back, please:
     * Say who the gift went to and what it was for in the notes; and
     * Include a copy of the email approval

## Consequences

So what happens if you don't follow the policy and GitHub ends up shouting a possible customer and his family first-class flights to Japan to go to the Tokyo Olympics? Or if you leave a suitcase full of Euros for a government official in a train station locker you've sorted out beforehand?

FINES and JAIL. And not just for GitHub, either. You could end up doing a few years behind bars yourself, and it won't matter if the customer ever put in an order with GitHub after their trip to Japan, or if the government official even picked up the suitcase of dosh. Basically, it doesn't matter whether you actually _made_ a bribe. Sorry, Yoda, but here it's the trying that counts.
