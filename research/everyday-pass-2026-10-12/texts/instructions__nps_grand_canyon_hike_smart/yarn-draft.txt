Walking the Grand Canyon is a whole different kettle of fish: you've got the heat, the height, and terrain that'll work you over properly, all at once. If you want to enjoy your walk and come home in one piece, you've got to look after your water and your tucker.

Stay Hydrated and Energised

As you walk, especially uphill and out in the full sun, you've got to keep the water up. In the Grand Canyon's hot, dry air your sweat dries off before you know it, so you mightn't realise how much you're losing. Keep an eye out for salt stains or white rings on your gear: that's a dead giveaway you're drying out.

To stay topped up, sip water regularly the whole way, even if you're not thirsty. It's just as important to put back the salts you've sweated out by eating salty snacks, which help your body hang on to water and keep everything in balance. Wondering what the inner canyon rangers chuck in their day packs? A few of their favourite salty snacks are pretzels, Pringles, chips and cheese crackers: tasty, they travel well, and they'll keep you going.

Fuel Your Body for the Journey
Walking the Grand Canyon can knock you about a bit, so make sure you eat enough to keep the engine running. Snacking regularly on a mix of carbs, protein and salts will keep your energy up. Eat and drink even if you're not hungry or thirsty, because your body won't always tell you when the tank's getting low. Getting the balance of water and tucker right is how you avoid ending up knackered and keep your body working at its best.

Stay on top of your water, your food and your rest, and you'll have a much better time of it out on the tracks.

Even if you're eating and drinking right, you still need to stay off the track in the blazing sun during the hottest part of the day. In the sun it's 15F to 20F (9C-11C) hotter than the posted shade temperatures. And keep in mind, the further down into the canyon you go, the hotter it gets!

Plan your day so you're not walking between 10am and 4pm. Take a breather somewhere near shade and water to sit out the worst of the heat. Get going before sunrise and finish up late in the arvo. Seasoned desert walkers know that when you walk is the single biggest thing in staying out of strife. Most of the punters who end up needing emergency medical help in the canyon for heat illness were walking between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m.

KEEP YOURSELF SOAKING WET TO STAY COOL.

One of the best ways to keep your body temperature in check walking the Grand Canyon is to stay soaking wet. The heat, especially down in the inner canyon, can send your core temperature up in a hurry, so grab every chance you get to cool down. Whenever you're near water, whether it's a creek or a water station, take the time to give your clothes and yourself a good soaking.

Wear clothes that hold water well (cotton's a ripper in the desert!), and if you can, wear a hat or a bandanna you can dunk often. It's a simple trick, but it works, and it'll keep you cool and full of beans all day. In the canyon, where it can get bloody hot, take every chance to get wet, because it'll make your walk a whole lot more comfortable. By the end of the day you'll feel the difference, with your body staying cooler and fresher.

Remember, staying soaking wet isn't just about feeling better. It's a crucial way to keep your energy up and avoid heat exhaustion, so don't skip this simple but vital way to keep cool!
