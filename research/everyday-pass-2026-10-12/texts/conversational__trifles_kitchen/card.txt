unharnessed: The strongest: faithful, natural, and keeps "trifles" and the scene's tension, with only small repetition.
  minor: "Well, here's a bloody mess." (for "Here's a nice mess."): "Nice mess" is dry irony. "Bloody mess" is literal.
  minor: "Well, wouldn't it rot your socks, women!" (for 'Well, can you beat the women!'): The idiom is good, but ", women!" sits awkwardly after it.
  minor: 'sticking their noses in and bagging everything' (for 'snooping around and criticising'): "Bagging" recurs a few lines later ("bagging her").

yarn: A natural, well-pitched level-4 retelling. Its main loss is the key word "trifles".
  major: "women are used to frettin' over little things" (for 'women are used to worrying over trifles'): "Trifles" is the play's title and its thematic hinge. Swapping it for "little things" loses that.
  minor: "Well, it's only their job, I s'pose." (for "Of course it's no more than their duty."): "I s'pose" softens Mrs Peters' dutiful certainty.
  minor: "the women don't thaw an inch" (for 'the women do not unbend'): A mixed idiom ("thaw" and "give an inch").
  minor: 'Bugger all of a housekeeper' (for 'Not much of a housekeeper'): "Bugger all of a" is a strained construction.

engine: A mechanical, broken pass. Tags and swears are spliced into stage directions and speaker names, and it wrecks both the format and the tension.
  critical: 'Anyway, HALE:' (for 'HALE: Well, women are used to worrying over trifles.'): A filler is inserted in front of the speaker tag, which breaks the script format. This recurs throughout ("Anyway, MRS HALE:", "Bugger me, MRS HALE:").
  major: 'Crikey, he gets up on a chair' (for 'He gets up on a chair'): An exclamation inside a stage direction makes no sense.
  major: 'would you say, ladies, mate?' (for 'would you say, ladies?'): "Mate" addressed to the women is wrong and comic, and it is stacked after "ladies".
  major: "Blokes's mitts" (for "Men's hands"): "Blokes's" is broken grammar.
  major: 'grotty bloody awful quick' (for 'dirty awful quick'): A pile-up of intensifiers, and swearing out of character for Mrs Hale's stiff reply.
  minor: "didn't get on properly well" (for "didn't get on very well"): An unidiomatic swap.
