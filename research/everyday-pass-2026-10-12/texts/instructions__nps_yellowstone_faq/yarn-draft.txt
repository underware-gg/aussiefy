Where do I book a bed in Yellowstone?
Yellowstone National Park Lodges runs all nine lodges in the park. They're all open from late spring right through autumn, but come winter only two of 'em keep the lights on: Old Faithful Snow Lodge and Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel. Get your booking in early, or you'll be sleepin' in the car!

Do I need a booking just to get into the park?
Nah, you don't need to book your car in. All you need is a park entrance pass. Save yourself a wait at the gate and buy your pass online before you lob. Mind you, summer's flat chat, so expect crowds at the popular spots and queues at the entrance gates, at the roadworks, and wherever somebody's pulled over to gawk at a bison. Be patient, take a breath, and enjoy your visit.

Where can I see the animals?

How'd Yellowstone get its name?
Yellowstone National Park is named after the Yellowstone River, the big river that runs right through it. The story goes, according to French-Canadian trappers in the 1800s, that they asked the Minnetaree people, who live in what's now eastern Montana, what the river was called. The answer was "Mi tse a-da-zi," which word for word comes out as "Rock Yellow River." The trappers turned that into French, "Roche Jaune" or "Pierre Jaune." In 1797 the explorer and geographer David Thompson used the English version, "Yellow Stone." Lewis and Clark called the Yellowstone River by both the French and the English names. After enough people kept using it, the name settled down for good as "Yellowstone."

Is Yellowstone the biggest national park?
Not by a long shot. More than half of Alaska's national park units are bigger, including Wrangell–St. Elias National Park and Preserve, the biggest unit in the whole National Park System at 13 million acres. Up till 1994, Yellowstone (at 2.2 million acres) was the biggest national park in the contiguous United States, the lower 48 that all join up. Then that year Death Valley National Monument got stretched out and made a national park, and it's got more than 3 million acres. So Yellowstone got bumped.

Is Yellowstone the most visited national park?
It's in the top five for the number of punters rocking up for a bit of fun. Great Smoky Mountains National Park usually takes the cake. Head to https://irma.nps.gov/Stats/ for the latest figures on every national park.

What's the difference between a national park and a national forest?
National parks are run by the Department of the Interior, and national forests by the Department of Agriculture. The National Park Service's job is to keep everything just as it is, untouched. The Forest Service's job is to look after the bush sensibly so it can be used for all sorts of things for the long haul. Six national forests wrap right round Yellowstone National Park, so there's a fair bit of bush out there.

How many rangers work in Yellowstone?
About 750 people work for the National Park Service in the busiest stretch of summer. About 392 of them are permanent, on the books all year round. Park rangers work in education, looking after the park's resources, law enforcement, emergency medical services, and work out in the backcountry. Other staff specialise in research, maintenance, management, admin, keeping the trails in shape, fire management, and collecting fees. The whole box and dice, really.

How do you become a park ranger?
Plenty of National Park Service staff start out as volunteers or seasonal workers. Jobs are hotly contested, and the hiring goes through the Office of Personnel Management. You can find out more about working with them.

Can we have a swim in the rivers and lakes?
Wouldn't recommend it. Most of the lakes and creeks are cold enough to be properly dangerous. You'd be in and out quicker than a cat in a bath.

What's the highest peak in the park?
Eagle Peak, down in the south-east of Yellowstone, tops the lot at 11,358 feet.

Why's Yellowstone called a biosphere reserve and a world heritage site?
The United Nations gave Yellowstone National Park both titles to recognise that its natural and cultural treasures matter to the whole world.

What's the Continental Divide?
Think of the Continental Divide as the ridge running along the roof of the continent. In theory, when rain or snow falls on the west side of the Divide, it ends up in the Pacific Ocean sooner or later. When it falls on the east side, it ends up in the Atlantic Ocean. In Yellowstone (same as everywhere else), the ridge doesn't run straight, it wanders all over the shop. You cross the Continental Divide three times between the South Entrance and the Old Faithful area. Craig Pass is the highest crossing, at 8,262 feet.

How'd Mt. Washburn come about?
At 10,219 feet, you can see this peak from all over the park. It's what's left of an extinct volcano from the Absaroka Volcanics, about 50 million years back. The volcano was literally cut in half by an eruption 631,000 years ago. Only the northern half of the old volcano is still standing for you to see.

Does Yellowstone have any federally designated wilderness?
No bloody way, not yet. Most of the park was put forward for it back in 1972, but Congress, their version of Parliament House, still hasn't got round to doing anything about it. Fifty-odd years and countin'.
