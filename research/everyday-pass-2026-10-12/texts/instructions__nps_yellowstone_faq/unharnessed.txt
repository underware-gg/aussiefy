Where do I book a place to stay in Yellowstone?
Yellowstone National Park Lodges runs the nine lodges in the park. They're all open from late spring through autumn, but only two stay open over winter: Old Faithful Snow Lodge and Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel. Get your booking in early, mate!

Do I need a booking to get into the park?
You don't need a vehicle booking to get in – just a park entrance pass. Save yourself some time at the entrance stations by buying your pass online before you rock up. Keep in mind summer's flat out, so be ready for crowds at the popular spots and queues at the entrance gates, through the roadworks, and wherever people have pulled over to gawk at the wildlife. Be patient, and have a ripper of a visit.

How did Yellowstone get its name?
Yellowstone National Park is named after the Yellowstone River, the big river running through the park. According to French-Canadian trappers back in the 1800s, they asked the Minnetaree tribe, who live in what's now eastern Montana, what the river was called. They said "Mi tse a-da-zi," which literally means "Rock Yellow River." The trappers turned that into French as "Roche Jaune" or "Pierre Jaune." In 1797, explorer-geographer David Thompson used the English version—"Yellow Stone." Lewis and Clark called the Yellowstone River by both the French and English names. Over time the name got locked in as "Yellowstone."

Is Yellowstone the biggest national park?
Nah. More than half of Alaska's national park units are bigger, including Wrangell–St. Elias National Park and Preserve, which is the biggest unit of the lot (13 million acres) in the National Park System. Up until 1994, Yellowstone (at 2.2 million acres) was the biggest national park in the contiguous United States. That year Death Valley National Monument got expanded and turned into a national park—it's got more than 3 million acres.

Is Yellowstone the most visited national park?
Yellowstone's in the top five national parks for number of recreational visitors. Great Smoky Mountains National Park usually pulls the biggest crowds. Head to https://irma.nps.gov/Stats/ for up-to-date stats on all the national parks.

What's the difference between a national park and a national forest?
National parks are run by the Department of the Interior and national forests by the Department of Agriculture. The National Park Service's job is to keep resources unimpaired, while the Forest Service's job is to manage resources wisely for heaps of sustainable uses. There's six national forests surrounding Yellowstone National Park.

How many rangers work in Yellowstone?
About 750 people work for the National Park Service in the busy summer season. About 392 of them are permanent, year-round staff. Park rangers work in education, resource management, law enforcement, emergency medical services and backcountry operations. Other staff specialise in research, maintenance, management, admin, track maintenance, fire management and collecting fees.

Can we have a swim in the rivers and lakes?
Swimming's not recommended, because most of the lakes and creeks are dangerously cold – cold enough to freeze the bits off a brass monkey.

What's the highest peak in the park?
Eagle Peak, down in the south-east of Yellowstone, is the highest at 11,358 feet.

Why is Yellowstone called a biosphere reserve and a world heritage site?
The United Nations named Yellowstone National Park a biosphere reserve and a world heritage site because its natural and cultural resources matter to the whole world.

What is the Continental Divide?
Think of the Continental Divide as the ridge along the top of the continent. In theory, when rain or snow falls on the west side of the Divide, it ends up in the Pacific Ocean eventually. When it falls on the east side, it ends up in the Atlantic Ocean. In Yellowstone (same as everywhere else), this ridgeline isn't straight. You cross the Continental Divide three times between the South Entrance and the Old Faithful area. Craig Pass is the highest crossing, at 8,262 feet.

How did Mt. Washburn form?
At 10,219 feet, this peak can be seen from all over the park. It's what's left of an extinct volcano from the Absaroka Volcanics, about 50 million years ago. The volcano got literally sliced in half by an eruption 631,000 years ago. Only the northern bit of the original volcano is still standing.

Does Yellowstone include a federally designated wilderness?
Nope. Most of the park was recommended for it back in 1972, but Congress still hasn't got round to doing anything about it.
