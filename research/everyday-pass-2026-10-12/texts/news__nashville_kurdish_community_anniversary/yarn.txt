So get this. The Metropolitan Council of Nashville and Davidson County, down in Tennessee in the south-east of the States, has gone and made it official: it's marked fifty years since the first mob of Kurdish families landed in the U.S., and given 'em a proper thank-you for everything they've chipped in to the city, culturally, socially and in the hip pocket.

According to an official proclamation VOA got its mitts on, that first lot of Kurdish families fronted up in the United States on May 1, 1976. Plenty of 'em ended up putting down roots in Nashville, Tennessee, and the rest set up their own little communities in cities and states right across the country.

The proclamation makes a point of how hard the Kurdish community's worked to hang on to its language, its culture and its traditions for five decades, all while doing a fair bit of the hard yakka in Nashville's growth and development. Keeping your own ways alive on the other side of the world for half a century? That's no small effort, I reckon.

According to the document, Nashville's now home to more than 20,000 Kurds, which makes it one of the biggest Kurdish communities anywhere in the United States. The Big Smoke's turned into a real hub for Kurdish culture and community life. Not bad for a town most of us only know for country music.

The proclamation also tips its hat to the long friendship between the United States and Kurdistan, singling out the way the Kurdish Peshmerga, U.S. troops and their allies have stood shoulder to shoulder in the fight against terrorism. Game as, the lot of 'em.

Metropolitan Nashville Council member Mike Cortese, who looks after District 4, led the charge on the official recognition, working it through with the other council members.

And the proclamation got signed on October 6, 2026. Fifty years in the making, that one.
