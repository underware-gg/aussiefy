The Metropolitan Council of Nashville and Davidson County, over in the southeastern U.S. state of Tennessee, has gone and officially marked the 50th anniversary of the first mob of Kurdish families landing in the U.S., giving them their dues for everything they've put into the city's cultural, social and economic development.

According to an official proclamation VOA got hold of, the first lot of Kurdish families arrived in the United States on May 1, 1976. Plenty of them later settled in Nashville, Tennessee, while others set up communities in cities and states right across the country.

The proclamation gives a big rap to the Kurdish community for hanging on to their language, culture and traditions over the last five decades, while pulling their weight in Nashville's growth and development.

According to the document, Nashville's now home to more than 20,000 Kurds, which makes it one of the biggest Kurdish communities in the United States. The city's become a proper hub for Kurdish cultural and community life.

The proclamation also gives a nod to the long-running relationship between the United States and Kurdistan, pointing to the Kurdish Peshmerga forces working shoulder to shoulder with U.S. troops and their allies in the fight against terrorism.

Metropolitan Nashville Council member Mike Cortese, who represents District 4, led the official recognition of the anniversary, teaming up with other council members.

The proclamation was signed on October 6, 2026.
